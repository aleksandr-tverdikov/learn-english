# More adjectives: I

> Adjectives I beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### Iberian

**Pronunciation:** /aɪˈbɪriən/ &middot; *y-BEER-ee-uhn*
**Русский:** иберийский
**Type:** origin
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ian from *Iberia*

Relating to Spain and Portugal, or the peninsula they share.

1. Spain and Portugal make up the *Iberian* Peninsula.
2. The restaurant serves *Iberian* ham and cheese.
3. The architecture of the old mission is clearly *Iberian*.

### ice-cold

**Pronunciation:** /ˌaɪsˈkoʊld/ &middot; *ys-KOHLD*
**Русский:** ледяной; холодный, бесстрастный
**Type:** quality; also compound
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — extreme: *already ice-cold*, not *very ice-cold*
**Form:** compound — *ice* + *cold*

Ice-cold has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Extremely cold.** (ледяной, очень холодный)

1. Nothing beats an *ice-cold* lemonade in July.
2. The water in the lake was *ice-cold*.
3. My hands were *ice-cold* after shoveling snow.

**2. Showing no emotion or friendliness; calm under pressure.** (холодный, бесстрастный, хладнокровный)

4. She gave him an *ice-cold* stare.
5. The kicker was *ice-cold* in the final seconds.

### Icelandic

**Pronunciation:** /aɪsˈlændɪk/ &middot; *ys-LAN-dik*
**Русский:** исландский
**Type:** origin
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ic from *Iceland*
**Contrast:** Always a capital letter. The language is *Icelandic* (noun); a person is *an Icelander*.

From or relating to Iceland, its people or its language.

1. We tried *Icelandic* yogurt on our trip.
2. The band is *Icelandic*.
3. *Icelandic* horses are small but very strong.

### icky

**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *ickier*, *ickiest*
**Form:** -y, from a sound of disgust

Informal: unpleasant, sticky or disgusting.

1. There's something *icky* on the bottom of my shoe.
2. The whole situation made me feel *icky*.

### icy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈaɪsi/ &middot; *Y-see*
**Русский:** обледенелый; ледяной
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *icier*, *iciest*
**Form:** -y from *ice*

Icy has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Covered with ice.** (обледенелый, скользкий)

1. Drive slowly — the roads are *icy*.
2. She slipped on the *icy* sidewalk.

**2. Extremely cold.** (ледяной, морозный)

3. An *icy* wind blew off the lake.
4. January is the *iciest* month in Chicago.

**3. Unfriendly and showing no warm feelings.** (ледяной, холодный (о тоне, взгляде))

5. He gave me an *icy* stare.
6. Her voice was polite but *icy*.

### ideal

**Pronunciation:** /aɪˈdiːəl/ &middot; *y-DEE-uhl*
**Русский:** идеальный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — already means 'the best'; *more ideal* is heard in speech but avoided in careful writing
**Takes:** *for* + noun or *-ing*
**Contrast:** *an ideal* is also a noun (a principle). *In an ideal world* means 'if things were perfect', which they are not.

Ideal has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Perfect, or the best possible for a purpose.** (идеальный, самый подходящий)

1. This park is *ideal* for a picnic.
2. She's the *ideal* candidate for the job.
3. The weather was *ideal* for sailing.

**2. Existing only in the imagination, not in reality.** (идеальный, воображаемый)

4. In an *ideal* world, nobody would go hungry.
5. The *ideal* family on TV has nothing to do with real life.

### idle

**Pronunciation:** /ˈaɪdəl/ &middot; *Y-duhl*
**Русский:** простаивающий; праздный; пустой
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *idle* gossip, threats and curiosity only before a noun
**Comparison:** more/most — *more idle*, *most idle*
**Opposite:** *busy*
**Contrast:** Sounds exactly like *idol* (кумир). *To idle* is also a verb: *the engine idled*.

Idle has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Not working or not being used.** (простаивающий, незанятый)

1. Half the machines in the factory are *idle*.
2. The land has been sitting *idle* for years.
3. Thousands of *idle* workers are waiting for the strike to end.

**2. Lazy; spending time doing nothing useful.** (праздный, ленивый)

4. The novel is about rich, *idle* young people in New York.
5. He never stays *idle* for long.

**3. Without real purpose or result.** (пустой, праздный)

6. It was just *idle* gossip.
7. I asked out of *idle* curiosity.
8. Don't make *idle* threats to your kids.

### ignorant

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪɡnərənt/ &middot; *IG-nur-uhnt*
**Русский:** невежественный; не знающий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more ignorant*, *most ignorant*
**Takes:** *of* or *about* + noun
**Form:** -ant from *ignore* (in its old sense 'not know')
**Opposite:** *knowledgeable*, *well-informed*
**Contrast:** Not 'ignoring': *ignorant* means not knowing. Someone who pays no attention on purpose *ignores* you.

Ignorant has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having little knowledge or education.** (невежественный, необразованный)

1. He made some *ignorant* comments about immigrants.
2. People are often *ignorant* when it comes to mental illness.
3. I was more *ignorant* about politics at twenty than I like to admit.

**2. Not knowing about a particular fact or situation.** (не знающий, не осведомлённый)

4. Most voters are *ignorant* of the details of the bill.
5. She was completely *ignorant* about what her husband had done.

### illicit

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈlɪsɪt/ &middot; *ih-LIS-it*
**Русский:** запрещённый; тайный, запретный
**Type:** classifying
