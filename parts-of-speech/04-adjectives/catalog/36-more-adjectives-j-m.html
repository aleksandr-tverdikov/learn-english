More adjectives: J–M#

Adjectives J–M beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after be, and they compare with -er or more. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

Jammed has 2 distinct senses.

  1. He writes about the mystical traditions of medieval Spain.
  2. She had a mystical experience during the retreat.

2. Strange and full of awe, as if beyond ordinary explanation. (таинственный, загадочный)

  1. There's something mystical about the light in that valley.
  2. The old house has a mystical pull on him.

Mythical has 2 distinct senses.

1. Coming from myths and old stories. (мифический)

  1. The griffin is a mythical creature, half eagle and half lion.
  2. He collects stories about mythical beasts.

2. Talked about but not real. (вымышленный, несуществующий)

  1. That mythical raise never showed up.
  2. Everyone keeps chasing the mythical perfect job.
