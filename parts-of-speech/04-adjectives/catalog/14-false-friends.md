# False friends for Russian speakers

> *actual*, *sympathetic*, *accurate*, *intelligent* — English adjectives that look Russian and mean something else.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These look like Russian words and mean something else. *Actual* is not *актуальный* — it means *real* — and *sympathetic* is not *симпатичный*, it means *understanding*. *Accurate* means *precise*, not *аккуратный*; *intelligent* means *clever*, not *интеллигентный*. Each entry puts the trap first and gives the English word that does mean what the Russian one does.

---
### actual

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæktʃuəl/ &middot; *AK-choo-uhl*
**Русский:** действительный, фактический
**Type:** classifying; also emphasizing (*the actual ceremony*)
**Position:** attributive only — *the actual cost*; not *the cost was actual*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -al from *act*; the adverb *actually* means на самом деле
**Contrast:** Not *актуальный*. *actual* = real, the one that exists or happened, as opposed to what was planned, expected or imagined (действительный, фактический). For *актуальный* say *relevant*, *current*, *timely* or *pressing*: *актуальная тема* = *a timely (relevant) topic*, *актуальная информация* = *up-to-date information*, *это уже не актуально* = *that's no longer relevant*. The adverb *actually* is на самом деле, вообще-то — never актуально. *In actual fact* sounds British and formal; Americans say *in fact* or *actually*. *actual* goes only before a noun.

Actual has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Real; existing or happening in fact, as opposed to what was planned, expected or imagined.** (действительный, фактический, реальный)

1. The *actual* cost of the repairs was almost twice the estimate.
2. Is that based on an *actual* study, or did you just read it online?
3. The movie is loosely based on *actual* events.

**2. The thing itself — the exact or main one, as opposed to what comes before it or around it.** (сам, самый; настоящий)

4. The reception lasted all night, but the *actual* ceremony took twenty minutes.
5. Those were his *actual* words — I'm not making this up.
6. We drove around for an hour before we found the *actual* entrance.

### sympathetic

**Pronunciation:** /ˌsɪmpəˈθɛtɪk/ &middot; *sim-puh-THET-ik*
**Русский:** сочувствующий, отзывчивый
**Type:** emotion; also evaluative (*a sympathetic character*)
**Position:** both — the nervous-system sense is attributive only: *the sympathetic nervous system*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more sympathetic*, *most sympathetic*
**Takes:** *to* or *toward* + a person or their trouble (понимающий); *to* or *toward* + an idea or cause (поддерживающий); *about* + a problem
**Preposition patterns:** [sympathetic to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#sympathetic-to)
**Form:** -etic from *sympathy*
**Opposite:** *unsympathetic*
**Contrast:** Not *симпатичный*. *sympathetic* = understanding and kind about someone's problems (сочувствующий, отзывчивый), or in favor of an idea. *He's very sympathetic* does not mean he is good-looking or nice to be around. For *симпатичный* say *cute*, *attractive* or *good-looking* (о внешности) and *nice* or *likable* (о человеке). What follows *to* changes the meaning: *sympathetic to her* (сочувствует ей) but *sympathetic to the plan* (одобряет план). In a movie or novel, *a sympathetic character* is one the audience likes and cares about — the one place where it comes close to симпатичный.

Sympathetic has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Showing that you understand and care about someone's problems or pain.** (сочувствующий, отзывчивый, участливый)

1. My boss was really *sympathetic* when my dad got sick.
2. She gave me a *sympathetic* smile.
3. The nurses couldn't have been *more sympathetic*.

**2. Agreeing with or supporting an idea, a group or a cause.** (благосклонный, поддерживающий)

4. Several senators are *sympathetic* to the proposal.
5. The judge seemed *sympathetic* toward the union's side.

**3. Of a character in a story, movie or show: easy to like, so that you care what happens to them.** (вызывающий симпатию, располагающий)

6. The writers made the villain surprisingly *sympathetic*.
7. It's hard to write a *sympathetic* hero who lies to everyone.

**4. In medicine: relating to the part of the nervous system that gets the body ready for action.** (симпатический)

8. Stress switches on the *sympathetic* nervous system.
9. The drug blocks *sympathetic* nerve signals to the heart.

### accurate

**Pronunciation:** /ˈækjərət/ &middot; *AK-yur-uht*
**Русский:** точный, верный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more accurate*, *most accurate*
**Form:** from Latin *accuratus* 'done with care' — English kept 'exact', Russian kept 'neat'
**Opposite:** *inaccurate*
**Contrast:** Not *аккуратный*. *accurate* = correct and exact, without mistakes (точный, верный). For *аккуратный* say *neat* or *tidy* (о вещах, почерке, внешности) and *careful* (о работе и человеке): *аккуратный почерк* = *neat handwriting*, *аккуратный водитель* = *a careful driver*, *он очень аккуратный* = *he's very neat*. *She's very accurate* would mean her answers or her aim are exact, not that she keeps things tidy. The noun is *accuracy* (точность).

Accurate has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Correct and true in every detail; free from mistakes.** (точный, верный, правильный)

1. Is this map still *accurate*?
2. The article gives an *accurate* picture of life in a small town.
3. The new tests are much *more accurate* than the old ones.

**2. Able to hit a target or to measure something exactly.** (точный, меткий)

4. He's the *most accurate* passer in the league.
5. My kitchen scale isn't very *accurate*.
6. You need an *accurate* throw to get the runner out at first.

### decent

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdiːsənt/ &middot; *DEE-suhnt*
**Русский:** приличный, порядочный
**Type:** evaluative; also quality
**Position:** both — the 'dressed' sense is predicative only: *Are you decent?*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more decent*, *most decent*
**Takes:** *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*decent of you to call*); *to* + person (*He's been decent to me*)
**Opposite:** *indecent* (only for the 'proper' sense — непристойный)
**Contrast:** *decent* is praise with a limit. *The food was decent* means неплохая, вполне нормальная — acceptable, not excellent; for отличная say *great* or *excellent*. *A decent salary* is one you can live on, not a big one. Said of a person, *decent* is порядочный — honest and kind — not *достойный* in the sense of 'distinguished'. The opposite *indecent* is only непристойный: a mediocre meal is not *indecent*. Do not mix up *decent* (DEE-suhnt) with the noun *descent* (dih-SENT, спуск, происхождение).

Decent has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Good enough; of an acceptable or fairly good quality, size or amount.** (приличный, неплохой, нормальный)

1. Is there a *decent* coffee shop around here?
2. He makes a *decent* living as a plumber.
3. The seats were pretty *decent* for the price.

**2. Honest, fair and kind; morally good.** (порядочный, честный, хороший)

4. Her father is a *decent*, hardworking man.
5. It was *decent* of you to drive me all the way home.
6. He's the *most decent* guy I've ever worked for.

**3. Proper and socially acceptable; not likely to shock or offend.** (приличный, пристойный)

7. He was told to wear something *decent* to court.
8. Wait until a *decent* hour to call them — don't wake them up at six.

**4. Informal: dressed enough to be seen by other people.** (одетый, в приличном виде)

9. Can I come in? Are you *decent*?
10. Give me a minute — I'm not *decent* yet.

### intelligent

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈtɛlɪdʒənt/ &middot; *in-TEL-ih-juhnt*
**Русский:** умный, сообразительный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more intelligent*, *most intelligent*
**Form:** noun *intelligence*
**Opposite:** *unintelligent*, *stupid*
**Contrast:** Not *интеллигентный*. *intelligent* = smart, quick to learn and understand (умный). It says nothing about manners, education or culture — a rude, uneducated person can be very *intelligent*. For *интеллигентный* say *cultured*, *well-educated*, *refined* or *well-mannered*; *интеллигенция* is *the intelligentsia* or *educated people*. *intellectual* is someone who lives by ideas and books (интеллектуал). In everyday American speech *smart* is more common than *intelligent*.

Intelligent has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Good at learning, understanding and thinking; clever.** (умный, сообразительный)

1. She's one of the *most intelligent* students I've ever taught.
2. Dolphins are highly *intelligent* animals.
3. He's *intelligent* but lazy.

**2. Of a question, choice or remark: showing good thinking or judgment.** (разумный, толковый, умный)

4. That's a very *intelligent* question.
5. Selling the house before prices dropped was an *intelligent* move.

**3. Able to think and understand, as humans can.** (разумный)

6. Are we the only *intelligent* species in the universe?
7. Scientists are still looking for signs of *intelligent* life in space.

**4. Of a machine or system: using a computer to react to conditions and make simple decisions on its own.** (интеллектуальный, «умный»)

8. The car has an *intelligent* braking system that stops it before a crash.
9. *Intelligent* thermostats learn when you're usually home.

### sensible

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɛnsəbəl/ &middot; *SEN-suh-buhl*
**Русский:** разумный, благоразумный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more sensible*, *most sensible*
**Takes:** *It is sensible* + *to*-infinitive; *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*sensible of you to wait*)
**Form:** -ible from *sense* 'good judgment'
**Opposite:** *foolish*, *silly*
**Contrast:** *sensible* is not *sensitive*. *sensible* = reasonable and practical, showing good sense (разумный, благоразумный); *чувствительный* is *sensitive*: *sensitive skin*, *a sensitive child*. *He's very sensible* means he makes wise, practical choices, not that he is easily hurt. The nouns split the same way: *sense* (здравый смысл) goes with *sensible*, *sensitivity* with *sensitive*; *sensibility* is taste and feeling (*an artistic sensibility*). In old or very formal writing, *sensible of* means *aware of* (*sensible of the honor*); do not use it in everyday English.

Sensible has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Showing good judgment; reasonable and practical.** (разумный, благоразумный, здравый)

1. Wearing a helmet is the *sensible* thing to do.
2. She's a *sensible* kid — she won't do anything stupid.
3. It would be *more sensible* to wait until spring.

**2. Of clothes and shoes: practical and comfortable rather than fashionable.** (практичный, удобный)

4. Wear *sensible* shoes — we'll be walking all day.
5. She traded her heels for a pair of *sensible* flats.

### ambitious

**Pronunciation:** /æmˈbɪʃəs/ &middot; *am-BISH-uhs*
**Русский:** целеустремлённый, честолюбивый; масштабный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more ambitious*, *most ambitious*
**Takes:** *for* + person (*ambitious for her son*) or thing (*ambitious for power*); *to*-infinitive (*ambitious to succeed*)
**Form:** -ous from *ambition*
**Opposite:** *unambitious*; *modest* (a plan)
**Contrast:** Russian *амбициозный* often sounds negative — proud, touchy, full of oneself — and *с амбициями* can mean vain. English *ambitious* is usually positive or neutral: it means wanting to achieve a lot (целеустремлённый). For the negative Russian sense say *arrogant*, *conceited*, *full of himself* or *self-important*. *An ambitious plan* is about size, not vanity — it is big and hard to carry out; *too ambitious* or *overly ambitious* means it probably can't be done.

Ambitious has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having a strong wish to succeed, to become important, or to get rich or powerful.** (целеустремлённый, честолюбивый)

1. She's smart and *ambitious*, and she wants to run her own company someday.
2. *Ambitious* young lawyers often work eighty hours a week.
3. His parents are very *ambitious* for him.

**2. Of a plan, goal or project: large and needing a lot of effort, money or skill to succeed.** (масштабный, смелый, грандиозный)

4. The city has an *ambitious* plan to plant a million trees.
5. Finishing the whole house in a month was probably too *ambitious*.
6. This is the band's *most ambitious* album yet.

### pathetic

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈθɛtɪk/ &middot; *puh-THET-ik*
**Русский:** жалкий
**Type:** evaluative; also emotion
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more pathetic*, *most pathetic*
**Form:** -etic from Greek *pathos* 'suffering'
**Contrast:** Not *патетический* and not *пафосный*. *pathetic* = pitiful (жалкий), and in everyday American speech it is usually an insult: *That's pathetic!* = Жалкое зрелище! Позорище! For *патетический* (passionate, lofty, solemn) say *impassioned*, *passionate* or *solemn*: *патетическая речь* = *an impassioned speech*. For *пафосный* (showy, pretentious) say *pompous*, *pretentious* or *over the top*. Calling a speech *pathetic* says it was terrible, not emotional.

Pathetic has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Informal: so bad, weak or useless that it makes you feel contempt or annoyance.** (жалкий, никудышный, позорный)

1. That's the *most pathetic* excuse I've ever heard.
2. Our defense was *pathetic* last night.
3. Only three people showed up? That's *pathetic*.

**2. Making you feel pity or sadness.** (жалкий, вызывающий жалость, жалобный)

4. The *pathetic* little puppy was shaking in the rain.
5. There was something *pathetic* about the old man eating alone every night.

### dramatic

**Pronunciation:** /drəˈmætɪk/ &middot; *druh-MAT-ik*
**Русский:** резкий, разительный; эффектный; театральный
**Type:** quality; also classifying (*dramatic arts*)
**Position:** both — the theater sense is attributive only: *dramatic arts*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more dramatic*, *most dramatic*
**Form:** -ic from *drama*
**Contrast:** Russian *драматический* and *драматичный* often mean tragic or full of suffering. English *dramatic* most often means sudden and big — *a dramatic change* (резкое, разительное изменение), *a dramatic improvement* — and the change can be a good one. For *драматическая ситуация* say *a tragic*, *dire* or *desperate situation*. *Don't be so dramatic* = Не устраивай драму, не преувеличивай. *драматический театр* is simply *a theater*.

Dramatic has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Of a change or event: sudden, large and very noticeable.** (резкий, разительный, значительный)

1. There's been a *dramatic* drop in crime downtown.
2. The new coach made a *dramatic* difference.
3. The rise in rents has been *more dramatic* here than anywhere else in the state.

**2. Exciting and impressive to watch or look at.** (захватывающий, эффектный)

4. The movie ends with a *dramatic* rescue at sea.
5. The house has *dramatic* views of the canyon.

**3. Of a person or behavior: showing feelings in an exaggerated way, to get attention.** (театральный, показной)

6. Stop being so *dramatic* — it's just a scratch.
7. She made a *dramatic* exit, slamming the door behind her.

**4. Relating to plays, acting or the theater.** (драматический, театральный)

8. He studied *dramatic* arts in college.
9. The festival includes *dramatic* readings of new plays.

### concrete

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːnkriːt, kɑːnˈkriːt/ &middot; *KAHN-kreet, kahn-KREET*
**Русский:** конкретный, определённый
**Type:** quality; also classifying (*a concrete noun*)
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more concrete*, *most concrete*
**Opposite:** *vague* (sense 1); *abstract* (sense 2)
**Contrast:** Russian *конкретный* is much wider than *concrete*. Use *concrete* with *evidence*, *proof*, *plans*, *proposals*, *steps*, *examples* and *results* — things that are definite instead of vague. For one particular thing or person say *specific* or *particular*: *конкретная дата* = *a specific date*, *конкретный человек* = *a particular person*, *в конкретном случае* = *in this particular case*, *конкретно* = *specifically*. Slang *конкретный* ('real, serious') has no match in *concrete*. In *a concrete wall* (бетонная стена) the word is the noun *concrete* used as a modifier, not this adjective.

Concrete has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Based on facts, clearly stated and definite; not vague or general.** (конкретный, определённый, реальный)

1. The police have no *concrete* evidence against him.
2. Our plans for the summer aren't *concrete* yet.
3. Could you give me a *more concrete* example?

**2. Existing as a real thing that can be seen and touched; not abstract.** (материальный, вещественный, конкретный)

4. Young children learn math faster with *concrete* objects like blocks.
5. A *concrete* noun names something you can see or touch, like a chair or a dog.

### delicate

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɛlɪkət/ &middot; *DEL-ih-kuht*
**Русский:** хрупкий, нежный; щекотливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more delicate*, *most delicate*
**Opposite:** *sturdy*, *robust*
**Contrast:** Not *деликатный* about a person. *a delicate person* is physically weak or easily hurt (хрупкий, болезненный), not tactful. For *деликатный человек* say *tactful*, *considerate* or *discreet*: *он очень деликатный* = *he's very tactful*. The words overlap only for situations: *a delicate matter* = деликатный, щекотливый вопрос. *деликатес* is the noun *a delicacy*.

Delicate has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Easily broken or damaged; needing careful handling.** (хрупкий, нежный)

1. Wash *delicate* fabrics by hand.
2. Babies have very *delicate* skin.
3. These old photos are too *delicate* to touch.

**2. Of a person or their health: weak and likely to get sick.** (слабый, болезненный, хрупкий)

4. She was a *delicate* child who missed a lot of school.
5. Grandpa's health is *delicate*, so we keep our visits short.

**3. Of a situation or subject: needing tact and care, because it could easily cause trouble or hurt feelings.** (деликатный, щекотливый)

6. Money is a *delicate* subject in our family.
7. The peace talks are at a *delicate* stage.
8. This is going to need a *more delicate* approach.

**4. Small, fine and graceful.** (изящный, тонкий)

9. She has *delicate* features and dark eyes.
10. The earrings hang from a *delicate* gold chain.

**5. Of a taste, smell or color: light and pleasant, not strong.** (тонкий, нежный, мягкий)

11. This fish has a *delicate* flavor, so go easy on the sauce.
12. The bedroom is painted a *delicate* shade of blue.

**6. Of work or an instrument: needing great skill and precision, or precise enough to notice very small changes.** (тонкий, точный, чувствительный)

13. Removing the tumor was a *delicate* operation.
14. These *delicate* instruments can detect the smallest movement.

### spectacular

**Pronunciation:** /spɛkˈtækjələr/ &middot; *spek-TAK-yuh-lur*
**Русский:** потрясающий, зрелищный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more spectacular*, *most spectacular*
**Form:** -ar from *spectacle*
**Contrast:** It has nothing to do with *спектакль*, which is *a play*, *a show* or *a performance*. *spectacular* = amazing, stunning, very impressive to see (потрясающий, зрелищный). *The play was spectacular* means it was wonderful, not that it was theatrical. *A spectacular failure* is a huge, very public one. As a noun, *a spectacular* is a big, showy TV or stage show.

Spectacular has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very impressive and exciting to see; stunning.** (потрясающий, зрелищный, эффектный)

1. The view from the top of the trail is *spectacular*.
2. This year's Fourth of July fireworks were *more spectacular* than ever.
3. She scored a *spectacular* goal from midfield.

**2. Unusually great in size or degree, often suddenly so.** (ошеломительный, грандиозный)

4. The company's first year was a *spectacular* success.
5. His run for mayor ended in *spectacular* failure.
6. The town has seen *spectacular* growth since the factory opened.

### prospective

**Pronunciation:** /prəˈspɛktɪv/ &middot; *pruh-SPEK-tiv*
**Русский:** будущий, потенциальный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — *prospective buyers*; not *the buyers are prospective*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ive from *prospect*
**Contrast:** Not *перспективный*. *prospective* = likely to become something or to happen in the future (будущий, потенциальный): *a prospective student* is someone thinking of applying, not a promising one. For *перспективный* say *promising*, *up-and-coming* or *with a future*: *перспективный молодой игрок* = *a promising young player*, *перспективное направление* = *a promising field*. Do not confuse it with the noun *perspective* (точка зрения; перспектива в рисунке).

Prospective has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Likely or expected to become a particular kind of person in the future.** (будущий, потенциальный)

1. The realtor is showing the house to *prospective* buyers this weekend.
2. *Prospective* students can tour the campus on Fridays.
3. She met her *prospective* in-laws for the first time at Thanksgiving.

**2. Expected or likely to happen; relating to the future.** (предполагаемый, ожидаемый, предстоящий)

4. The report looks at the *prospective* costs of a new stadium.
5. Researchers ran a *prospective* study that followed 5,000 patients for ten years.

### genial

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒiːniəl/ &middot; *JEE-nee-uhl*
**Русский:** добродушный, приветливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more genial*, *most genial*
**Contrast:** Not *гениальный*. *genial* = friendly and cheerful (добродушный, приветливый), and it describes people and their manner, not ideas. For *гениальный* say *brilliant* or *ingenious*: *гениальная идея* = *a brilliant idea*, *гениальный изобретатель* = *a brilliant (ingenious) inventor*; informally, *That's genius!* *A genial idea* is not English. The first syllable is JEE, not GEN.

Genial has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Friendly, cheerful and pleasant to be with.** (добродушный, приветливый, радушный)

1. Our *genial* host made sure everyone had a drink.
2. He has a *genial* smile and a firm handshake.
3. Her husband is *more genial* than she is, and everyone likes him.

**2. Literary: of weather or a climate, pleasantly mild and warm.** (мягкий, тёплый)

4. They moved to Arizona for its *genial* climate.
5. The *genial* spring sun brought everyone out to the park.

### artistic

**Pronunciation:** /ɑːrˈtɪstɪk/ &middot; *ahr-TIS-tik*
**Русский:** художественный; творчески одарённый
**Type:** classifying; also quality (*a very artistic child*)
**Position:** both — the 'relating to art' sense is attributive only: *artistic director*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more artistic*, *most artistic*
**Form:** -ic from *artist*
**Contrast:** Russian *артистичный* and *артистический* come from *артист* — a performer or actor. English *artistic* comes from *artist* — above all a painter, sculptor or other creative person — and means good at art or creative. For *артистичный* say *theatrical*, *a natural performer* or *has stage presence*; *артистическая уборная* is *a dressing room*. Informal *artsy* (American) is often mocking: *an artsy coffee shop*.

Artistic has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Relating to art or artists.** (художественный, творческий)

1. The director was given complete *artistic* freedom.
2. The painting has more *artistic* value than market value.

**2. Of a person: naturally good at art, or creative in other ways.** (художественно одарённый, творческий)

3. Both of her kids are very *artistic* — they're always drawing.
4. I'm not *artistic* at all; I can't even draw a straight line.
5. He's *more artistic* than his brother, who's into math.

**3. Made or arranged with skill and good taste; attractive.** (художественный, изящный, со вкусом)

6. The cake had an *artistic* design of sugar flowers.
7. The chef arranges every plate in an *artistic* way.

### sophisticated

**Pronunciation:** /səˈfɪstɪkeɪtɪd/ &middot; *suh-FIS-tih-kay-tid*
**Русский:** искушённый, утончённый; сложный, современный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more sophisticated*, *most sophisticated*
**Form:** -ed adjective, pronounced /ɪd/; there is no everyday verb *sophisticate*
**Opposite:** *unsophisticated*, *naive* (senses 1 and 3); *primitive*, *crude* (sense 2)
**Contrast:** It is not about *софистика* (sophistry — clever but dishonest arguing), and it is not simply *complicated*. *sophisticated* is usually praise: of a person, worldly and cultured (искушённый, утончённый); of technology, advanced and well designed (сложный, современный). *sophisticated software* is advanced; *complicated software* is hard to use. For the negative *изощрённый* (*изощрённая жестокость*) say *elaborate* or *calculated*, not *sophisticated*. The noun *a sophisticate* means a sophisticated person.

Sophisticated has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Of a person, place or style: knowing a lot about the world, culture and fashion; elegant.** (искушённый, утончённый, изысканный)

1. She was the *most sophisticated* woman in our small town.
2. The restaurant has a *sophisticated*, grown-up feel.
3. Four years in New York made him a lot *more sophisticated*.

**2. Of a machine, system or method: advanced and complex.** (сложный, передовой, современный)

4. The bank uses *sophisticated* software to spot fraud.
5. Today's phones are far *more sophisticated* than the computers that went to the moon.

**3. Of a person's thinking or an audience: able to understand difficult ideas; not simple or naive.** (тонкий, глубокий; подготовленный)

6. Voters are more *sophisticated* than politicians think.
7. The novel offers a *sophisticated* look at race and class.

### extravagant

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈstrævəɡənt/ &middot; *ik-STRAV-uh-guhnt*
**Русский:** расточительный; непомерный; пышный
**Type:** quality; also evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more extravagant*, *most extravagant*
**Takes:** *with* + money or time (*extravagant with money*); *in* + praise or claims (*extravagant in their praise*)
**Form:** *extra-* 'beyond' + Latin *vagari* 'to wander'
**Opposite:** *thrifty*, *frugal* (sense 1)
**Contrast:** Not *экстравагантный* in its usual Russian sense — eccentric, unusual, outlandish. English *extravagant* is mainly about money and excess: *an extravagant gift* costs too much, *an extravagant person* spends too much (расточительный). For *экстравагантный* say *eccentric*, *flamboyant*, *outlandish* or *unconventional*: *экстравагантный наряд* = *a flamboyant (outlandish) outfit*, *экстравагантный человек* = *an eccentric*. The two words meet only in sense 3, where a party or costume is both costly and showy.

Extravagant has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Spending much more money than is wise, or costing much more than is wise.** (расточительный; дорогостоящий)

1. They live an *extravagant* lifestyle on a teacher's salary.
2. A new car is an *extravagant* birthday present for a sixteen-year-old.
3. He was never *extravagant* with money.

**2. Of claims, praise or promises: far more than is reasonable or true.** (непомерный, преувеличенный)

4. Be careful of *extravagant* claims about weight-loss pills.
5. The critics were *extravagant* in their praise.

**3. Very elaborate and showy.** (пышный, роскошный, броский)

6. It was the *most extravagant* wedding I've ever been to.
7. She wore an *extravagant* hat covered in feathers.

### comprehensive

**Pronunciation:** /ˌkɑːmprɪˈhɛnsɪv/ &middot; *kahm-prih-HEN-siv*
**Русский:** всеобъемлющий, исчерпывающий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — the insurance sense is attributive only: *comprehensive coverage*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more comprehensive*, *most comprehensive*
**Form:** -ive from *comprehend* in its old sense 'take in, include'
**Opposite:** *partial*, *limited*
**Contrast:** Not *comprehensible* (понятный). *comprehensive* = complete, covering everything (всеобъемлющий, исчерпывающий): *a comprehensive report* is long and thorough, not easy to understand. Russian *комплексный* is often *comprehensive* in English — *комплексный подход* = *a comprehensive approach*, *комплексное обследование* = *a comprehensive checkup* — and not *complex*, which means сложный. In American car insurance, *comprehensive coverage* pays for damage that does not come from a collision: theft, hail, a falling tree.

Comprehensive has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Including all or almost all the facts, parts or details; complete and thorough.** (всеобъемлющий, исчерпывающий, полный)

1. The list of trails on the website isn't *comprehensive*, but it covers the popular ones.
2. Graduate students take *comprehensive* exams at the end of their second year.
3. It's the *most comprehensive* guide to the city I've seen.

**2. Of insurance: covering most kinds of loss; in American car insurance, covering damage not caused by a collision.** (полный, комплексный (о страховке))

4. My *comprehensive* coverage paid for the hail damage on the car.
5. If the car is stolen, you'll only get paid if you have *comprehensive* insurance.

### complex

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːmplɛks, kɑːmˈplɛks/ &middot; *KAHM-pleks, kahm-PLEKS*
**Русский:** сложный
**Type:** quality; also classifying (*complex numbers*)
**Position:** both — the technical sense is attributive only: *complex numbers*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more complex*, *most complex*
**Opposite:** *simple*
**Contrast:** Not *комплексный*. *complex* = made of many parts and hard to understand (сложный). For *комплексный* say *comprehensive*, *integrated* or *combined*: *комплексный подход* = *a comprehensive approach*, *комплексные меры* = *a package of measures*, *комплексный обед* = *a lunch special*. Only in math is *complex number* = комплексное число. As a noun, *a complex* is a group of buildings (*a sports complex*) or a psychological problem (*an inferiority complex*); *у него комплексы* is more naturally *he's insecure* or *he has hang-ups*. *complex* and *complicated* are close: *complicated* is more everyday and suggests confusion; *complex* is more neutral and formal. Never *complexer*.

Complex has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Made of many connected parts, and so difficult to understand or deal with.** (сложный, запутанный)

1. Climate change is a *complex* problem with no easy answer.
2. The tax code gets *more complex* every year.
3. The human brain is the *most complex* thing we know of.

**2. In science, math and grammar: made of two or more parts joined together, as in *complex numbers* or *complex sentences*.** (комплексный, сложный, составной)

4. Whole grains are full of *complex* carbohydrates.
5. A *complex* sentence has a main clause and at least one dependent clause.

### eventual

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈvɛntʃuəl/ &middot; *ih-VEN-choo-uhl*
**Русский:** окончательный, конечный, итоговый
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — *the eventual winner*; not *the winner was eventual*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -al from *event* in its old sense 'outcome'
**Contrast:** Not *эвентуальный* (possible, in case something happens). *eventual* = final, coming at the end of a long process: *the eventual winner* is the one who won in the end. For *эвентуальный* or *возможный* say *possible* or *potential*: *эвентуальные риски* = *possible risks*. The adverb has the same trap: *eventually* = в конце концов, в итоге — never возможно (*possibly*).

Happening or existing at the end of a process or a period of time.

1. The Giants, the *eventual* champions, lost their first three games.
2. The *eventual* cost of the bridge was three times the original budget.
3. Doctors hope the drug will lead to an *eventual* cure.
