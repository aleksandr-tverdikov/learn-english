Colors, shapes and materials#

gray, navy, oval, wooden — the adjectives that sit last before the noun.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Colors, shapes and materials sit near the end of the adjective order, just before the noun: a big round wooden table. Colors compare, and some shapes only loosely (rounder is fine; more square is common). For materials, English often uses the noun itself (a cotton shirt, a glass door) rather than an adjective; the entries here are the true adjectives, such as wooden and woolen, and they note where the noun is the normal choice.

black#

Pronunciation
/blæk/ · BLAK
Русский
чёрный; чернокожий; мрачный
Type
color; also classifying (people)
Position
both — a black car, The car is black
Comparison
-er/-est — blacker, blackest
Opposite
white
Contrast
For people, American newspapers now capitalize Black: Black voters. Use it as an adjective, never as a noun: a Black man, not a black, which sounds offensive; Negro is outdated and offensive too, so Russian негр has no neutral English equivalent. A black eye is a bruised eye (синяк под глазом), and black and blue means covered in bruises. For humor, dark is now more common than black: dark humor.

Black has 7 distinct senses.

1. Of the darkest color, like coal or the night sky. (чёрный)

  1. She wore a long black dress to the gala.
  2. My car is black, so it shows every speck of dust.
  3. Her hair looked even blacker under the stage lights.

2. Having little or no light; completely dark. (тёмный, кромешный)

  1. It was pitch black in the basement.
  2. The windows were black — nobody was home.
  3. The blackest skies are in the desert, far from city lights.

3. Belonging to a group of people with dark skin, especially of African descent; usually written Black. (чернокожий)

  1. She was the first Black woman elected to the city council.
  2. The museum tells the story of Black families in Chicago.

4. Of coffee or tea: served without milk or cream. (чёрный (без молока и сливок))

  1. I take my coffee black, no sugar.
  2. A black coffee and a blueberry muffin, please.

5. Angry, gloomy or without hope; full of bad feeling. (мрачный, злой, безнадёжный)

  1. He's been in a black mood all morning.
  2. She gave me a black look when I mentioned her ex.
  3. After the plant closed, the town's future looked black.

6. Evil or harmful; connected with the devil. (чёрный, злой)

  1. The villagers believed she practiced black magic.
  2. The villain is a man with a black heart.

7. Of humor or comedy: finding something funny in death, suffering or disaster. (чёрный (о юморе))

  1. The show is a black comedy about a family funeral home.
  2. The play mixes black humor with real grief.

white#

Pronunciation
/waɪt/ · WYT
Русский
белый; бледный; седой
Type
color; also classifying (people)
Position
both — a white shirt, The shirt is white; a white lie only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — whiter, whitest
Opposite
black
Contrast
White hair is седые волосы, and go white means turn pale from shock. A white lie is a small, polite lie (невинная ложь), not a serious one. Americans say coffee with cream or coffee with milk; white coffee is British. For people, American style usually writes white in lowercase.

White has 7 distinct senses.

1. Of the color of snow or milk. (белый)

  1. We painted the kitchen white to make it look bigger.
  2. He was wearing a white T-shirt and jeans.
  3. This toothpaste promises whiter teeth in two weeks.

2. Belonging to a group of people with light skin, especially of European descent. (белый (о расе))

  1. Most of the town's residents are white.
  2. The survey asked white voters and Black voters the same questions.

3. Pale in the face from fear, shock or illness. (бледный, побелевший)

  1. He went white when he saw the bill.
  2. She was white as a sheet after the accident.

4. Of hair: completely gray from age. (седой)

  1. My grandfather's hair turned white in his sixties.
  2. A woman with short white hair answered the door.

5. Of wine, bread or meat: lighter in color than other kinds. (белый, светлый)

  1. Would you like red or white wine with the fish?
  2. Chicken breast is white meat.
  3. The kids will only eat white bread.

6. Harmless and told to be kind or polite, in a white lie. (невинный (о лжи))

  1. I told a white lie and said I loved the sweater.
  2. Everyone tells a white lie now and then.

7. Of a holiday or season: with snow on the ground. (снежный)

  1. Denver hasn't had a white Christmas in years.
  2. The kids were hoping for a white Christmas.

gray#

Pronunciation
/ɡreɪ/ · GRAY
Русский
серый; седой; пасмурный
Type
color
Position
both — a gray sky, The sky is gray
Comparison
-er/-est — grayer, grayest
Contrast
American spelling is gray; grey is British, though it survives in names (Greyhound, Earl Grey). Gray hair is седые волосы, not серые, and go gray means поседеть. A gray area is a situation where the rules are unclear, not a place.

Gray has 6 distinct senses.

1. Of the color between black and white, like ash or rain clouds. (серый)

  1. He wore a gray suit to the interview.
  2. The paint dried grayer than it looked in the can.
  3. Her eyes are gray, not blue.

2. Of hair: turning white with age; of a person: having such hair. (седой)

  1. My dad started going gray at forty.
  2. She has a few gray hairs and doesn't care.

3. Of weather or the sky: cloudy, dull and without sun. (пасмурный, хмурый)

  1. It was a cold, gray morning in Seattle.
  2. The sky has been gray all week.

4. Of the face or skin: pale and unhealthy from illness, tiredness or shock. (серый, землистый, бледный)

  1. He looked gray and exhausted after the night shift.
  2. Her face went gray when the doctor walked in.

5. Dull and without interest or personality. (серый, скучный, безликий)

  1. Life in the suburbs felt gray after New York.
  2. The hero is a gray little clerk who never takes a risk.

6. Not clearly right or wrong, or not clearly defined. (неопределённый, спорный)

  1. Cash tips are a gray area for a lot of workers.
  2. The law is pretty gray on this point.

brown#

Pronunciation
/braʊn/ · BROWN
Русский
коричневый; карий; смуглый; загорелый
Type
color
Position
both — brown shoes, The shoes are brown
Comparison
-er/-est — browner, brownest
Contrast
Brown eyes are карие глаза — English has no separate word. Brown hair is каштановые or тёмно-русые волосы; Russian русые is usually light brown or dark blond. Brown rice and brown sugar are about how the food is processed, not only about the color. About people, brown is an adjective, mostly in phrases like Black and brown communities; never use it as a noun for a person.

Brown has 5 distinct senses.

1. Of the color of wood, soil or coffee. (коричневый)

  1. He bought a brown leather jacket.
  2. The lawn turns brown every August.
  3. The leaves get browner as fall goes on.

2. Of eyes: dark; of hair: any shade from light to dark brown. (карий (о глазах); каштановый, тёмно-русый (о волосах))

  1. The baby has big brown eyes.
  2. His hair is light brown with a little gray.

3. Belonging to a group of people with light to moderately dark skin, especially Latino, Middle Eastern or South Asian; sometimes written Brown. (смуглый, темнокожий (о латиноамериканцах, выходцах из Южной Азии и др.))

  1. The program mostly serves Black and brown students.
  2. Advocates say brown and Black neighborhoods get fewer parks.

4. Of skin: darkened by the sun; tanned. (загорелый, смуглый)

  1. The kids came back from camp brown from the sun.
  2. By August he was as brown as a lifeguard.

5. Of rice, bread or sugar: not fully refined, keeping more of the grain or the dark syrup. (бурый (о рисе), из муки грубого помола; коричневый (о сахаре))

  1. I'll have the chicken with brown rice.
  2. Add a cup of packed brown sugar.

pink#

Pronunciation
/pɪŋk/ · PINK
Русский
розовый; раскрасневшийся
Type
color
Position
both — a pink dress, The dress is pink
Comparison
-er/-est — pinker, pinkest
Contrast
Tickled pink means very pleased, not ticklish. A pink slip (a noun) is a notice that you have lost your job. For a face red from embarrassment, pink is milder than red: She went pink is a light blush.

Pink has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a pale red color. (розовый)

  1. She painted her nails bright pink.
  2. My daughter wanted a pink bike for her birthday.
  3. The sky got pinker as the sun went down.

2. Of the face or skin: slightly red from health, heat or embarrassment. (розовый, румяный, раскрасневшийся)

  1. His cheeks went pink when she thanked him.
  2. The baby's skin was soft and pink.

purple#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɜːrpəl/ · PUR-puhl
Русский
фиолетовый, лиловый; багровый
Type
color
Position
both — a purple scarf, The scarf is purple; purple prose only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — purpler, purplest, more purple
Contrast
False friend: Russian пурпурный leans toward crimson, while purple is фиолетовый or лиловый — the color of eggplant and grape juice. Violet is a bluer purple; lavender and lilac are pale purples. In US politics red is Republican and blue is Democratic, so a purple state is split between them.

Purple has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of a color between red and blue. (фиолетовый, лиловый)

  1. The grapes were dark purple and very sweet.
  2. She dyed her hair purple for the concert.
  3. The bruise turned more purple overnight.

2. Of the face or skin: dark red from anger, cold or effort. (багровый)

  1. He turned purple with rage.
  2. Her hands were purple from the cold.

3. Of writing: too elaborate, full of fancy words and emotion. (напыщенный, цветистый)

  1. The novel is full of purple prose about sunsets.
  2. Cut the purple passages and get to the point.

4. Of a US state or area: with about equal support for Republicans and Democrats. (колеблющийся (о штате, округе))

  1. Colorado used to be a purple state.
  2. The suburbs around Atlanta are turning purple.

orange#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːrɪndʒ/ · OR-inj
Русский
оранжевый; рыжий
Type
color
Position
both — an orange vest, The vest is orange
Comparison
more/most — more orange, most orange
Contrast
Red human hair is red in English, not orange: a рыжий человек is a redhead. For animals, orange is normal: an orange cat. In orange juice the modifier is the noun orange (the fruit), not the color adjective.

Orange has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a color between red and yellow. (оранжевый)

  1. Hunters wear orange vests in the woods.
  2. The maple leaves turn orange in October.
  3. The sunset got more orange as we watched.

2. Of an animal's fur: reddish yellow. (рыжий)

  1. We adopted an orange tabby from the shelter.
  2. Our dog is white with orange patches on her back.

yellow#

Pronunciation
/ˈjɛloʊ/ · YEL-oh
Русский
жёлтый; трусливый
Type
color
Position
both — a yellow bus, The bus is yellow
Comparison
-er/-est — yellower, yellowest
Contrast
Russian жёлтая пресса is the tabloids or tabloid journalism in modern American English; yellow journalism is mainly a historical term for the 1890s newspaper wars. Yellow meaning cowardly is informal and old-fashioned. The middle traffic light is yellow in American English (run a yellow light); amber is British. Never use yellow to describe people of East Asian descent: it is a racial slur.

Yellow has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of the color of lemons or butter. (жёлтый)

  1. The kids ran out to the yellow school bus.
  2. Our house is yellow with white trim.
  3. The old photos have gotten yellower with age.

2. Cowardly — informal and old-fashioned. (трусливый)

  1. He was too yellow to stand up to his boss.
  2. Don't be yellow — just go talk to her.

3. Of news reporting: shocking and exaggerated to sell papers, in yellow journalism. (жёлтый, бульварный (о прессе))

  1. Hearst's papers were famous for yellow journalism.
  2. Critics called the cable coverage a new kind of yellow journalism.

green#

Pronunciation
/ɡriːn/ · GREEN
Русский
зелёный; незрелый; неопытный; экологичный
Type
color; also quality
Position
both — green eyes, Her eyes are green
Comparison
-er/-est — greener, greenest
Takes
with envy — only in the 'jealous' sense: green with envy
Contrast
Green for 'inexperienced' matches Russian зелёный. Envy is green with envy, not green from envy. Green in green energy or go green means good for the environment, not the color. A green card is the US permanent resident card, and greens (a noun) are leafy vegetables.

Green has 8 distinct senses.

1. Of the color of grass and leaves. (зелёный)

  1. She has bright green eyes.
  2. Wait until the light turns green.
  3. The grass is always greener on the other side.

2. Covered with grass, trees or other plants. (зелёный, озеленённый)

  1. Portland has a lot of green space for a big city.
  2. The hills are green after the spring rains.

3. Of fruit: not yet ripe. (незрелый, зелёный)

  1. These bananas are still green.
  2. Don't pick the tomatoes while they're green.

4. Young and without experience. (неопытный, зелёный)

  1. The new hires are pretty green, so be patient with them.
  2. I was green when I started, and I made every mistake.

5. Good for the environment, or supporting its protection. (экологичный, зелёный)

  1. The company is trying to go green.
  2. Green energy now powers half the city.
  3. Stores are switching to greener packaging.

6. Pale and sick-looking, as if about to vomit. (позеленевший, бледный (от тошноты))

  1. He looked a little green after the roller coaster.
  2. She turned green as soon as the boat left the dock.

7. Very jealous, in green with envy. (завидующий)

  1. My friends were green with envy when they saw my new car.
  2. His success left the other writers green with envy.

8. Of wood: freshly cut and not yet dried. (сырой (о древесине))

  1. Green wood smokes a lot when you burn it.
  2. Don't build the deck with green lumber.

blue#

Pronunciation
/bluː/ · BLOO
Русский
синий, голубой; грустный
Type
color; also emotion
Position
both — blue eyes, Her eyes are blue; 'sad' mostly after a verb: feeling blue
Comparison
-er/-est — bluer, bluest
Contrast
English has one basic word, blue, for both синий and голубой: say light blue for голубой and dark blue or navy for тёмно-синий. Russian slang голубой ('gay') does not carry over — blue never means gay. In US politics blue is Democratic and red is Republican, the reverse of most European party colors. Spelling: bluer, bluest, with no extra e.

Blue has 5 distinct senses.

1. Of the color of a clear sky or the sea. (синий, голубой)

  1. She has bright blue eyes.
  2. The sky was blue all weekend.
  3. The water gets bluer the farther out you go.

2. Sad or depressed — informal. (грустный, подавленный)

  1. I've been feeling kind of blue since the holidays.
  2. Rainy Sundays always make me blue.

3. In US politics: voting for or controlled by Democrats. (демократический (о штате, округе))

  1. California is a solidly blue state.
  2. The district turned blue in the last election.

4. Of the skin or lips: bluish from cold or lack of air. (посиневший)

  1. The kids' lips were blue after swimming in the lake.
  2. His fingers were blue with cold.

5. Of jokes or language: rude, especially about sex. (неприличный, непристойный)

  1. The comedian's act was a little too blue for TV.
  2. He told some blue jokes at the bachelor party.

Contrast
The word ends in the soft zh sound of garage, not the j sound of badge. Americans also say tan for a darker beige and khaki for the light brownish color of casual pants. Beige meaning 'boring' is informal.

Beige has 2 distinct senses.

2. Boring and without character — informal. (скучный, безликий)

  1. The hotel was clean but completely beige.
  2. Their music is pleasant but a little beige.

golden#

Pronunciation
/ˈɡoʊldən/ · GOHL-duhn
Русский
золотистый; золотой
Type
color; also evaluative
Position
both — the color and you're golden after a verb; a golden opportunity, golden age, golden anniversary only before a noun
Comparison
more/most — more golden, most golden
Form
-en from gold
Contrast
Gold (the noun) is the usual word for things made of gold: a gold ring, gold earrings. Golden is mostly the color (golden hair, golden brown) and the figurative senses (a golden opportunity); a golden ring sounds like a fairy tale. You're golden is informal American English for 'you're all set'.

Golden has 7 distinct senses.

1. Having the color of gold; shiny yellow. (золотистый)

  1. Bake the pie until the crust is golden brown.
  2. She has long golden hair.
  3. The fields looked more golden in the evening light.

2. Made of gold — literary or old-fashioned. (золотой)

  1. The king wore a golden crown.
  2. In the story, the goose lays golden eggs.

3. Of a period: very happy, successful or prosperous. (золотой (о времени))

  1. The 1930s and '40s were the golden age of Hollywood.
  2. They spent their golden years traveling the country.

4. Very good and likely to bring success, of a chance or opportunity. (прекрасный, уникальный (о возможности))

  1. This is a golden opportunity to meet investors.
  2. He missed a golden chance to tie the game.

5. Admired by everyone and expected to succeed, in golden boy and golden girl. (всеобщий любимец, баловень судьбы)

  1. He was the golden boy of Silicon Valley.
  2. In high school she was the golden girl who could do no wrong.

6. Of an anniversary: the fiftieth. (золотой (о юбилее))

  1. My parents celebrated their golden anniversary in June.
  2. A golden anniversary calls for a big party.

7. In a good position, with nothing to worry about — informal, after a verb. (всё в порядке, в шоколаде)

  1. Get a B on the final and you're golden.
  2. Once the paperwork is signed, we're golden.

silver#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɪlvər/ · SIL-vur
Русский
серебристый; седой; серебряный (о юбилее)
Type
color
Position
both — a silver car, The car is silver; the anniversary sense only before a noun
Comparison
not gradable — silvery is the word for 'somewhat silver'
Contrast
A silver ring (серебряное кольцо) uses the noun silver for the material, like a gold ring, so 'made of silver' is not a sense here. The adjective is the color: a silver car is серебристая машина. Silvery means 'somewhat silver' and also describes a soft, clear sound: a silvery voice. Silver hair is a flattering way to say gray hair.

Silver has 3 distinct senses.

1. Having the shiny gray color of silver. (серебристый)

  1. She drives a silver Honda.
  2. Her dress was silver and covered in sequins.
  3. The silver balloons matched the tablecloths.

2. Of hair: gray or white in a shiny, attractive way. (седой, серебристый)

  1. The anchor was a handsome man with silver hair.
  2. Her hair went silver in her fifties, and she loved it.

3. Of an anniversary: the twenty-fifth. (серебряный (о юбилее))

  1. They threw a party for their silver anniversary.
  2. The theater celebrates its silver jubilee this year.

blond#

Pronunciation

color

Contrast
American style usually writes blond for the adjective, whoever it describes; blonde is also common, especially about women and for the noun a blonde. Blond hair is светлые волосы; Russian русые is usually light brown or dark blond. Fair also means light hair or skin and sounds more literary.

2. Of a person: having light-colored hair. (светловолосый)

  1. Their son is blond like his mother.
  2. A tall blond guy in a red jacket was waiting outside.

3. Of wood, beer and some other things: light in color. (светлый)

  1. The table is made of blond oak.
  2. The brewery's blond ale is light and crisp.

pale#

Pronunciation
/peɪl/ · PAYL
Русский
бледный; светлый
Type
color; also quality
Position
both — a pale face, You look pale; a pale imitation only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — paler, palest
Opposite
dark (color); flushed (face)
Contrast
Pale skin often suggests illness or too little sun; fair skin is neutral or flattering. Before a color word, pale means light: pale blue, pale pink. Spelling: paler, palest, adding only -r and -st. Beyond the pale (unacceptable) uses an old noun, not this adjective.

Pale has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of the face or skin: having less color than usual, from illness, shock or little sun. (бледный)

  1. You look pale — are you OK?
  2. She's naturally pale and burns easily.
  3. He looked even paler after the second round of tests.

2. Of a color: light, with a lot of white in it. (бледный, светлый, бледно-)

  1. He wore a pale blue shirt and a dark tie.
  2. We chose the palest shade of yellow for the nursery.

3. Of light: weak and not bright. (тусклый, слабый)

  1. We left in the pale light of early morning.
  2. A pale moon hung over the lake.

4. Weaker or worse than the original, in a pale imitation or a pale shadow. (жалкий, бледный (о подобии))

  1. The sequel is a pale imitation of the first movie.
  2. The team is a pale shadow of what it was ten years ago.

dark#

Pronunciation
/dɑːrk/ · DAHRK
Русский
тёмный; мрачный
Type
color; also quality
Position
both — a dark room, The room is dark
Comparison
-er/-est — darker, darkest
Opposite
light (light and color); bright
Contrast
Russian темно is It's dark, with the adjective, not It's darkly. Dark chocolate is горький шоколад, and dark meat is the meat of chicken legs and thighs. Dark skin is тёмная or смуглая кожа. Russian тёмная личность is a shady character, not a dark person. In the dark (не в курсе) is a noun phrase.

Dark has 7 distinct senses.

1. With little or no light. (тёмный)

  1. It gets dark around five in December.
  2. The hallway was dark and quiet.
  3. The sky got darker as the storm rolled in.

2. Of a color: deep and closer to black. (тёмный, тёмно-)

  1. He wore a dark blue suit to the funeral.
  2. Her car is dark green.

3. Of hair, eyes or skin: brown or black. (тёмный, смуглый)

  1. He has dark hair and dark eyes.
  2. She's tall, with dark skin and short hair.

4. Sad, frightening or unpleasant; without hope. (мрачный, тяжёлый)

  1. Those were dark days for the company.
  2. The movie takes a dark turn halfway through.
  3. The book's humor is darker than I expected.
