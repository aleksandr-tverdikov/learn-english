Spelling changes in comparison#

big, bigger; happy, happier; simple, simpler — regular comparison with irregular spelling.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

The comparative and superlative are regular, but the spelling is not. One-syllable adjectives ending in consonant–vowel–consonant double the last letter (big, bigger); a final -y becomes -i- (happy, happier); a final -e just takes -r (nice, nicer); and a few two-syllable words take -er where learners expect more (simpler, narrower, quieter). Each entry spells both forms out.

big#

Pronunciation
/bɪɡ/ · BIG
Русский
большой; крупный; важный
Type
size; also evaluative
Position
both — but big meaning older (my big sister) only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — bigger, biggest
Takes
on + noun or -ing (informal: very enthusiastic about); of + person in That's big of you
Opposite
small, little
Contrast
Double the g: bigger, biggest, never biger. Big and large both mean большой, but large is more formal and goes with amount, number, quantity and sizes; only big means important (a big decision), older (my big brother) or popular (big in Japan), so a large mistake and my large brother are wrong. In think big and talk big, big is an adverb.

Big has 6 distinct senses.

1. Large in size, number or amount. (большой, крупный)

  1. They live in a big house outside Denver.
  2. Texas is bigger than France.
  3. That was the biggest pizza I've ever seen.

2. Important, serious or significant. (важный, серьёзный)

  1. Getting married is a big decision.
  2. It's no big deal, so don't worry about it.
  3. The biggest problem is money.

3. Older or grown-up; used mainly with children and about brothers and sisters. (старший; взрослый)

  1. My big sister taught me to drive.
  2. You're a big boy now, so you can tie your own shoes.

4. Very popular or successful. (популярный, успешный)

  1. The band is still big in Japan.
  2. Podcasts got really big during the pandemic.
  3. He was one of the biggest stars of the '80s.

5. Doing something a lot or with great enthusiasm; informal big on: very enthusiastic about. (большой (любитель), заядлый; увлечённый)

  1. I'm a big fan of her early work.
  2. He's never been a big eater.
  3. My dad is big on camping.

6. Generous or kind; often said with irony. (великодушный, благородный)

  1. It was big of him to admit he was wrong.
  2. You're lending me a dollar? That's really big of you.

fat#

Pronunciation
/fæt/ · FAT
Русский
толстый; жирный; крупный
Type
size; also quality
Position
both — but fat about money (a fat bonus) and in fat chance only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — fatter, fattest
Opposite
thin
Contrast
Double the t: fatter, fattest. Calling a person fat is rude in American English; say overweight, heavy or big. For food with a lot of fat, use fatty or rich (fatty meat), not fat meat; low-fat and fat-free use the noun fat (жир). Fat chance means the opposite of what it seems: almost no chance.

Fat has 4 distinct senses.

1. Having a lot of extra flesh on the body. (толстый, полный)

  1. The cat got fat after we moved to the city.
  2. He was a fat kid, but he lost the weight in high school.
  3. Our dog is fatter than the neighbor's.

2. Thick, full or swollen. (толстый, пухлый, распухший)

  1. She pulled a fat envelope out of her bag.
  2. He came home from the game with a fat lip.
  3. It's the fattest book on the reading list.

3. Of money: large. (крупный, солидный)

  1. She signed a fat contract with a big law firm.
  2. The company lost money, but the CEO still got a fat bonus.

4. Informal and ironic: very little or none, in fat chance and a fat lot of good. (никакой, ничтожный)

  1. Will he pay you back? Fat chance.
  2. A fat lot of good that did us.

hot#

Pronunciation
/hɑːt/ · HAHT
Русский
горячий; жаркий; острый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — hotter, hottest
Opposite
cold; mild (food)
Contrast
Double the t: hotter, hottest. Russian splits heat in two, жаркий for weather and rooms and горячий for things you touch; English uses hot for both. I'm hot = мне жарко, never It's hot to me. Weather that is only pleasant is warm (тепло); hot is too much. Hot food can mean spicy (острый), so say spicy when you mean pepper. Hot about a person is informal and means sexy, so be careful with it.

Hot has 10 distinct senses.

1. Of weather, a place or a time: having a high temperature. (жаркий)

  1. It was a hot day in July.
  2. Phoenix is one of the hottest cities in the country.
  3. Why is it so hot in here?

2. Of food, drink or an object: at a high temperature. (горячий)

  1. Careful, the pan is hot.
  2. I'd love a cup of hot coffee.
  3. The water for tea should be hotter than that.

3. Of a person: feeling too warm. ((кому-то) жарко)

  1. I'm hot. Can we turn on the AC?
  2. The kids were hot and tired after the hike.

4. Spicy, with a burning taste. (острый)

  1. This salsa is really hot.
  2. Do you want the mild sauce or the hot one?
  3. Their wings are the hottest in town.

5. Very popular, successful or in demand at the moment. (модный, популярный, актуальный)

  1. It's the hottest restaurant in Brooklyn right now.
  2. Immigration was a hot topic in the debate.
  3. The Celtics are hot and have won eight straight games.

6. Full of strong feeling, especially anger or excitement. (горячий, бурный, вспыльчивый)

  1. The meeting turned into a hot argument.
  2. He has a hot temper.

7. Informal: sexually attractive. (сексуальный, привлекательный)

  1. All her friends thought the new lifeguard was hot.
  2. He's even hotter in person.

8. Informal: stolen. (краденый)

  1. He didn't know the watch was hot.
  2. The police traced the hot TVs to a warehouse.

9. Of an electrical wire: carrying current. (под напряжением)

  1. Don't touch the black wire; it's hot.
  2. Connect the hot wire to the brass screw.

10. In guessing and hiding games: very close to the answer. (горячо (в игре))

  1. You're getting hotter. It's somewhere near the couch.
  2. Now you're hot! Look under the pillow.

sad#

Pronunciation
/sæd/ · SAD
Русский
грустный; печальный
Type
emotion; also evaluative
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — sadder, saddest
Takes
about + noun or -ing; for + person (sad on their behalf); to-infinitive (sad to hear); that-clause
Preposition patterns
sad about
Opposite
happy
Contrast
Double the d: sadder, saddest. The same word describes the person and the cause: I'm sad (мне грустно) and a sad movie (грустный фильм), with no -ing/-ed pair to choose from. Russian мне грустно is I'm sad or I feel sad, never It's sad to me. A sad excuse for is informal and scornful: a very bad example of something.

Sad has 4 distinct senses.

1. Feeling unhappy. (грустный, печальный)

  1. She was sad to leave her friends behind.
  2. Don't look so sad. It's only a week.
  3. I've never felt sadder than on the day we sold the farm.

2. Making people feel unhappy. (грустный, печальный)

  1. It's a sad movie, so bring tissues.
  2. The saddest part is that nobody called her.

3. Unfortunate and deserving regret or blame. (прискорбный, плачевный)

  1. It's sad that so many kids go to school hungry.
  2. The old theater is in a sad state.

4. Informal: poor, pathetic or embarrassing. (жалкий)

  1. That's a sad excuse for a burger.
  2. I spent Saturday night alone with my laptop. How sad is that?

thin#

Pronunciation
/θɪn/ · THIN
Русский
тонкий; худой; жидкий; редкий
Type
size; also quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — thinner, thinnest
Opposite
thick; fat
Contrast
Double the n: thinner, thinnest. Russian splits what English calls thin: тонкий (a slice, a wall), худой (a person), жидкий (soup, hair). About people, thin is neutral or slightly negative, slim is a compliment, and skinny is often negative. Russian тонкий about taste, humor or a difference is subtle, not thin: a subtle joke.

Thin has 6 distinct senses.

1. Having a small distance between the two sides or surfaces. (тонкий)

  1. Cut the cucumber into thin slices.
  2. The walls in this apartment are really thin.
  3. The ice is thinner near the middle of the lake.

2. Of a person or animal: having little fat on the body. (худой)

  1. He was tall and thin, with glasses.
  2. She looked thinner after being sick.

3. Of a liquid: watery, not thick. (жидкий)

  1. The soup was too thin and bland.
  2. Add water until the paint is thin enough to spray.

4. Not dense; with few people, hairs or things, or with little oxygen in the air. (редкий, жидкий, немногочисленный; разреженный)

  1. His hair is getting thin on top.
  2. The crowd was thin for a Friday night.
  3. The air is thinner up in the mountains.

5. Weak and not convincing. (неубедительный, слабый)

  1. The evidence against him is pretty thin.
  2. That's a thin excuse.

6. Of a voice or sound: high and weak. (тонкий, слабый)

  1. She answered in a thin, shaky voice.
  2. The cheap speakers sounded thin.

wet#

Pronunciation
/wɛt/ · WET
Русский
мокрый; сырой; дождливый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — wetter, wettest
Opposite
dry
Contrast
Double the t: wetter, wettest. Russian влажный is usually not wet: say damp for clothes or walls, humid for air, moist for cake or skin; wet means really covered with water (мокрый). Wet is also a verb whose past form is usually wet (The baby wet the bed).

Wet has 4 distinct senses.

1. Covered with or full of water or another liquid. (мокрый)

  1. Take off those wet shoes before you come in.
  2. My hair is still wet.
  3. The grass was wetter than it looked.

2. Of paint, ink or cement: not yet dry. (невысохший, непросохший)

  1. Wet paint. Do not touch.
  2. Don't walk there; the concrete is still wet.

3. Of weather or a season: rainy. (дождливый, сырой)

  1. It's been a cold, wet spring.
  2. November is usually the wettest month here.

4. US: of a town or county: allowing the sale of alcohol. (где разрешена продажа алкоголя)

  1. The county voted to go wet in 2012.
  2. You have to drive to the next wet county to buy beer.

fit#

Pronunciation
/fɪt/ · FIT
Русский
в хорошей форме; подходящий; пригодный
Type
quality
Position
both — but fit for and fit to (suitable) mostly after a verb
Comparison
-er/-est — fitter, fittest
Takes
for + noun (fit for work); to-infinitive (fit to drive)
Opposite
unfit; out of shape
Contrast
Double the t: fitter, fittest (survival of the fittest). Russian подходить по размеру is the verb fit (The jacket fits; past fit in American English), not the adjective, so The jacket is fit is wrong. The adjective means healthy, or good enough in quality (fit to eat). In everyday American speech in shape is often more natural: I'm out of shape. British slang fit (attractive) is not American.

Fit has 3 distinct senses.

1. Healthy and strong, especially from exercise. (в хорошей форме, спортивный)

  1. My grandmother is really fit for her age.
  2. He's a fit, athletic guy who runs every morning.
  3. I'm fitter now than I was in college.

2. Good enough or suitable for a purpose. (подходящий, пригодный)

  1. The water isn't fit to drink.
  2. After the flood, the house was no longer fit for people to live in.
  3. He'd had three beers and wasn't fit to drive.

3. Right or proper, in see fit: to decide that something is right. ((считать) нужным, уместным)

  1. Spend the money however you see fit.
  2. The judge can give whatever sentence she sees fit.

flat#

Pronunciation
/flæt/ · FLAT
Русский
плоский; ровный; спущенный; фиксированный
Type
shape; also quality
Position
both — but flat meaning fixed (a flat fee) or complete (a flat refusal) only before a noun, and after the note name in B flat
Comparison
-er/-est — flatter, flattest
Opposite
hilly, bumpy; sharp (music)
Contrast
Double the t: flatter, flattest, spelled like the verb flatter (льстить). A flat meaning an apartment is British; Americans say apartment, and in American speech a flat is usually a flat tire. An empty battery is dead in American English, not flat. In lie flat, fall flat and in ten seconds flat, flat is an adverb.

Flat has 10 distinct senses.

1. Level and smooth, without hills, bumps or holes. (ровный, плоский)

  1. Kansas is famously flat.
  2. Lay the map on a flat surface.
  3. The land gets flatter as you drive east.

2. Wide but not deep or high; of shoes: without a raised heel. (плоский; без каблука)

  1. She keeps her jewelry in a flat wooden box.
  2. Wear flat shoes, because we'll be walking a lot.

3. Of a tire or ball: without enough air in it. (спущенный)

  1. We got a flat tire on the way to the airport.
  2. The basketball is flat again.

4. Of a drink: no longer fizzy. (выдохшийся)

  1. The soda went flat because someone left the cap off.
  2. This beer tastes flat.

5. Of paint or a surface finish: not shiny. (матовый)

  1. We used flat paint on the ceiling.
  2. A flat finish hides small dents in the wall better than a glossy one.

6. Dull, without feeling, energy or interest. (монотонный, безжизненный, вялый)

  1. He spoke in a flat, tired voice.
  2. The second half of the movie felt flat.

7. Of sales, prices or growth: not rising. (без изменений, на одном уровне)

  1. Sales were flat in the third quarter.
  2. Wages have stayed flat for years.

8. Of a price or charge: the same in every case. (фиксированный)

  1. The lawyer charges a flat fee of $500.
  2. They offer a flat rate for shipping.

9. Complete and firm, with no discussion. (категорический)

  1. Her answer was a flat no.
  2. The senator issued a flat denial.

10. In music: lower than the correct note; after a note name, a half step lower. (ниже нужного тона; бемоль)

  1. The piece is in B flat.
  2. The violins sounded a little flat.

slim#

Pronunciation
/slɪm/ · SLIM
Русский
стройный; тонкий; небольшой
Type
size; also quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — slimmer, slimmest
Opposite
heavy; good (a good chance)
Contrast
Double the m: slimmer, slimmest. About people, slim is a compliment, thin is neutral and skinny often is not. A slim chance and slim hopes are very small ones (мало шансов) and have nothing to do with shape. Slim down is a phrasal verb (худеть).

Slim has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of a person: thin in an attractive way. (стройный)

  1. She's tall and slim, with short dark hair.
  2. He looks slimmer since he started cycling.

2. Of an object: thin or narrow. (тонкий, узкий)

  1. The new phone is slimmer and lighter.
  2. He published a slim book of poems.
  3. These jeans have a slim fit.

3. Of a chance, amount or difference: very small. (небольшой, незначительный)

  1. There's only a slim chance of finding survivors.
  2. She won by the slimmest of margins.
  3. Pickings are slim this late in the season.

grim#

Pronunciation
/ɡrɪm/ · GRIM
Русский
мрачный; суровый; удручающий
Type
quality
Position
both — but grim meaning determined (grim determination) only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — grimmer, grimmest
Contrast
Double the m: grimmer, grimmest. Grim is not simply dark: about a face it means serious and stern (суровый), about news or a situation very bad and worrying (мрачный, удручающий). A room with little light is dark or dim, not grim; a grim room is depressing to be in. The Grim Reaper is the figure of Death.

Grim has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of a face or expression: very serious and stern. (суровый, мрачный)

  1. The doctor came out with a grim look on her face.
  2. His expression grew grimmer as he read the letter.

2. Very bad, worrying or depressing. (мрачный, удручающий, безрадостный)

  1. The economic news is grim.
  2. Things look grim for the home team.
  3. It was the grimmest winter anyone could remember.

3. Of a place: unpleasant and depressing to be in. (унылый, мрачный)

  1. They spent the night in a grim motel off the highway.
  2. The waiting room was gray and grim.

4. Determined and not giving up, even when things are hard. (упорный, отчаянный)

  1. She held on with grim determination.
  2. The workers fought a grim battle to keep the factory open.

dim#

Pronunciation
/dɪm/ · DIM
Русский
тусклый; смутный; туповатый
Type
quality
Position
both — but dim in take a dim view only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — dimmer, dimmest

bright
Contrast
Double the m: dimmer, dimmest. A dimmer is also a noun, a switch that makes lights less bright (диммер). Take a dim view of means disapprove of (относиться неодобрительно), not see badly. Calling a person dim is informal and unkind. Dim is also a verb: dim the lights.

Dim has 5 distinct senses.

1. Not bright; with little light. (тусклый, полутёмный)

  1. The bar was dim and smoky.
  2. She read by the dim light of a candle.

2. Hard to see, hear or remember clearly. (смутный, неясный)

  1. I have only a dim memory of my grandfather.
  2. We saw a dim shape moving in the fog.

3. Of chances or prospects: not hopeful. (слабый, призрачный)

  1. Prospects for a deal look dim.
  2. His chances of winning grow dimmer every day.

4. Informal: not intelligent. (туповатый, недалёкий)

  1. He's nice, but a little dim.
  2. He's not dim, just a little lazy.

5. Disapproving, in take a dim view of. (неодобрительный)

  1. The school takes a dim view of cheating.
  2. My parents took a dim view of my new boyfriend.

red#

Pronunciation
/rɛd/ · RED
Русский
красный; рыжий
Type
color
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — redder, reddest
Contrast
Double the d: redder, reddest. Red hair is red (рыжий) in American English; ginger is mainly British and orange hair means dyed. In US politics red means Republican and blue means Democratic, the opposite of the old link between red and the left, so a red state is a conservative one. In the red (в минусе) uses the noun.

Red has 4 distinct senses.

  1. Both of their kids have red hair.
  2. A red fox ran across the road.

3. Of the face or eyes: red because of anger, embarrassment, crying or tiredness. (покрасневший, красный)

  1. He turned red when she mentioned his name.
  2. Her eyes were red from crying.
  3. His face got redder and redder.

4. US politics: voting mostly for the Republican Party. (республиканский (о штате, округе))

  1. Wyoming is one of the reddest states in the country.
  2. The rural counties are deep red.

happy#

Pronunciation
/ˈhæpi/ · HAP-ee
Русский
счастливый; довольный; радостный
Type
emotion
Position
both — but happy in greetings (Happy birthday) and meaning lucky (a happy accident) only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — happier, happiest
Takes
with or about + noun (satisfied); for + person (glad for their sake); to-infinitive (glad, willing); that-clause
Preposition patterns
happy about
Opposite
unhappy, sad
Contrast
y becomes i: happier, happiest, happily, happiness; more happy is rare. Happy to often means willing (I'm happy to help = охотно помогу), not счастлив. Happy for you = рад за тебя; happy with = доволен (I'm happy with my new car). Russian счастливый meaning lucky (счастливый билет) is lucky, not happy.

Happy has 7 distinct senses.

1. Feeling pleasure, joy or contentment. (счастливый, радостный)

  1. The kids looked so happy on Christmas morning.
  2. We're very happy together.
  3. I've never been happier.

2. Satisfied with something. (довольный)

  1. Are you happy with the service?
  2. My boss wasn't happy about the delay.

3. Pleased about something, or willing to do something. (рад; охотно готов)

  1. I'd be happy to show you around.
  2. We're happy that you could come.

4. Glad because something good has happened to someone else. (рад (за кого-то))

  1. I'm so happy for you both!
  2. We were happy for her when she got the job.

5. Bringing or showing happiness. (счастливый, радостный)

  1. The movie has a happy ending.
  2. That was the happiest day of my life.

6. Used in good wishes for a holiday or special day. (с (праздником, днём рождения))

  1. Happy birthday, Mom!
  2. Happy holidays from all of us.

7. Lucky and fortunate. (удачный, счастливый)

  1. Meeting her there was a happy accident.
  2. By a happy coincidence, we were on the same flight.

funny#

Pronunciation
/ˈfʌni/ · FUHN-ee
Русский
смешной; забавный; странный
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — funnier, funniest
Contrast
y becomes i: funnier, funniest. Funny is not fun: funny makes you laugh (смешной) or is strange (странный), while fun means enjoyable (весёлый, a fun party). The trip was funny means something made you laugh, not that you had a good time. Because funny can also mean odd, Americans sometimes ask funny ha-ha or funny weird?

Funny has 4 distinct senses.

1. Making you laugh. (смешной, забавный)

  1. He told a really funny story about his first job.
  2. She's the funniest person I know.
  3. The sequel is even funnier than the original.

2. Strange or surprising. (странный, чудной)

  1. The car is making a funny noise.
  2. That's funny. I'm sure I left my keys on the table.
  3. It's funny how things work out.

3. Informal: slightly ill, or not working normally. (нехорошо, не по себе)

  1. I feel a little funny, so I think I'll sit down.
  2. My knee has been funny since I fell.

4. Informal: dishonest or suspicious. (подозрительный, нечестный)

  1. Don't try anything funny.
  2. The auditors noticed something funny in the accounts.

heavy#

Pronunciation
/ˈhɛvi/ · HEV-ee
Русский
тяжёлый; сильный; плотный
Type
quality
Position
both — but heavy meaning doing a lot (a heavy smoker) only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est — heavier, heaviest
Takes
with + noun (full of, loaded with)
Opposite
light
Contrast
y becomes i: heavier, heaviest. Russian сильный with rain, snow, traffic, drinking or smoking is heavy in English: heavy rain, heavy traffic, a heavy smoker, never strong rain or big traffic. About a person, heavy is a polite word for fat. Heavy cream is the American name for whipping cream (жирные сливки).

Heavy has 10 distinct senses.

1. Weighing a lot; hard to lift or move. (тяжёлый)

  1. This suitcase is too heavy to carry up the stairs.
  2. Gold is heavier than silver.
  3. Jupiter is the heaviest planet.

2. Of a person: large and fat. (полный, грузный)

  1. He was a heavy man with a gray beard.
  2. She got heavier after she quit smoking.

3. Greater in amount or force than usual. (сильный, интенсивный, большой)

  1. Heavy rain is expected tonight.
  2. Traffic on I-95 was heavy this morning.
  3. The storm caused heavy damage.

4. Doing something a lot. (заядлый; сильно (пьющий, курящий))

  1. My uncle was a heavy smoker.
  2. He's a heavy user of social media.

5. Serious, sad or hard to deal with. (тяжёлый, серьёзный, гнетущий)

  1. The movie is pretty heavy for a Friday night.
  2. She left with a heavy heart.
  3. Things got heavy when we started talking about money.

6. Thick, strong or dense. (плотный, толстый)

  1. Bring a heavy coat, because it's cold up there.
  2. Whip the heavy cream until it forms peaks.

7. Of food: rich and hard to digest. (тяжёлый, сытный)

  1. I had a heavy lunch and now I'm sleepy.
  2. The sauce is too heavy for summer.

8. Of air or weather: warm, humid and unpleasant. (душный, тяжёлый)

  1. The air was hot and heavy before the storm.
  2. It feels heavier today, like it's going to rain.

9. Full of or loaded with something. (полный, отягощённый)

Do not confuse quiet /ˈkwaɪət/ (two syllables, тихий) with quite /kwaɪt/ (one syllable, довольно): the e and the t swap places. Though it has two syllables, quiet takes -er: quieter, quietest. Be quiet! means stop talking (замолчи), and a quiet day at work is one with little to do, not only one without noise.

Quiet has 5 distinct senses.

1. Making little or no noise. (тихий)

  1. The house was quiet after the kids left.
  2. Could you be a little quieter? The baby's asleep.
  3. She spoke in a quiet voice.

2. Calm and peaceful, with little activity. (спокойный, тихий)

  1. We live on a quiet street.
  2. It's been a quiet week at the office.
  3. Mondays are usually our quietest day.

3. Of a person: not talking much. (тихий, молчаливый, сдержанный)

  1. He's a quiet guy, but he's funny once you know him.
  2. You're very quiet tonight. Is something wrong?

4. Not showy or loud, but still felt. (неброский, спокойный)

  1. She has a quiet confidence.
  2. The hotel has a quiet elegance.

5. Private and not told to other people. (негласный, по секрету)

  1. Keep it quiet until we tell the family.
  2. The company kept the layoffs quiet for weeks.

Drop the e and add -r: larger, largest. Large is more formal than big and is the normal word with amount, number, quantity, scale and sizes (a large amount, a large coffee). It cannot mean important, older or popular: a big mistake, my big brother, never a large mistake or my large brother. At large is a set phrase: still at large = на свободе, the public at large = в целом. The adverb largely means mostly, not on a large scale.

Large has 4 distinct senses.

1. Big in size, number or amount. (большой, крупный)

  1. They need a larger apartment now that the baby's here.
  2. A large number of people signed the petition.
  3. Alaska is the largest state.

2. Of a size of clothes, food or drink: bigger than medium. (большой (размер L))

  1. Can I get a large coffee?
  2. The large T-shirts sold out first.

3. Of a person: tall and heavy; a polite word. (крупный, полный)

  1. He was a large man with a deep voice.
  2. The dress looks good on larger women too.

4. Wide in scope; general. (более широкий, общий)

  1. This case raises larger questions about free speech.
  2. We need to look at the larger picture.

Drop the e and add -r: wider, widest. Russian широкий for shoulders or a general idea is usually broad: broad shoulders, in broad terms. In wide open and wide awake, wide is an adverb, and widely means by many people (widely known). The suffix -wide means across a whole area (nationwide).

Wide has 6 distinct senses.

1. Measuring a large distance from side to side. (широкий)

  1. The road is wide enough for two trucks.
  2. They sat on a wide front porch.
  3. The Mississippi is wider than I expected.

2. Measuring a given distance from side to side. (шириной)

  1. The river is about 200 yards wide here.
  2. How wide is the doorway?

3. Including many different things or people. (широкий, разнообразный)

  1. The store sells a wide range of products.
  2. The show appeals to a wide audience.
  3. This school offers the widest choice of classes in the district.

4. Large in degree or difference. (большой, значительный)

  1. There is a wide gap between rich and poor.
  2. She won by a wide margin.

5. Of eyes: fully open. (широко раскрытый)

  1. The children watched with wide eyes.
  2. His eyes went wide with surprise.

6. Of a shot or throw: missing the target to one side. (мимо (цели))

  1. The kick was wide to the right.
  2. His first shot was just wide of the goal.

Drop the e and add -r: truer, truest, but the adverb truly loses the e completely (not truely). Russian верно! or правда! as an answer is That's true or That's right; about a person, say You're right, never You're true. True of or true for means applies to (справедливо для): The same is true of cats.

True has 5 distinct senses.

1. Based on facts; not false. (правдивый, верный)

  1. Is it true that you're moving to Denver?
  2. The movie is based on a true story.
  3. Nothing could be truer.

2. Real and genuine. (настоящий, истинный)

  1. She's a true friend.
  2. Nobody knew his true feelings.
  3. Their dream finally came true.

3. Loyal and faithful. (верный)

  1. He stayed true to his word.
  2. The band has remained true to its roots.
  3. Be true to yourself.

4. Accurate; exactly matching the real thing. (точный)

  1. The painting is true to life.
  2. A compass doesn't point to true north.

5. Applying to someone or something. (справедливый (для), верный)

  1. The same is true of most big cities.
  2. That's especially true for older workers.
