Adjectives used as nouns with the#

the rich, the young, the homeless — adjectives that name a whole group of people.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

With the, some adjectives stand for a whole group of people: the rich, the young, the homeless. The group is always plural — the rich are, never the rich is — and it cannot take a plural -s (the riches is a different word). For one person, English needs a noun: a homeless man, not a homeless.

Takes

Preposition patterns

Opposite

Contrast
The rich means rich people as a group and always takes a plural verb: The rich are getting richer, not The rich is. It never takes a plural -s: the riches is a different word, a noun meaning great wealth. For one person say a rich man, a rich woman or a rich person, never a rich. Russian богатый чем-то is rich in, not rich of: rich in oil. About food, rich means full of butter, cream or sugar and can be praise; Russian жирный is fatty or greasy, which sound negative. That's rich! is ironic: the critic is guilty of the same thing. Wealthy is the more formal, more polite word, and only for money.

Rich has 8 distinct senses.

1. Having a lot of money or property. (богатый)

  1. His uncle got rich selling real estate in Phoenix.
  2. Tax cuts for the rich are a big issue this election.
  3. They're one of the richest families in the country.

2. rich in: containing or having a large amount of something useful or good. (богатый (чем-то))

  1. Spinach is rich in iron.
  2. The region is rich in oil and natural gas.
  3. Old newspapers are a rich source of local history.

3. Of food: containing a lot of butter, cream, eggs or sugar, so that a little fills you up. (сытный, калорийный)

  1. The chocolate cake was too rich for me to finish.
  2. She makes a rich, creamy sauce for the pasta.

4. Of a color, sound or smell: deep, strong and pleasant. (насыщенный, густой, глубокий)

  1. The walls are painted a rich dark red.
  2. He has a rich, warm voice that's perfect for radio.
  3. The rich smell of coffee filled the kitchen.

5. Full of interesting variety, detail or meaning. (богатый, насыщенный)

  1. New Orleans has a rich cultural history.
  2. She's had a long and rich life.
  3. The book gives a richer picture of the war than the movie does.

6. Of soil or land: good for growing plants. (плодородный)

  1. The valley has rich soil for farming.
  2. Adding compost makes the soil richer.

7. Expensive and beautiful; luxurious. (роскошный, дорогой)

  1. The hotel lobby was full of rich fabrics and dark wood.
  2. The dining room was decorated in rich velvet and gold.

8. That's rich: used informally to say that a criticism is unfair or ridiculous because of who is making it. (кто бы говорил; забавно слышать это от тебя)

  1. You're calling me lazy? That's rich, coming from you.
  2. Him complaining about people being late is pretty rich.

young#

Pronunciation
/jʌŋ/ · YUHNG
Русский
молодой; маленький (о детях)
Type
age
Position
both — also with the for young people as a group: the young
Comparison
-er/-est — younger, youngest, both said with a hard g: /ˈjʌŋɡər/, /ˈjʌŋɡɪst/
Opposite
old
Contrast
The young means young people as a group and takes a plural verb: The young are often the first to try new apps. For one person say a young man, a young woman or a young person, never a young. Its young (a bird feeding its young) is a different use: a noun meaning an animal's babies. The comparative and superlative add a /ɡ/ the base word doesn't have: young /jʌŋ/ but younger /ˈjʌŋɡər/. Russian младший брат is my younger brother (or little brother), and младший сын among three or more is the youngest son; junior is for rank, not family. About children, young often means small: young children are маленькие дети, not teenagers.

Young has 4 distinct senses.

1. Having lived or existed for only a short time; not old. (молодой)

  1. She was very young when she became mayor.
  2. The coach wants a younger team next season.
  3. My younger sister lives in Denver.

2. Being a child; not yet grown up. (маленький, юный)

  1. They have two young kids, ages three and five.
  2. When I was young, we spent every summer at the lake.
  3. The show isn't suitable for young children.

3. In an early stage; recently started or formed. (молодой, недавно созданный)

  1. It's still a young company, only three years old.
  2. Come on, the night is still young!

4. Looking, feeling or acting like a young person, or meant for young people. (моложавый; молодёжный)

  1. My grandmother is eighty but very young at heart.
  2. You look younger with short hair.
  3. That dress is a little young for her.

Contrast
The homeless takes a plural verb: The homeless need more than a bed for the night. It never takes -s, and one person is a homeless man, a homeless woman or a homeless person, never a homeless. Many American writers and charities now prefer homeless people or people experiencing homelessness, because the homeless can sound as if people were only a category. The noun for the problem is homelessness: Homelessness is rising, not Homeless is rising. Don't confuse it with homesick (missing home) or homely, which in American English means plain or unattractive.

Having no home, and usually living on the street, in a car or in a shelter.

  1. A homeless man was sleeping in the doorway.
  2. The shelter serves hot meals to the homeless every night.
  3. After the fire, forty families were homeless.

Type

Position
both — also with the for injured people as a group: the injured; the 'offended' and legal senses go mostly before a noun: injured pride, the injured party
Comparison
not gradable — say badly, seriously or slightly injured
Form

Contrast
The injured means the people who were hurt and takes a plural verb: The injured were taken to three hospitals. For one person say an injured man or one of the injured, never an injured. English splits Russian раненый, травмированный, пострадавший by cause: injured is hurt in an accident, a fall or a game (an injured player); wounded is hurt by a weapon (wounded soldiers); hurt is the everyday word, mostly after a verb (Was anyone hurt?). Things are damaged, not injured: a damaged car. He was injured in a crash is usually the passive of the verb injure.

Injured has 3 distinct senses.

1. Physically hurt, for example in an accident, a fall or a game. (травмированный, пострадавший)

  1. Two injured passengers were taken to the hospital.
  2. The injured were treated at the scene.
  3. Our best player is injured and will miss the game.

2. Of feelings: offended or upset because you have been treated unfairly. (уязвлённый, обиженный)

  1. Behind his jokes there was a lot of injured pride.
  2. She spoke in an injured tone, as if we had betrayed her.

3. In law: harmed or treated wrongly by someone else's actions. (пострадавший, потерпевший)

  1. The injured party can sue for damages.
  2. The contract lets the injured party cancel the deal.

Pronunciation
/blaɪnd/ · BLYND
Русский
слепой, незрячий
Type
quality
Position
both — also with the for blind people as a group: the blind; the 'without judgment', 'without seeing', 'hard to see around' and 'no opening' senses go only before a noun: blind faith, a blind curve
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — blinder, blindest, more blind; rare in the sight sense, where English says partially, completely or legally blind
Takes
to + noun (not noticing: blind to the risks); in + one eye (blind in one eye)
Opposite

Contrast
The blind means blind people as a group and takes a plural verb: The blind are, a school for the blind. One person is a blind man, a blind woman or a blind person, never a blind; many people prefer blind people or people who are blind, and visually impaired includes people with some sight. The noun blind is a window covering: Pull down the blinds. Losing your sight is go blind. Russian вслепую after a verb is usually without looking or blindly (He signed the papers without reading them), but set phrases keep the adjective: a blind date, a blind taste test. A street with no way out is a dead end; a blind alley is mostly figurative.

Blind has 6 distinct senses.

1. Unable to see, from birth or because of illness or injury. (слепой, незрячий)

  1. Her grandfather went blind in his seventies.
  2. He's been blind in one eye since the accident.
  3. The museum offers tours for the blind with objects you can touch.

2. blind to: not noticing, or not wanting to accept, something such as a fault or a danger. (не замечающий, слепой (к))

  1. Parents are often blind to their own kids' faults.
  2. The company seemed blind to the risks.
  3. No one was more blind to the problem than the boss himself.

3. Not based on reason or careful thought; too strong to control. (слепой, безрассудный)

  1. The soldiers followed orders with blind obedience.
  2. He stormed out in a blind rage.
  3. It takes blind faith to invest in a company like that.

4. Done without seeing or knowing something important in advance. (проводимый вслепую; анонимный)

  1. In a blind taste test, most people picked the cheaper cola.
  2. My friends set me up on a blind date.
  3. The judges do a blind review, so they never see the writers' names.

5. Hard or impossible to see around or past, so you can't see what is coming. (с плохим обзором, скрытый)

  1. Slow down, there's a blind curve ahead.
  2. The motorcycle was right in my blind spot.

6. Of a wall, an alley or a passage: having no opening or no way through. (глухой (о стене), тупиковый)

  1. The back of the warehouse is a blind wall with no windows.
  2. Every idea we tried led us up a blind alley.

Deaf is /dɛf/, rhyming with chef, not /diːf/. The deaf takes a plural verb (a school for the deaf), and one person is a deaf man or a deaf person, never a deaf. With a capital, Deaf names the community and culture of people who use sign language; deaf and dumb and deaf-mute are offensive. For partial hearing loss Americans say hard of hearing. Russian глухой has meanings deaf doesn't: a remote place is remote (a remote village, not a deaf village), a muffled sound is dull or muffled (a dull thud), a wall with no windows is blank, and thick forest is dense.

Deaf has 3 distinct senses.

1. Unable to hear, completely or partly. (глухой, неслышащий)

  1. She's deaf in her left ear.
  2. Grandpa is getting deafer every year, so speak up.
  3. The theater has captioned shows for the deaf.

2. deaf to: unwilling to listen to or pay attention to something. (глухой (к), не желающий слушать)

  1. The city council was deaf to the neighbors' complaints.
  2. Our warnings fell on deaf ears.
  3. He turned a deaf ear to all our advice.

3. Deaf: belonging to the community of deaf people who share sign language and a culture. (относящийся к сообществу глухих)

  1. Deaf culture has its own theater, poetry and humor.
  2. She grew up in a Deaf family where everyone signed.

Russian инвалид is not English invalid: for a person that word is old-fashioned and offensive, and pronounced /ɪnˈvælɪd/ it means 'not valid' (an invalid password). Disabled has 3 distinct senses.

1. Having a physical or mental condition that limits how a person moves, senses things or does everyday activities. (с инвалидностью)

  1. The law protects disabled workers from discrimination.
  2. Her son has been disabled since a car accident.
  3. The new buses make it easier for the disabled to get around town.

2. Meant for use by people with disabilities. (для инвалидов)

  1. You need a disabled parking placard to park there.
  2. All the disabled parking spots near the entrance were taken.

3. Of a machine, a vehicle or a feature: not working, or switched off. (неисправный; отключённый)

  1. A disabled truck is blocking the left lane.
  2. Cookies are disabled in your browser.

The wealthy takes a plural verb (The wealthy are moving out of the city) and never takes -s; one person is a wealthy man or a wealthy woman, never a wealthy.   1. Wyoming is wealthy in coal and natural gas.
  2. The country is wealthy in natural resources but has few good roads.

The disadvantaged takes a plural verb and never takes -s; one person is a disadvantaged student or someone from a disadvantaged background, never a disadvantaged.   1. Small farmers feel disadvantaged next to the big agricultural companies.
  2. Students without internet at home were more disadvantaged than anyone when classes moved online.

Unlike the rich or the young, the accused often means one person, so the verb follows the meaning: The accused was found guilty (one defendant), The accused were all released (several). Privileged has 4 distinct senses.

Unlike the rich or the young, the unknown is not a group of people: it means everything that is not known and takes a singular verb. Fear of the unknown is страх перед неизвестностью. For people you don't know, add a noun: an unknown caller, unknown attackers. As a countable noun, an unknown is a person who isn't famous (a total unknown) or a fact nobody knows yet (too many unknowns); those are nouns.

Unknown has 3 distinct senses.

1. Not known; not identified or not yet discovered. (неизвестный)

  1. The cause of the fire is still unknown.
  2. He left the company for unknown reasons.
  3. An unknown number of people were still inside.

2. Not famous; not known to the public. (неизвестный, безвестный)

  1. The movie turned an unknown actress into a star.
  2. The band was unknown outside Seattle until 1991.

3. unknown to someone: not known by that person, or happening without their knowledge. (без ведома; незнакомый (кому-то))

  1. Unknown to his wife, he had borrowed money against the house.
  2. The word was unknown to most of the class.
  3. It was a disease unknown to doctors fifty years ago.
