Adjectives that end in -ly#

friendly, lonely, lovely, elderly — adjectives that look like adverbs.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

These words end in -ly but are adjectives, not adverbs: a friendly dog, a lonely night. Most of them have no adverb form at all, so she smiled friendly is wrong and English needs a phrase instead: she smiled in a friendly way. A few — daily, weekly, early — are both, and those entries show each use.

friendly#

Pronunciation
/ˈfrɛndli/ · FREND-lee
Русский
дружелюбный; дружественный
Type
quality
Position
both — a friendly neighbor, Our neighbors are friendly
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — friendlier, friendliest, more friendly
Takes
to or toward a person (how you treat them: friendly to newcomers); to a group or an idea (supportive of it: friendly to small businesses); with a person (on good terms with them: friendly with the neighbors)
Preposition patterns
friendly with
Form
friend + -ly
Opposite
unfriendly; hostile
Contrast
There is no adverb friendlily. She smiled friendly is wrong; say She smiled in a friendly way or She gave me a friendly smile. After a linking verb the adjective is right: He seems friendly, Be friendly. Friendly to someone is about how you treat them; friendly with someone means you are on good terms: I'm friendly with her brother (мы с её братом приятели). In user-friendly, eco-friendly and kid-friendly the word means suitable for, or not harmful to.

Friendly has 7 distinct senses.

1. Kind and pleasant to other people; behaving like a friend. (дружелюбный, приветливый)

  1. The people at the front desk were really friendly.
  2. She has a warm, friendly smile.
  3. My new coworkers are a lot friendlier than my old ones.

2. On good terms with someone; not hostile. (дружественный, в дружеских отношениях)

  1. The two countries have had friendly relations for decades.
  2. I'm friendly with a few of the other parents at school.
  3. We parted on friendly terms.

3. Supportive of a person, a group or an idea; ready to agree or help. (благосклонный, доброжелательно настроенный)

  1. The senator gave her speech to a friendly crowd of supporters.
  2. The new mayor has been friendly to small businesses.
  3. Don't expect the reviewers to be friendly to a sequel.

4. Of a place or atmosphere: welcoming and comfortable. (уютный, радушный)

  1. It's a friendly little diner where everyone knows your name.
  2. The office has a relaxed, friendly atmosphere.

5. On your own side in a fight or a war. (свой, союзный)

  1. The soldier was killed by friendly fire.
  2. The damaged plane made it back to a friendly airfield.

6. Done for fun or in a spirit of goodwill, not as serious competition. (товарищеский, дружеский)

  1. It's just a friendly game — nobody's keeping score.
  2. They made a friendly bet on the Super Bowl.
  3. A little friendly competition keeps the team motivated.

7. Not harmful to something, or well suited to a particular group; usually after another word. (безопасный для; удобный для)

  1. We switched to environmentally friendly cleaning products.
  2. The museum is very kid-friendly.
  3. Is the hotel pet-friendly?

lovely#

Pronunciation
/ˈlʌvli/ · LUHV-lee
Русский
прекрасный, чудесный
Type
evaluative
Position
both — a lovely view, The view is lovely
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — lovelier, loveliest, more lovely
Form
love + -ly
Contrast
There is no adverb lovelily: She sings lovely is wrong; say She sings beautifully. lovely has nothing to do with being in love or being loved: влюблённый is in love, любимый is favorite or beloved. It is far more frequent in British speech; Americans use it too, but nice, great and beautiful are more usual in casual talk.

Lovely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Beautiful or very attractive to look at. (красивый, прелестный)

  1. What a lovely view of the lake!
  2. You look lovely in that dress.
  3. It was the loveliest garden on the whole street.

2. Very pleasant or enjoyable. (чудесный, замечательный)

  1. We had a lovely evening — thanks for inviting us.
  2. It was lovely to see you again.
  3. The weather has been lovely all week.

3. Of a person: kind, friendly and easy to like. (милый, славный)

  1. Her parents are lovely people.
  2. He's a lovely man and a great teacher.

lonely#

Pronunciation
/ˈloʊnli/ · LOHN-lee
Русский
одинокий; безлюдный
Type
emotion; also quality
Position
both — a lonely old man, He felt lonely
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — lonelier, loneliest, more lonely
Form
lone + -ly
Contrast
alone means without other people — a plain fact — and it is predicative only: She lives alone, never an alone woman. lonely means sad because you are alone or have no friends: You can be alone without being lonely. lone goes only before a noun and means the only one: a lone tree, a lone gunman. There is no adverb lonelily; rephrase: She sat by herself, feeling lonely.

Lonely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Unhappy because you are alone or have no friends; or, of a time or a life, spent that way. (одинокий, тоскующий)

  1. After his wife died, he felt terribly lonely.
  2. Moving to a new city can be lonely at first.
  3. It was the loneliest year of my life.

2. Of a place: far from where people live and seldom visited. (безлюдный, уединённый)

  1. The car broke down on a lonely stretch of highway in Nevada.
  2. The cabin sits on a lonely hillside miles from town.

3. Standing or moving alone, with nobody or nothing else nearby. (одинокий, одиноко стоящий)

  1. A lonely figure walked along the empty beach.
  2. One lonely light was still on in the office building.

lively#

Pronunciation
/ˈlaɪvli/ · LYV-lee
Русский
живой, оживлённый
Type
quality
Position
both — a lively debate, The debate got lively
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — livelier, liveliest, more lively
Form
life + -ly
Opposite
dull; quiet
Contrast
Pronounced LYV-lee, with the vowel of five, not the vowel of the verb live. Keep three words apart: alive = not dead, predicative only (The fish is still alive); live (LYV) = happening now or not recorded, before a noun (live music, a live broadcast); lively = full of energy (a lively crowd). A live concert is not recorded; a lively concert is energetic. There is no adverb livelily; say in a lively way.

Lively has 6 distinct senses.

1. Full of energy; cheerful and active. (живой, энергичный, бойкий)

  1. Their kids are bright and lively.
  2. At ninety, she's still as lively as ever.
  3. He was the liveliest person at the party.

2. Of a place or an event: busy, with a lot happening. (оживлённый, шумный)

  1. The neighborhood has a lively bar scene.
  2. The market gets livelier on weekends.

3. Of a discussion or debate: full of strong opinions and eager talk. (оживлённый, бурный)

  1. The proposal led to a lively debate at the town meeting.
  2. The class discussion got pretty lively.

4. Of the mind, interest or imagination: quick, strong and active. (живой (ум, интерес))

  1. She has a lively imagination.
  2. He took a lively interest in local politics.

5. Of music or dancing: fast and cheerful. (задорный, весёлый)

  1. The band played a lively polka.
  2. The song has a lively, catchy beat.

6. Of colors: bright and strong. (яркий, сочный)

  1. The kitchen was painted in lively shades of yellow and green.
  2. A lively print can brighten up a plain room.

silly#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɪli/ · SIL-ee
Русский
глупый; дурашливый
Type
quality
Position
both — a silly mistake, Don't be silly; the 'bored silly' sense only after a verb
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — sillier, silliest, more silly
Takes
of + a person + to-infinitive (judging the person: It was silly of me to forget); for + a person + to-infinitive (the plan makes no sense: It's silly for us to take two cars)
Opposite
sensible
Contrast
There is no adverb sillily in normal use: say in a silly way, or use a linking verb: Stop acting silly. silly is milder and friendlier than stupid: calling an idea silly is teasing, calling it stupid is an insult. To judge a person, the pattern is It was silly of you, not silly from you.

Silly has 4 distinct senses.

1. Showing poor judgment; foolish. (глупый)

  1. It was a silly mistake, and it cost us the game.
  2. Don't be silly — of course you can come.
  3. That's the silliest excuse I've ever heard.

2. Playful and not serious, in a way meant to be funny. (дурашливый, смешной)

  1. The kids made silly faces for the camera.
  2. We all wore silly hats to the party.
  3. He gets sillier the more tired he is.

3. Small and unimportant; not worth worrying about. (пустячный, ерундовый)

  1. They had a fight over something silly.
  2. It's just a silly little gift.

4. Into a dazed or helpless state; only after verbs such as bore, scare and laugh. (до одури, до смерти)

  1. The lecture bored me silly.
  2. The movie scared the kids silly.
  3. We laughed ourselves silly.

ugly#

Pronunciation
/ˈʌɡli/ · UHG-lee
Русский
некрасивый, уродливый
Type
evaluative
Position
both — an ugly building, The building is ugly
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — uglier, ugliest, more ugly
Opposite
beautiful; attractive
Contrast
There is no adverb uglily: say in an ugly way. Said of a person, ugly is much harsher than Russian некрасивый; Americans soften it to not very attractive or plain. In turn ugly and get ugly it means dangerous or violent, not unattractive: The protest turned ugly.

Ugly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Unpleasant to look at. (некрасивый, уродливый)

  1. They painted the house an ugly shade of brown.
  2. Honestly, I think the new stadium is ugly.
  3. That's the ugliest sweater I've ever seen.

2. Of a situation or mood: unpleasant and threatening, likely to turn violent. (скверный, угрожающий)

  1. Things got ugly when the police arrived.
  2. There was an ugly scene outside the bar.
  3. The crowd was in an ugly mood.

3. Morally unpleasant; cruel or offensive. (мерзкий, гнусный)

  1. Someone started an ugly rumor about her.
  2. The trial exposed an ugly side of the town's history.

elderly#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛldərli/ · EL-dur-lee
Русский
пожилой
Type
age
Position
both — an elderly neighbor, My parents are elderly now
Comparison
more/most — more elderly, most elderly, both rare; very elderly is the usual way to grade it
Form
elder + -ly
Opposite
young
Contrast
elderly is a polite word for people who are old; old is plainer and can sound blunt about a person, and many Americans now prefer older (older adults) or senior. It is not a comparative: my older brother or my elder brother is старший брат, never my elderly brother. It is not an adverb either. The elderly (пожилые люди as a group) is a noun use.

Elderly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a person: old; a polite word. (пожилой)

  1. An elderly woman asked me to help with her bags.
  2. The program delivers hot meals to elderly residents.
  3. Her father is elderly and can't drive anymore.

2. Of a thing: old and no longer working well; used humorously. (старенький, видавший виды)

  1. He still drives an elderly Buick.
  2. Our elderly laptop takes five minutes to start up.

costly#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɔːstli/ · KAWST-lee
Русский
дорогостоящий
Type
quality
Position
both — costly repairs, The repairs were costly
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — costlier, costliest, more costly
Form
cost + -ly
Opposite
cheap; inexpensive
Contrast
For prices in stores and restaurants, expensive is the everyday word: an expensive restaurant, not usually a costly restaurant. costly is more formal and goes with repairs, delays, lawsuits and mistakes, where it often means causing loss: a costly error = ошибка, которая дорого обошлась. There is no adverb costlily.

Costly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Costing a lot of money. (дорогостоящий, дорогой)

  1. The old roof needed costly repairs.
  2. Treating the disease is extremely costly.
  3. Flying turned out to be costlier than driving.

2. Causing a serious loss, damage or suffering. (дорого обошедшийся, губительный)

  1. Leaving the door unlocked was a costly mistake.
  2. The delay proved costly for the company.
  3. The fumble was the costliest error of the game.

deadly#

Pronunciation
/ˈdɛdli/ · DED-lee
Русский
смертельный, смертоносный
Type
quality
Position
both — a deadly virus, The virus is deadly; deadly enemies only before a noun
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — deadlier, deadliest, more deadly
Form
dead + -ly
Contrast
deadly = able to kill (a deadly disease, смертельный). deathly = like death (a deathly silence, deathly pale, мертвенный). dead = no longer alive. Before another adjective, deadly is an adverb meaning completely (deadly serious, deadly dull) — that use belongs to the adverb dictionary. In the seven deadly sins (семь смертных грехов) it means fatal to the soul, not to the body. There is no deadlily.

Deadly has 5 distinct senses.

1. Able or likely to cause death. (смертельный, смертоносный)

  1. Some wild mushrooms are deadly.
  2. Fentanyl is far deadlier than heroin.
  3. It was the deadliest hurricane in fifty years.

2. Of enemies or a conflict: extreme and aiming to destroy the other side. (смертельный, заклятый)

  1. The two gangs were deadly rivals.
  2. They had been deadly enemies since high school.

3. Extremely accurate or effective. (убийственный, беспощадно точный)

  1. He's a deadly shooter from three-point range.
  2. She hit the target with deadly accuracy.

4. Complete or total; with a few nouns such as silence and earnest. (полный, мёртвый)

  1. A deadly silence fell over the room.
  2. She was in deadly earnest about quitting.

5. Very boring; informal. (смертельно скучный)

  1. The first hour of the movie is deadly.
  2. Budget meetings can be absolutely deadly.

cowardly#

Pronunciation
/ˈkaʊərdli/ · KOW-urd-lee
Русский
трусливый
Type
quality
Position
both — a cowardly act, It was cowardly
Comparison
more/most — more cowardly, most cowardly
Form
coward + -ly
Opposite
brave; courageous
Contrast
coward is the noun, so it needs an article: He's a coward or He's cowardly, never He is coward. Though it ends in -ly, cowardly is not an adverb in modern English: He ran away like a coward, not He ran away cowardly.

Cowardly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Lacking courage; too afraid to face danger or difficulty. (трусливый, малодушный)

  1. He was too cowardly to tell her the truth himself.
  2. Don't be so cowardly — just go talk to her.
  3. It was the most cowardly thing I've ever seen anyone do.

2. Of an act: done so as to avoid risk to yourself, often by attacking people who cannot fight back. (подлый, трусливый)

  1. The mayor called the bombing a cowardly attack on innocent people.
  2. Breaking up by text is a cowardly way to end things.

orderly#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːrdərli/ · OR-dur-lee
Русский
упорядоченный, организованный
Type
quality
Position
both — orderly rows, The room was neat and orderly
Comparison
more/most — more orderly, most orderly
Form
order + -ly
Opposite
disorderly; messy
Contrast
There is no adverb form: Leave the building orderly is wrong; say in an orderly way or in an orderly fashion. An orderly (санитар в больнице) is a noun. The legal phrase disorderly conduct (нарушение общественного порядка) is common in American news.

Orderly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Neatly arranged, with everything in its place. (аккуратный, упорядоченный)

  1. Her desk is always neat and orderly.
  2. The books were lined up in orderly rows.

2. Calm and well behaved, without confusion or disorder. (организованный, спокойный)

  1. Please leave the building in an orderly fashion.
  2. The protest was peaceful and orderly.
  3. The transfer of power was more orderly than people expected.

3. Of a person, a mind or a way of life: organized, and following a regular system. (методичный, аккуратный)

  1. She has a very orderly mind.
  2. He lived a quiet, orderly life.

timely#

Pronunciation
/ˈtaɪmli/ · TYM-lee
Русский
своевременный
Type
evaluative
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a timely reminder; also The warning was timely
Comparison
more/most — more timely, most timely
Form
time + -ly
Opposite
untimely
Contrast
timely is not an adverb: Please pay timely is wrong; say pay on time or, in formal writing, in a timely manner. The opposite untimely is mostly met in an untimely death (безвременная смерть). Russian актуальный is often timely or topical — never actual, which means фактический.

Timely has 2 distinct senses.

1. Done or happening at the right time, or without delay. (своевременный, сделанный в срок)

  1. Please respond in a timely manner.
  2. We appreciate your timely payment.
  3. Thanks for the timely reminder — I almost forgot.

2. Dealing with something that matters right now; topical. (актуальный, злободневный)

  1. Her book on artificial intelligence couldn't be more timely.
  2. The documentary is especially timely in an election year.

motherly#

Pronunciation
/ˈmʌðərli/ · MUHTH-ur-lee
Русский
материнский, заботливый
Type
quality
Position
both — motherly advice, She was very motherly toward the new nurses
Comparison
more/most — more motherly, most motherly
Form
mother + -ly
Contrast
motherly describes warm, caring behavior like a good mother's. maternal is the classifying word for anything connected with mothers: maternal health, and my maternal grandmother (бабушка по матери), never my motherly grandmother. There is no adverb: She looked after him in a motherly way.

Kind, warm and protective, like a good mother.

  1. An older, motherly nurse held my hand the whole time.
  2. She's always been motherly toward the younger players.
  3. Nobody in the office is more motherly than our receptionist.

Kind, protective and ready to guide, like a good father.

scholarly#

Pronunciation
/ˈskɑːlərli/ · SKAH-lur-lee
Русский
учёный; научный
Type
classifying; also quality
Position
both — a scholarly journal, The book is scholarly but easy to read
Comparison
more/most — more scholarly, most scholarly
Form
scholar + -ly
Contrast
Russian научный splits in English. scientific is about the sciences and their methods (a scientific experiment); scholarly is about serious academic research in any field, especially the humanities (a scholarly book on Tolstoy); academic is about universities in general. In American English a scholar is учёный or a student with a scholarship, never a schoolchild.

Scholarly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of writing or work: based on serious academic research. (научный, академический)

  1. The article appeared in a scholarly journal.
  2. The book is scholarly but still easy to read.
  3. It's the most scholarly biography of Lincoln to date.

2. Of a person: having a lot of knowledge from serious study, and fond of study. (учёный, эрудированный)

  1. He was a quiet, scholarly man who read Greek for fun.
  2. Her scholarly manner made the students take her seriously.

leisurely#

Pronunciation
/ˈliːʒərli/ · LEE-zhur-lee
Русский
неторопливый
Type
quality
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a leisurely lunch; also The pace was leisurely
Comparison
more/most — more leisurely, most leisurely
Form
leisure + -ly
Opposite
hurried; rushed
Contrast
Unlike most -ly adjectives, it is also an adverb: They strolled leisurely along the river is correct but bookish, and Americans usually say at a leisurely pace. Done slowly and in a relaxed way, without hurrying.

  1. We took a leisurely walk along the beach.
  2. Sunday breakfast is always a leisurely affair at our house.
  3. The second half of the trip was more leisurely.

heavenly#

Pronunciation
/ˈhɛvənli/ · HEV-uhn-lee
Русский
небесный; божественный
Type
classifying; also evaluative
Position
both — heavenly bodies only before a noun; The cake was heavenly
Comparison
more/most — more heavenly in the 'delightful' sense; not gradable when it means 'of heaven'
Form
heaven + -ly
Contrast
A heavenly body is a star, planet or moon (небесное тело), not a beautiful person. Russian небесный is heavenly mainly in religion and in heavenly bodies; for the color of the sky say sky blue, and science uses celestial. In everyday speech heavenly is mostly about food, smells and comfort: That bed is heavenly.

Heavenly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of or from heaven, in religion; or in the sky, in the phrase heavenly bodies. (небесный)

  1. The choir sang of angels and the heavenly host.
  2. Our heavenly Father, hear our prayer.
  3. Galileo studied the moons of Jupiter and other heavenly bodies.

2. Very pleasant; delightful. (божественный, чудесный)

  1. This chocolate cake is heavenly.
  2. After the long hike, a hot shower felt heavenly.
  3. I can't imagine anything more heavenly than a week at the beach.

ghostly#

Pronunciation
/ˈɡoʊstli/ · GOHST-lee
Русский
призрачный
Type
quality
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a ghostly figure; also Her face looked ghostly

Form
ghost + -ly
Contrast
Do not confuse it with ghastly (GAST-lee), which means horrible or very bad: a ghastly accident, a ghastly mistake. ghostly means like a ghost. Neither word is an adverb.

Ghostly has 2 distinct senses.

  1. A ghostly figure appeared at the end of the hallway.
  2. The fog gave the streetlights a ghostly glow.

2. Strange and frightening, as if caused by a ghost. (жуткий, зловещий)

  1. We heard ghostly footsteps in the attic.
  2. The wind made a ghostly moaning sound through the trees.

worldly#

Pronunciation
/ˈwɜːrldli/ · WURLD-lee
Русский
мирской; искушённый
Type
quality
Position
both — worldly goods only before a noun; She was older and more worldly
Comparison
more/most — more worldly, most worldly
Form

spiritual (material sense); naive (experience sense)
Contrast
worldly does not mean мировой or всемирный. For those use world before a noun (a world record, world history), worldwide or global: world-famous (всемирно известный), never worldly famous. worldly is about material life (мирской) or experience of life (искушённый).

  1. The monks gave up all their worldly possessions.
  2. He left all his worldly goods to his niece.

2. Experienced and knowledgeable about life, people and society. (искушённый, бывалый)

  1. She seemed so worldly to us small-town kids.
  2. He was older and more worldly than the other students.
  3. A year in Paris made her more worldly.

daily#

Pronunciation
/ˈdeɪli/ · DAY-lee
Русский
ежедневный
Type
classifying
Position
attributive only — a daily walk; after a verb English uses the adverb: I walk daily
Comparison
not gradable
Form
day + -ly, with ay spelled ai
Contrast
The same spelling is also an adverb (The museum is open daily) and a noun (a daily — ежедневная газета). Keep apart everyday, one word and an adjective (everyday clothes, повседневный), and every day, two words and an adverb phrase (I wear it every day). Daily life and everyday life both mean повседневная жизнь.

Daily has 3 distinct senses.

1. Happening, done or published every day, or every working day. (ежедневный)

  1. She takes a daily walk after lunch.
  2. There are three daily flights to Denver.
  3. The manager sends us daily updates on the project.

2. Calculated for one day. (суточный, дневной)

  1. The daily rate for the rental car is $45.
  2. Try to stay under the recommended daily amount of sugar.
  3. The daily high was 94 degrees.

3. Belonging to ordinary life as it goes on day after day. (повседневный)
