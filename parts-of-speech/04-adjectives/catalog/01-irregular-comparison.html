Adjectives with irregular comparison#

good, better, best — and the adjectives whose comparative splits into two words with different jobs.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Almost every gradable adjective compares with -er/-est (taller) or more/most (more careful). A handful do neither, and their forms have to be learned: good, better, best; bad, worse, worst. Several also split into two words with different jobs — farther for distance and further for degree, older for age and elder for family rank, later for time and latter for the second of two — so each form has its own entry.

good#

Pronunciation
/ɡʊd/ · GUUD
Русский
хороший
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both — a good book, The book is good; in the senses 'large in amount' and 'complete and thorough' only before a noun (a good hour, a good look)
Comparison
irregular — better, best
Takes
at + noun or -ing for skill (good at chess); with + people or things you handle (good with kids); for + the person or thing it helps (good for your heart); to + a person for kindness (good to me); of + person + to-infinitive (good of you to come); about after feel (feel good about it); to-infinitive (good to know); that-clause (It's good that you called)
Preposition patterns
good at
Opposite
bad
Contrast
Good is the adjective and well the adverb: She's a good driver / She drives well — She drives good is heard in casual speech but is marked wrong in careful English. After linking verbs use good: It looks good, That smells good. I feel good means happy or in good shape; I feel well means not sick. In American English I'm good means I'm fine and, when you are offered something, No, thanks. Skill is good at (not good in), kindness is good to, benefit is good for: She's good at math, He was good to me, Fish is good for you. The forms are better and best, never gooder or more good. Russian добрый about a person is usually kind, not good.

Good has 16 distinct senses.

1. Of high quality; done or made well. (хороший, качественный)

  1. That was a really good movie.
  2. The food here is good, but the service is slow.
  3. Her second novel is even better than the first.

2. Skillful; able to do something well. (умелый, способный)

  1. She's good at math.
  2. He's a good driver — I always feel safe with him.
  3. My dad is really good with his hands.

3. Pleasant or enjoyable. (приятный, хороший)

  1. Did you have a good time at the party?
  2. I've got some good news.
  3. The weather should be better this weekend.

4. Morally right; kind and honest. (хороший, порядочный; добрый (к кому-то))

  1. He's a good man who would never lie to you.
  2. It was good of you to drive me home.
  3. She's always been good to her neighbors.

5. Well-behaved — of a child or an animal. (послушный)

  1. Be good for the babysitter, okay?
  2. The kids were very good on the flight.
  3. Who's a good dog?

6. Beneficial; having a good effect on your health or well-being. (полезный)

  1. Walking every day is good for you.
  2. Too much soda isn't good for your teeth.
  3. Getting out of the city was good for her.

7. Suitable or convenient. (подходящий, удобный)

  1. Is this a good time to talk?
  2. Tuesday is good for me.
  3. What's the best way to get to the airport?

8. Acceptable; good enough for what is needed. (годный, приемлемый; ладно)

  1. "Let's meet at seven." "Sounds good."
  2. The photo isn't perfect, but it's good enough for the website.
  3. Your English is good enough for the job.

9. Attractive or pleasing to look at. (привлекательный; (хорошо) выглядящий)

  1. You look good in that jacket.
  2. The new kitchen looks really good.

10. Valid; still able to be used. (действительный)

  1. This ticket is good for one ride.
  2. The coupon is good until the end of the month.

11. Still fresh; not spoiled. (свежий, неиспорченный)

  1. Is this milk still good?
  2. The bread should be good for another day or two.

12. Strong or sensible enough to be accepted. (веский, уважительный)

  1. Do you have a good reason for being late?
  2. That's a good point.
  3. She had a good excuse for missing the meeting.

13. Fine; satisfied and not needing anything — informal American. (в порядке; не нужно, спасибо)

  1. "How are you?" "Good, thanks."
  2. "Do you want more coffee?" "No, I'm good."
  3. We're good — you don't owe me anything.

14. Large in amount; at least as much as stated. (изрядный; добрый (о количестве))

  1. The hotel is a good ten miles from the beach.
  2. We spent a good deal of money on repairs.
  3. It took a good hour to get there.

15. Complete and thorough. (основательный; как следует)

  1. Take a good look before you sign anything.
  2. What I need is a good night's sleep.
  3. She had a good cry and felt a lot better.

16. Sure to pay back money owed — in good for. (надёжный (о должнике))

  1. I'll lend you fifty bucks — I know you're good for it.
  2. Don't worry, his brother is good for the money.

bad#

Pronunciation
/bæd/ · BAD
Русский
плохой
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both — a bad idea, The idea is bad
Comparison
irregular — worse, worst
Takes
at + noun or -ing for skill (bad at math); with + things you handle (bad with names); for + the person or thing harmed (bad for your back); about after feel for guilt (feel bad about it); for after feel for pity (feel bad for him); to-infinitive (It's bad to skip breakfast); that-clause (It's bad that nobody told her)
Preposition patterns
bad at
Opposite
good
Contrast
Bad is the adjective and badly the adverb: a bad singer / She sings badly. After linking verbs use bad: It smells bad, I feel bad. I feel bad about it means guilty; I feel bad for him means sorry for him; I feel badly is common in American speech but disliked by careful writers. The forms are worse and worst — more bad is wrong, and badder is only slang. Not bad is praise: it means pretty good. Russian мне плохо is I feel sick or I feel bad, not I'm bad, which means I behave badly. My bad (my mistake) is a slang noun, not this adjective.

Bad has 15 distinct senses.

1. Of low quality; not good. (плохой, некачественный)

  1. The service at that restaurant is really bad.
  2. His handwriting is so bad I can't read it.
  3. That was the worst movie I've ever seen.

2. Unpleasant or unwelcome. (плохой, неприятный)

  1. I'm afraid I have some bad news.
  2. It's been a bad day.
  3. The weather got worse in the afternoon.

3. Serious or severe. (сильный, тяжёлый)

  1. She has a bad cold.
  2. There was a bad accident on the interstate.
  3. Traffic is worst on Friday afternoons.

4. Harmful. (вредный)

  1. Smoking is bad for you.
  2. Is coffee bad for kids?
  3. Sitting all day is bad for your back.

5. Not suitable or convenient. (неподходящий, неудобный)

  1. Is this a bad time to talk?
  2. Friday is bad for me — how about Monday?
  3. He picked the worst possible moment to ask for a raise.

6. Not skillful. (неумелый, слабый)

  1. I'm bad at math.
  2. He's terrible with names, and I'm even worse.
  3. She's a bad driver.

7. Morally wrong; evil. (дурной, нехороший, злой)

  1. He's not a bad person — he just made a mistake.
  2. Lying to her was a bad thing to do.

8. Naughty — of a child or an animal. (непослушный)

  1. Bad dog! Get off the couch!
  2. Were the kids bad for the sitter?

9. Rude or offensive — of words and language. (неприличный, грубый)

  1. Mom, he said a bad word!
  2. The movie is rated R for bad language.

10. Spoiled or rotten. (испорченный, тухлый)

  1. The milk has gone bad.
  2. Throw out any bad apples.

11. Guilty or embarrassed about something you did — with feel. (стыдно, неловко)

  1. I feel bad about forgetting your birthday.
  2. Don't feel bad — it wasn't your fault.

12. Sorry about someone else's trouble — in feel bad for. (жалко (кого-то))

  1. I feel bad for her — she worked so hard for that job.
  2. We all felt bad for the new guy.

13. Unwell; or injured and painful. (больной; плохо (о самочувствии))

  1. I've had a bad back for years.
  2. He has a bad knee, so he can't run.
  3. She was feeling worse by evening, so we called the doctor.

14. Unfortunate; a pity — in too bad. (жаль)

  1. It's too bad you can't come.
  2. Too bad — the store's already closed.

15. Not valid; not able to be paid or collected. (недействительный, фальшивый; безнадёжный (о долге))

  1. He paid for the car with a bad check.
  2. The bank lost millions on bad loans.

far#

Pronunciation
/fɑːr/ · FAHR
Русский
дальний; далёкий
Type
quality
Position
attributive only — the far end, the far right; in It isn't far and How far is it? the word is the adverb
Comparison
irregular — farther, farthest for distance; further, furthest for distance or degree
Opposite
near
Contrast
As an adjective far stands before a noun and usually means the more distant of two sides or ends: the far side of the lake. It is not the everyday word for далёкий — say a distant country or a faraway place; a far country sounds literary. It's not far and How far is it? are adverb uses, and plain It's far is informal — It's a long way is safer. The comparison splits: farther and farthest are for physical distance; further and furthest work for distance too and are the only choice for additional (further questions). The far right is a political label (крайне правые), not a direction.

Far has 5 distinct senses.

1. Being the more distant of two sides, ends or edges. (дальний, противоположный)

  1. Our room was at the far end of the hall.
  2. They live on the far side of town.
  3. We took the farthest table from the kitchen because it was quieter.

2. At the most distant part of a region. (крайний)

  1. She grew up in the far north of the state, near the Canadian border.
  2. The storm hit the far western part of the state.

3. Holding the most extreme political views — in far left and far right. (крайний (левый, правый))

  1. The party has moved toward the far right.
  2. Far-left groups organized the protest.

4. Distant in space or time — mostly literary, and in the far future. (далёкий)

  1. Robots may do that kind of work in the far future.
  2. Pilgrims came from far lands to see the shrine.

5. Very different from something — in a far cry from. (совсем не то, что; далеко не)

  1. The new apartment is a far cry from our old place.
  2. The final product was a far cry from what they promised.

little#

Pronunciation
/ˈlɪtəl/ · LIT-uhl
Русский
маленький; мало
Type
size; also quantity
Position
both — before a noun in every sense (a little house); after a verb mainly for age (when I was little), since small sounds more natural there for size (The house is small)
Comparison
irregular — less, least for amount; for size smaller, smallest, with littler, littlest in informal speech
Opposite
big; much for amount
Contrast
Little often carries feeling (a cute little house, a nasty little man); small is neutral and is the word to compare: a smaller room, rarely a littler room. For amount, little with an uncountable noun is negative, like мало: We have little time; a little is positive, like немного: We have a little time. Many grammars call this amount use a determiner, and its forms are less and least. With countable nouns use few: few friends, not little friends. In a little tired the word is an adverb, and in I speak a little a pronoun.

Little has 7 distinct senses.

1. Small in size. (маленький, небольшой)

  1. They live in a little house by the lake.
  2. Cut the potatoes into little pieces.
  3. That's the littlest puppy I've ever seen.

2. Young. (маленький (о возрасте))

  1. We have two little kids.
  2. I lived in Ohio when I was little.

3. Younger — of a brother or sister. (младший)

  1. This is my little brother, Sam.
  2. Her little sister just started college.

4. Short in time or distance. (недолгий, короткий)

  1. Can you wait a little while?
  2. Let's take a little walk after dinner.

5. Not important; minor. (незначительный, мелкий)

  1. It's just a little problem — don't worry about it.
  2. She always notices the little things.

6. Not much; hardly any — before uncountable nouns. (мало)

  1. There's little hope of finding them now.
  2. He showed little interest in the job.
  3. We had less time than we expected.

7. Used to show affection or annoyance. (маленький (ласкательно или пренебрежительно))

  1. What a sweet little baby!
  2. Get out of my room, you little brat!

old#

Pronunciation
/oʊld/ · OHLD
Русский
старый
Type
age
Position
both — an old house, The house is old; in the senses 'former' and 'known for a long time' only before a noun (my old boss, an old friend)
Comparison
-er/-est — older, oldest; elder, eldest only for rank in a family
Opposite
young; new
Contrast
Age is be + number + years old, never have: She is ten years old, not She has ten years. Before a noun the phrase is hyphenated and singular: a ten-year-old girl. Position changes the meaning: an old friend is a friend you have known for years, not an elderly one, and my old job is my former job. Older and oldest are the normal forms, in families too (my older brother); elder and eldest go only before a noun and never take than. About people, old can sound blunt — older or elderly is politer.

Old has 8 distinct senses.

1. Having lived for a long time; not young. (старый, пожилой)

  1. An old man was feeding the pigeons.
  2. My grandparents are getting old.
  3. She's the oldest person in our town.

2. Of a particular age. (в возрасте (… лет))

  1. How old are you?
  2. Our son is six years old.
  3. My sister is two years older than me.

3. Having existed or been used for a long time; not new. (старый, поношенный)

  1. I wear my old sneakers for yard work.
  2. They bought an old farmhouse and fixed it up.
  3. This is the oldest building in the city.

4. Belonging to the distant past; traditional. (старинный, древний)

  1. It's an old family recipe.
  2. The town has kept many of its old customs.

5. Former; from an earlier time in your life. (бывший, прежний)

  1. I ran into my old boss at the mall.
  2. Do you miss your old school?
  3. In the old days, nobody locked their doors.

6. Known for a long time. (давний, старый)

  1. Mike is an old friend from college.
  2. The two teams are old rivals.

7. Boring because it keeps happening — in get old and the same old. (надоевший)

  1. His jokes are getting old.
  2. It's the same old story every Monday.

8. Used informally to show affection or for emphasis. (старый добрый; какой угодно)

  1. Good old Dave — he always shows up.
  2. Just grab any old shirt.
  3. He drives a big old pickup truck.

late#

Pronunciation
/leɪt/ · LAYT
Русский
опоздавший; поздний
Type
quality
Position
both — a late train, The train is late; in the sense 'dead' only before a noun (her late husband)
Comparison
-er/-est — later, latest; latter and last are separate words
Takes
for + an event (late for work); to + an event or place, common in American speech (late to class); with + something owed or due (late with the rent)
Preposition patterns
late for
Opposite
early; on time
Contrast
Position changes the meaning: her late husband is dead, but Her husband is late means he has not arrived yet. Lately is not the adverb of this word — it means recently: I've been busy lately; He arrived late uses the adverb late. Russian уже поздно is It's late, with the adjective. Later is the comparative (a later flight) and latest the superlative, which also means newest; latter (the second of two) and last (final) have their own entries.

Late has 4 distinct senses.

1. Arriving or happening after the expected or arranged time. (опоздавший; запоздалый)

  1. Sorry I'm late — the traffic was terrible.
  2. The bus was twenty minutes late.
  3. Don't be late for your interview.

2. Happening after the usual time. (поздний)

  1. We had a late lunch.
  2. Spring was late this year.
  3. Is there a later flight to Denver?

3. Near the end of a day or of a period of time. (поздний; конец (периода))

  1. It's getting late — let's go home.
  2. The house was built in the late 1800s.
  3. She's in her late thirties.

4. Dead, especially recently. (покойный)

  1. The book is dedicated to her late husband.
  2. The foundation was started by the late senator.

near#

Pronunciation
/nɪr/ · NEER
Русский
близкий; ближний
Type
quality
Position
both — after a verb for distance and time (The station is near); before a noun only in set uses (the near future, a near miss) and in the comparative and superlative (the nearest store)
Comparison
-er/-est — nearer, nearest
Opposite
far
Contrast
Do not put plain near before a noun to mean close by: say a nearby store or a store nearby, not a near store — though the nearest store is correct. Near the park is the preposition and Come near the adverb; neither is this adjective. American English says near the beach, rarely near to the beach. A near miss is an accident that almost happened. For family, a close relative is far more common in the US than a near relative.

Near has 5 distinct senses.

1. Only a short distance away. (близкий, недалёкий)

  1. The hotel is quite near — we can walk.
  2. Is the subway station very near?
  3. Let's eat at the nearest diner.

2. Soon in time. (близкий, ближайший)

  1. Prices will rise in the near future.
  2. The holidays are near, and the stores are packed.

3. Almost happening or almost complete. (едва не случившийся; почти полный)

  1. That was a near miss — the truck almost hit us.
  2. The game ended in near silence.
  3. The flood was a near disaster for the town.

4. Being the closer of two sides. (ближний)

  1. Park on the near side of the street.
  2. We swam from the near bank to the island.

5. Closely related — formal. (близкий (о родстве))

  1. Only near relatives can inherit under the will.
  2. She has no near family left.

ill#

Pronunciation
/ɪl/ · IL
Русский
больной
Type
quality
Position
both — in the sense 'sick' mostly after a verb (She's ill) or with an adverb before a noun (seriously ill patients); in the senses 'bad' and 'hostile' only before a noun (ill effects, ill will)
Comparison
irregular — worse, worst
Takes
with + an illness (ill with the flu)
Opposite
well; healthy
Contrast
In everyday American English sick is the usual word and ill is more formal: I'm sick / She is seriously ill. An ill child sounds stiff — say a sick child — but an adverb makes ill normal before a noun (terminally ill patients). In British English sick often means vomiting; in American English it means ill. Before a noun ill usually means bad, not sick: ill effects, ill will. The forms worse and worst belong to both ill and bad: She got worse. Ill- in compounds (ill-advised, ill-treated) is the adverb, and the ills of society is a noun.

Ill has 4 distinct senses.

1. Sick; not in good health. (больной)

  1. Her father is seriously ill.
  2. I felt ill after the long bus ride.
  3. By Tuesday she was worse, so we took her to the ER.

2. Bad or harmful. (плохой, вредный)

  1. The drug has no known ill effects.
  2. He retired early because of ill health.

3. Unfriendly or hostile. (недобрый, враждебный)

  1. I bear him no ill will.
  2. There was a lot of ill feeling after the vote.

4. Nervous and uncomfortable — in ill at ease. (не по себе, неловко)

  1. He looked ill at ease in a suit and tie.
  2. Big parties make me feel ill at ease.

well#

Pronunciation
/wɛl/ · WEL
Русский
здоровый
Type
quality
Position
predicative only — I'm not well; before a noun only in medical phrases like a well-baby visit
Comparison
irregular — better, best
Opposite
ill; sick
Contrast
The adjective well means healthy; the adverb well means in a good way: I'm well (not sick) / She sings well. I feel well is about health; I feel good is about health or mood, and in casual American English I'm good is the usual answer to How are you? The comparative is better: I feel better, not more well. Because it only follows a verb, a well man is wrong — say a healthy man. Well the noun (колодец) and the interjection (Well, maybe) are other words.

Well has 4 distinct senses.

1. Healthy; not sick. (здоровый)

  1. I'm not feeling well today.
  2. Is your mother well?
  3. She was sick last week, but she's better now.

2. In a good or satisfactory state. (в порядке, хорошо)

  1. All is well at home.
  2. I hope all is well with you and your family.

3. Lucky or a good thing, considering what happened — in just as well. (к лучшему; и хорошо, что)

  1. It's just as well we left early — the roads got icy.
  2. She didn't get the job, which was just as well, since she hated the commute.

4. Sensible or advisable — formal. (разумно, желательно)

  1. It would be well to get a second opinion.
  2. It might be well to call ahead before you go.

better#

Pronunciation
/ˈbɛtər/ · BET-ur
Русский
лучший; лучше
Type
evaluative
Position
both — a better job, This one is better
Comparison
irregular — better is the comparative of good and well; the superlative is best
Takes
than + noun or clause; at + noun or -ing (better at math); with + people or things you handle; for + the person or thing it helps; to-infinitive (It's better to wait)
Opposite
worse
Contrast
Better is already comparative, so it never takes more or very: much better, even better, a lot better, not more better or very better. I'm better means I have recovered; I'm good only means I'm fine. You'd better go (had better) is a modal phrase, and She sings better is the adverb. Better off means in a better situation, richer: We're better off without him. Russian лучший is better when two things are compared (a better car) and the best for all others; careful English says the better of the two.

Better has 5 distinct senses.

1. Of higher quality, more pleasant or more suitable than someone or something else. (лучше, лучший)

  1. This laptop is much better than my old one.
  2. The weather is better today.
  3. Would Friday be better for you?

2. More skillful. (лучше (умеющий), сильнее)

  1. She's better at chess than her brother.
  2. He's better with kids than I am.

3. Less sick; or fully recovered. (лучше (о здоровье); выздоровевший)

  1. Are you feeling better today?
  2. My cold is better, but I still have a cough.
  3. Stay home until you're completely better.

4. More sensible or advisable. (лучше, разумнее)

  1. It's better to ask than to guess.
  2. It would be better if you told her yourself.

5. Most of something — in the better part of. (бо́льшая (часть))

  1. I waited the better part of an hour.
  2. They spent the better part of the day at the beach.

best#

Pronunciation
/bɛst/ · BEST
Русский
лучший; самый лучший
Type
evaluative
Position
both — the best pizza in town, Which one is best?; before a noun it needs the or a possessive (my best friend)
Comparison
irregular — best is the superlative of good and well; the comparative is better
Takes
at + noun or -ing (best at math); for + the person or purpose it suits; to-infinitive (It's best to wait)
Opposite
worst
Contrast
Best is already superlative: the best, the very best, never most best. Before a noun it needs the or a possessive — She's the best student, not She's best student. Without the it often means most sensible: It's best to call first. When only two things are compared, careful English uses better: the better of the two, though the best of the two is common in speech. Do your best and at best use the noun, and She sings best is the adverb.

Best has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of the highest quality; better than all others. (лучший, самый лучший)

  1. This is the best pizza in town.
  2. Emma has been my best friend since third grade.
  3. It was the best day of my life.

2. Most skillful. (лучший, самый сильный)

  1. She's the best player on the team.
  2. Who's best at math in your class?

3. Most suitable or advisable. (лучше всего; самый подходящий)

  1. It's best to book early.
  2. What's the best way to get downtown?
  3. Do whatever you think is best.

worse#

Pronunciation
/wɜːrs/ · WURS
Русский
худший; хуже
Type
evaluative
Position
both — a worse problem, The problem is worse
Comparison
irregular — worse is the comparative of bad and ill; the superlative is worst
Takes
than + noun or clause; at + noun or -ing (worse at math); for + the person or thing harmed
Opposite
better
Contrast
Worse is already comparative: much worse, even worse, never more worse or badder. Do not mix it up with worst: This is worse than yesterday (two things) / This is the worst day ever (all others). Worse ends in an s sound, worst in st. Worse off means in a worse situation, poorer. She sings worse than I do is the adverb.

Worse has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of lower quality or less pleasant than someone or something else. (хуже, худший)

  1. The sequel is worse than the original.
  2. The food was worse than I expected.
  3. We couldn't have picked a worse day for a picnic.
