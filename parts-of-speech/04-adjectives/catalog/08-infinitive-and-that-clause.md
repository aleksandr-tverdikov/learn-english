# Adjectives with a to-infinitive or that-clause

> *easy to use*, *likely to rain*, *glad that you came*, *kind of you to help*.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These adjectives take a *to*-infinitive or a *that*-clause: *easy to use*, *likely to rain*, *glad that you came*. Two traps run through the group. *It is kind of you* judges a person but *it is important for you* states a need, and a few adjectives — *essential*, *vital*, *imperative* — take the bare verb in their *that*-clause: *it is essential that he be there*.

---
### easy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈiːzi/ &middot; *EE-zee*
**Русский:** лёгкий; простой; спокойный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *easier*, *easiest*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (*easy to use*, *easy to talk to*); *for* + person (*easy for me*); *on* + person (not strict) or + thing (pleasant for: *easy on the eyes*)
**Opposite:** *difficult*, *hard*
**Contrast:** Russian *мне легко* is *It's easy for me*, never *I am easy*. In *This app is easy to use* the subject is the thing you use, so *He is easy to talk to* means people find it easy to talk to him. The adverb is *easily* (*She won easily*), but fixed phrases keep *easy*: *take it easy*, *go easy on him*, *easier said than done*, *easy come, easy go*. Said of a woman, *easy* is an insulting old slang word for 'promiscuous'; the compliment for a relaxed person is *easygoing*.

Easy has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Not difficult; done or understood without much effort.** (лёгкий, простой)

1. The first test was pretty *easy*.
2. This camera is *easy* to use, even for kids.
3. It's *easier* to take the subway than to find parking.

**2. Relaxed and comfortable; free from worry or stiffness.** (непринуждённый, спокойный)

4. She has an *easy*, friendly way with customers.
5. We walked at an *easy* pace along the beach.
6. The two of them fell into an *easy* silence.

**3. easy on + person, or before a noun: not strict; not demanding much.** (мягкий, снисходительный)

7. The coach was *easy* on the new players at first.
8. Her grandparents are much *easier* on her than her parents are.
9. Mr. Alvarez is an *easy* grader.

**4. easy on + the eyes, ears, stomach or budget: pleasant, not harsh or costly.** (приятный; щадящий)

10. The new font is much *easier* on the eyes.
11. The lunch menu is *easy* on the wallet.

### hard

**Pronunciation:** /hɑːrd/ &middot; *HAHRD*
**Русский:** трудный; твёрдый; тяжёлый; строгий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *harder*, *hardest*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (*hard to believe*, *hard to please*); *for* + person (*hard for me*); *on* + person (strict with) or + thing (causing wear: *hard on your knees*)
**Opposite:** *easy* (difficult); *soft* (solid)
**Contrast:** *Hard* is also an adverb with the same form: *work hard* (усердно), *try hard*, *It's raining hard*. *Hardly* is a different word meaning 'almost not' (едва): *She hardly works* is the opposite of *She works hard*. As with *easy*, Russian *мне трудно* is *It's hard for me*, not *I am hard*. *Hard water* has a lot of minerals (жёсткая вода); *hard liquor* is strong alcohol such as vodka or whiskey, and *hard cider* is the American name for alcoholic cider. For character, Russian *твёрдый* is usually *firm*; *a hard person* sounds cold and unkind. *No hard feelings* is 'no resentment', said after an argument or a loss.

Hard has 9 distinct senses.

**1. Difficult to do, understand or deal with.** (трудный, тяжёлый)

1. It's *hard* to believe she's only twelve.
2. The *hardest* part was waiting for the results.
3. That's a *hard* question to answer.

**2. Solid and firm; not easy to bend, cut or press.** (твёрдый, жёсткий)

4. The mattress in the hotel was too *hard*.
5. Let the caramel cool until it's *hard*.
6. Diamonds are *harder* than any other natural material.

**3. Needing or using a lot of effort or energy.** (тяжёлый; усердный)

7. Moving furniture is *hard* work.
8. She's the *hardest* worker on the team.
9. After a *hard* day at the office, I just want to sleep.

**4. Full of problems and suffering; harsh.** (тяжёлый, суровый)

10. Times were *hard* during the Great Depression.
11. It was the *hardest* winter the town had seen in decades.
12. He's had a *hard* life.

**5. Strict, severe or unfriendly.** (строгий, суровый)

13. Don't be so *hard* on yourself.
14. Her father was a *hard* man who rarely smiled.
15. She gave him a *hard* look and said nothing.

**6. Done with a lot of force.** (сильный)

16. He gave the door a *hard* push.
17. She took a *hard* fall on the ice.

**7. Definite and based on facts that can be proved.** (точный, неопровержимый)

18. The police have no *hard* evidence against him.
19. We need *hard* numbers, not guesses.

**8. Of drinks and drugs: very strong; containing a lot of alcohol, or highly addictive.** (крепкий (об алкоголе); тяжёлый (о наркотиках))

20. The bar serves beer and wine but no *hard* liquor.
21. He never touched *hard* drugs.

**9. Of water: containing a lot of minerals, so that soap does not lather well.** (жёсткий (о воде))

22. The water here is so *hard* it leaves spots on the glasses.
23. *Hard* water can damage your pipes over time.

### difficult

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɪfɪkəlt/ &middot; *DIF-ih-kuhlt*
**Русский:** трудный; сложный; несговорчивый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more difficult*, *most difficult*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (*difficult to explain*); *for* + person (*difficult for me*); *about* + noun, of a person (making trouble over it)
**Opposite:** *easy*
**Contrast:** Never *difficulter*: the forms are *more difficult* and *most difficult*. Russian *мне трудно* is *It's difficult for me to…*, not *I am difficult*, which means that I am hard to deal with. Russian *сложный* meaning 'made of many parts' is *complicated* or *complex* (*a complicated plan*), not always *difficult*. For tasks, everyday American speech prefers *hard* (*a hard test*); *difficult* sounds a little more formal.

Difficult has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Not easy; needing a lot of effort, skill or thought.** (трудный, сложный)

1. The exam was more *difficult* than I expected.
2. It's *difficult* to explain over the phone.
3. Choosing a college was the most *difficult* decision of her life.

**2. Full of problems; causing worry or trouble.** (тяжёлый, трудный)

4. The family is going through a *difficult* time.
5. His question put me in a *difficult* position.

**3. Of a person: not easy to please or deal with; unwilling to cooperate.** (трудный, несговорчивый, капризный)

6. Stop being *difficult* and just get in the car.
7. He can be *difficult* about money.
8. She's one of our more *difficult* clients.

### tough

**Pronunciation:** /tʌf/ &middot; *TUHF*
**Русский:** трудный; крепкий; строгий; жёсткий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *tougher*, *toughest*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (*tough to beat*); *for* + person (*tough for her*); *on* + person or problem (strict with: *tough on crime*; hard for: *tough on the kids*)
**Opposite:** *easy* (difficult); *weak* (strong); *tender* (of meat)
**Contrast:** In American speech *tough* is the everyday word for 'difficult' (*a tough week*, *a tough call*), more common than *difficult*. Russian *жёсткий* covers several English words: *tough* for meat and for strict rules, *hard* for a surface, *harsh* for criticism. *Tough luck!* and *Tough!* are not sympathy but the opposite: 'too bad, deal with it'. *Tough* rhymes with *stuff*; do not confuse it with *though* or *through*.

Tough has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Difficult; full of problems or needing a lot of effort.** (трудный, тяжёлый)

1. It's been a *tough* year for the business.
2. The Yankees will be *tough* to beat.
3. That was the *toughest* exam I've ever taken.

**2. Strong and able to deal with pain, hard work or trouble.** (крепкий, выносливый, стойкий)

4. She's *tougher* than she looks.
5. You have to be *tough* to survive in this business.

**3. Strict and firm; not willing to give way.** (строгий, жёсткий)

6. The senator promised to be *tough* on crime.
7. Our math teacher was *tough* but fair.
8. The city passed *tough* new rules on short-term rentals.

**4. Of food, especially meat: hard to cut or chew.** (жёсткий)

9. The steak was overcooked and *tough*.
10. Older chickens have *tougher* meat.

**5. Of a material: strong and not easily broken or torn.** (прочный)

11. These boots are made of *tough* leather.
12. The phone case is made of *tough* plastic.

**6. Of a place or person: rough and likely to be violent.** (опасный, неблагополучный; крутой)

13. He grew up in a *tough* neighborhood.
14. He likes to act *tough* in front of his friends.

**7. Unfortunate or unfair; as *Tough!* or *tough luck*, said without sympathy.** (неудачный; не повезло)

15. "I don't want to go." "*Tough*. You're going."
16. Losing your job right before the holidays is *tough*.
17. You missed the deadline? *Tough* luck.

### likely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlaɪkli/ &middot; *LYK-lee*
**Русский:** вероятный; подходящий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *more likely*, *most likely*; *likelier*, *likeliest* in writing
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive with a person or thing as subject (*She's likely to win*); *that*-clause after *it* (*It's likely that she'll win*)
**Form:** -ly from *like* in its old sense 'similar', so it is an adjective, not a manner adverb
**Opposite:** *unlikely*
**Contrast:** Although it ends in *-ly*, *likely* is an adjective: *a likely result*, *Rain is likely*. American English also uses it as an adverb meaning 'probably', usually with *most*, *very* or *quite* or right before the verb: *They'll most likely be late*, *She will likely win*. Russian *вероятно* is safest as *probably*. *Likely* allows a personal subject (*He is likely to call*), but *probable* does not: say *It is probable that he will call*, never *He is probable to call*. *Not likely!* is a spoken way to say 'no chance'.

Likely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Probably going to happen or be true.** (вероятный)

1. Snow is *likely* later tonight.
2. She's *likely* to win the election.
3. It's *more likely* that he just forgot.

**2. Seeming right for a purpose; promising.** (подходящий, многообещающий)

4. We searched every *likely* hiding place.
5. The bend in the river looked like a *likely* spot for trout.

**3. A likely story: used to say you do not believe what someone has told you.** (так я и поверил)

6. He says the dog ate his homework. A *likely* story!
7. "I was at the library all night." "A *likely* story."

### unlikely

**Pronunciation:** /ʌnˈlaɪkli/ &middot; *uhn-LYK-lee*
**Русский:** маловероятный; неожиданный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *more unlikely*, *most unlikely*; *unlikeliest* before a noun
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*He's unlikely to call*); *that*-clause after *it* (*It's unlikely that he'll call*)
**Form:** un- + *likely*
**Opposite:** *likely*
**Contrast:** Unlike *likely*, *unlikely* is never an adverb: *He probably won't come* or *He is unlikely to come*, not *He will unlikely come*. Do not confuse it with the preposition *unlike* (в отличие от): *Unlike his brother, he's quiet*. *Most unlikely* can be a true superlative or just mean 'very unlikely' (*That's most unlikely*).

Unlikely has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not probably going to happen or be true.** (маловероятный)

1. He's *unlikely* to call back before Monday.
2. It's *unlikely* that the store is still open.
3. A deal now seems *more unlikely* than ever.

**2. Not what you would expect; surprising.** (неожиданный, странный)

4. The two men formed an *unlikely* friendship.
5. She became the *unlikeliest* of heroes.
6. The idea came from an *unlikely* source.

### able

**Pronunciation:** /ˈeɪbəl/ &middot; *AY-buhl*
**Русский:** в состоянии; способный, умелый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *able to* + verb only after a verb; before a noun *able* means 'skilled': *an able student*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *abler*, *ablest*, *more able*; with *to* + verb, *better able*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (required in the 'can' sense: *able to swim*)
**Opposite:** *unable* (can't); *incompetent* (skilled)
**Contrast:** *Be able to* fills the gaps where *can* has no form: *will be able to*, *have been able to*, *might be able to* (never *will can*). For one success in the past, use *was able to* or *managed to*, not *could*: *I was able to get two tickets*. Russian *способный на* + noun or verb is *capable of* + noun or *-ing*: *capable of lying*, not *able of lying*. The noun is *ability*; *disabled* is a different word, about a lasting physical or mental condition.

Able has 2 distinct senses.

**1. able to: having the skill, strength, time or chance to do something.** (в состоянии, способный (сделать))

1. Will you be *able* to come on Saturday?
2. I've never been *able* to whistle.
3. With the new software, we're *better able* to track orders.

**2. Clever and good at what you do; skilled.** (способный, умелый)

4. She's a very *able* student.
5. He was one of the *ablest* lawyers in the state.

### willing

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwɪlɪŋ/ &middot; *WIL-ing*
**Русский:** готовый, согласный; охотный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — with *to* + verb only after a verb; before a noun it means 'glad to help': *a willing helper*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more willing*, *most willing*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*willing to help*)
**Form:** -ing from the old verb *will* 'want'
**Opposite:** *unwilling*, *reluctant*
**Contrast:** *Willing* means you will do it if needed, not that you want to: *I'm willing to drive* is *я не против* or *я готов*, while *хочу* is *want* and *очень хочу* is *eager* or *can't wait*. *Willing* is not a verb form and is not followed by *for* + verb: *willing to help*, not *willing for helping*. *God willing* is a fixed phrase, *если Бог даст*.

Willing has 2 distinct senses.

**1. willing to: ready to do something if it is needed or asked; not objecting.** (готовый, согласный (сделать))

1. Are you *willing* to work weekends?
2. How much are you *willing* to pay?
3. After the strike, management seemed more *willing* to talk.

**2. Doing something gladly and by choice.** (охотный, добровольный)

4. There was no shortage of *willing* volunteers.
5. She was a *willing* participant in the experiment.

### unwilling

**Pronunciation:** /ʌnˈwɪlɪŋ/ &middot; *uhn-WIL-ing*
**Русский:** не желающий; невольный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — with *to* + verb only after a verb; before a noun it means 'forced, against your will': *an unwilling witness*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more unwilling*, *most unwilling*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*unwilling to help*)
**Form:** un- + *willing*
**Opposite:** *willing*
**Contrast:** *Unwilling* means you refuse and will not do it; *reluctant* means you do not want to and hesitate, but may do it in the end. *He was unwilling to sign* suggests he did not sign; *He was reluctant to sign* leaves it open. In everyday speech Russian *не хочу* is *I don't want to*; *unwilling* is more formal.

Unwilling has 2 distinct senses.

**1. unwilling to: not ready to do something; refusing.** (не желающий, не готовый)

1. He was *unwilling* to talk about what happened.
2. Many banks are *unwilling* to lend to new businesses.
3. Voters seem more *unwilling* than ever to pay higher taxes.

**2. Made to do something or be part of something against your will.** (невольный, вынужденный)

4. She became an *unwilling* celebrity after the video went viral.
5. The prosecutor called him as an *unwilling* witness.

### ready

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrɛdi/ &middot; *RED-ee*
**Русский:** готовый; быстрый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — in the 'prepared', 'willing' and 'about to' senses usually after a verb; before a noun it can mean 'quick' or 'available': *a ready answer*, *ready cash*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *readier*, *readiest*, *more ready*
**Takes:** *for* + noun (prepared for: *ready for the test*; wanting: *ready for bed*); *to*-infinitive (prepared, willing or about to: *ready to go*, *ready to help*, *ready to cry*)
**Preposition patterns:** [ready for](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#ready-for)
**Contrast:** *Ready* is not *already* (уже): *Dinner is ready* (готов) vs *Dinner is already on the table*. Russian *Готово!* about finished work is *Done!* or *All set!*. Before a noun the meaning shifts: *a ready answer* is a quick answer, not a prepared one (that is *a canned answer*), and *ready cash* is money you have on hand. The everyday phrase for preparing yourself is *get ready*: *Go get ready for school*. *Ready-made* is a compound: *ready-made curtains*.

Ready has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Prepared for something; or finished and available to use or eat.** (готовый)

1. Dinner's *ready*!
2. Are you *ready* for the test tomorrow?
3. Some kids are *readier* for kindergarten than others.

**2. ready to: willing and glad to do something.** (готовый (охотно))

4. She's always *ready* to help a friend.
5. I'm not *ready* to forgive him yet.

**3. ready to: so upset, tired or excited that you are about to do something.** (готовый (вот-вот))

6. By the end of the meeting I was *ready* to scream.
7. He looked *ready* to cry.

**4. ready for: wanting or needing something soon.** (которому хочется, пора)

8. I'm *ready* for bed.
9. After six months without a break, we're more than *ready* for a vacation.

**5. Quick; or easy to get and use when needed.** (быстрый; имеющийся под рукой)

10. He always has a *ready* excuse.
11. Students have *ready* access to computers in the library.

### reluctant

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈlʌktənt/ &middot; *rih-LUHK-tuhnt*
**Русский:** неохотный; вынужденный
**Type:** quality; also emotion
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more reluctant*, *most reluctant*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*reluctant to leave*)
**Preposition patterns:** [reluctant to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#reluctant-to)
**Opposite:** *willing*, *eager*
**Contrast:** Russian *неохотно* is usually the adverb *reluctantly*: *She agreed reluctantly*, not *She agreed reluctant*. *Reluctant* means you hesitate and do not want to, but may still do it; *unwilling* means you will not. It takes a *to*-infinitive, not *about* or *for* + *-ing*: *reluctant to ask*, not *reluctant for asking*.

Reluctant has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not wanting to do something, and slow or hesitant to do it.** (неохотно делающий, не желающий)

1. He was *reluctant* to admit he'd made a mistake.
2. Young people are more *reluctant* to buy houses than their parents were.
3. I was *reluctant* to leave the kids with a new sitter.

**2. Taking part in something, or given, without real willingness.** (вынужденный, неохотный)

4. He became a *reluctant* hero after pulling a child from the river.
5. She gave a *reluctant* nod.

### bound

**Pronunciation:** /baʊnd/ &middot; *BOWND*
**Русский:** обязательный, непременный; обязанный; направляющийся
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also after a noun in the 'heading for' sense (*a flight bound for Denver*); before a noun only in compounds: *a Denver-bound flight*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (certain to; obliged to); *by* + noun (a law, promise or contract); *for* + place (traveling toward); *up with* or *up in* + noun (closely connected with)
**Preposition patterns:** [bound by](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#bound-by)
**Form:** past participle of *bind* 'tie'
**Contrast:** *Bound to* usually means 'certain to', not 'obliged to': *He's bound to be late* is *он наверняка опоздает*, not *он обязан опоздать*. The 'obliged' sense mostly comes with *legally*, *by law*, *by contract* or *duty-bound*. This *bound* has nothing to do with the verb and noun *bound* 'jump' or with *out of bounds*. The suffix *-bound* has two meanings: *Chicago-bound* is heading to Chicago, but *snowbound* and *housebound* mean unable to leave. *The prisoner was bound* is the passive of *bind* (связан), a verb form.

Bound has 4 distinct senses.

**1. bound to: certain or very likely to happen or to do something.** (обязательно, непременно, наверняка)

1. It's *bound* to rain the day we go to the beach.
2. You're *bound* to feel nervous on your first day.
3. With so many people involved, there are *bound* to be mistakes.

**2. Required to do something by a law, a promise or duty.** (обязанный, связанный (обязательством))

4. The company is legally *bound* to refund your money.
5. We're *bound* by the terms of the contract.
6. I felt *bound* to tell her the truth.

**3. bound for: traveling toward a place.** (направляющийся (в))

7. The plane was *bound* for Seattle.
8. Passengers *bound* for Newark should change trains here.

**4. bound up with: closely connected with something.** (тесно связанный (с))

9. Her sense of self is *bound* up with her job.
10. The town's history is *bound* up with the railroad.

### due

**Pronunciation:** /duː/ &middot; *DOO*
**Русский:** ожидаемый; причитающийся; вызванный; должный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — the 'proper' sense only before a noun (*due care*, *in due course*); the other senses after a verb or noun (*The rent is due*, *the money due to her*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (scheduled to: *due to arrive*); *to* + noun (caused by: *due to the weather*; owed to: *the credit due to her*); *for* + noun (going to need soon: *due for a checkup*)
**Contrast:** *Due to* has two patterns with different meanings: *due to* + verb is 'scheduled' (*The plane is due to land at six*, по расписанию), and *due to* + noun is 'caused by' (*The delay was due to fog*, из-за). It does not mean Russian *должен* in the sense of duty: *You are due to pay on Monday* means payment is scheduled; for obligation use *have to* or *should*. Careful writers keep *due to* after *be* and start a sentence with *Because of*, though *Due to the storm, the game was canceled* is everywhere in American English. *Past due* and *overdue* mean late. The plural noun *dues* is a membership fee (членские взносы), and in *due north* the word is an adverb.

Due has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Expected or planned to happen or arrive at a certain time.** (ожидаемый (по плану, по расписанию))

1. The baby is *due* in March.
2. The next train is *due* at 6:15.
3. The report is *due* to come out next week.

**2. Owed; that must be paid, returned or handed in by a certain time.** (подлежащий уплате, сдаче; причитающийся)

4. The rent is *due* on the first of the month.
5. Your library books are *due* back on Friday.
6. She finally got the credit she was *due*.

**3. due to: caused by.** (вызванный (чем-то), из-за)

7. The delay was *due* to bad weather.
8. Most of the damage was *due* to water, not fire.

**4. due for: going to need or get something soon.** (которому пора)

9. I'm *due* for a haircut.
10. After three losing seasons, the team is *due* for some luck.

**5. Proper and appropriate; as much as is needed.** (должный, надлежащий)

11. Drive with *due* care on wet roads.
12. With all *due* respect, I think you're wrong.
13. You'll hear from us in *due* course.

### apt

**Pronunciation:** /æpt/ &middot; *APT*
**Русский:** склонный; уместный, меткий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *apt to* + verb only after a verb; the 'fitting' sense before or after a noun: *an apt description*, *The name is apt*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more apt*, *most apt*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*apt to forget*)

**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more generous*, *most generous*
**Takes:** *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*generous of you to pay*); *with* + noun (what you give: *generous with his time*); *to* or *toward* + person (who gets it); *in* + noun (*generous in her praise*)
**Preposition patterns:** [generous with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#generous-with)
**Opposite:** *stingy*, *cheap*
**Contrast:** Adjectives that judge someone's action take *of*, not *for*: *It was generous of you to lend me the car*, not *generous for you*. Compare *It was important for you to be there*, which states a need, not a judgment. *Generous with* is what you give (*generous with money*), *generous to* is who gets it. The everyday American opposite is *cheap* or *stingy*; *mean* in American English usually means *unkind*, not *жадный*.

Generous has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Giving money, time or help freely and willingly.** (щедрый)

1. It was very *generous* of you to pay for dinner.
2. Her grandparents are *generous* with their time.
3. He's the most *generous* person I know.

**2. Larger or more than usual.** (щедрый, обильный, большой)

4. The restaurant serves *generous* portions.
5. He got a *generous* offer from a tech company.
6. The new seats have a more *generous* amount of legroom.

**3. Kind and fair in judging other people.** (великодушный, благородный)

7. She was *generous* in her praise of her rival.
8. That's a *generous* way of looking at what he did.

### stupid

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstuːpɪd/ &middot; *STOO-pid*
**Русский:** глупый, тупой; дурацкий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *stupider*, *stupidest*, *more stupid*
**Takes:** *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*stupid of me to forget*); *to*-infinitive (*stupid to believe him*)
**Opposite:** *smart*, *intelligent*
**Contrast:** It takes *of*, not *for*, when you judge an action: *It was stupid of me to leave the door open*, not *stupid for me*. Calling a person *stupid* is rude; *silly* and *foolish* are softer, and *dumb* is common in informal American speech. Russian *тупой* for a knife is *dull*, not *stupid*. Both *stupider* and *more stupid* are correct.

Stupid has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not intelligent; showing poor judgment.** (глупый, тупой)

1. That was a *stupid* mistake.
2. It was *stupid* of me to leave my phone in the car.
3. It's the *stupidest* idea I've ever heard.

**2. Informal: used to show that something annoys you.** (дурацкий)

4. I can't get this *stupid* printer to work.
5. The *stupid* car won't start again.

### careless

**Русский:** небрежный, неосторожный; непринуждённый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more careless*, *most careless*

**Form:** *care* + -less 'without'
**Opposite:** *careful*
**Contrast:** *Careless* is not *беззаботный*: that is *carefree* (*a carefree summer*), a happy word, while *careless* is criticism (небрежный, неосторожный). Judging an action takes *of*, not *for*: *It was careless of me to lose the keys*. The adverb is *carelessly*: *He drives carelessly*, not *He drives careless*.

1. The test was full of *careless* mistakes.
2. It was *careless* of me to leave the gate open.
3. I've never met anyone more *careless* with money.

**2. Relaxed and natural; done without effort or worry.** (непринуждённый, беспечный)

4. She moved with *careless* grace.
5. He gave a *careless* shrug.

### wise

**Pronunciation:** /waɪz/ &middot; *WYZ*
**Русский:** мудрый; разумный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *wiser*, *wisest*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive after *it* (*wise to wait*); *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*wise of you to call*); *to* + noun (informal, aware of a trick: *wise to his lies*); *with* + person (US informal, rude to)
**Opposite:** *foolish*, *unwise*
**Contrast:** Russian *умный* is usually *smart*; *wise* means good judgment from experience (мудрый). Judging an action takes *of*: *It was wise of you to wait*. *None the wiser* means not understanding any better (*I read the manual and was none the wiser*). *Get wise to* means realize someone is tricking you; *get wise with* means be rude. *A wise guy* (a noun) is someone who acts smart in an annoying way, or a mobster. The suffix *-wise* (*clockwise*, *moneywise*) makes adverbs.

Wise has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Having good judgment and knowledge from experience.** (мудрый)

1. My grandmother was a very *wise* woman.
2. I came back from that trip older and *wiser*.

**2. Of an action or choice: sensible and likely to have a good result.** (разумный, благоразумный)

3. It would be *wise* to call before you go.
4. Buying the house before prices rose was a *wise* move.
5. It was *wise* of you to bring an umbrella.

**3. Informal, wise to: aware of someone's tricks or dishonest plans.** (раскусивший (кого-то))

6. The teachers got *wise* to his excuses.
7. Shoppers are *wise* to that kind of scam now.

**4. US informal, wise with: rude and disrespectful.** (дерзкий)

8. Don't get *wise* with me, young man.
9. He got *wise* with the officer and ended up with a bigger ticket.

### foolish

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfuːlɪʃ/ &middot; *FOO-lish*
**Русский:** глупый, неразумный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more foolish*, *most foolish*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive after *it* (*foolish to ignore it*); *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*foolish of me to believe him*)
**Form:** *fool* + -ish 'like'
**Opposite:** *wise*, *sensible*
**Contrast:** Here *-ish* means 'like' (*like a fool*), not 'somewhat' as in *reddish* or *sixish*. Judging an action takes *of*, not *for*: *It was foolish of me to trust him*. *Foolish* is softer and a bit more formal than *stupid*; *silly* is lighter still and often friendly. *Feel foolish* is to feel embarrassed (чувствовать себя глупо).

1. It would be *foolish* to ignore the doctor's advice.
2. It was *foolish* of me to believe him.
3. Driving through a blizzard for a party was the most *foolish* thing he'd ever done.

**2. Feeling or looking silly and embarrassed.** (глупый (о виде); неловкий)

4. I felt *foolish* when I realized I'd gone to the wrong building.
5. He looked a little *foolish* in his brother's suit.

### right

**Pronunciation:** /raɪt/ &middot; *RYT*
**Русский:** правильный; правый; подходящий
**Type:** evaluative; also quality
**Position:** both — the side sense and the geometry sense only before a noun (*my right hand*, *a right angle*); the sense 'correct in what you think' only after a verb (*You're right*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — strengthen with *exactly*, *absolutely* or *just* (*You're absolutely right*)
**Takes:** *about* + noun (correct about); *to*-infinitive (*right to worry*); *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (morally right: *right of you to tell me*); *for* + person or job (suitable: *right for you*); *with* + noun (in order: *not right with the car*)
**Opposite:** *wrong*; *left* (side)
**Contrast:** Russian *ты прав* is *You're right*, with *be*, not *You have right*: *have the right to* uses the noun (иметь право). *Right of you to* judges an action (*It was right of you to call the police*); *right for you* means suitable (*This job isn't right for you*). *Right* is also an adverb (*turn right*, *right now*), a noun (*human rights*, *on the right*) and a verb (*right a wrong*). It sounds like *write*. In geometry Russian *прямой угол* is a *right angle*, ninety degrees — never a 'straight' one.

Right has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Correct or true.** (правильный, верный)

1. Is this the *right* answer?
2. "Your name is Sam?" "That's *right*."
3. Make sure you have the *right* address.

**2. Of a person: correct in what you think, say or do.** (правый (прав))

4. You were *right* about the weather.
5. She was *right* to worry about him.

**3. Morally good or fair.** (правильный, справедливый)

6. It's not *right* to take credit for someone else's work.
7. I hope I did the *right* thing.
8. It was *right* of you to tell me the truth.

**4. Best or most suitable for a situation.** (подходящий, нужный)

9. He's the *right* person for the job.
10. Wait for the *right* moment to ask.

**5. On the side of the body that is toward the east when you face north.** (правый)

11. She writes with her *right* hand.
12. Stay in the *right* lane until the exit.

**6. Healthy, or working as it should.** (в порядке)

13. Something isn't *right* with the car; it's making a strange noise.
14. I haven't felt *right* since the flu.

**7. In geometry: of an angle, exactly ninety degrees.** (прямой (об угле))

15. Check that the corners form a *right* angle before you nail them.
16. The two hallways meet at a *right* angle.

### wrong

**Pronunciation:** /rɔːŋ/ &middot; *RAWNG*
**Русский:** неправильный; не тот; нехороший
**Type:** evaluative; also quality
**Position:** both — the sense 'not working properly' only after a verb (*What's wrong?*, *Something is wrong with it*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — strengthen with *completely*, *totally* or *dead* (*dead wrong*)
**Takes:** *about* + noun (mistaken about); *with* + noun (having a problem: *wrong with the car*); *to*-infinitive (*wrong to lie*); *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*wrong of him to yell*); *for* + person or job (not suitable)
**Preposition patterns:** [wrong with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#wrong-with)
**Opposite:** *right*, *correct*
**Contrast:** *Wrong* is also an adverb after a verb: *go wrong*, *get it wrong*, *spell it wrong*; the adverb *wrongly* goes before a past participle (*wrongly accused*). Russian *не тот* is *the wrong*: *We took the wrong bus*, not *We took not that bus*, and *You have the wrong number* is *Вы ошиблись номером*. *What's wrong?* is *Что случилось?*, but *What's wrong with you?* can sound rude. Judging an action takes *of*: *It was wrong of him to yell*.

Wrong has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Not correct or true.** (неправильный, неверный)

1. Two of my answers were *wrong*.
2. That's the *wrong* date; the party is on Saturday.

**2. Of a person: mistaken in what you think or say.** (неправый (не прав), ошибающийся)

3. You're *wrong* about her; she's really nice.
4. I was *wrong* to doubt you.

**3. Morally bad or unfair.** (нехороший, дурной, несправедливый)

5. Stealing is *wrong*.
6. It was *wrong* of him to yell at the kids.

**4. Not the one you need or want; not suitable.** (не тот, неподходящий)

7. We got on the *wrong* bus.
8. Sorry, you have the *wrong* number.
9. It's the *wrong* time to ask for a raise.

**5. Not working properly; causing a problem.** (неладный, не в порядке)

10. What's *wrong*? You look upset.
11. Something is *wrong* with my laptop.
