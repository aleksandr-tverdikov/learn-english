# More adjectives: P

> Adjectives P beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### Pacific

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈsɪfɪk/ &middot; *puh-SIF-ik*
**Русский:** тихоокеанский
**Type:** origin; also classifying
**Position:** attributive only — *the Pacific coast*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** With a capital it means the ocean. Lowercase *pacific* is a formal word for peaceful (*a pacific gesture*), and it is rare in speech.

Of the Pacific Ocean or the lands around it.

1. We drove up the *Pacific* coast from San Diego.
2. *Pacific* storms hit Oregon first.
3. She studies *Pacific* island languages.

### pacifist

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpæsəfɪst/ &middot; *PAS-uh-fist*
**Русский:** пацифистский
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ist from *pacifism*
**Contrast:** Much more often a noun: *She's a pacifist*. As an adjective it describes beliefs, families and movements.

Opposed to war and to violence on principle.

1. He came from a *pacifist* family.
2. *Pacifist* groups organized the march.
3. Her politics are firmly *pacifist*.

### packaged

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpækɪdʒd/ &middot; *PAK-ijd*
**Русский:** расфасованный, упакованный; комплексный (о туре)
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *package*

Packaged has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Sold in a box, bag or wrapper rather than loose.** (расфасованный, упакованный)

1. *Packaged* foods list the salt on the side of the box.
2. We buy *packaged* rice because it keeps longer.

**2. Sold as one deal with everything included.** (комплексный, пакетный)

3. They booked a *packaged* tour of Italy.
4. It's a *packaged* deal: hotel, car and flights.

### padded

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpædɪd/ &middot; *PAD-id*
**Русский:** мягкий, с подкладкой; раздутый (о счёте, резюме)
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *pad*

Padded has 2 distinct senses.

**1. With soft material inside for comfort or protection.** (мягкий, с подкладкой)

1. She sat on a *padded* bench by the window.
2. The jacket is *padded* at the shoulders.

**2. Made bigger by adding things that do not belong there.** (раздутый, дутый)

3. The bill was *padded* with fees nobody explained.
4. He turned in a *padded* resume and got caught.

### pagan

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪɡən/ &middot; *PAY-guhn*
**Русский:** языческий
**Type:** classifying; also origin
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** Also a noun (*the pagans of northern Europe*). Capitalized *Pagan* usually means the living modern religion; lowercase points to the pre-Christian world.

Belonging to an old religion that came before Christianity, or to modern nature worship.

1. Many Christmas customs have *pagan* roots.
2. The museum has a room of *pagan* statues.

### paid

**Pronunciation:** /peɪd/ &middot; *PAYD*
**Русский:** оплачиваемый, платный; оплаченный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — but compounds compare: *better paid*, *the best-paid job in town*
**Form:** -ed from *pay*
**Opposite:** *unpaid*

Paid has 2 distinct senses.

**1. That you receive money for.** (оплачиваемый, платный)

1. She took a *paid* internship in Denver.
2. We get ten days of *paid* leave a year.

**2. Already settled, of a bill or an account.** (оплаченный)

3. The invoice is *paid* in full.
4. Mark the account *paid* and file it.

### pained

**Pronunciation:** /peɪnd/ &middot; *PAYND*
**Русский:** страдальческий, обиженный (о выражении лица)
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *a pained expression*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more pained*, *most pained*
**Form:** -ed from *pain*
**Contrast:** *Pained* shows hurt feelings on a face; *painful* causes pain. A *pained* smile hurts nobody; a *painful* shot does.

Showing that you are hurt, upset or embarrassed.

1. He gave me a *pained* smile and changed the subject.
2. There was a *pained* silence after the question.
3. She looked *pained* when I mentioned his name.

### painful

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪnfəl/ &middot; *PAYN-fuhl*
**Русский:** болезненный; мучительный, тягостный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more painful*, *most painful*
**Form:** -ful from *pain*
**Opposite:** *painless*
**Contrast:** A thing that hurts you is *painful*; a face that shows hurt is *pained*.

Painful has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Hurting the body.** (болезненный)

1. The shot was quick but *painful*.
2. He has a *painful* knee and won't see anyone about it.

**2. Hard to go through, to remember or to watch.** (мучительный, тягостный)

3. It was a *painful* conversation for both of them.
4. Losing that game was *more painful* than the whole season.

### palatable

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpælətəbəl/ &middot; *PAL-uh-tuh-buhl*

**Type:** quality
**Position:** both

**2. Acceptable to people, of an idea or a decision.** (приемлемый)

3. They rewrote the bill to make it *palatable* to both parties.

### palpable

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpælpəbəl/ &middot; *PAL-puh-buhl*

3. The lump was *palpable* on the left side.

### parallel

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpærəlɛl/ &middot; *PAIR-uh-lel*
**Русский:** параллельный; аналогичный, сходный
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *to* or *with* + noun
**Preposition patterns:** [parallel to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#parallel-to)
**Form:** Greek *para-* (beside) + *allelos* (one another)
**Contrast:** Watch the spelling: one *r*, then two *l*s, then one. It is also a noun (*draw a parallel*) and a verb (*Her story parallels his*).

Parallel has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Running side by side, always the same distance apart.** (параллельный)

1. The street runs *parallel* to the river.
2. Draw two *parallel* lines an inch apart.

**2. Similar and happening at the same time.** (аналогичный, параллельный)

3. The two investigations were *parallel* but separate.
4. He led a *parallel* life in another city.
