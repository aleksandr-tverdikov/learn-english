Compound adjectives#

well-known, part-time, old-fashioned, a five-year plan — two words working as one.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Compound adjectives join two or more words into a single modifier, and the hyphen is part of the grammar: a well-known author, but the author is well known. Number compounds take a singular noun — a five-year plan, never a five-years plan — which is the error a Russian speaker is most likely to make with this group.

well-known#

Pronunciation
/ˌwɛlˈnoʊn/ · wel-NOHN
Русский
известный; общеизвестный
Type
compound — adverb + past participle
Position
both — always hyphenated before a noun (a well-known writer); after a verb often written open (she is well known)
Comparison
irregular — better-known, best-known; after a verb better known, best known
Takes
for + noun or -ing (the reason people know it); as + noun (the role it is known in); to + person (who knows it); that-clause in it is well known that
Form
compound — well + past participle known
Opposite
unknown; little-known
Contrast
The comparative is better-known, best-known, not weller-known; more well-known is common in speech but is avoided in careful writing. The preposition changes the meaning: well known for her novels (the reason), well known as a novelist (the role), well known to the police (who knows her). A well-known fact is общеизвестный, not just известный, so it needs no famous: say a well-known fact, never a famous fact.

Well-known has 2 distinct senses.

1. Known about by many people; famous. (известный)

  1. The restaurant is owned by a well-known chef from Chicago.
  2. She is well known for her work on climate policy.
  3. He is better known as a songwriter than as a singer.

2. Of a fact or idea: known to most people, so that nobody needs to explain it. (общеизвестный, всем известный)

  1. It's a well-known fact that smoking causes cancer.
  2. It is well known that the first year of a startup is the hardest.

well-behaved#

Pronunciation
/ˌwɛlbɪˈheɪvd/ · wel-bi-HAYVD
Русский
послушный; хорошо себя ведущий
Type
compound — adverb + past participle
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a well-behaved dog); after a verb often written open (the kids were well behaved)
Comparison
irregular — better-behaved, best-behaved; after a verb better behaved
Form
compound — well + past participle of behave
Opposite
badly behaved
Contrast
Russian воспитанный about an adult is usually polite or well-mannered; well-behaved is mostly said of children, pets and students, and describes how they act, not their upbringing. The comparative is better-behaved, not more well-behaved in careful writing.

Well-behaved has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a child, student or animal: behaving politely and doing what they are told. (послушный, хорошо себя ведущий)

  1. Our neighbors have three very well-behaved kids.
  2. The dog was surprisingly well behaved at the vet.
  3. This year's class is the best-behaved group I've ever taught.

2. Of a machine, program or process: working in a predictable way, without surprises. (предсказуемый, работающий без сбоев)

  1. A well-behaved app doesn't drain your battery in the background.
  2. The function is well behaved for all positive values of x.

well-off#

Pronunciation
/ˌwɛlˈɔːf/ · wel-AWF
Русский
обеспеченный, состоятельный
Type
compound — adverb + adverb particle
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a well-off family); more often after a verb and written open (they're well off); the 'in a good situation' sense only after a verb
Comparison
irregular — better-off, best-off; after a verb better off, best off
Takes
in the 'in a good situation' sense, better off often takes -ing or without + noun: You'd be better off taking the bus; She's better off without him
Form
compound — well + off
Opposite
badly off; poor
Contrast
Better off is the everyday comparative and usually means 'in a better situation', not 'richer': You'd be better off asking your boss = тебе лучше спросить начальника. Well-off is politer than rich and a little less than wealthy. The British well off for (well supplied with: well off for cash) is rare in American English.

Well-off has 2 distinct senses.

1. Having plenty of money; comfortably rich. (обеспеченный, состоятельный)

  1. She grew up in a well-off suburb outside Boston.
  2. His parents are pretty well off, so he graduated without loans.
  3. The tax cut mostly helps the best-off households.

2. In a good or lucky situation. (в хорошем положении; которому повезло)

  1. You don't know how well off you are with a boss like that.
  2. We'd be better off leaving before the traffic starts.
  3. Honestly, she's better off without him.

well-dressed#

Pronunciation
/ˌwɛlˈdrɛst/ · wel-DREST
Русский
хорошо одетый, элегантный
Type
compound — adverb + past participle
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a well-dressed man); after a verb often written open (he was well dressed)
Comparison
irregular — better-dressed, best-dressed; after a verb better dressed
Form
compound — well + past participle dressed
Opposite
badly dressed; shabby
Contrast
Dressed alone only means 'wearing clothes' (Are you dressed yet? = ты оделся?); well-dressed means wearing good, stylish clothes. Say the best-dressed guest, not the most well-dressed guest in careful writing.

Wearing attractive, stylish or expensive clothes.

  1. A well-dressed woman in a gray suit walked into the lobby.
  2. Everyone at the wedding was well dressed except the groom's brother.
  3. The magazine publishes a list of the year's best-dressed celebrities.

well-paid#

Pronunciation
/ˌwɛlˈpeɪd/ · wel-PAYD
Русский
высокооплачиваемый, хорошо оплачиваемый
Type
compound — adverb + past participle
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a well-paid job); after a verb often written open (nurses aren't well paid)
Comparison
irregular — better-paid, best-paid; after a verb better paid
Form
compound — well + past participle paid
Opposite
poorly paid; low-paid
Contrast
Well-paid describes both the job and the person doing it. Well-paying describes only the job or the employer: a well-paying job, but not a well-paying lawyer. Highly paid means the same as well-paid and is common for people: highly paid executives.

Of a job or a person: earning or paying a lot of money.

  1. He left teaching for a well-paid job in software.
  2. Truck drivers are fairly well paid in North Dakota.
  3. Surgeons are among the best-paid workers in the country.

high-speed#

Pronunciation
/ˌhaɪˈspiːd/ · hy-SPEED
Русский
скоростной, высокоскоростной
Type
compound — adjective + noun; also classifying
Position
attributive only — after a verb use fast: a high-speed train, but the train is fast
Comparison
not gradable — technical writing has higher-speed, but in everyday English compare with faster
Form
compound — high + speed
Contrast
It goes before a noun: a high-speed chase, not the chase was high-speed, which sounds odd — say the chase was fast. Speed stays singular inside the compound. Russian скоростной интернет is high-speed internet or broadband, never speed internet.

High-speed has 2 distinct senses.

1. Moving, working or sending data very fast. (скоростной, высокоскоростной)

  1. California has been trying to build high-speed rail for years.
  2. The suspect led police on a high-speed chase down the interstate.
  3. Every room in the hotel has free high-speed internet.

2. Of a camera or photography: recording in a very short time, so that fast motion can be seen. (скоростной (о съёмке, камере))

  1. A high-speed camera caught the moment the balloon burst.
  2. The scientists used high-speed video to study how the bird lands.

part-time#

Pronunciation
/ˌpɑːrtˈtaɪm/ · pahrt-TYM
Русский
с неполной занятостью; на неполный рабочий день
Type
compound — noun + noun; also classifying
Position
both — a part-time job; the job is part-time
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — part + time
Opposite
full-time
Contrast
In She works part-time the word is an adverb, not an adjective. Russian полставки is part-time or half-time in a job, but halftime on its own is the break in a sports game. The job is part-time, never part-timed; a person who works part-time is a part-timer (noun).

Part-time has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a job, course or schedule: for only part of the usual working or study hours. (с неполной занятостью, неполный (рабочий день))

  1. I had a part-time job at a pizza place all through college.
  2. The position is part-time, about twenty hours a week.

2. Of a person: working or studying fewer than the usual hours, or doing something in addition to a main job. (работающий неполный день; по совместительству)

  1. The store hired three part-time workers for the holidays.
  2. He's an accountant by day and a part-time musician on weekends.
  3. She was a part-time student while her kids were small.

full-time#

Pronunciation
/ˌfʊlˈtaɪm/ · fuul-TYM
Русский
с полной занятостью; штатный
Type
compound — adjective + noun; also classifying
Position
both — a full-time job; the job is full-time
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — full + time
Opposite
part-time
Contrast
In She works full-time the word is an adverb, not an adjective. Full time as a noun is British sports talk for the end of a match; Americans say the end of regulation or the final whistle. Russian штатный сотрудник is usually a full-time employee or staff member.

Full-time has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of a job, course or schedule: for the whole of the normal working or study week. (с полной занятостью, на полный рабочий день)

  1. After the internship, they offered her a full-time position.
  2. The program is full-time, so most students don't work.

2. Of a person: working or studying the full number of hours, or doing something as their main activity. (штатный, постоянный; занятый полный день)

  1. The company has forty full-time employees.
  2. She became a full-time mom after the twins were born.

3. Taking up all your time and energy, in a full-time job. (отнимающий всё время)

  1. Taking care of my dad has become a full-time job.
  2. Keeping that old house in shape is practically full-time work.

long-term#

Pronunciation
/ˌlɔːŋˈtɜːrm/ · lawng-TURM
Русский
долгосрочный; длительный
Type
compound — adjective + noun; also classifying
Position
both — mostly before a noun: long-term goals; after a verb: the damage could be long-term
Comparison
not gradable — but longer-term is common: a longer-term plan
Form
compound — long + term
Opposite
short-term
Contrast
In the long term is a noun phrase; Americans more often say in the long run. Long-term is about time into the future or over a long period; long-lasting is about how long a thing keeps working (a long-lasting battery, not a long-term battery). Term stays singular: never long-terms plans.

Long-term has 3 distinct senses.

1. Planned for, or having effects over, a long time into the future. (долгосрочный)

  1. Nobody really knows the long-term effects of the drug.
  2. We need a long-term solution, not another quick fix.
  3. The board wants a longer-term strategy for the company.

2. Lasting or having existed for a long time. (длительный, давний; хронический)

  1. They've been in a long-term relationship for eight years.
  2. Her grandmother lives in a long-term care facility.
  3. The problem isn't temporary; it's long-term.

3. In finance: lasting more than a year before it must be paid or sold. (долгосрочный (о кредите, инвестиции))

  1. Long-term capital gains are taxed at a lower rate.
  2. The city took out a long-term loan to fix the bridge.

short-term#

Pronunciation
/ˌʃɔːrtˈtɜːrm/ · short-TURM
Русский
краткосрочный; кратковременный
Type
compound — adjective + noun; also classifying
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a short-term contract; after a verb: the effect is only short-term
Comparison
not gradable — but shorter-term is used: shorter-term goals
Form
compound — short + term
Opposite
long-term
Contrast
Short-term describes something planned or meant to last a short time; short-lived describes something that ended sooner than expected (a short-lived marriage). Russian кратковременная память is short-term memory, never short memory.

Short-term has 3 distinct senses.

1. Planned for, or having effects over, only the near future. (краткосрочный)

  1. Cutting prices is only a short-term fix.
  2. The layoffs brought a short-term boost in profits but hurt morale.
  3. Most investors care more about shorter-term returns.

2. Lasting only a short time. (кратковременный, временный)

  1. The city is cracking down on short-term rentals.
  2. Alcohol affects your short-term memory.
  3. The side effects are usually mild and short-term.

3. In finance: lasting a year or less before it must be paid or sold. (краткосрочный (о кредите, инвестиции))

  1. The company borrowed money with a short-term loan.
  2. Short-term interest rates went up again this month.

both — a world-famous pianist; the bakery is world-famous

Form
compound — world + famous

Known by people in many countries.

sugar-free#

Pronunciation
/ˌʃʊɡərˈfriː/ · shuug-ur-FREE
Русский
без сахара, не содержащий сахара
Type
compound — noun + -free; also classifying
Position
both — sugar-free gum; the soda is sugar-free
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — noun + -free ('without')
Contrast
In compounds -free means 'without', not 'at no cost': sugar-free = без сахара, tax-free = без налога. Sugar-free food is usually sweetened with something else; unsweetened means nothing sweet was added, and no sugar added means the food may still contain natural sugar.

Containing no sugar, often sweetened with something artificial instead.

  1. Could I get a sugar-free vanilla latte?
  2. The label says the candy is sugar-free, but it still has calories.
  3. Chewing sugar-free gum after meals is good for your teeth.

state-of-the-art#

Pronunciation
/ˌsteɪt əv ði ˈɑːrt/ · stayt uhv thee AHRT
Русский
новейший, передовой, самый современный
Type
compound — phrase used as an adjective
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a state-of-the-art lab); after a verb often written open (the equipment is state of the art)
Comparison
not gradable — the most state-of-the-art is heard in advertising but avoided in careful writing
Form
compound — noun phrase the state of the art turned into a modifier
Contrast
The noun phrase the state of the art means the current level of development in a field: the state of the art in cancer research. The adjective means 'the newest and best'. Russian последнее слово техники is usually state-of-the-art or cutting-edge.

Using the newest and most advanced ideas, methods or technology.

  1. The hospital just opened a state-of-the-art cancer center.
  2. The gym's equipment is state of the art.
  3. The team trains in a state-of-the-art facility outside Dallas.

up-to-date#

Pronunciation
/ˌʌp tə ˈdeɪt/ · up tuh DAYT
Русский
актуальный, свежий; современный; в курсе
Type
compound — phrase used as an adjective
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (up-to-date information); after a verb usually written open (the list is up to date)
Comparison
more/most — more up-to-date, most up-to-date
Takes
on or with + noun: up to date on the news (informed about), up to date with your payments or on your shots (not behind)
Form
compound — prepositional phrase up to date used as a modifier
Opposite
out-of-date
Contrast
Russian актуальный is up-to-date or current, never actual: actual means 'real' (the actual price = реальная цена). Common verb patterns: keep someone up to date, bring something up to date, stay up to date on.

Up-to-date has 4 distinct senses.

1. Containing the latest information. (актуальный, свежий)

  1. Make sure your résumé is up to date before you apply.
  2. The app gives you up-to-date traffic reports.
  3. This is the most up-to-date map we have.

2. Of a person: knowing the latest news or developments. (в курсе, осведомлённый)

  1. I try to stay up to date on local politics.
  2. Keep me up to date on how the surgery goes.

3. Done or paid when due, with nothing left overdue. (без задолженности; сделанный вовремя)

  1. Is your dog up to date on its shots?
  2. We're up to date with the rent now.

4. Modern; using the newest methods, styles or equipment. (современный)

  1. The kitchen needs more up-to-date appliances.
  2. The school uses fairly up-to-date teaching methods.

out-of-date#

Pronunciation
/ˌaʊt əv ˈdeɪt/ · owt uhv DAYT
Русский
устаревший; просроченный
Type
compound — phrase used as an adjective
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (out-of-date software); after a verb usually written open (the software is out of date)
Comparison
more/most — more out-of-date, most out-of-date
Form
compound — prepositional phrase out of date used as a modifier
Opposite
up-to-date
Contrast
For passports, credit cards, licenses and food, Americans usually say expired: expired milk, an expired passport; out-of-date milk sounds British. Outdated is a close synonym of the first sense. Russian просроченный is therefore most often expired.

Out-of-date has 2 distinct senses.

1. No longer modern, useful or correct because things have changed. (устаревший, неактуальный)

  1. Our office still runs on out-of-date computers.
  2. The phone number on the website is out of date.
  3. This guidebook is even more out of date than the last one.

2. Of a document or ticket: no longer valid because the date on it has passed. (просроченный, недействительный)

  1. You can't fly with an out-of-date ID.
  2. His license was out of date, so he got a ticket.

middle-aged#

Pronunciation
/ˌmɪdəlˈeɪdʒd/ · mid-uhl-AYJD
Русский
средних лет; для людей средних лет
Type
compound — adjective + -ed from age; also age
Position
both — a middle-aged man; my parents are middle-aged
Comparison
not gradable — informal more middle-aged is possible in the 'unexciting' sense
Form
compound — middle + aged (adjective from the noun age)
Contrast
Middle-aged has nothing to do with the Middle Ages: Russian средневековый is medieval. Here aged is one syllable /eɪdʒd/; the separate adjective aged meaning 'very old' (aged parents) is two syllables /ˈeɪdʒɪd/. Middle-aged usually means about 45 to 65.

Middle-aged has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of a person: no longer young but not yet old, roughly between 45 and 65. (средних лет)

  1. A middle-aged couple bought the house next door.
  2. Most of the people at the concert were middle-aged.
  3. The drug is tested mainly on middle-aged adults.

2. Typical of middle-aged people, especially in being dull or conservative. (как у немолодых людей; скучный, консервативный)

  1. Minivans have a middle-aged image he wanted to avoid.
  2. Staying home on Friday night feels so middle-aged.

left-handed#

Pronunciation
/ˌlɛftˈhændɪd/ · left-HAN-did
Русский
леворукий; для левшей
Type
compound — adjective + noun + -ed
Position
both — a left-handed pitcher; she is left-handed
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — left + hand + -ed
Opposite
right-handed
Contrast
Russian левша is a noun, but English usually uses the adjective: She is left-handed, not She is a left-handed; the nouns are a left-hander or informal a lefty. Left-hand without -ed means 'on the left': the left-hand lane. A left-handed compliment (US) is a remark that sounds nice but is really an insult.

Left-handed has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of a person: using the left hand more easily than the right. (леворукий, левша)

  1. About one in ten people is left-handed.
  2. The Dodgers need another left-handed pitcher.

2. Made for, or done with, the left hand. (для левой руки, для левшей; левой рукой)

  1. Do you have any left-handed scissors?
  2. He learned to play on a left-handed guitar.

3. Of a compliment: seeming to praise but really meant as criticism. (сомнительный, двусмысленный (о комплименте))

  1. "You look great for your age" is a left-handed compliment.
  2. Calling her essay "surprisingly good" was a pretty left-handed compliment.

4. Of a screw thread: tightened by turning counterclockwise. (с левой резьбой)

  1. The left pedal has a left-handed thread, so you loosen it clockwise.
  2. Some gas fittings use left-handed threads so they can't be mixed up.

right-handed#

Pronunciation
/ˌraɪtˈhændɪd/ · ryt-HAN-did
Русский
праворукий; для правой руки
Type
compound — adjective + noun + -ed
Position
both — a right-handed batter; most people are right-handed
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — right + hand + -ed

left-handed
Contrast
Right-hand without -ed means 'on the right' (the right-hand side), and your right-hand man is your most trusted helper, not someone who uses the right hand. Russian правша is a noun, but English says He is right-handed; the noun a right-hander is used mostly in sports.

Right-handed has 3 distinct senses.

2. Made for, or done with, the right hand. (для правой руки; правой рукой)

  1. Most golf clubs in the store are right-handed.
  2. Desks with a right-handed writing surface are hard for lefties.

3. Of a screw thread: tightened by turning clockwise, as most are. (с правой резьбой)

  1. Almost every bolt you buy has a right-handed thread.
  2. The right pedal is right-handed, so it loosens counterclockwise.

Attractive in appearance, of a person or sometimes a thing.

покладистый, спокойный; непринуждённый

open-minded#

Pronunciation
/ˌoʊpənˈmaɪndɪd/ · oh-puhn-MYN-did
Русский
непредубеждённый, широких взглядов, открытый новому
Type
compound — adjective + noun + -ed; also quality
Position
both — an open-minded teacher; try to be open-minded
Comparison
more/most — more open-minded, most open-minded
Takes
about + noun or -ing (the subject); toward + noun (people or ideas)
Form
compound — open + mind + -ed
Opposite
narrow-minded; closed-minded
Contrast
Russian открытый человек usually means sociable or frank: outgoing, open or friendly. Open-minded means willing to accept new ideas and people without prejudice.

Willing to listen to and consider ideas and ways of life different from your own.

  1. My grandparents are surprisingly open-minded about politics.
  2. An open-minded hiring manager will look past the gap in your résumé.
  3. Travel has made her more open-minded toward other cultures.

absent-minded#

Pronunciation
/ˌæbsəntˈmaɪndɪd/ · ab-suhnt-MYN-did
Русский
рассеянный
Type
compound — adjective + noun + -ed; also quality
Position
both — an absent-minded professor; he's getting absent-minded
Comparison
more/most — more absent-minded, most absent-minded
Form
compound — absent + mind + -ed
Contrast
Absent alone means 'not present' (отсутствующий): absent from school. Russian рассеянный about a person is absent-minded; about light it is diffuse or scattered.

Tending to forget things or not notice what you are doing because you are thinking about something else.

  1. My absent-minded uncle once drove home without his wife.
  2. I'm so absent-minded lately that I left my keys in the fridge.
  3. He's gotten more absent-minded since he started the new job.

broken-hearted#

Pronunciation
/ˌbroʊkənˈhɑːrtɪd/ · broh-kuhn-HAHR-tid
Русский
убитый горем, с разбитым сердцем
Type
compound — past participle + noun + -ed; also emotion
Position
both — a broken-hearted widow; she was broken-hearted
Comparison
more/most — more broken-hearted, most broken-hearted; rare — usually strengthened with completely or absolutely
Takes
about or over + noun (the cause)
Form
compound — broken + heart + -ed
Contrast
Heartbroken means the same and is more common in everyday American English; broken-hearted sounds a little more literary. The noun phrase is a broken heart: He has a broken heart = He is broken-hearted.

Extremely sad, especially because someone you love has left you or died.

  1. She was broken-hearted when her dog died.
  2. The song is about a broken-hearted cowboy.
  3. Fans were broken-hearted over the team's move to another city.

kind-hearted#

добросердечный, добрый
Type

Comparison
more/most — more kind-hearted, most kind-hearted
Form
compound — kind + heart + -ed

Contrast
Kind can describe one action (That was kind of you); kind-hearted describes a person's character. Do not confuse either with kind of, which means 'somewhat' (kind of tired).

Having a kind and caring nature.

old-fashioned#

Pronunciation
/ˌoʊldˈfæʃənd/ · ohld-FASH-uhnd
Русский
старомодный; традиционный
Type
compound — adjective + past participle; also evaluative
Position
both — an old-fashioned phone; his ideas are old-fashioned
Comparison
more/most — more old-fashioned, most old-fashioned
Form
compound — old + fashioned
Opposite
modern; fashionable
Contrast
It can be praise: good old-fashioned hard work = старый добрый упорный труд. An Old Fashioned (no hyphen, capitalized) is a noun — a whiskey cocktail. Old alone is about age; old-fashioned is about style or ideas.

Old-fashioned has 3 distinct senses.

1. No longer modern or in fashion. (старомодный, устаревший)

  1. Her parents still have an old-fashioned TV with an antenna.
  2. That hairstyle looks a little old-fashioned now.

2. Of a person or their views: following traditional ideas about how people should live and behave. (старомодный, консервативный)

  1. Call me old-fashioned, but I think you should write a thank-you note.
  2. My grandfather is more old-fashioned about dating than my dad.

3. Traditional in a good way, like things used to be. (старый добрый, традиционный)

  1. The diner still makes old-fashioned milkshakes.
  2. What this team needs is some good old-fashioned defense.

brand-new#

Pronunciation
/ˌbrændˈnuː/ · brand-NOO
Русский
совершенно новый, новенький
Type
compound — noun + adjective; also age
Position
both — hyphenated before a noun (a brand-new car); after a verb often written open (the car is brand new)
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — brand + new
Contrast
Word order matters: a brand-new car = новенькая машина; a new brand of car = новая марка машины. Brand here is the old word for a burning piece of wood (as new as metal fresh from the fire), not the modern 'company brand'.

Brand-new has 2 distinct senses.

1. Completely new: never used before, or only just made or launched. (новенький, с иголочки; совершенно новый)

  1. He showed up in a brand-new pickup truck.
  2. The shoes are brand new — I've never worn them.
  3. The network is launching a brand-new comedy series this fall.

2. Of a person: having only just started or arrived. (только что пришедший, новенький)

  1. Go easy on her; she's brand new here.
  2. The brand-new coach hasn't picked a starting quarterback yet.

so-called#

Pronunciation
/ˌsoʊˈkɔːld/ · soh-KAWLD
Русский
так называемый
Type
compound — adverb + past participle; also evaluative
Position
attributive only — my so-called friends; after a verb say what is called or known as
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — so + past participle called
Contrast
Russian так называемый is often neutral, but English so-called usually shows doubt or sarcasm: the so-called experts means you don't think they are experts. For a neutral term, known as or what is called is safer. Do not add quotation marks as well: the so-called experts, not the so-called "experts".

So-called has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used to show that you think a name or description is wrong or not deserved. (так называемый (с иронией, недоверием))

  1. My so-called best friend didn't even come to my party.
  2. These so-called diet pills are a waste of money.
  3. The so-called experts got the election completely wrong.

2. Used neutrally to introduce the name that is commonly used for something. (так называемый)

  1. The disease hits hardest in the so-called Stroke Belt of the South.
  2. The bill targets so-called forever chemicals in drinking water.

one-way#

Pronunciation
/ˌwʌnˈweɪ/ · wun-WAY
Русский
односторонний; в один конец
Type
compound — number + noun; also classifying
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a one-way street; after a verb: this street is one-way
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — one + way
Opposite
two-way (street, communication); round-trip (ticket)
Contrast
Americans buy a one-way ticket and a round-trip ticket; the British single and return sound foreign in the US. Like every number compound, the noun inside stays singular: a one-way street, a five-year plan, a ten-dollar bill, never a five-years plan. A one-way street is also a noun phrase for a relationship where only one side gives.

One-way has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of a street or traffic: moving in only one direction. (с односторонним движением)

  1. You can't turn left there; it's a one-way street.
  2. Most of the streets downtown are one-way.

2. Of a ticket, fare or trip: for traveling to a place but not back. (в один конец)

  1. A one-way ticket to Denver costs almost as much as a round trip.
  2. She booked a one-way flight and never came back.

3. Going or working in one direction only; not shared by both sides. (односторонний)

  1. The meeting was a one-way conversation — the boss talked and we listened.
  2. Police watched the interview through a one-way mirror.

first-class#

Pronunciation
/ˌfɜːrstˈklæs/ · furst-KLAS
Русский
первоклассный; первого класса
Type
compound — ordinal + noun; also evaluative
Position
both — the travel and mail senses mostly before a noun (a first-class seat); 'excellent' in both positions (the service was first-class)
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — first + class
Contrast
In We flew first class the words are an adverb phrase, usually without a hyphen. Russian первоклассник is a first grader, not a first-class student, which would mean an excellent student. First-rate is a synonym of the 'excellent' sense.

First-class has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of travel: in the most expensive and comfortable section of a plane or train. (первого класса)

  1. He got a free upgrade to a first-class seat.
  2. First-class passengers board before everyone else.

2. Excellent; of the highest quality. (первоклассный, превосходный)

  1. The hospital has a first-class heart surgery team.
  2. The food was good, but the service was first-class.

3. Of US mail: in the standard class used for letters and small packages. (первого класса (о почтовом отправлении))

  1. A first-class stamp covers a letter up to one ounce.
  2. Send the check by first-class mail.

second-hand#

Pronunciation
/ˌsɛkəndˈhænd/ · sek-uhnd-HAND
Русский
подержанный, б/у; из вторых рук
Type
compound — ordinal + noun; also classifying
Position
both — a second-hand car; all her furniture is second-hand
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — second + hand
Opposite
new; firsthand (information)
Contrast
American dictionaries and most US writing spell it as one word, secondhand. In I bought it second-hand it is an adverb. Russian секонд-хенд (the store) is a thrift store or a secondhand store in American English — a second-hand alone is not a noun. The second hand (two words) is the hand on a clock that counts seconds.

Second-hand has 3 distinct senses.

1. Owned or used by someone else before; not new. (подержанный, б/у)

  1. We furnished our first apartment with second-hand furniture.
  2. Most of the textbooks I bought were second-hand.
  3. There's a great second-hand bookstore near campus.

2. Of information: heard from other people, not seen or learned directly. (из вторых рук, с чужих слов)

  1. The report is based on second-hand accounts from neighbors.
  2. My knowledge of the war is all second-hand.

3. Reaching you because of what other people do, especially in second-hand smoke. (пассивный (о курении); испанский (о стыде))

  1. Second-hand smoke is dangerous for young children.
  2. Watching his awkward speech gave me second-hand embarrassment.

long-distance#

Pronunciation
/ˌlɔːŋˈdɪstəns/ · lawng-DIS-tuhns
Русский
дальний, на большие расстояния; междугородний
Type
compound — adjective + noun; also classifying
Position
both — mostly before a noun: a long-distance runner; after a verb in US speech: Is the call long-distance?
Comparison
not gradable
Form
compound — long + distance
Contrast
In call long-distance it is an adverb. Russian дальнобойщик is a long-haul trucker or long-distance trucker. Working from home far away is remote work, not long-distance work. Russian отношения на расстоянии is a long-distance relationship.

Long-distance has 3 distinct senses.

1. Traveling or covering a long distance. (дальний, на большие расстояния)

  1. He trained for years as a long-distance runner.
  2. Long-distance truck drivers spend weeks away from home.
  3. The airline is adding more long-distance flights to Asia.

2. Of a phone call: between places far apart, usually costing more. (междугородний)

  1. In the '80s, a long-distance call to Grandma was a big deal.
  2. Is it still long-distance if you call from a cell phone?

3. Of a relationship: between people who live far away from each other. (на расстоянии)

  1. They had a long-distance relationship all through college.
  2. Keeping a long-distance friendship going takes effort.

time-consuming#

Pronunciation
/ˈtaɪmkənˌsuːmɪŋ/ · TYM-kuhn-soo-ming
Русский
отнимающий много времени, трудоёмкий
Type
compound — noun + present participle
Position
both — a time-consuming process; the paperwork is time-consuming
Comparison
more/most — more time-consuming, most time-consuming
Form
compound — noun time + consuming (what it uses up)

It means 'taking a lot of time', not 'wasting time': a time-consuming job can be worth it. It often comes in it is time-consuming to + verb. Russian трудоёмкий stresses effort, and English may need labor-intensive instead.

Taking a lot of time to do.

hard-working#

Pronunciation
/ˌhɑːrdˈwɜːrkɪŋ/ · hahrd-WUR-king
Русский
трудолюбивый, работящий
Type
compound — adjective + present participle; also quality
Position
both — a hard-working student; her parents are hard-working
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — harder-working, hardest-working; more hard-working, most hard-working
Form
compound — hard + working
Opposite
lazy
Contrast
American dictionaries usually spell it as one word, hardworking. The superlative is usually hardest-working (the hardest-working man in show business), though most hardworking is also correct. The adjective describes a person; the verb phrase is work hard (adverb): She works hard = She is hard-working = She is a hard worker. Hardly working is a joke meaning 'almost not working'.

Putting a lot of effort and care into work.

  1. The town is full of hard-working families.
  2. My grandmother was honest and hard-working her whole life.
  3. He's the hardest-working guy on the crew.
