# More adjectives: N–O

> Adjectives N–O beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### nagging

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnæɡɪŋ/ &middot; *NAG-ing*
**Русский:** ноющий, не дающий покоя; придирчивый
**Type:** participial
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a nagging doubt*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nagging*, *most nagging*

Nagging has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not serious but never going away, of a pain or a worry.** (ноющий, не отпускающий)

1. I had a *nagging* feeling I'd left the stove on.
2. A *nagging* pain in his knee kept him up half the night.

**2. Always complaining and asking you to do things.** (придирчивый, вечно пилящий)

3. He complained about his *nagging* boss the whole drive home.
4. Her *nagging* questions finally wore me down.

### nail-biting

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪlbaɪtɪŋ/ &middot; *NAYL-by-ting*
**Русский:** держащий в напряжении, нервный
**Type:** compound
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a nail-biting finish*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nail-biting*, *most nail-biting*
**Form:** compound — noun + participle: *nail* + *biting*

So tense and exciting that you cannot sit still.

1. The game came down to a *nail-biting* final minute.
2. It was a *nail-biting* wait for the test results.
3. They won in the most *nail-biting* finish of the season.

### naive

**Pronunciation:** /naɪˈiːv/ &middot; *ny-EEV*
**Русский:** наивный, доверчивый
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more naive*, *most naive*
**Form:** from French; also spelled *naïve*
**Opposite:** *worldly*
**Contrast:** The noun is *naivety* or *naiveté*, not *naiveness*. *Naive* is mildly critical — it says someone has too little experience, not that they are simply natural.

Too ready to believe people, from lack of experience.

1. It was *naive* of me to think he'd pay me back.
2. She asked a few *naive* questions about how the company made money.
3. He's more *naive* than stupid.

### naked

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪkɪd/ &middot; *NAY-kid*
**Русский:** голый, обнажённый; неприкрытый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more naked*, *most naked*
**Contrast:** Two syllables — the *-ed* is always /ɪd/, like *wicked* and *rugged*, never /neɪkt/.

Naked has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Wearing no clothes.** (голый, обнажённый)

1. The kids ran *naked* through the sprinkler.
2. He answered the door half *naked*.

**2. Bare, without the covering something usually has.** (голый, ничем не прикрытый)

3. A *naked* bulb hung over the kitchen table.
4. By November the branches were *naked*.

**3. Plain and not hidden, of a feeling or a fact.** (неприкрытый, откровенный)

5. It was *naked* greed, plain and simple.
6. You can't see the comet with the *naked* eye.

### nameless

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪmləs/ &middot; *NAYM-luhs*
**Русский:** безымянный; неназванный; неизъяснимый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -less from *name*

Nameless has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having no name, or not named on purpose.** (безымянный, неназванный)

1. A *nameless* donor paid for the new roof.
2. The report quotes a *nameless* official at the agency.

**2. Too vague to put a name to, of a feeling.** (неизъяснимый, смутный)

3. A *nameless* fear kept him awake until dawn.
4. She felt a *nameless* sadness all that week.

### Napoleonic

**Pronunciation:** /nəˌpoʊliˈɑːnɪk/ &middot; *nuh-poh-lee-AH-nik*
**Русский:** наполеоновский
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both — usually before a noun
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from the name *Napoleon*; always capitalized

Napoleonic has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Belonging to Napoleon or to the years he ruled France.** (наполеоновский (об эпохе))

1. He teaches a seminar on the *Napoleonic* wars.
2. The museum owns a *Napoleonic* uniform.

**2. Grand and domineering, the way Napoleon is imagined to have been.** (наполеоновский (о замыслах, амбициях))

3. The new director came in with *Napoleonic* ambitions.
4. He runs the department with *Napoleonic* confidence.

### narcissistic

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnɑːrsəˈsɪstɪk/ &middot; *nar-suh-SIS-tik*
**Русский:** самовлюблённый, нарциссический
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more narcissistic*, *most narcissistic*
**Form:** from *Narcissus*, who fell in love with his own reflection

Obsessed with yourself and with how you look to others.

1. His posts got so *narcissistic* that I stopped following him.
2. She was diagnosed with *narcissistic* personality disorder.
3. The whole speech was a *narcissistic* performance.

### narcotic

**Pronunciation:** /nɑːrˈkɑːtɪk/ &middot; *nar-KAH-tik*
**Русский:** наркотический; усыпляющий
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** Also a noun, usually plural: *the narcotics squad*.

Narcotic has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of a drug that dulls pain and brings sleep.** (наркотический)

1. The patient was given a *narcotic* painkiller after surgery.
2. *Narcotic* drugs are tightly controlled in every state.

**2. Making you sleepy or dull, like such a drug.** (усыпляющий, дурманящий)

3. The professor's voice had a *narcotic* effect on the class.
4. There's something *narcotic* about a long afternoon by the lake.

### narrative

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnærətɪv/ &middot; *NAR-uh-tiv*
**Русский:** повествовательный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *narrative poetry*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** Far more common as a noun (*the official narrative*). The adjective goes only before a noun.

Telling a story; having to do with how a story is told.

1. The class spends a month on *narrative* poetry.
2. The film has a simple *narrative* structure.
3. Her *narrative* voice changes from chapter to chapter.

### narrow-minded

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnæroʊˈmaɪndɪd/ &middot; *nar-oh-MYN-did*
**Русский:** ограниченный, узколобый, недалёкий
**Type:** compound; also evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more narrow-minded*, *most narrow-minded*
**Form:** compound — adjective + noun + *-ed*: *narrow* + *mind*
**Opposite:** *open-minded*

Unwilling to consider ideas or people different from what you are used to.

1. It's *narrow-minded* to judge a book by its cover.
2. He grew up in a small, *narrow-minded* town.
3. The older he gets, the more *narrow-minded* he sounds.

### nasal

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪzəl/ &middot; *NAY-zuhl*
**Русский:** носовой; гнусавый
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nasal*, *most nasal*

Nasal has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having to do with the nose.** (носовой)

1. The doctor prescribed a *nasal* spray.
2. *Nasal* congestion is usually the first symptom.

**2. Sounding as if the air is coming through the nose, of a voice.** (гнусавый, в нос)

3. He has a flat, *nasal* voice that carries across the room.
4. Her accent gets more *nasal* when she's tired.

**Type:** quality
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a nascent industry*
**Comparison:** not gradable

Just coming into existence and starting to grow.

1. The country's *nascent* film industry needs money and time.

### nasty

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnæsti/ &middot; *NAS-tee*
**Русский:** мерзкий, противный; злобный; скверный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *nastier*, *nastiest*

Nasty has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Very unpleasant to taste, smell or be out in.** (мерзкий, противный)

1. The medicine leaves a *nasty* taste in your mouth.
2. We drove home in *nasty* weather.

**2. Unkind and deliberately hurtful.** (злобный, гадкий)

3. She said something *nasty* and walked off.
4. He gets *nastier* the longer the meeting goes.

**3. Serious and likely to cause harm, of an injury or a problem.** (скверный, серьёзный (о травме, ситуации))

5. He took a *nasty* fall on the ice.
6. That's a *nasty* cut — you should get it looked at.

### national

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnæʃənəl/ &middot; *NASH-uh-nuhl*
**Русский:** национальный; общенациональный, государственный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a national holiday*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** Also a noun: *a foreign national* is a citizen of another country.

National has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Covering the whole country rather than one part of it.** (общенациональный, по всей стране)

1. Thanksgiving is a *national* holiday.
2. The story made the *national* news by Tuesday.

**2. Belonging to a nation as a people.** (национальный, государственный)

3. The flag is a symbol of *national* identity.
4. The team plays in the *national* colors.

### nationalist

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnæʃənəlɪst/ &middot; *NASH-uh-nuh-list*
**Русский:** националистический (нейтрально: национально ориентированный)
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a nationalist party*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** *Nationalist* is the plain label for a party or movement; *nationalistic* adds disapproval. *Nationalist* is also a noun.

Supporting the independence or the interests of one's own nation.

1. A *nationalist* party won the election in March.
2. He gave a *nationalist* speech about borders and trade.
3. The movement had *nationalist* roots going back a century.

### nationalistic

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnæʃənəˈlɪstɪk/ &middot; *nash-uh-nuh-LIS-tik*
**Русский:** националистический (с оттенком осуждения)
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nationalistic*, *most nationalistic*
**Contrast:** *Nationalistic* almost always disapproves; *nationalist* is the neutral label: *a nationalist party* vs *a nationalistic rant*.

Showing too much pride in your own nation, often against others.

1. The rally turned *nationalistic* and ugly.
2. His columns have grown more *nationalistic* over the years.
3. *Nationalistic* slogans covered the walls of the stadium.

### nationwide

**Pronunciation:** /ˌneɪʃənˈwaɪd/ &middot; *nay-shuhn-WYD*
**Русский:** общенациональный, по всей стране
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — *nation* + *wide*
**Contrast:** Also an adverb: *The store ships nationwide*. Before a noun the stress moves forward: *a NATIONwide recall*.

Happening or existing in every part of a country.

1. The chain announced a *nationwide* recall.
2. There is a *nationwide* shortage of nurses.
3. Their coverage is *nationwide*.

### native

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪtɪv/ &middot; *NAY-tiv*
**Русский:** родной; коренной; местный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *to* + place, in sense 2: *native to Japan*
**Preposition patterns:** [native to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#native-to)
**Contrast:** *A native* as a noun for a local person now sounds colonial; say *a local*. For indigenous peoples, *Native* is capitalized: *Native American*.

Native has 2 distinct senses.

**1. That you were born with or born into.** (родной (о языке, стране))

1. Her *native* language is Portuguese.
2. He moved back to his *native* Ohio after college.

**2. Growing or living naturally in a place rather than brought there.** (местный, произрастающий в данной местности)

3. The sugar maple is *native* to eastern North America.
4. *Native* grasses need far less water than a lawn.

### Native American

**Pronunciation:** /ˌneɪtɪv əˈmɛrɪkən/ &middot; *NAY-tiv uh-MAIR-i-kuhn*
**Русский:** индейский, относящийся к коренным народам США
**Type:** origin
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** Also a noun. *Indian* for these peoples is dated in American English; many people prefer *Native American*, *Native*, or the name of the nation (*Navajo*, *Lakota*).

Belonging to the indigenous peoples of the United States.

1. The museum has a large *Native American* art collection.
2. She is *Native American* on her mother's side.
3. Several *Native American* languages are taught at the university.

### native-born

**Pronunciation:** /ˌneɪtɪvˈbɔːrn/ &middot; *nay-tiv-BORN*
**Русский:** коренной, рождённый в данной стране
**Type:** compound
**Position:** attributive only — before a noun: *a native-born citizen*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — *native* + *born*

Born in the country where you live, rather than having moved there.

1. Only a *native-born* citizen can become president.
2. The city's *native-born* population is shrinking every year.
3. He's a *native-born* New Yorker who's never left the block.

### natural

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnætʃərəl/ &middot; *NACH-ur-uhl*
**Русский:** природный, естественный; натуральный; врождённый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more natural*, *most natural*
**Takes:** in sense 2, *for* + noun with a *to*-infinitive, or a *that*-clause: *It's natural for kids to test the rules*; *It's natural that you'd worry*
**Opposite:** *artificial*

Natural has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Existing in nature, not made or added by people.** (природный, натуральный)

1. The park protects the last *natural* wetland in the county.
2. We use only *natural* ingredients in the soap.

**2. Normal and to be expected.** (естественный, закономерный)

3. It's *natural* to be nervous before a test.
4. His reaction was perfectly *natural* under the circumstances.

**3. Born with an ability rather than having to learn it.** (врождённый, прирождённый)

5. She's a *natural* athlete.
6. He has a *natural* ear for languages.

### naturalistic

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnætʃərəˈlɪstɪk/ &middot; *nach-ur-uh-LIS-tik*
**Русский:** натуралистический, реалистичный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more naturalistic*, *most naturalistic*

Showing people and things exactly as they are, in art or writing.

1. The novel's *naturalistic* dialogue sounds like a recording.
2. He paints in a *naturalistic* style.
3. The lighting is *naturalistic* rather than theatrical.

### naturalized

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnætʃərəlaɪzd/ &middot; *NACH-ur-uh-lyzd*
**Русский:** натурализованный; прижившийся, акклиматизировавшийся
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — usually before a noun
**Comparison:** not gradable

Naturalized has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having become a citizen of a country you were not born in.** (натурализованный, получивший гражданство)

1. She became a *naturalized* citizen last spring.
2. The rule applies to *naturalized* Americans as well.

**2. Settled and growing wild in a place it was brought to, of a plant or animal.** (прижившийся, одичавший)

3. The shrub is now *naturalized* all along the coast.
4. *Naturalized* daffodils come back every spring on their own.

### nauseated

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɔːzieɪtɪd/ &middot; *NAW-zee-ay-tid*
**Русский:** испытывающий тошноту
**Type:** participial
**Position:** predicative only — after a verb: *I felt nauseated*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nauseated*, *most nauseated*
**Takes:** *by* + noun
**Contrast:** *Nauseated* is how you feel; *nauseating* is what causes it. In everyday American speech many people say *nauseous* instead of *nauseated*.

Feeling as if you are about to vomit.

1. I felt *nauseated* for the whole flight.
2. She was *nauseated* by the smell of gasoline.
3. He gets *nauseated* on winding roads.

### nauseating

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɔːzieɪtɪŋ/ &middot; *NAW-zee-ay-ting*
**Русский:** тошнотворный; отвратительный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nauseating*, *most nauseating*
**Contrast:** *Nauseating* is the cause, *nauseated* the feeling: *the nauseating smell left us nauseated*.

Nauseating has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Making you feel like vomiting.** (тошнотворный, вызывающий тошноту)

1. The smell in the back of the fridge was *nauseating*.
2. He described the wound in *nauseating* detail.

**2. Disgusting because of how someone behaves.** (отвратительный, мерзкий)

3. Their *nauseating* self-congratulation went on for an hour.
4. It's *nauseating* to watch him take credit for her work.

### nauseous

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɔːʃəs/ &middot; *NAW-shuhs*
**Русский:** чувствующий тошноту
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — after a verb: *I feel nauseous*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nauseous*, *most nauseous*
**Contrast:** In American English *nauseous* normally means *feeling sick*. Careful writers keep *nauseated* for the feeling and *nauseous* for the cause, but almost nobody says *I feel nauseated* in conversation.

Feeling sick to your stomach.

1. I felt *nauseous* after the second slice of cake.
2. She gets *nauseous* in the back seat.
3. He woke up dizzy and *nauseous*.

### Neanderthal

**Pronunciation:** /niˈændərθɔːl/ &middot; *nee-AN-dur-thawl*
**Русский:** неандертальский; первобытный, дремучий
**Type:** classifying; also evaluative
**Position:** both

Neanderthal has 2 distinct senses.

1. *Neanderthal* remains were found in the cave in 1908.

**2. Crude and badly out of date in attitudes.** (первобытный, дремучий, отсталый)

3. His views on women are frankly *Neanderthal*.
4. The office had a *Neanderthal* culture until she took over.

### nearsighted

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnɪrˈsaɪtɪd/ &middot; *neer-SY-tid*
**Русский:** близорукий
**Type:** compound
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more nearsighted*, *most nearsighted*
**Form:** compound — *near* + *sighted*
**Opposite:** *farsighted*

4. The ruling was *overturned* on appeal.

### overweening

**Pronunciation:** /ˌoʊvərˈwiːnɪŋ/ &middot; *oh-vur-WEE-ning*
**Русский:** непомерный, чрезмерный (о гордыне, самомнении)
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** attributive only — with a small set of nouns: *pride*, *ambition*, *confidence*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more overweening*, *most overweening*
**Contrast:** Formal and literary, and it describes the feeling, not the person: *overweening pride*, not *an overweening man*.

Far too great, of pride or ambition.

1. His *overweening* pride cost him the friendship.
2. *Overweening* ambition brought the company down.
3. She wrote about the *overweening* confidence of young traders.

### overweight

**Pronunciation:** /ˌoʊvərˈweɪt/ &middot; *oh-vur-WAYT*
**Русский:** имеющий лишний вес, полный; сверхнормативный (о багаже)
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — of people almost always after a verb: *He is overweight*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more overweight*, *most overweight*
**Form:** over- + *weight*
**Contrast:** Of a person this is the neutral word; *fat* is blunt and often rude. Of luggage it simply means too heavy.

Overweight has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Heavier than is healthy, of a person or an animal.** (имеющий лишний вес, полный)

1. The doctor said he was slightly *overweight*.
2. Their cat is *overweight* and lazy.

**2. Heavier than the limit allowed.** (сверхнормативный, с перевесом)

3. They charged me for an *overweight* bag.
4. An *overweight* truck pays a fine at the scales.

### overwrought

**Pronunciation:** /ˌoʊvərˈrɔːt/ &middot; *oh-vur-RAWT*

Overwrought has 2 distinct senses.

3. The prose is *overwrought* and hard to finish.
4. It's an *overwrought* ending to a quiet movie.

### own

**Pronunciation:** /oʊn/ &middot; *OHN*
**Русский:** собственный, свой
**Type:** emphasizing
**Position:** attributive only — always after a possessive: *my own room*, *the city's own bus line*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** *Own* never stands alone: *my own car*, never *an own car* — the usual mistake for Russian *свой*. It also comes after the noun in *a place of my own*. The verb *own* (*She owns the building*) is a different word.

Belonging to that person and to nobody else.

1. She has her *own* room now.
2. I'd like a place of my *own*.
3. They grow their *own* vegetables.
