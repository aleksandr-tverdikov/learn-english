# More adjectives: D

> Adjectives D beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### dainty

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdeɪnti/ &middot; *DAYN-tee*
**Русский:** изящный, миниатюрный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *daintier*, *daintiest*

Small, delicate and pretty.

1. She wore *dainty* gold earrings.
2. They served *dainty* little sandwiches with the tea.
3. Her handwriting was *daintier* than mine.

### damaged

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdæmɪdʒd/ &middot; *DAM-ijd*
**Русский:** поврежденный; травмированный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more damaged*, *most damaged*
**Form:** -ed from *damage*

Damaged has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Harmed or broken, so it no longer works or looks as it should.** (поврежденный, испорченный)

1. The package arrived *damaged*.
2. The storm left dozens of *damaged* homes.
3. The *most damaged* buildings were near the coast.

**2. Emotionally hurt by bad experiences in the past.** (травмированный, надломленный)

4. He was a *damaged* kid from a violent home.
5. She seemed deeply *damaged* by the divorce.

### damaging

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdæmɪdʒɪŋ/ &middot; *DAM-ij-ing*
**Русский:** вредный, пагубный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more damaging*, *most damaging*
**Takes:** *to* + noun
**Form:** -ing from *damage* — what causes the damage

Causing harm or damage.

1. The scandal was *damaging* to her career.
2. Too much sun can have *damaging* effects on your skin.
3. The *most damaging* evidence came from his own emails.

### damn

**Pronunciation:** /dæm/ &middot; *DAM*
**Русский:** чертов, проклятый
**Type:** emphasizing
**Position:** attributive only
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** A mild swear word: fine among friends, not in a job interview. *Damn* is also an adverb (*damn good*), an interjection (*Damn!*) and a noun (*I don't give a damn*). *Damned* means the same before a noun; *darn* is the polite version.

Used before a noun to show anger or annoyance, or just for emphasis.

1. The *damn* car won't start again.
2. Turn off that *damn* TV!
3. It's a *damn* shame they closed the old diner.

### damned

**Pronunciation:** /dæmd/ &middot; *DAMD*
**Русский:** чертов, проклятый; обреченный
**Type:** emphasizing; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — except the idiom *damnedest* (*the damnedest thing* = the strangest)
**Contrast:** Stronger than *darned*, about as strong as *damn*. *The damned* (noun) are the souls in hell. *I'll be damned!* shows surprise; *I'll be damned if I pay that* means *I refuse to*. *Damned good* is the adverb.

Damned has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Used before a noun to show anger or annoyance, or just for emphasis.** (чертов, проклятый)

1. The *damned* printer is jammed again.
2. I can't find my *damned* phone.
3. It was the *damnedest* thing — the dog opened the door by itself.

**2. Condemned to hell, or certain to fail whatever you do.** (обреченный, проклятый)

4. They believed the souls of sinners were *damned* forever.
5. You're *damned* if you do and *damned* if you don't.

### dandy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdændi/ &middot; *DAN-dee*
**Русский:** отличный, превосходный (часто иронично)

### darned

**Pronunciation:** /dɑːrnd/ &middot; *DAHRND*
**Русский:** чертов (смягченное)
**Type:** emphasizing

**Comparison:** not gradable — except the idiom *darnedest* (*the darnedest things*)

### Darwinian

**Pronunciation:** /dɑːrˈwɪniən/ &middot; *dahr-WIN-ee-uhn*
**Русский:** дарвиновский; беспощадный (о конкуренции)
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — except sense 2: *more Darwinian*
**Form:** -ian from *Darwin*

Darwinian has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Relating to Charles Darwin or his theory of evolution.** (дарвиновский, дарвинистский)

1. The course covers *Darwinian* evolution.

**2. Brutally competitive, so that only the strongest survive.** (беспощадный, как естественный отбор)

3. Silicon Valley can be a *Darwinian* place.
4. The airline industry is *Darwinian*: the weak go bankrupt.

### dear

**Pronunciation:** /dɪr/ &middot; *DEER*
**Русский:** дорогой, милый
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *dearer*, *dearest*
**Takes:** *to* + person
**Contrast:** In British English *dear* also means *expensive*; Americans say *expensive*. *Oh dear!* is an interjection, and *dear* is also used to address someone (*Thanks, dear*).

Dear has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Loved very much, or very important to someone.** (дорогой, любимый)

1. She's a *dear* friend of mine.
2. The house was very *dear* to him.
3. My *dearest* wish is to see my family again.

**2. Used before a name or title at the start of a letter or email.** (дорогой, уважаемый)

4. *Dear* Mr. Johnson, thank you for your letter.
5. She began the email with *Dear* Team.
