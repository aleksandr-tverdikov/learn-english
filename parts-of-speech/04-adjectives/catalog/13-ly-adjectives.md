# Adjectives that end in -ly

> *friendly*, *lonely*, *lovely*, *elderly* — adjectives that look like adverbs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These words end in *-ly* but are adjectives, not adverbs: *a friendly dog*, *a lonely night*. Most of them have no adverb form at all, so *she smiled friendly* is wrong and English needs a phrase instead: *she smiled in a friendly way*. A few — *daily*, *weekly*, *early* — are both, and those entries show each use.

---
### friendly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfrɛndli/ &middot; *FREND-lee*
**Русский:** дружелюбный; дружественный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a friendly neighbor*, *Our neighbors are friendly*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *friendlier*, *friendliest*, *more friendly*
**Takes:** *to* or *toward* a person (how you treat them: *friendly to newcomers*); *to* a group or an idea (supportive of it: *friendly to small businesses*); *with* a person (on good terms with them: *friendly with the neighbors*)
**Preposition patterns:** [friendly with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#friendly-with)
**Form:** *friend* + -ly
**Opposite:** *unfriendly*; *hostile*
**Contrast:** There is no adverb *friendlily*. *She smiled friendly* is wrong; say *She smiled in a friendly way* or *She gave me a friendly smile*. After a linking verb the adjective is right: *He seems friendly*, *Be friendly*. *Friendly to* someone is about how you treat them; *friendly with* someone means you are on good terms: *I'm friendly with her brother* (мы с её братом приятели). In *user-friendly*, *eco-friendly* and *kid-friendly* the word means suitable for, or not harmful to.

Friendly has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Kind and pleasant to other people; behaving like a friend.** (дружелюбный, приветливый)

1. The people at the front desk were really *friendly*.
2. She has a warm, *friendly* smile.
3. My new coworkers are a lot *friendlier* than my old ones.

**2. On good terms with someone; not hostile.** (дружественный, в дружеских отношениях)

4. The two countries have had *friendly* relations for decades.
5. I'm *friendly* with a few of the other parents at school.
6. We parted on *friendly* terms.

**3. Supportive of a person, a group or an idea; ready to agree or help.** (благосклонный, доброжелательно настроенный)

7. The senator gave her speech to a *friendly* crowd of supporters.
8. The new mayor has been *friendly* to small businesses.
9. Don't expect the reviewers to be *friendly* to a sequel.

**4. Of a place or atmosphere: welcoming and comfortable.** (уютный, радушный)

10. It's a *friendly* little diner where everyone knows your name.
11. The office has a relaxed, *friendly* atmosphere.

**5. On your own side in a fight or a war.** (свой, союзный)

12. The soldier was killed by *friendly* fire.
13. The damaged plane made it back to a *friendly* airfield.

**6. Done for fun or in a spirit of goodwill, not as serious competition.** (товарищеский, дружеский)

14. It's just a *friendly* game — nobody's keeping score.
15. They made a *friendly* bet on the Super Bowl.
16. A little *friendly* competition keeps the team motivated.

**7. Not harmful to something, or well suited to a particular group; usually after another word.** (безопасный для; удобный для)

17. We switched to environmentally *friendly* cleaning products.
18. The museum is very kid-*friendly*.
19. Is the hotel pet-*friendly*?

### lovely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlʌvli/ &middot; *LUHV-lee*
**Русский:** прекрасный, чудесный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — *a lovely view*, *The view is lovely*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *lovelier*, *loveliest*, *more lovely*
**Form:** *love* + -ly
**Contrast:** There is no adverb *lovelily*: *She sings lovely* is wrong; say *She sings beautifully*. *lovely* has nothing to do with being in love or being loved: влюблённый is *in love*, любимый is *favorite* or *beloved*. It is far more frequent in British speech; Americans use it too, but *nice*, *great* and *beautiful* are more usual in casual talk.

Lovely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Beautiful or very attractive to look at.** (красивый, прелестный)

1. What a *lovely* view of the lake!
2. You look *lovely* in that dress.
3. It was the *loveliest* garden on the whole street.

**2. Very pleasant or enjoyable.** (чудесный, замечательный)

4. We had a *lovely* evening — thanks for inviting us.
5. It was *lovely* to see you again.
6. The weather has been *lovely* all week.

**3. Of a person: kind, friendly and easy to like.** (милый, славный)

7. Her parents are *lovely* people.
8. He's a *lovely* man and a great teacher.

### lonely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈloʊnli/ &middot; *LOHN-lee*
**Русский:** одинокий; безлюдный
**Type:** emotion; also quality
**Position:** both — *a lonely old man*, *He felt lonely*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *lonelier*, *loneliest*, *more lonely*
**Form:** *lone* + -ly
**Contrast:** *alone* means without other people — a plain fact — and it is predicative only: *She lives alone*, never *an alone woman*. *lonely* means sad because you are alone or have no friends: *You can be alone without being lonely*. *lone* goes only before a noun and means the only one: *a lone tree*, *a lone gunman*. There is no adverb *lonelily*; rephrase: *She sat by herself, feeling lonely*.

Lonely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Unhappy because you are alone or have no friends; or, of a time or a life, spent that way.** (одинокий, тоскующий)

1. After his wife died, he felt terribly *lonely*.
2. Moving to a new city can be *lonely* at first.
3. It was the *loneliest* year of my life.

**2. Of a place: far from where people live and seldom visited.** (безлюдный, уединённый)

4. The car broke down on a *lonely* stretch of highway in Nevada.
5. The cabin sits on a *lonely* hillside miles from town.

**3. Standing or moving alone, with nobody or nothing else nearby.** (одинокий, одиноко стоящий)

6. A *lonely* figure walked along the empty beach.
7. One *lonely* light was still on in the office building.

### lively

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlaɪvli/ &middot; *LYV-lee*
**Русский:** живой, оживлённый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a lively debate*, *The debate got lively*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *livelier*, *liveliest*, *more lively*
**Form:** *life* + -ly
**Opposite:** *dull*; *quiet*
**Contrast:** Pronounced LYV-lee, with the vowel of *five*, not the vowel of the verb *live*. Keep three words apart: *alive* = not dead, predicative only (*The fish is still alive*); *live* (LYV) = happening now or not recorded, before a noun (*live music*, *a live broadcast*); *lively* = full of energy (*a lively crowd*). *A live concert* is not recorded; *a lively concert* is energetic. There is no adverb *livelily*; say *in a lively way*.

Lively has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Full of energy; cheerful and active.** (живой, энергичный, бойкий)

1. Their kids are bright and *lively*.
2. At ninety, she's still as *lively* as ever.
3. He was the *liveliest* person at the party.

**2. Of a place or an event: busy, with a lot happening.** (оживлённый, шумный)

4. The neighborhood has a *lively* bar scene.
5. The market gets *livelier* on weekends.

**3. Of a discussion or debate: full of strong opinions and eager talk.** (оживлённый, бурный)

6. The proposal led to a *lively* debate at the town meeting.
7. The class discussion got pretty *lively*.

**4. Of the mind, interest or imagination: quick, strong and active.** (живой (ум, интерес))

8. She has a *lively* imagination.
9. He took a *lively* interest in local politics.

**5. Of music or dancing: fast and cheerful.** (задорный, весёлый)

10. The band played a *lively* polka.
11. The song has a *lively*, catchy beat.

**6. Of colors: bright and strong.** (яркий, сочный)

12. The kitchen was painted in *lively* shades of yellow and green.
13. A *lively* print can brighten up a plain room.

### silly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪli/ &middot; *SIL-ee*
**Русский:** глупый; дурашливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a silly mistake*, *Don't be silly*; the 'bored silly' sense only after a verb
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *sillier*, *silliest*, *more silly*
**Takes:** *of* + a person + *to*-infinitive (judging the person: *It was silly of me to forget*); *for* + a person + *to*-infinitive (the plan makes no sense: *It's silly for us to take two cars*)
**Opposite:** *sensible*
**Contrast:** There is no adverb *sillily* in normal use: say *in a silly way*, or use a linking verb: *Stop acting silly*. *silly* is milder and friendlier than *stupid*: calling an idea *silly* is teasing, calling it *stupid* is an insult. To judge a person, the pattern is *It was silly of you*, not *silly from you*.

Silly has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Showing poor judgment; foolish.** (глупый)

1. It was a *silly* mistake, and it cost us the game.
2. Don't be *silly* — of course you can come.
3. That's the *silliest* excuse I've ever heard.

**2. Playful and not serious, in a way meant to be funny.** (дурашливый, смешной)

4. The kids made *silly* faces for the camera.
5. We all wore *silly* hats to the party.
6. He gets *sillier* the more tired he is.

**3. Small and unimportant; not worth worrying about.** (пустячный, ерундовый)

7. They had a fight over something *silly*.
8. It's just a *silly* little gift.

**4. Into a dazed or helpless state; only after verbs such as bore, scare and laugh.** (до одури, до смерти)

9. The lecture bored me *silly*.
10. The movie scared the kids *silly*.
11. We laughed ourselves *silly*.

### ugly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʌɡli/ &middot; *UHG-lee*
**Русский:** некрасивый, уродливый
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — *an ugly building*, *The building is ugly*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *uglier*, *ugliest*, *more ugly*
**Opposite:** *beautiful*; *attractive*
**Contrast:** There is no adverb *uglily*: say *in an ugly way*. Said of a person, *ugly* is much harsher than Russian некрасивый; Americans soften it to *not very attractive* or *plain*. In *turn ugly* and *get ugly* it means dangerous or violent, not unattractive: *The protest turned ugly*.

Ugly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Unpleasant to look at.** (некрасивый, уродливый)

1. They painted the house an *ugly* shade of brown.
2. Honestly, I think the new stadium is *ugly*.
3. That's the *ugliest* sweater I've ever seen.

**2. Of a situation or mood: unpleasant and threatening, likely to turn violent.** (скверный, угрожающий)

4. Things got *ugly* when the police arrived.
5. There was an *ugly* scene outside the bar.
6. The crowd was in an *ugly* mood.

**3. Morally unpleasant; cruel or offensive.** (мерзкий, гнусный)

7. Someone started an *ugly* rumor about her.
8. The trial exposed an *ugly* side of the town's history.

### elderly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛldərli/ &middot; *EL-dur-lee*
**Русский:** пожилой
**Type:** age
**Position:** both — *an elderly neighbor*, *My parents are elderly now*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more elderly*, *most elderly*, both rare; *very elderly* is the usual way to grade it
**Form:** *elder* + -ly
**Opposite:** *young*
**Contrast:** *elderly* is a polite word for people who are old; *old* is plainer and can sound blunt about a person, and many Americans now prefer *older* (*older adults*) or *senior*. It is not a comparative: *my older brother* or *my elder brother* is старший брат, never *my elderly brother*. It is not an adverb either. *The elderly* (пожилые люди as a group) is a noun use.

Elderly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of a person: old; a polite word.** (пожилой)

1. An *elderly* woman asked me to help with her bags.
2. The program delivers hot meals to *elderly* residents.
3. Her father is *elderly* and can't drive anymore.

**2. Of a thing: old and no longer working well; used humorously.** (старенький, видавший виды)

4. He still drives an *elderly* Buick.
5. Our *elderly* laptop takes five minutes to start up.

### costly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɔːstli/ &middot; *KAWST-lee*
**Русский:** дорогостоящий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *costly repairs*, *The repairs were costly*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *costlier*, *costliest*, *more costly*
**Form:** *cost* + -ly
**Opposite:** *cheap*; *inexpensive*
**Contrast:** For prices in stores and restaurants, *expensive* is the everyday word: *an expensive restaurant*, not usually *a costly restaurant*. *costly* is more formal and goes with repairs, delays, lawsuits and mistakes, where it often means causing loss: *a costly error* = ошибка, которая дорого обошлась. There is no adverb *costlily*.

Costly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Costing a lot of money.** (дорогостоящий, дорогой)

1. The old roof needed *costly* repairs.
2. Treating the disease is extremely *costly*.
3. Flying turned out to be *costlier* than driving.

**2. Causing a serious loss, damage or suffering.** (дорого обошедшийся, губительный)

4. Leaving the door unlocked was a *costly* mistake.
5. The delay proved *costly* for the company.
6. The fumble was the *costliest* error of the game.

### deadly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɛdli/ &middot; *DED-lee*
**Русский:** смертельный, смертоносный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a deadly virus*, *The virus is deadly*; *deadly enemies* only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *deadlier*, *deadliest*, *more deadly*
**Form:** *dead* + -ly
**Contrast:** *deadly* = able to kill (*a deadly disease*, смертельный). *deathly* = like death (*a deathly silence*, *deathly pale*, мертвенный). *dead* = no longer alive. Before another adjective, *deadly* is an adverb meaning completely (*deadly serious*, *deadly dull*) — that use belongs to the adverb dictionary. In *the seven deadly sins* (семь смертных грехов) it means fatal to the soul, not to the body. There is no *deadlily*.

Deadly has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Able or likely to cause death.** (смертельный, смертоносный)

1. Some wild mushrooms are *deadly*.
2. Fentanyl is far *deadlier* than heroin.
3. It was the *deadliest* hurricane in fifty years.

**2. Of enemies or a conflict: extreme and aiming to destroy the other side.** (смертельный, заклятый)

4. The two gangs were *deadly* rivals.
5. They had been *deadly* enemies since high school.

**3. Extremely accurate or effective.** (убийственный, беспощадно точный)

6. He's a *deadly* shooter from three-point range.
7. She hit the target with *deadly* accuracy.

**4. Complete or total; with a few nouns such as silence and earnest.** (полный, мёртвый)

8. A *deadly* silence fell over the room.
9. She was in *deadly* earnest about quitting.

**5. Very boring; informal.** (смертельно скучный)

10. The first hour of the movie is *deadly*.
11. Budget meetings can be absolutely *deadly*.

### cowardly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkaʊərdli/ &middot; *KOW-urd-lee*
**Русский:** трусливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a cowardly act*, *It was cowardly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more cowardly*, *most cowardly*
**Form:** *coward* + -ly
**Opposite:** *brave*; *courageous*
**Contrast:** *coward* is the noun, so it needs an article: *He's a coward* or *He's cowardly*, never *He is coward*. Though it ends in *-ly*, *cowardly* is not an adverb in modern English: *He ran away like a coward*, not *He ran away cowardly*.

Cowardly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Lacking courage; too afraid to face danger or difficulty.** (трусливый, малодушный)

1. He was too *cowardly* to tell her the truth himself.
2. Don't be so *cowardly* — just go talk to her.
3. It was the *most cowardly* thing I've ever seen anyone do.

**2. Of an act: done so as to avoid risk to yourself, often by attacking people who cannot fight back.** (подлый, трусливый)

4. The mayor called the bombing a *cowardly* attack on innocent people.
5. Breaking up by text is a *cowardly* way to end things.

### orderly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːrdərli/ &middot; *OR-dur-lee*
**Русский:** упорядоченный, организованный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *orderly rows*, *The room was neat and orderly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more orderly*, *most orderly*
**Form:** *order* + -ly
**Opposite:** *disorderly*; *messy*
**Contrast:** There is no adverb form: *Leave the building orderly* is wrong; say *in an orderly way* or *in an orderly fashion*. *An orderly* (санитар в больнице) is a noun. The legal phrase *disorderly conduct* (нарушение общественного порядка) is common in American news.

Orderly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Neatly arranged, with everything in its place.** (аккуратный, упорядоченный)

1. Her desk is always neat and *orderly*.
2. The books were lined up in *orderly* rows.

**2. Calm and well behaved, without confusion or disorder.** (организованный, спокойный)

3. Please leave the building in an *orderly* fashion.
4. The protest was peaceful and *orderly*.
5. The transfer of power was *more orderly* than people expected.

**3. Of a person, a mind or a way of life: organized, and following a regular system.** (методичный, аккуратный)

6. She has a very *orderly* mind.
7. He lived a quiet, *orderly* life.

### timely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtaɪmli/ &middot; *TYM-lee*
**Русский:** своевременный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *a timely reminder*; also *The warning was timely*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more timely*, *most timely*
**Form:** *time* + -ly
**Opposite:** *untimely*
**Contrast:** *timely* is not an adverb: *Please pay timely* is wrong; say *pay on time* or, in formal writing, *in a timely manner*. The opposite *untimely* is mostly met in *an untimely death* (безвременная смерть). Russian актуальный is often *timely* or *topical* — never *actual*, which means фактический.

Timely has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Done or happening at the right time, or without delay.** (своевременный, сделанный в срок)

1. Please respond in a *timely* manner.
2. We appreciate your *timely* payment.
3. Thanks for the *timely* reminder — I almost forgot.

**2. Dealing with something that matters right now; topical.** (актуальный, злободневный)

4. Her book on artificial intelligence couldn't be *more timely*.
5. The documentary is especially *timely* in an election year.

### motherly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmʌðərli/ &middot; *MUHTH-ur-lee*
**Русский:** материнский, заботливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *motherly advice*, *She was very motherly toward the new nurses*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more motherly*, *most motherly*
**Form:** *mother* + -ly
**Contrast:** *motherly* describes warm, caring behavior like a good mother's. *maternal* is the classifying word for anything connected with mothers: *maternal health*, and *my maternal grandmother* (бабушка по матери), never *my motherly grandmother*. There is no adverb: *She looked after him in a motherly way*.

Kind, warm and protective, like a good mother.

1. An older, *motherly* nurse held my hand the whole time.
2. She's always been *motherly* toward the younger players.
3. Nobody in the office is *more motherly* than our receptionist.

### fatherly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɑːðərli/ &middot; *FAH-thur-lee*
**Русский:** отеческий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *fatherly advice*, *The coach was fatherly toward his players*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more fatherly*, *most fatherly*
**Form:** *father* + -ly
**Contrast:** *fatherly* describes kind, guiding behavior like a good father's. *paternal* is the classifying word for anything connected with fathers: *my paternal grandfather* (дедушка по отцу), never *my fatherly grandfather*. There is no adverb: *He talked to me in a fatherly way*.

Kind, protective and ready to guide, like a good father.

1. My boss gave me some *fatherly* advice about saving money.
2. He put a *fatherly* arm around the boy's shoulders.
3. The old coach was *more fatherly* than strict.

### brotherly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbrʌðərli/ &middot; *BRUHTH-ur-lee*
**Русский:** братский
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *brotherly love*, *His concern was purely brotherly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more brotherly*, *most brotherly*
**Form:** *brother* + -ly
**Contrast:** *fraternal* is the formal, classifying word: *fraternal twins* (разнояйцевые близнецы), *a fraternal organization*. For sisters the word is *sisterly*. There is no adverb: *He treated me like a brother*.

Showing the love, loyalty or support expected between brothers.

1. Philadelphia calls itself the City of *Brotherly* Love.
2. He gave his younger teammate a *brotherly* slap on the back.
3. Their rivalry was *more brotherly* than bitter.

### scholarly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈskɑːlərli/ &middot; *SKAH-lur-lee*
**Русский:** учёный; научный
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both — *a scholarly journal*, *The book is scholarly but easy to read*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more scholarly*, *most scholarly*
**Form:** *scholar* + -ly
**Contrast:** Russian научный splits in English. *scientific* is about the sciences and their methods (*a scientific experiment*); *scholarly* is about serious academic research in any field, especially the humanities (*a scholarly book on Tolstoy*); *academic* is about universities in general. In American English a *scholar* is учёный or a student with a scholarship, never a schoolchild.

Scholarly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of writing or work: based on serious academic research.** (научный, академический)

1. The article appeared in a *scholarly* journal.
2. The book is *scholarly* but still easy to read.
3. It's the *most scholarly* biography of Lincoln to date.

**2. Of a person: having a lot of knowledge from serious study, and fond of study.** (учёный, эрудированный)

4. He was a quiet, *scholarly* man who read Greek for fun.
5. Her *scholarly* manner made the students take her seriously.

### leisurely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈliːʒərli/ &middot; *LEE-zhur-lee*
**Русский:** неторопливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *a leisurely lunch*; also *The pace was leisurely*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more leisurely*, *most leisurely*
**Form:** *leisure* + -ly
**Opposite:** *hurried*; *rushed*
**Contrast:** Unlike most *-ly* adjectives, it is also an adverb: *They strolled leisurely along the river* is correct but bookish, and Americans usually say *at a leisurely pace*. Most Americans say LEE-zhur-lee; LEZH-ur-lee is also heard in the US, and LEZH-uh-lee is British. *leisurely* is about speed, not free time: свободное время is *free time* or *leisure time*.

Done slowly and in a relaxed way, without hurrying.

1. We took a *leisurely* walk along the beach.
2. Sunday breakfast is always a *leisurely* affair at our house.
3. The second half of the trip was *more leisurely*.

### heavenly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɛvənli/ &middot; *HEV-uhn-lee*
**Русский:** небесный; божественный
**Type:** classifying; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *heavenly bodies* only before a noun; *The cake was heavenly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more heavenly* in the 'delightful' sense; not gradable when it means 'of heaven'
**Form:** *heaven* + -ly
**Contrast:** *A heavenly body* is a star, planet or moon (небесное тело), not a beautiful person. Russian небесный is *heavenly* mainly in religion and in *heavenly bodies*; for the color of the sky say *sky blue*, and science uses *celestial*. In everyday speech *heavenly* is mostly about food, smells and comfort: *That bed is heavenly*.

Heavenly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of or from heaven, in religion; or in the sky, in the phrase heavenly bodies.** (небесный)

1. The choir sang of angels and the *heavenly* host.
2. Our *heavenly* Father, hear our prayer.
3. Galileo studied the moons of Jupiter and other *heavenly* bodies.

**2. Very pleasant; delightful.** (божественный, чудесный)

4. This chocolate cake is *heavenly*.
5. After the long hike, a hot shower felt *heavenly*.
6. I can't imagine anything *more heavenly* than a week at the beach.

### ghostly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡoʊstli/ &middot; *GOHST-lee*
**Русский:** призрачный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *a ghostly figure*; also *Her face looked ghostly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more ghostly*, *most ghostly*
**Form:** *ghost* + -ly
