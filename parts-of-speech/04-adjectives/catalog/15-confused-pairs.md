# Adjectives that are easily confused

> *economic* and *economical*, *historic* and *historical*, *continual* and *continuous*.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Pairs built on the same root that mean different things: *economic* is about the economy, *economical* is about saving money; *historic* means *important in history*, *historical* means *about the past*. Native speakers mix some of these up too, but in writing they matter, and each entry names its partner and the test that separates them.

---
### economic

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪk/ &middot; *ek-uh-NAH-mik*
**Русский:** экономический; рентабельный
**Type:** classifying — about the economy; also quality in the *profitable* sense
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun (*economic growth*); after *be* to name the kind of problem (*The reasons were economic*) and in the *profitable* sense (*It isn't economic to repair it*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — in the *economy* sense; *more economic* occurs only in the *profitable* sense
**Opposite:** *uneconomic* (in the *profitable* sense)
**Contrast:** *economic* is about the economy; *economical* is about spending little. Test: if you can say *cheap to run* or *saves money*, use *economical*: *an economical car* (экономичный автомобиль), not *an economic car*. *economic growth*, *an economic crisis* (экономический кризис), never *an economical crisis*. Russian *экономический* = *economic*; *экономичный* and *экономный* = *economical*. The *profitable* sense (*It isn't economic to fix it*) is the one place the two come close. Russian *экономика* is two English nouns: *the economy* (экономика страны) and *economics* (экономика как наука). Both first syllables are heard in American speech: ek-uh- and ee-kuh-.

Economic has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Relating to the economy of a country or region — trade, industry, jobs and money — or to economics as a subject.** (экономический)

1. The country is going through a period of slow *economic* growth.
2. Many families left the town for *economic* reasons.
3. Their problems are *economic*, not political.

**2. Making enough money to be worth doing; profitable — mostly in business and energy writing.** (рентабельный, выгодный)

4. The mine closed because it was no longer *economic* to run.
5. At today's prices, it isn't *economic* to repair an old TV.
6. Solar power is getting *more economic* every year.

### economical

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪkəl/ &middot; *ek-uh-NAH-mih-kuhl*
**Русский:** экономичный; экономный, бережливый
**Type:** quality; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an economical car*, *The car is economical*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more economical*, *most economical*
**Takes:** *with* + noun = using little of (*economical with words*); *it's economical to* + verb
**Form:** -al from *economic*
**Opposite:** *uneconomical*, *wasteful*
**Contrast:** See *economic*. *economical* never means *about the economy*: *economic policy*, not *economical policy*; *economic reforms* (экономические реформы), not *economical reforms*. In everyday American speech people often say *cheap to run* or *fuel-efficient* about machines and *thrifty* or *careful with money* about people. *economical with the truth* (не договаривающий, лукавящий) is a polite, originally British way to say someone is lying by leaving things out.

Economical has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Cheap to use or run, because it uses little money, fuel or other resources.** (экономичный, выгодный)

1. Small cars are usually *more economical* to run.
2. Buying rice in bulk is the *most economical* way to shop.
3. The new furnace is quiet and *economical*.

**2. Of a person: careful not to spend or waste money.** (экономный, бережливый)

4. My grandmother was an *economical* cook who wasted nothing.
5. We have to be *economical* until I find a new job.

**3. Using no more words, movements or effort than are needed.** (сжатый, лаконичный; скупой (о движениях))

6. He writes in a clean, *economical* style.
7. The pitcher has a smooth, *economical* delivery.
8. Her essay is short and *economical*: every sentence does a job.

### historic

**Pronunciation:** /hɪˈstɔːrɪk/ &middot; *hih-STOR-ik*
**Русский:** исторический, знаменательный; старинный
**Type:** evaluative; also classifying
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun (*a historic day*); after *be* too (*This moment is historic*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more historic*, *most historic*
**Contrast:** *historic* = important in history, or famous for its past: *a historic victory* (историческая победа), *a historic building*. *historical* = about the past or the study of it: *historical records* (исторические документы), *a historical novel* (исторический роман). Test: if you can say *famous* or *memorable*, use *historic*; if you can say *from the past* or *about the past*, use *historical*. *a historical event* simply happened; *a historic event* changed things. Russian *исторический* covers both, so decide each time. Americans say *a historic* with the h pronounced; *an historic* sounds old-fashioned or British. In *historic lows* and *historic times* the two words overlap, and both are used.

Historic has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Important in history, or likely to be remembered as important.** (исторический, знаменательный)

1. The two leaders signed a *historic* peace agreement.
2. It was a *historic* day for the city when the team finally won.
3. Fans called it the *most historic* game they had ever seen.

**2. Old and interesting because of its past, and worth preserving.** (старинный, исторический (о зданиях, районах))

4. We stayed at a *historic* inn built in 1790.
5. The *historic* district is full of brick row houses.
6. Charleston is famous for its *historic* homes.

**3. Of a level or amount: the highest or lowest ever recorded.** (рекордный)

7. Mortgage rates fell to *historic* lows that spring.
8. Home prices reached *historic* highs last year.

**4. Belonging to the time of written history, as opposed to prehistoric times — mostly in *historic times*.** (исторический (в отличие от доисторического))

9. The lake has never dried up in *historic* times.
10. Only two volcanoes in the region have erupted in *historic* times.

### historical

**Pronunciation:** /hɪˈstɔːrɪkəl/ &middot; *hih-STOR-ih-kuhl*
**Русский:** исторический (относящийся к прошлому)
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both — almost always before a noun (*historical records*); after *be* mainly in the *really existed* sense (*Is the story historical or made up?*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** See *historic*. *historical* is the neutral word for anything connected with the past or with history as a subject; it never means *famous* or *memorable*: *a historic moment* (исторический момент), not *a historical moment*. *historical* before *data*, *prices* or *trends* just means *from past years*. Russian *исторический* covers both words.

Historical has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Relating to history or the study of history; about the past.** (исторический)

1. The museum has a large collection of *historical* documents.
2. She's doing *historical* research on the Civil War.
3. The town's *historical* society runs tours every Saturday.

**2. Of a book, movie or painting: telling a story set in the past.** (исторический (о жанре))

4. My mom loves *historical* novels set in the Old West.
5. The show is a *historical* drama about the first English settlers.

**3. Really having lived or happened in the past, not only in stories.** (реально существовавший, исторически достоверный)

6. Scholars still argue about whether King Arthur was a *historical* figure.
7. The movie mixes *historical* fact with fiction.
8. Is the story *historical*, or did the author make it up?

**4. Based on figures from past years, as opposed to current ones.** (прошлый, за прошлые периоды; ретроспективный)

9. *Historical* data shows that prices rise every December.
10. The model predicts sales from *historical* trends.

### classic

**Pronunciation:** /ˈklæsɪk/ &middot; *KLAS-ik*
**Русский:** классический, образцовый; типичный
**Type:** evaluative; also quality
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun (*a classic mistake*); after *be* in the *style* and informal senses (*Her look is classic*, *That's classic!*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more classic*, *most classic*
**Contrast:** *classic* = excellent and lasting, typical, or traditional in style: *a classic movie*, *a classic mistake* (типичная ошибка), *a classic suit*. *classical* = of ancient Greece and Rome, of serious Western music, or of an old established system: *classical architecture*, *classical music* (классическая музыка), *classical ballet*. Test: if you can say *typical* or *great and lasting*, use *classic*; if you can say *ancient* or *traditional art form*, use *classical*. *classic music* is a mistake for *classical music*, and *a classical example* for *a classic example*. Russian *классический* covers both. *a classic* (a great book or movie) and *the classics* (Greek and Latin literature) are nouns.

Classic has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Of the highest quality and admired for a long time; one of the best of its kind.** (классический, признанный, образцовый)

1. It's a *classic* American novel that every teenager reads.
2. The band played all their *classic* hits.
3. We watched a *classic* Hitchcock movie last night.

**2. Very typical; a perfect example of its kind.** (типичный, хрестоматийный)

4. Forgetting to back up your files is a *classic* mistake.
5. He showed all the *classic* signs of a heart attack.
6. It's a *classic* case of too little, too late.

**3. Simple and traditional in style, so it does not go out of fashion.** (классический (о стиле), строгий)

7. You can't go wrong with a *classic* white shirt.
8. Her style is *more classic* than her sister's.

**4. Of cars, music or TV shows: from an earlier period and still loved today.** (ретро, винтажный, классический)

9. He spends every weekend restoring *classic* cars.
10. The station plays *classic* rock all day.
11. We grew up watching *classic* cartoons on Saturday mornings.

**5. Informal: very funny, or exactly what you would expect of someone.** (в своём репертуаре; ну это классика (разг.))

12. He fell asleep at his own party? That's *classic*.
13. Showing up an hour late is *classic* Mike.

### classical

**Pronunciation:** /ˈklæsɪkəl/ &middot; *KLAS-ih-kuhl*
**Русский:** классический; античный; академический
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both — almost always before a noun (*classical music*); after *be* mostly in the *style* sense (*The lines of the building are classical*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — except in the *style* sense: *more classical*
**Contrast:** See *classic*. *classical music* covers everything from Bach to modern concert music, not only the Classical period of Haydn and Mozart. *classical* never means *typical*: *a classic example*, not *a classical example*. Nor does it mean *old and still loved* about cars or rock: *classic cars*, *classic rock*.

Classical has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Relating to ancient Greece and Rome, their languages, art and ideas.** (античный, классический)

1. She studied *classical* Greek and Latin in college.
2. The Capitol is built in the *classical* style, with tall columns.
3. Many *classical* writers told stories about the gods of Olympus.

**2. Of music: in the serious Western tradition of composers like Bach and Beethoven, not pop, rock or jazz.** (классический (о музыке), академический)

4. He listens to *classical* music while he works.
5. She trained as a *classical* pianist.
6. The *classical* station plays Mozart every morning.

**3. Traditional and long established, as opposed to newer or modern forms.** (классический, традиционный)

7. She trained in *classical* ballet before switching to jazz dance.
8. *Classical* physics can't explain how atoms behave.
9. Modern Arabic grew out of *Classical* Arabic.

**4. Simple, balanced and elegant, like the art of ancient Greece.** (классический, строгий, гармоничный)

10. The actress was known for her *classical* beauty.
11. The new library is *more classical* in design than the old one.

### electric

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈlɛktrɪk/ &middot; *ih-LEK-trik*
**Русский:** электрический; наэлектризованный, волнующий
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both — before a noun or after *be* in the *powered by electricity* sense (*an electric stove*, *Is the stove gas or electric?*); mostly after a verb in the *exciting* sense (*The mood was electric*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — except in the *exciting* sense: *more electric*, *most electric*
**Contrast:** *electric* is for a thing that runs on electricity or carries it: *an electric toothbrush*, *an electric shock*. *electrical* is the general word for electricity as a system, a subject or a kind of fault: *electrical wiring*, *an electrical engineer*, *an electrical fire*. Test: name one machine and use *electric*; name a category, a job or a problem and use *electrical*. Many phrases take only one: *electric guitar*, *electric chair*; *electrical engineering*, *electrical storm*. Only *electric* has the *exciting* sense. Americans say *the electric bill* and *the electric company*, where British English says *the electricity bill*. Neither is *electronic* (электронный: using chips, as in *electronic devices*). Russian *электрик* (the worker) is *electrician*; the color *электрик* is *electric blue*.

Electric has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Working by electricity, or carrying or producing it.** (электрический, электро-)

1. We just bought an *electric* car.
2. The dog got a shock from the *electric* fence.
3. Is your stove gas or *electric*?

**2. Relating to the supply of electricity to homes and businesses.** (за электричество; энергетический (о компании))

4. Our *electric* bill doubled in July.
5. The *electric* company shut off their power.

**3. Full of excitement and energy; thrilling.** (наэлектризованный, волнующий, захватывающий)

6. The atmosphere in the stadium was *electric*.
7. The crowd was even *more electric* in the second half.
8. She gave an *electric* performance on opening night.

**4. Of a color: very bright and strong.** (яркий; (цвет) электрик)

9. She wore an *electric* blue dress to the prom.
10. The walls were painted an *electric* green.

### electrical

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈlɛktrɪkəl/ &middot; *ih-LEK-trih-kuhl*
**Русский:** электрический; электротехнический
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both — almost always before a noun (*electrical wiring*); after *be* to name the kind of fault (*The problem is electrical*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** See *electric*. *electrical* usually names a category (*electrical equipment*) or a field (*electrical engineering*); *electric* names the machine (*an electric heater*). *an electrical heater* is not wrong, but *an electric heater* is what people say, and *electrical engineering* is fixed: never *electric engineering*. *electrical* has no *exciting* sense: *an electric atmosphere*, not *an electrical atmosphere*.

Electrical has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Using, carrying or involving electricity; relating to electricity in general.** (электрический, электро-)

1. Unplug all *electrical* appliances before you leave.
2. An *electrical* fire started in the basement.
3. The problem with the car turned out to be *electrical*.

**2. Relating to the science of electricity or to work with it as a job.** (электротехнический)

4. He has a degree in *electrical* engineering.
5. Hire a licensed *electrical* contractor to move the outlet.

### alternate

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːltərnət/ &middot; *AWL-tur-nuht*
**Русский:** запасной, другой; каждый второй; чередующийся
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — *on alternate days*, *an alternate route*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from the verb *alternate*
**Contrast:** Pronunciation: the adjective is AWL-tur-nuht; the verb *to alternate* is AWL-tur-nayt. Only *alternate* means *every other*: *on alternate days* (через день). Only *alternative* means *unconventional*: *alternative medicine*, *alternative rock*, never *alternate medicine*. For *another choice* American English uses both: *an alternate route* and *an alternative route*; some editors and British readers accept only *alternative*, so prefer it in formal writing. *an alternate* is also a noun: a substitute juror, delegate or player. Russian *альтернативный* is usually *alternative*; *через день* is *every other day* or *on alternate days*.

Alternate has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Available as another choice, in place of the usual or planned one.** (запасной, другой, альтернативный)

1. Take an *alternate* route if the bridge is closed.
2. We need an *alternate* plan in case it rains.
3. The judge replaced the sick juror with an *alternate* juror.

**2. Happening on every second day, week or other unit; every other.** (каждый второй, через один)

4. The class meets on *alternate* Tuesdays.
5. The kids spend *alternate* weekends with their dad.
6. Write your answers on *alternate* lines.

**3. Of two things: coming one after the other, again and again.** (чередующийся, перемежающийся)

7. Arrange *alternate* layers of pasta and cheese in the dish.
8. The flag has *alternate* red and white stripes.

**4. Existing as another version of events or of the world.** (параллельный, альтернативный)

9. The show is set in an *alternate* universe where dinosaurs never died out.
10. She writes *alternate* history novels.

### alternative

**Pronunciation:** /ɔːlˈtɜːrnətɪv/ &middot; *awl-TUR-nuh-tiv*
**Русский:** альтернативный, другой; нетрадиционный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both — almost always before a noun (*an alternative plan*); after a verb only in the informal *unconventional* sense (*Her taste in music is pretty alternative*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — except in the informal *unconventional* sense: *more alternative*
**Contrast:** See *alternate*. The noun *an alternative* (вариант, выбор) is more common than the adjective: *There's no alternative*, *an alternative to surgery*; the *to* belongs to the noun. *alternative* never means *every other*: *on alternate days*, not *on alternative days*. The stress is on the second syllable: awl-TUR-nuh-tiv.

Alternative has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Available as another choice; able to be used instead of something else.** (альтернативный, другой, запасной)

1. If the flight is canceled, the airline will book you on an *alternative* flight.
2. We need to find an *alternative* way to pay for the project.
3. Police posted *alternative* routes around the parade.

**2. Different from what is usual or traditional, and often chosen as a rejection of it.** (альтернативный, нетрадиционный, неформальный)

4. More patients are trying *alternative* medicine like acupuncture.
5. She listened to *alternative* rock in high school.
6. Her taste in music is pretty *alternative*.

**3. Of energy or fuel: not coming from oil, coal or natural gas.** (альтернативный, возобновляемый)

7. The state is investing in *alternative* energy such as wind and solar.
8. City buses run on *alternative* fuels.

### childish

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃaɪldɪʃ/ &middot; *CHYL-dish*
**Русский:** ребяческий, инфантильный; детский
**Type:** quality; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a childish remark*, *Don't be childish*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more childish*, *most childish*
**Form:** -ish from *child* — like a child, in a bad way
**Opposite:** *mature*, *grown-up*
**Contrast:** *childish* criticizes: an older person acting like a small child (ребячество). *childlike* praises or describes: the good qualities of a child, such as trust and wonder (детская непосредственность). Test: *Don't be childish* is a reproach; *childlike wonder* is a compliment. Neither is the word for things made for children: Russian *детские книги* is *children's books*, *детская одежда* is *kids' clothes*, *детская площадка* is *a playground*.

Childish has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of an older person or their behavior: silly and immature, like a small child.** (ребяческий, инфантильный, детский (неодобр.))

1. Stop being so *childish* and apologize to your sister.
2. It was a *childish* thing to do.
3. The argument got *more childish* by the minute.

**2. Typical of a child or of childhood.** (детский)

4. The note was written in big, *childish* handwriting.
5. We could hear *childish* voices coming from the yard.

### childlike

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃaɪldlaɪk/ &middot; *CHYLD-lyk*
**Русский:** по-детски непосредственный; похожий на ребёнка
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *childlike wonder*, *Her voice sounded childlike*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more childlike*, *most childlike*
**Form:** compound — *child* + *-like*
**Contrast:** See *childish*. *childlike* is usually positive or neutral; *childish* is negative. Said about an adult's mind or judgment, *childlike* can sound patronizing (*a childlike understanding of money*), so use it with care.

Childlike has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Having the good qualities of a child, such as innocence, trust and enthusiasm.** (по-детски непосредственный, простодушный, наивный)

1. My grandfather never lost his *childlike* sense of wonder.
2. She has an almost *childlike* trust in people.
3. He was *more childlike* than any of the kids he taught.

**2. Looking, sounding or moving like a child.** (детский, похожий на ребёнка)

4. The robot has a *childlike* face with big round eyes.
5. She spoke in a soft, *childlike* voice.

### continual

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɪnjuəl/ &middot; *kuhn-TIN-yoo-uhl*
**Русский:** постоянный, беспрестанный (с перерывами)
**Type:** quality
**Position:** attributive only — *continual delays*; after *be* it sounds odd, so use *constant*: *The noise was constant*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -al from *continue*
**Contrast:** *continual* = happening again and again, with breaks: *continual phone calls* (звонки один за другим). *continuous* = without any break: *continuous rain* (непрерывный дождь). Test: a dripping faucet is *continual*; a running faucet is *continuous*. Careful writers keep this line; in general use *continual* also covers *without stopping* (sense 2), and *constant* covers both. Only *continuous* works for space (*a continuous line*) and grammar (*the present continuous*). Russian *постоянный* is most often *constant*.

Continual has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Happening again and again, often in an annoying way.** (постоянный, беспрестанный, повторяющийся)

1. I'm tired of your *continual* complaining.
2. The project suffered from *continual* delays.
3. *Continual* interruptions made it impossible to work.

**2. Going on for a long time without stopping.** (непрерывный, непрекращающийся)

4. He lived in *continual* pain after the accident.
5. The city was under *continual* attack for three months.

### continuous

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɪnjuəs/ &middot; *kuhn-TIN-yoo-uhs*
**Русский:** непрерывный, сплошной
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *continuous rain*, *The noise was continuous*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ous from *continue*
**Opposite:** *intermittent*; *broken*
**Contrast:** See *continual*. *continuous* never means *repeated with breaks*: *continual interruptions*, not *continuous interruptions*, unless there was no pause at all. In grammar, American textbooks usually say *progressive* (*the present progressive*) where British ones say *continuous*; both mean the *be* + *-ing* form.

Continuous has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Going on without a pause or break.** (непрерывный, беспрерывный)

1. Patients in the ICU need *continuous* monitoring.
2. The noise from the highway is *continuous*, day and night.
3. The company is committed to *continuous* improvement.

**2. Without gaps in space; forming an unbroken line or surface.** (сплошной, непрерывный)

4. Draw one *continuous* line without lifting your pen.
5. A *continuous* fence runs along the highway.

**3. In grammar: formed with *be* + *-ing* to show an action in progress; progressive.** (продолженный (о времени))

6. *I am reading* is in the present *continuous*.
7. The past *continuous* describes what was happening at a moment in the past.

### efficient

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈfɪʃənt/ &middot; *ih-FISH-uhnt*
**Русский:** эффективный (без потерь), экономичный; расторопный
**Type:** quality; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an efficient system*, *The system is efficient*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more efficient*, *most efficient*
**Form:** also in compounds: *energy-efficient*, *fuel-efficient*, *cost-efficient*
**Opposite:** *inefficient*
**Contrast:** *efficient* = doing something without wasting time, money or energy; *effective* = getting the result you want. Test: a medicine that cures you is *effective*; a factory that makes more cars with less power is *efficient*. A thing can be one without the other: a sledgehammer is an *effective* way to crack a nut, but not an *efficient* one. Russian *эффективный* covers both, so ask whether you mean *it works* (*effective*) or *it works without waste* (*efficient*).

Efficient has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Working well without wasting time, money or energy.** (эффективный (без потерь), экономичный, производительный)

1. LED bulbs are much *more efficient* than old light bulbs.
2. We need an *efficient* way to handle all these orders.
3. The new trains are fast and *efficient*.

**2. Of a person: organized and able to get work done quickly and well.** (расторопный, толковый, деловитый)

4. Our office manager is incredibly *efficient*.
5. An *efficient* waiter kept our glasses full all night.

### effective

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈfɛktɪv/ &middot; *ih-FEK-tiv*

6. The general was the *effective* ruler of the country.
7. Rebels are in *effective* control of the north.
8. After deductions, their *effective* tax rate was 12 percent.

**4. Officially in use or in force, starting from a stated time.** (вступающий в силу, действующий)

9. The new rules are *effective* as of January 1.
10. She resigned, *effective* immediately.
