# More adjectives: A

> Adjectives A beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### a cappella

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː kəˈpɛlə/ &middot; *ah kuh-PEL-uh*
**Русский:** а капелла, без музыкального сопровождения
**Type:** classifying — about singing
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *an a cappella group*; after a verb: *the whole album is a cappella*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from Italian *a cappella*, 'in the style of the chapel'
**Contrast:** In *They sang a cappella* the phrase is an adverb, the most common use; the adjective is the one before a noun or after *be*.

Sung by voices alone, without musical instruments.

1. She joined an *a cappella* group in college.
2. The last song on the album is *a cappella*.
3. They gave an *a cappella* performance of the national anthem.

### a la carte

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː lə ˈkɑːrt/ &middot; *ah luh KAHRT*
**Русский:** а-ля карт, по меню, отдельно
**Type:** classifying — how things are priced and sold
**Position:** both — *an a la carte menu*; *everything here is a la carte*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from French *à la carte*, 'by the menu'; Americans usually write it without the accent
**Contrast:** In *You can order a la carte* the phrase is an adverb. Outside restaurants it means 'chosen one by one': *a la carte cable channels*.

Chosen and paid for item by item, not as part of a set meal or package.

1. The *a la carte* menu is more expensive than the lunch special.
2. At this steakhouse the side dishes are *a la carte*.
3. More people want *a la carte* streaming plans instead of big bundles.

### a la mode

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː lə ˈmoʊd/ &middot; *ah luh MOHD*
**Русский:** с шариком мороженого
**Type:** classifying — about desserts
**Position:** after the noun — *pie a la mode*, never *a la mode pie*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from French *à la mode*, 'in the fashion'
**Contrast:** A trap for anyone who knows French: in American English it almost always means 'served with ice cream', not 'fashionable'.

Served with a scoop of ice cream on top or on the side.

1. I'll have the apple pie *a la mode*, please.
2. The diner is famous for its peach cobbler *a la mode*.
3. Do you want your brownie *a la mode*?

### a priori

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː priˈɔːri/ &middot; *ah pree-OR-ee*
**Русский:** априорный
**Type:** classifying — about reasoning
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *a priori knowledge*; after a verb: *the claim is a priori*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** Latin, 'from what comes before'
**Opposite:** *a posteriori*; *empirical*

Based on reasoning or assumptions rather than on observation or experience.

1. Philosophers argue about whether math is *a priori* knowledge.
2. There's no *a priori* reason to think the new drug won't work.
3. His argument rests on a few *a priori* assumptions about human nature.

### A-OK

**Pronunciation:** /ˌeɪ oʊˈkeɪ/ &middot; *ay oh-KAY*
**Русский:** в полном порядке, отличный (разг.)
**Type:** evaluative — informal
**Position:** both — mostly after a verb: *everything is A-OK*; before a noun: *an A-OK job*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** *A* + *OK*, first used in the US space program

Completely fine; in perfect order.

1. The doctor said I'm *A-OK* to go back to work.
2. Don't worry, the car is *A-OK* now.
3. She gave us the *A-OK* sign from the stage.

### abandoned

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbændənd/ &middot; *uh-BAN-duhnd*
**Русский:** заброшенный; брошенный
**Type:** participial; also quality
**Position:** both — *an abandoned house*; *the factory has been abandoned for years*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *abandon*

Abandoned has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Left empty and no longer used or looked after.** (заброшенный, покинутый)

1. Kids used to play in the *abandoned* warehouse by the river.
2. Police found an *abandoned* car on the side of the highway.
3. The old mining town looks completely *abandoned*.

**2. Left alone by the people who should take care of it.** (брошенный (о ребенке, животном))

4. The shelter takes in *abandoned* dogs and cats.
5. He felt *abandoned* when his father moved away.

### abashed

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbæʃt/ &middot; *uh-BASHT*
**Русский:** смущенный, сконфуженный
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — mostly after a verb: *she looked abashed*; also *an abashed smile*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abashed*, *most abashed*
**Form:** -ed from the old verb *abash*
**Opposite:** *unabashed*
**Contrast:** The opposite *unabashed* (*an unabashed fan*) is more common today than *abashed* itself.

Embarrassed or ashamed, especially after being caught or corrected.

1. He looked a little *abashed* when the teacher read his note out loud.
2. She was not the least bit *abashed* by the criticism.
3. The boys stood there, *abashed*, while their mother cleaned up the mess.

### abbreviated

**Русский:** сокращенный
**Type:** participial; also quality
**Position:** both — *an abbreviated version*; *the name is often abbreviated*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *abbreviate*, pronounced /ɪd/
**Opposite:** *full*; *complete*

Made shorter than the full form or the usual length.

1. "Dept." is the *abbreviated* form of "department."
2. Because of the storm, the game was played in an *abbreviated* season.
3. We only had time for an *abbreviated* meeting.

### abdominal

**Pronunciation:** /æbˈdɑːmənəl/ &middot; *ab-DAH-muh-nuhl*
**Русский:** брюшной, абдоминальный
**Type:** classifying — part of the body
**Position:** attributive only — *abdominal pain*; after a verb say *in the abdomen*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -al from *abdomen*
**Contrast:** The plural noun *abdominals* (or *abs*) means the stomach muscles: *work on your abs*.

In or relating to the stomach area of the body.

1. He went to the ER with severe *abdominal* pain.
2. These exercises strengthen your *abdominal* muscles.
3. The doctor ordered an *abdominal* ultrasound.

### aberrant

**Pronunciation:** /æˈbɛrənt/ &middot; *a-BER-uhnt*
**Русский:** отклоняющийся от нормы, аномальный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *aberrant behavior*; *the result was aberrant*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more aberrant*, *most aberrant*
**Opposite:** *normal*; *typical*

Different from what is normal or expected, usually in a bad way.

1. Scientists threw out one *aberrant* result from the study.
2. His *aberrant* behavior worried his teachers.
3. One cold week in July is not that *aberrant* for this city.

### abhorrent

**Pronunciation:** /əbˈhɔːrənt/ &middot; *uhb-HOR-uhnt*
**Русский:** отвратительный, омерзительный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — *an abhorrent practice*; *the idea is abhorrent to me*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abhorrent*, *most abhorrent*
**Takes:** *to* + person: *abhorrent to most voters*
**Form:** -ent from *abhor*

Morally unacceptable and causing strong disgust.

1. Racism is *abhorrent* to everyone in this room.
2. The report described *abhorrent* conditions at the prison.
3. Of all his crimes, lying to the families was the most *abhorrent*.

### abiding

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbaɪdɪŋ/ &middot; *uh-BY-ding*
**Русский:** неизменный, прочный, постоянный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** attributive only — *an abiding love*; after a verb say *lasting*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ing from *abide* (to last)
**Contrast:** *Law-abiding* (*a law-abiding citizen*) comes from *abide by* 'obey' and has a different meaning.

Lasting for a long time and not changing.

1. She has an *abiding* love of the ocean.
2. His *abiding* interest in history started with his grandfather's stories.
3. Our *abiding* memory of that trip is the kindness of strangers.

### abject

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæbdʒɛkt/ &middot; *AB-jekt*
**Русский:** крайний, жалкий; униженный
**Type:** emphasizing; also evaluative
**Position:** attributive only — *abject poverty*, *an abject apology*
**Comparison:** not gradable

Abject has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Extremely bad and without hope, used about poverty, misery or failure.** (крайний, беспросветный)

1. Millions of people still live in *abject* poverty.
2. The launch was an *abject* failure.

**2. Showing no pride or self-respect.** (униженный, жалкий)

3. He made an *abject* apology to the whole team.
4. She looked at him with *abject* fear.

### able-bodied

**Русский:** здоровый, трудоспособный
**Type:** compound; also quality
**Position:** both — *able-bodied adults*; *anyone who is able-bodied can help*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — *able* + *bodied*
**Opposite:** *disabled*

Physically strong and healthy, without a disability.

1. The program requires *able-bodied* adults to work or look for work.
2. We need every *able-bodied* volunteer to fill sandbags.
3. Some of the buildings were designed only for people who are *able-bodied*.

### abnormal

**Pronunciation:** /æbˈnɔːrməl/ &middot; *ab-NOR-muhl*
**Русский:** ненормальный, аномальный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *abnormal results*; *his heart rate was abnormal*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abnormal*, *most abnormal*
**Form:** ab- + *normal*
**Opposite:** *normal*
**Contrast:** *Abnormal* is formal and often medical. For a strange person in everyday speech Americans say *weird* or *strange*; calling someone *abnormal* sounds insulting.

Different from what is usual or expected, often in a way that suggests a problem.

1. The blood test showed some *abnormal* results.
2. It's not *abnormal* for toddlers to throw tantrums.
3. This has been the *most abnormal* winter anyone can remember.

Extremely bad, unpleasant or wrong.

### aboriginal

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæbəˈrɪdʒənəl/ &middot; *ab-uh-RIJ-uh-nuhl*
**Русский:** коренной, исконный; аборигенный
**Type:** origin; also classifying
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun: *aboriginal peoples*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -al from *aborigine*
**Contrast:** For Australia it is capitalized: *Aboriginal art*. About Native Americans, Americans say *Native American* or *Indigenous*, not *aboriginal*.

Aboriginal has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Living or existing in a place from the earliest times, before anyone arrived from elsewhere.** (коренной, исконный)

1. Canada recognizes the rights of its *aboriginal* peoples.
2. The island still has patches of *aboriginal* forest.

**2. Relating to the first peoples of Australia; written with a capital A.** (относящийся к аборигенам Австралии)

3. The museum has a large collection of *Aboriginal* art.
4. She studies *Aboriginal* languages of northern Australia.

### abortive

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbɔːrtɪv/ &middot; *uh-BOR-tiv*
**Русский:** неудавшийся, безуспешный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** attributive only — *an abortive attempt*; after a verb say *failed* or *unsuccessful*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ive from *abort* (to stop before finishing)
**Contrast:** Despite the look of the word, it has nothing to do with medical abortion in everyday use; it means a plan or attempt that failed.

Failing before it could be completed.

1. After an *abortive* attempt to fix the sink, he called a plumber.
2. The general was jailed after an *abortive* coup.
3. Their *abortive* peace talks ended after two days.

### above-mentioned

**Pronunciation:** /əˌbʌvˈmɛnʃənd/ &middot; *uh-buhv-MEN-shuhnd*
**Русский:** вышеупомянутый
**Type:** compound; also classifying
**Position:** attributive only — *the above-mentioned document*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — *above* + *mentioned*
**Contrast:** Formal, used in letters, contracts and reports. *The above-mentioned* can also stand alone as a noun: *the above-mentioned will attend*.

Mentioned earlier in the same document or text.

1. Please send the *above-mentioned* forms by Friday.
2. The *above-mentioned* tenant has not paid rent since March.
3. None of the *above-mentioned* problems have been fixed.

### aboveboard

**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — *above* + *board*, from card players keeping their hands above the table

Honest and legal, with nothing hidden.

1. I checked, and the whole sale was completely *aboveboard*.
2. He promised the investors that everything was *aboveboard*.
3. We want an *aboveboard* process that the public can trust.

### abrasive

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbreɪsɪv/ &middot; *uh-BRAY-siv*
**Русский:** абразивный; резкий, грубый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *an abrasive cleaner*; *her manner can be abrasive*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abrasive*, *most abrasive*
**Form:** -ive from *abrade*

Abrasive has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Rough enough to scratch or rub a surface clean.** (абразивный, шлифующий, царапающий)

1. Don't use an *abrasive* sponge on nonstick pans.
2. Sandpaper is *abrasive* by design.

**2. Rude and harsh in a way that annoys people.** (резкий, грубый (о человеке, манере))

3. The new manager's *abrasive* style upset the whole office.
4. He's smart, but he can be *more abrasive* than he realizes.

**Opposite:** *unabridged*

### abrupt

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbrʌpt/ &middot; *uh-BRUHPT*
**Русский:** внезапный; резкий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *an abrupt stop*; *his answer was abrupt*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abrupt*, *most abrupt*

Abrupt has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Sudden and unexpected.** (внезапный, неожиданный)

1. The bus came to an *abrupt* stop.
2. Her career came to an *abrupt* end after the scandal.
3. The change in weather was *more abrupt* than forecasters expected.

**2. Speaking or acting in a short way that seems rude or unfriendly.** (резкий, отрывистый, грубоватый)

4. Sorry if I was *abrupt* on the phone; I was in a hurry.
5. He gave an *abrupt* answer and walked away.

### absentminded

**Русский:** рассеянный, забывчивый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more absentminded*, *most absentminded*
**Form:** compound — *absent* + *mind* + *-ed*; also written *absent-minded*
**Opposite:** *attentive*

Forgetful because your thoughts are somewhere else.

1. My *absentminded* uncle left his keys in the front door again.
2. I'm getting more *absentminded* every year.

### absolute

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæbsəluːt/ &middot; *AB-suh-loot*
**Русский:** абсолютный, полный; неограниченный
**Type:** emphasizing; also classifying
**Position:** both — the 'complete' sense only before a noun: *an absolute mess*; the others also after a verb: *his power was absolute*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Opposite:** *relative*; *limited*
**Contrast:** The adverb *absolutely* is far more common in speech: *Absolutely!* means 'yes, definitely'.

Absolute has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Complete and total, used to make a noun stronger.** (полный, совершенный, абсолютный)

1. The house was an *absolute* mess after the party.
2. That's *absolute* nonsense, and you know it.
3. We waited in *absolute* silence.

**2. Not limited or controlled in any way.** (неограниченный, абсолютный (о власти))

4. The king had *absolute* power over his people.
5. Freedom of speech is important, but it is not *absolute*.

**3. Measured on its own, not in comparison with something else.** (абсолютный (не относительный))

6. In *absolute* terms, the company earned more this year.
7. The *absolute* value of -5 is 5.

### absolutist

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæbsəˈluːtɪst/ &middot; *ab-suh-LOO-tist*
**Русский:** абсолютистский; бескомпромиссный
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both — *an absolutist regime*; *her views on free speech are absolutist*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more absolutist*, *most absolutist*
**Form:** -ist from *absolute*
**Contrast:** Also a noun: *He's a free-speech absolutist*.

Absolutist has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Believing that a ruler or government should have unlimited power.** (абсолютистский)

1. The country was under *absolutist* rule for two centuries.
2. Historians compare the two *absolutist* monarchies.

**2. Holding a principle so firmly that no exceptions are allowed.** (бескомпромиссный, не допускающий исключений)

3. She takes an *absolutist* position on privacy.
4. His views became *more absolutist* as he got older.

Able to soak up liquid easily.

### absorbing

**Pronunciation:** /əbˈzɔːrbɪŋ/ &middot; *uhb-ZOR-bing*

So interesting that it holds all your attention.

### abstract

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæbstrækt/ &middot; *AB-strakt*
**Русский:** абстрактный, отвлеченный
**Type:** quality; also classifying
**Position:** both — *an abstract idea*; *the question is too abstract*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more abstract*, *most abstract*
**Opposite:** *concrete*; for art, *representational*
**Contrast:** Many Americans also stress the adjective ab-STRAKT; the verb *abstract* is always ab-STRAKT. The noun *abstract* is a summary of a scientific paper.

Abstract has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Based on ideas rather than on real, physical things or specific examples.** (абстрактный, отвлеченный)

1. Justice and freedom are *abstract* concepts.
2. Can you give me an example? This is all a bit *abstract*.
3. Algebra felt *more abstract* to me than geometry.

**2. In art, using shapes and colors instead of showing people or objects as they look.** (абстрактный (в искусстве))

4. The museum's second floor is all *abstract* art.
5. Her later paintings became completely *abstract*.

### abusive

**Type:** classifying; also quality

**3. Not especially good; ordinary.** (посредственный, заурядный)

6. Honestly, the food was pretty *average*.
