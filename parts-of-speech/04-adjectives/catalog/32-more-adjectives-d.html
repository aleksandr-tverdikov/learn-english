More adjectives: D#

Adjectives D beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after be, and they compare with -er or more. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

damn#

Pronunciation
/dæm/ · DAM
Русский

Type

Contrast
A mild swear word: fine among friends, not in a job interview. Damn is also an adverb (damn good), an interjection (Damn!) and a noun (I don't give a damn). Damned means the same before a noun; darn is the polite version.

Used before a noun to show anger or annoyance, or just for emphasis.

  1. The damn car won't start again.
  2. Turn off that damn TV!
  3. It's a damn shame they closed the old diner.

Stronger than darned, about as strong as damn. The damned (noun) are the souls in hell. I'll be damned! shows surprise; I'll be damned if I pay that means I refuse to. Damned good is the adverb.

Damned has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used before a noun to show anger or annoyance, or just for emphasis. (чертов, проклятый)

  1. The damned printer is jammed again.
  2. I can't find my damned phone.
  3. It was the damnedest thing — the dog opened the door by itself.

2. Condemned to hell, or certain to fail whatever you do. (обреченный, проклятый)

  1. They believed the souls of sinners were damned forever.
  2. You're damned if you do and damned if you don't.
