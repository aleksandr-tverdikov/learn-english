# More adjectives: G–H

> Adjectives G–H beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### gabby

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæbi/ &middot; *GAB-ee*
**Русский:** болтливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *gabbier*, *gabbiest*
**Form:** from the verb *gab* — to chatter
**Opposite:** *quiet*
**Contrast:** Informal and slightly critical.

Talking a lot, especially about nothing important.

1. Our *gabby* neighbor kept us on the porch for an hour.
2. He gets *gabbier* after a beer or two.
3. The show has a *gabby* host who never lets a guest finish.

### gaga

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɑːɡɑː/ &middot; *GAH-gah*
**Русский:** без ума (от кого-л.); выживший из ума
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only
**Comparison:** more/most — *more gaga*, *most gaga*
**Takes:** *over* or *about* + noun
**Contrast:** Informal, and only after a verb — *go gaga*, *be gaga over someone*. Never before a noun.

Gaga has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Wildly excited about someone or something.** (без ума, в восторге)

1. Everyone at work went *gaga* over the new baby.
2. Fans are still *gaga* about the band's first album.

**2. Confused or senile — informal, and rude about an older person.** (впавший в маразм)

3. He jokes that he is going *gaga* in his old age.
4. Grandpa isn't *gaga* — he just can't hear you.

### galactic

**Pronunciation:** /ɡəˈlæktɪk/ &middot; *guh-LAK-tik*
**Русский:** галактический; колоссальный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** from *galaxy*

Galactic has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of a galaxy or the space between stars.** (галактический)

1. The telescope maps *galactic* dust.
2. *Galactic* distances are measured in light-years.

**2. Enormous — informal, usually about a mistake or a scale.** (громадный, грандиозный)

3. The project failed on a *galactic* scale.
4. Sending that email was a *galactic* blunder.

### gallant

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡælənt/ &middot; *GAL-uhnt*
**Русский:** доблестный; галантный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more gallant*, *most gallant*
**Contrast:** A false friend to watch: Russian *галантный* covers only the second sense. The first sense — brave — is the commoner one in English, and often describes a brave loss.

Gallant has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Brave, especially in a fight that is being lost.** (доблестный, отважный)

1. They put up a *gallant* fight.
2. It was a *gallant* effort, but they lost by two.
3. No army was *more gallant* in defeat.

**2. Of a man: polite and attentive toward women.** (галантный)

4. He was *gallant* enough to carry her bags.
5. A *gallant* older gentleman held the door.

### galling

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɔːlɪŋ/ &middot; *GAW-ling*
**Русский:** раздражающий, унизительный
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — often after *it is*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more galling*, *most galling*

Annoying and humiliating at the same time.

### galvanized

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡælvənaɪzd/ &middot; *GAL-vuh-nyzd*
**Русский:** оцинкованный; взбудораженный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable — but the 'stirred to action' sense takes *more/most*

Galvanized has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of metal: covered with zinc so that it will not rust.** (оцинкованный)

1. The roof is *galvanized* steel.
2. Use *galvanized* nails outdoors.

**2. Shocked into sudden action.** (взбудораженный, встряхнутый)

3. The town was *galvanized* by the news.
4. Voters seem *galvanized* this year.

### game

**Pronunciation:** /ɡeɪm/ &middot; *GAYM*
**Русский:** готовый, не прочь (что-л. сделать)
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — mostly
**Comparison:** more/most — *more game*, *most game*
**Takes:** *for* + noun; *to*-infinitive
**Contrast:** Don't confuse the adjective with the noun *game*. *a game player* is read as somebody who plays games, so put the adjective after a verb: *He's game*.

Willing to try something, especially something new or risky.

1. I'm *game* if you are.
2. She's always *game* for a road trip.
3. He was *game* to try anything once.

### general

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɛnərəl/ &middot; *JEN-ur-uhl*
**Русский:** общий, всеобщий; в общих чертах; главный
**Type:** classifying; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more general*, *most general*
**Contrast:** Also a noun — an army rank (*General Lee*). The phrase *in general* and the adverb *generally* carry the first sense.

General has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Affecting most people or things, and not limited to a few.** (общий, всеобщий)

1. There's a *general* feeling that things are improving.
2. The *general* public was never told.
3. The strike caused *general* disruption downtown.

**2. Broad rather than detailed.** (общий, в общих чертах)

4. Give me the *general* idea first.
5. Her answer was too *general* to be useful.
6. Let's start with something *more general* and narrow it down.

**3. Highest in rank, or covering all areas of a job.** (генеральный, главный)

7. She's the company's *general* counsel.
8. The *general* manager made the call.

**Русский:** отличный; большой; великий
**Type:** evaluative; also size
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *greater*, *greatest*
**Takes:** *at* + noun or *-ing* (sense 4); *with* + people or things (sense 4); *for* + who or what it helps
**Opposite:** *terrible* (sense 1)
**Contrast:** In everyday speech *a great house* is a wonderful house, not a big one — use *big* or *large* for size. *Great* for size is mostly formal or fixed (*the Great Lakes*, *a great number*). Russian *великий* matches only sense 3.

Great has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Very good; excellent.** (отличный, замечательный)

1. We had a *great* time at the beach.
2. You look *great* in that jacket!
3. This is the *greatest* pizza in the city.

**2. Large in amount, degree or size.** (большой, огромный, сильный)

4. She spoke with *great* care.
5. A *great* number of people lost their homes.
6. The risk is *greater* than we thought.

**3. Important, famous and admired.** (великий, выдающийся)

7. Lincoln was one of the *great* presidents.
8. Many people call him the *greatest* boxer of all time.

**4. Very skilled at something, or very good for someone.** (умеющий хорошо, прекрасно подходящий)

9. She's *great* at math.
10. He's *great* with kids.
11. This park is *great* for families.

**Русский:** венгерский
**Type:** origin
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ian from *Hungary*
**Contrast:** Always a capital letter. *a Hungarian* is a person and *Hungarian* is the language — both nouns.

From or relating to Hungary, its people or its language.

1. She makes a great *Hungarian* goulash.
2. My grandparents were *Hungarian*.
3. *Hungarian* grammar is famously difficult.
