False friends for Russian speakers#

actual, sympathetic, accurate, intelligent — English adjectives that look Russian and mean something else.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

These look like Russian words and mean something else. Actual is not актуальный — it means real — and sympathetic is not симпатичный, it means understanding. Accurate means precise, not аккуратный; intelligent means clever, not интеллигентный. Each entry puts the trap first and gives the English word that does mean what the Russian one does.

actual#

Pronunciation
/ˈæktʃuəl/ · AK-choo-uhl
Русский
действительный, фактический
Type
classifying; also emphasizing (the actual ceremony)
Position
attributive only — the actual cost; not the cost was actual
Comparison
not gradable
Form
-al from act; the adverb actually means на самом деле
Contrast
Not актуальный. actual = real, the one that exists or happened, as opposed to what was planned, expected or imagined (действительный, фактический). For актуальный say relevant, current, timely or pressing: актуальная тема = a timely (relevant) topic, актуальная информация = up-to-date information, это уже не актуально = that's no longer relevant. The adverb actually is на самом деле, вообще-то — never актуально. In actual fact sounds British and formal; Americans say in fact or actually. actual goes only before a noun.

Actual has 2 distinct senses.

1. Real; existing or happening in fact, as opposed to what was planned, expected or imagined. (действительный, фактический, реальный)

  1. The actual cost of the repairs was almost twice the estimate.
  2. Is that based on an actual study, or did you just read it online?
  3. The movie is loosely based on actual events.

2. The thing itself — the exact or main one, as opposed to what comes before it or around it. (сам, самый; настоящий)

  1. The reception lasted all night, but the actual ceremony took twenty minutes.
  2. Those were his actual words — I'm not making this up.
  3. We drove around for an hour before we found the actual entrance.

sympathetic#

Pronunciation
/ˌsɪmpəˈθɛtɪk/ · sim-puh-THET-ik
Русский
сочувствующий, отзывчивый
Type
emotion; also evaluative (a sympathetic character)
Position
both — the nervous-system sense is attributive only: the sympathetic nervous system
Comparison
more/most — more sympathetic, most sympathetic
Takes
to or toward + a person or their trouble (понимающий); to or toward + an idea or cause (поддерживающий); about + a problem
Preposition patterns
sympathetic to
Form
-etic from sympathy
Opposite
unsympathetic
Contrast
Not симпатичный. sympathetic = understanding and kind about someone's problems (сочувствующий, отзывчивый), or in favor of an idea. He's very sympathetic does not mean he is good-looking or nice to be around. For симпатичный say cute, attractive or good-looking (о внешности) and nice or likable (о человеке). What follows to changes the meaning: sympathetic to her (сочувствует ей) but sympathetic to the plan (одобряет план). In a movie or novel, a sympathetic character is one the audience likes and cares about — the one place where it comes close to симпатичный.

Sympathetic has 4 distinct senses.

1. Showing that you understand and care about someone's problems or pain. (сочувствующий, отзывчивый, участливый)

  1. My boss was really sympathetic when my dad got sick.
  2. She gave me a sympathetic smile.
  3. The nurses couldn't have been more sympathetic.

2. Agreeing with or supporting an idea, a group or a cause. (благосклонный, поддерживающий)

  1. Several senators are sympathetic to the proposal.
  2. The judge seemed sympathetic toward the union's side.

3. Of a character in a story, movie or show: easy to like, so that you care what happens to them. (вызывающий симпатию, располагающий)

  1. The writers made the villain surprisingly sympathetic.
  2. It's hard to write a sympathetic hero who lies to everyone.

4. In medicine: relating to the part of the nervous system that gets the body ready for action. (симпатический)

  1. Stress switches on the sympathetic nervous system.
  2. The drug blocks sympathetic nerve signals to the heart.

accurate#

Pronunciation
/ˈækjərət/ · AK-yur-uht
Русский
точный, верный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more accurate, most accurate
Form
from Latin accuratus 'done with care' — English kept 'exact', Russian kept 'neat'
Opposite
inaccurate
Contrast
Not аккуратный. accurate = correct and exact, without mistakes (точный, верный). For аккуратный say neat or tidy (о вещах, почерке, внешности) and careful (о работе и человеке): аккуратный почерк = neat handwriting, аккуратный водитель = a careful driver, он очень аккуратный = he's very neat. She's very accurate would mean her answers or her aim are exact, not that she keeps things tidy. The noun is accuracy (точность).

Accurate has 2 distinct senses.

1. Correct and true in every detail; free from mistakes. (точный, верный, правильный)

  1. Is this map still accurate?
  2. The article gives an accurate picture of life in a small town.
  3. The new tests are much more accurate than the old ones.

2. Able to hit a target or to measure something exactly. (точный, меткий)

  1. He's the most accurate passer in the league.
  2. My kitchen scale isn't very accurate.
  3. You need an accurate throw to get the runner out at first.

decent#

Pronunciation
/ˈdiːsənt/ · DEE-suhnt
Русский
приличный, порядочный
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both — the 'dressed' sense is predicative only: Are you decent?
Comparison
more/most — more decent, most decent
Takes
of + person + to-infinitive (decent of you to call); to + person (He's been decent to me)
Opposite
indecent (only for the 'proper' sense — непристойный)
Contrast
decent is praise with a limit. The food was decent means неплохая, вполне нормальная — acceptable, not excellent; for отличная say great or excellent. A decent salary is one you can live on, not a big one. Said of a person, decent is порядочный — honest and kind — not достойный in the sense of 'distinguished'. The opposite indecent is only непристойный: a mediocre meal is not indecent. Do not mix up decent (DEE-suhnt) with the noun descent (dih-SENT, спуск, происхождение).

Decent has 4 distinct senses.

1. Good enough; of an acceptable or fairly good quality, size or amount. (приличный, неплохой, нормальный)

  1. Is there a decent coffee shop around here?
  2. He makes a decent living as a plumber.
  3. The seats were pretty decent for the price.

2. Honest, fair and kind; morally good. (порядочный, честный, хороший)

  1. Her father is a decent, hardworking man.
  2. It was decent of you to drive me all the way home.
  3. He's the most decent guy I've ever worked for.

3. Proper and socially acceptable; not likely to shock or offend. (приличный, пристойный)

  1. He was told to wear something decent to court.
  2. Wait until a decent hour to call them — don't wake them up at six.

4. Informal: dressed enough to be seen by other people. (одетый, в приличном виде)

  1. Can I come in? Are you decent?
  2. Give me a minute — I'm not decent yet.

intelligent#

Pronunciation
/ɪnˈtɛlɪdʒənt/ · in-TEL-ih-juhnt
Русский
умный, сообразительный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more intelligent, most intelligent
Form
noun intelligence
Opposite
unintelligent, stupid
Contrast
Not интеллигентный. intelligent = smart, quick to learn and understand (умный). It says nothing about manners, education or culture — a rude, uneducated person can be very intelligent. For интеллигентный say cultured, well-educated, refined or well-mannered; интеллигенция is the intelligentsia or educated people. intellectual is someone who lives by ideas and books (интеллектуал). In everyday American speech smart is more common than intelligent.

Intelligent has 4 distinct senses.

1. Good at learning, understanding and thinking; clever. (умный, сообразительный)

  1. She's one of the most intelligent students I've ever taught.
  2. Dolphins are highly intelligent animals.
  3. He's intelligent but lazy.

2. Of a question, choice or remark: showing good thinking or judgment. (разумный, толковый, умный)

  1. That's a very intelligent question.
  2. Selling the house before prices dropped was an intelligent move.

3. Able to think and understand, as humans can. (разумный)

  1. Are we the only intelligent species in the universe?
  2. Scientists are still looking for signs of intelligent life in space.

4. Of a machine or system: using a computer to react to conditions and make simple decisions on its own. (интеллектуальный, «умный»)

  1. The car has an intelligent braking system that stops it before a crash.
  2. Intelligent thermostats learn when you're usually home.

sensible#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɛnsəbəl/ · SEN-suh-buhl
Русский
разумный, благоразумный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more sensible, most sensible
Takes
It is sensible + to-infinitive; of + person + to-infinitive (sensible of you to wait)
Form
-ible from sense 'good judgment'
Opposite
foolish, silly
Contrast
sensible is not sensitive. sensible = reasonable and practical, showing good sense (разумный, благоразумный); чувствительный is sensitive: sensitive skin, a sensitive child. He's very sensible means he makes wise, practical choices, not that he is easily hurt. The nouns split the same way: sense (здравый смысл) goes with sensible, sensitivity with sensitive; sensibility is taste and feeling (an artistic sensibility). In old or very formal writing, sensible of means aware of (sensible of the honor); do not use it in everyday English.

Sensible has 2 distinct senses.

1. Showing good judgment; reasonable and practical. (разумный, благоразумный, здравый)

  1. Wearing a helmet is the sensible thing to do.
  2. She's a sensible kid — she won't do anything stupid.
  3. It would be more sensible to wait until spring.

2. Of clothes and shoes: practical and comfortable rather than fashionable. (практичный, удобный)

  1. Wear sensible shoes — we'll be walking all day.
  2. She traded her heels for a pair of sensible flats.

ambitious#

Pronunciation
/æmˈbɪʃəs/ · am-BISH-uhs
Русский
целеустремлённый, честолюбивый; масштабный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more ambitious, most ambitious
Takes
for + person (ambitious for her son) or thing (ambitious for power); to-infinitive (ambitious to succeed)
Form
-ous from ambition
Opposite
unambitious; modest (a plan)
Contrast
Russian амбициозный often sounds negative — proud, touchy, full of oneself — and с амбициями can mean vain. English ambitious is usually positive or neutral: it means wanting to achieve a lot (целеустремлённый). For the negative Russian sense say arrogant, conceited, full of himself or self-important. An ambitious plan is about size, not vanity — it is big and hard to carry out; too ambitious or overly ambitious means it probably can't be done.

Ambitious has 2 distinct senses.

1. Having a strong wish to succeed, to become important, or to get rich or powerful. (целеустремлённый, честолюбивый)

  1. She's smart and ambitious, and she wants to run her own company someday.
  2. Ambitious young lawyers often work eighty hours a week.
  3. His parents are very ambitious for him.

2. Of a plan, goal or project: large and needing a lot of effort, money or skill to succeed. (масштабный, смелый, грандиозный)

  1. The city has an ambitious plan to plant a million trees.
  2. Finishing the whole house in a month was probably too ambitious.
  3. This is the band's most ambitious album yet.

pathetic#

Pronunciation
/pəˈθɛtɪk/ · puh-THET-ik
Русский
жалкий
Type
evaluative; also emotion
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more pathetic, most pathetic
Form
-etic from Greek pathos 'suffering'
Contrast
Not патетический and not пафосный. pathetic = pitiful (жалкий), and in everyday American speech it is usually an insult: That's pathetic! = Жалкое зрелище! Позорище! For патетический (passionate, lofty, solemn) say impassioned, passionate or solemn: патетическая речь = an impassioned speech. For пафосный (showy, pretentious) say pompous, pretentious or over the top. Calling a speech pathetic says it was terrible, not emotional.

Pathetic has 2 distinct senses.

1. Informal: so bad, weak or useless that it makes you feel contempt or annoyance. (жалкий, никудышный, позорный)

  1. That's the most pathetic excuse I've ever heard.
  2. Our defense was pathetic last night.
  3. Only three people showed up? That's pathetic.

2. Making you feel pity or sadness. (жалкий, вызывающий жалость, жалобный)

  1. The pathetic little puppy was shaking in the rain.
  2. There was something pathetic about the old man eating alone every night.

dramatic#

Pronunciation
/drəˈmætɪk/ · druh-MAT-ik
Русский
резкий, разительный; эффектный; театральный
Type
quality; also classifying (dramatic arts)
Position
both — the theater sense is attributive only: dramatic arts
Comparison
more/most — more dramatic, most dramatic
Form
-ic from drama
Contrast
Russian драматический and драматичный often mean tragic or full of suffering. English dramatic most often means sudden and big — a dramatic change (резкое, разительное изменение), a dramatic improvement — and the change can be a good one. For драматическая ситуация say a tragic, dire or desperate situation. Don't be so dramatic = Не устраивай драму, не преувеличивай. драматический театр is simply a theater.

Dramatic has 4 distinct senses.

1. Of a change or event: sudden, large and very noticeable. (резкий, разительный, значительный)

  1. There's been a dramatic drop in crime downtown.
  2. The new coach made a dramatic difference.
  3. The rise in rents has been more dramatic here than anywhere else in the state.

2. Exciting and impressive to watch or look at. (захватывающий, эффектный)

  1. The movie ends with a dramatic rescue at sea.
  2. The house has dramatic views of the canyon.

3. Of a person or behavior: showing feelings in an exaggerated way, to get attention. (театральный, показной)

  1. Stop being so dramatic — it's just a scratch.
  2. She made a dramatic exit, slamming the door behind her.

4. Relating to plays, acting or the theater. (драматический, театральный)

  1. He studied dramatic arts in college.
  2. The festival includes dramatic readings of new plays.

concrete#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɑːnkriːt, kɑːnˈkriːt/ · KAHN-kreet, kahn-KREET
Русский
конкретный, определённый
Type
quality; also classifying (a concrete noun)
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more concrete, most concrete
Opposite
vague (sense 1); abstract (sense 2)
Contrast
Russian конкретный is much wider than concrete. Use concrete with evidence, proof, plans, proposals, steps, examples and results — things that are definite instead of vague. For one particular thing or person say specific or particular: конкретная дата = a specific date, конкретный человек = a particular person, в конкретном случае = in this particular case, конкретно = specifically. Slang конкретный ('real, serious') has no match in concrete. In a concrete wall (бетонная стена) the word is the noun concrete used as a modifier, not this adjective.

Concrete has 2 distinct senses.

1. Based on facts, clearly stated and definite; not vague or general. (конкретный, определённый, реальный)

  1. The police have no concrete evidence against him.
  2. Our plans for the summer aren't concrete yet.
  3. Could you give me a more concrete example?

2. Existing as a real thing that can be seen and touched; not abstract. (материальный, вещественный, конкретный)

  1. Young children learn math faster with concrete objects like blocks.
  2. A concrete noun names something you can see or touch, like a chair or a dog.

delicate#

Pronunciation
/ˈdɛlɪkət/ · DEL-ih-kuht
Русский
хрупкий, нежный; щекотливый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more delicate, most delicate
Opposite
sturdy, robust
Contrast
Not деликатный about a person. a delicate person is physically weak or easily hurt (хрупкий, болезненный), not tactful. For деликатный человек say tactful, considerate or discreet: он очень деликатный = he's very tactful. The words overlap only for situations: a delicate matter = деликатный, щекотливый вопрос. деликатес is the noun a delicacy.

Delicate has 6 distinct senses.

1. Easily broken or damaged; needing careful handling. (хрупкий, нежный)

  1. Wash delicate fabrics by hand.
  2. Babies have very delicate skin.
  3. These old photos are too delicate to touch.

2. Of a person or their health: weak and likely to get sick. (слабый, болезненный, хрупкий)

  1. She was a delicate child who missed a lot of school.
  2. Grandpa's health is delicate, so we keep our visits short.

3. Of a situation or subject: needing tact and care, because it could easily cause trouble or hurt feelings. (деликатный, щекотливый)

  1. Money is a delicate subject in our family.
  2. The peace talks are at a delicate stage.
  3. This is going to need a more delicate approach.

4. Small, fine and graceful. (изящный, тонкий)

  1. She has delicate features and dark eyes.
  2. The earrings hang from a delicate gold chain.

5. Of a taste, smell or color: light and pleasant, not strong. (тонкий, нежный, мягкий)

  1. This fish has a delicate flavor, so go easy on the sauce.
  2. The bedroom is painted a delicate shade of blue.

6. Of work or an instrument: needing great skill and precision, or precise enough to notice very small changes. (тонкий, точный, чувствительный)

  1. Removing the tumor was a delicate operation.
  2. These delicate instruments can detect the smallest movement.

spectacular#

Pronunciation
/spɛkˈtækjələr/ · spek-TAK-yuh-lur
Русский
потрясающий, зрелищный
Type
evaluative
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more spectacular, most spectacular
Form
-ar from spectacle
Contrast
It has nothing to do with спектакль, which is a play, a show or a performance. spectacular = amazing, stunning, very impressive to see (потрясающий, зрелищный). The play was spectacular means it was wonderful, not that it was theatrical. A spectacular failure is a huge, very public one. As a noun, a spectacular is a big, showy TV or stage show.

Spectacular has 2 distinct senses.

1. Very impressive and exciting to see; stunning. (потрясающий, зрелищный, эффектный)

  1. The view from the top of the trail is spectacular.
  2. This year's Fourth of July fireworks were more spectacular than ever.
  3. She scored a spectacular goal from midfield.

2. Unusually great in size or degree, often suddenly so. (ошеломительный, грандиозный)

  1. The company's first year was a spectacular success.
  2. His run for mayor ended in spectacular failure.
  3. The town has seen spectacular growth since the factory opened.

prospective#

Pronunciation
/prəˈspɛktɪv/ · pruh-SPEK-tiv
Русский
будущий, потенциальный
Type
classifying
Position
attributive only — prospective buyers; not the buyers are prospective
Comparison
not gradable
Form
-ive from prospect
Contrast
Not перспективный. prospective = likely to become something or to happen in the future (будущий, потенциальный): a prospective student is someone thinking of applying, not a promising one. Prospective has 2 distinct senses.

1. Likely or expected to become a particular kind of person in the future. (будущий, потенциальный)

  1. The realtor is showing the house to prospective buyers this weekend.
  2. Prospective students can tour the campus on Fridays.
  3. She met her prospective in-laws for the first time at Thanksgiving.

2. Expected or likely to happen; relating to the future. (предполагаемый, ожидаемый, предстоящий)

  1. The report looks at the prospective costs of a new stadium.
  2. Researchers ran a prospective study that followed 5,000 patients for ten years.

genial#

Pronunciation
/ˈdʒiːniəl/ · JEE-nee-uhl
Русский
добродушный, приветливый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more genial, most genial
Contrast
Not гениальный. genial = friendly and cheerful (добродушный, приветливый), and it describes people and their manner, not ideas. Genial has 2 distinct senses.

2. Literary: of weather or a climate, pleasantly mild and warm. (мягкий, тёплый)

  1. They moved to Arizona for its genial climate.
  2. The genial spring sun brought everyone out to the park.

artistic#

Pronunciation
/ɑːrˈtɪstɪk/ · ahr-TIS-tik
Русский
художественный; творчески одарённый
Type
classifying; also quality (a very artistic child)
Position
both — the 'relating to art' sense is attributive only: artistic director
Comparison
more/most — more artistic, most artistic
Form
-ic from artist
Contrast
Russian артистичный and артистический come from артист — a performer or actor. English artistic comes from artist — above all a painter, sculptor or other creative person — and means good at art or creative. For артистичный say theatrical, a natural performer or has stage presence; артистическая уборная is a dressing room. Informal artsy (American) is often mocking: an artsy coffee shop.

Artistic has 3 distinct senses.

1. Relating to art or artists. (художественный, творческий)

  1. The director was given complete artistic freedom.

  1. Both of her kids are very artistic — they're always drawing.

  3. He's more artistic than his brother, who's into math.

3. Made or arranged with skill and good taste; attractive. (художественный, изящный, со вкусом)

  1. The cake had an artistic design of sugar flowers.

sophisticated#

Pronunciation
/səˈfɪstɪkeɪtɪd/ · suh-FIS-tih-kay-tid
Русский
искушённый, утончённый; сложный, современный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more sophisticated, most sophisticated
Form
-ed adjective, pronounced /ɪd/; there is no everyday verb sophisticate
Opposite
unsophisticated, naive (senses 1 and 3); primitive, crude (sense 2)
Contrast
It is not about софистика (sophistry — clever but dishonest arguing), and it is not simply complicated. sophisticated is usually praise: of a person, worldly and cultured (искушённый, утончённый); of technology, advanced and well designed (сложный, современный). sophisticated software is advanced; complicated software is hard to use. For the negative изощрённый (изощрённая жестокость) say elaborate or calculated, not sophisticated. The noun a sophisticate means a sophisticated person.

Sophisticated has 3 distinct senses.

1. Of a person, place or style: knowing a lot about the world, culture and fashion; elegant. (искушённый, утончённый, изысканный)

  1. She was the most sophisticated woman in our small town.
  2. The restaurant has a sophisticated, grown-up feel.
  3. Four years in New York made him a lot more sophisticated.

2. Of a machine, system or method: advanced and complex. (сложный, передовой, современный)

  1. The bank uses sophisticated software to spot fraud.
  2. Today's phones are far more sophisticated than the computers that went to the moon.

3. Of a person's thinking or an audience: able to understand difficult ideas; not simple or naive. (тонкий, глубокий; подготовленный)

  1. Voters are more sophisticated than politicians think.
  2. The novel offers a sophisticated look at race and class.

extravagant#

Pronunciation
/ɪkˈstrævəɡənt/ · ik-STRAV-uh-guhnt
Русский
расточительный; непомерный; пышный
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more extravagant, most extravagant
Takes
with + money or time (extravagant with money); in + praise or claims (extravagant in their praise)
Form
extra- 'beyond' + Latin vagari 'to wander'
Opposite
thrifty, frugal (sense 1)
Contrast
Not экстравагантный in its usual Russian sense — eccentric, unusual, outlandish. English extravagant is mainly about money and excess: an extravagant gift costs too much, an extravagant person spends too much (расточительный). For экстравагантный say eccentric, flamboyant, outlandish or unconventional: экстравагантный наряд = a flamboyant (outlandish) outfit, экстравагантный человек = an eccentric. The two words meet only in sense 3, where a party or costume is both costly and showy.

Extravagant has 3 distinct senses.

1. Spending much more money than is wise, or costing much more than is wise. (расточительный; дорогостоящий)

  1. They live an extravagant lifestyle on a teacher's salary.
  2. A new car is an extravagant birthday present for a sixteen-year-old.
  3. He was never extravagant with money.

2. Of claims, praise or promises: far more than is reasonable or true. (непомерный, преувеличенный)

  1. Be careful of extravagant claims about weight-loss pills.
  2. The critics were extravagant in their praise.

3. Very elaborate and showy. (пышный, роскошный, броский)

  1. It was the most extravagant wedding I've ever been to.
  2. She wore an extravagant hat covered in feathers.

Comprehensive has 2 distinct senses.

1. Including all or almost all the facts, parts or details; complete and thorough. (всеобъемлющий, исчерпывающий, полный)

Complex has 2 distinct senses.
