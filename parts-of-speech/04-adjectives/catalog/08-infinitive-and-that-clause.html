Adjectives with a to-infinitive or that-clause#

easy to use, likely to rain, glad that you came, kind of you to help.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

These adjectives take a to-infinitive or a that-clause: easy to use, likely to rain, glad that you came. Two traps run through the group. It is kind of you judges a person but it is important for you states a need, and a few adjectives — essential, vital, imperative — take the bare verb in their that-clause: it is essential that he be there.

easy#

Pronunciation
/ˈiːzi/ · EE-zee
Русский
лёгкий; простой; спокойный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — easier, easiest
Takes
to-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (easy to use, easy to talk to); for + person (easy for me); on + person (not strict) or + thing (pleasant for: easy on the eyes)
Opposite
difficult, hard
Contrast
Russian мне легко is It's easy for me, never I am easy. In This app is easy to use the subject is the thing you use, so He is easy to talk to means people find it easy to talk to him. The adverb is easily (She won easily), but fixed phrases keep easy: take it easy, go easy on him, easier said than done, easy come, easy go. Said of a woman, easy is an insulting old slang word for 'promiscuous'; the compliment for a relaxed person is easygoing.

Easy has 4 distinct senses.

1. Not difficult; done or understood without much effort. (лёгкий, простой)

  1. The first test was pretty easy.
  2. This camera is easy to use, even for kids.
  3. It's easier to take the subway than to find parking.

2. Relaxed and comfortable; free from worry or stiffness. (непринуждённый, спокойный)

  1. She has an easy, friendly way with customers.
  2. We walked at an easy pace along the beach.
  3. The two of them fell into an easy silence.

3. easy on + person, or before a noun: not strict; not demanding much. (мягкий, снисходительный)

  1. The coach was easy on the new players at first.
  2. Her grandparents are much easier on her than her parents are.
  3. Mr. Alvarez is an easy grader.

4. easy on + the eyes, ears, stomach or budget: pleasant, not harsh or costly. (приятный; щадящий)

  1. The new font is much easier on the eyes.
  2. The lunch menu is easy on the wallet.

hard#

Pronunciation
/hɑːrd/ · HAHRD
Русский
трудный; твёрдый; тяжёлый; строгий
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — harder, hardest
Takes
to-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (hard to believe, hard to please); for + person (hard for me); on + person (strict with) or + thing (causing wear: hard on your knees)
Opposite
easy (difficult); soft (solid)
Contrast
Hard is also an adverb with the same form: work hard (усердно), try hard, It's raining hard. Hardly is a different word meaning 'almost not' (едва): She hardly works is the opposite of She works hard. As with easy, Russian мне трудно is It's hard for me, not I am hard. Hard water has a lot of minerals (жёсткая вода); hard liquor is strong alcohol such as vodka or whiskey, and hard cider is the American name for alcoholic cider. For character, Russian твёрдый is usually firm; a hard person sounds cold and unkind. No hard feelings is 'no resentment', said after an argument or a loss.

Hard has 9 distinct senses.

1. Difficult to do, understand or deal with. (трудный, тяжёлый)

  1. It's hard to believe she's only twelve.
  2. The hardest part was waiting for the results.
  3. That's a hard question to answer.

2. Solid and firm; not easy to bend, cut or press. (твёрдый, жёсткий)

  1. The mattress in the hotel was too hard.
  2. Let the caramel cool until it's hard.
  3. Diamonds are harder than any other natural material.

3. Needing or using a lot of effort or energy. (тяжёлый; усердный)

  1. Moving furniture is hard work.
  2. She's the hardest worker on the team.
  3. After a hard day at the office, I just want to sleep.

4. Full of problems and suffering; harsh. (тяжёлый, суровый)

  1. Times were hard during the Great Depression.
  2. It was the hardest winter the town had seen in decades.
  3. He's had a hard life.

5. Strict, severe or unfriendly. (строгий, суровый)

  1. Don't be so hard on yourself.
  2. Her father was a hard man who rarely smiled.
  3. She gave him a hard look and said nothing.

6. Done with a lot of force. (сильный)

  1. He gave the door a hard push.
  2. She took a hard fall on the ice.

7. Definite and based on facts that can be proved. (точный, неопровержимый)

  1. The police have no hard evidence against him.
  2. We need hard numbers, not guesses.

8. Of drinks and drugs: very strong; containing a lot of alcohol, or highly addictive. (крепкий (об алкоголе); тяжёлый (о наркотиках))

  1. The bar serves beer and wine but no hard liquor.
  2. He never touched hard drugs.

9. Of water: containing a lot of minerals, so that soap does not lather well. (жёсткий (о воде))

  1. The water here is so hard it leaves spots on the glasses.
  2. Hard water can damage your pipes over time.

difficult#

Pronunciation
/ˈdɪfɪkəlt/ · DIF-ih-kuhlt
Русский
трудный; сложный; несговорчивый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more difficult, most difficult
Takes
to-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (difficult to explain); for + person (difficult for me); about + noun, of a person (making trouble over it)
Opposite
easy
Contrast
Never difficulter: the forms are more difficult and most difficult. Russian мне трудно is It's difficult for me to…, not I am difficult, which means that I am hard to deal with. Russian сложный meaning 'made of many parts' is complicated or complex (a complicated plan), not always difficult. For tasks, everyday American speech prefers hard (a hard test); difficult sounds a little more formal.

Difficult has 3 distinct senses.

1. Not easy; needing a lot of effort, skill or thought. (трудный, сложный)

  1. The exam was more difficult than I expected.
  2. It's difficult to explain over the phone.
  3. Choosing a college was the most difficult decision of her life.

2. Full of problems; causing worry or trouble. (тяжёлый, трудный)

  1. The family is going through a difficult time.
  2. His question put me in a difficult position.

3. Of a person: not easy to please or deal with; unwilling to cooperate. (трудный, несговорчивый, капризный)

  1. Stop being difficult and just get in the car.
  2. He can be difficult about money.
  3. She's one of our more difficult clients.

tough#

Pronunciation
/tʌf/ · TUHF
Русский
трудный; крепкий; строгий; жёсткий
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est — tougher, toughest
Takes
to-infinitive, where the subject is what you act on (tough to beat); for + person (tough for her); on + person or problem (strict with: tough on crime; hard for: tough on the kids)
Opposite
easy (difficult); weak (strong); tender (of meat)
Contrast
In American speech tough is the everyday word for 'difficult' (a tough week, a tough call), more common than difficult. Russian жёсткий covers several English words: tough for meat and for strict rules, hard for a surface, harsh for criticism. Tough luck! and Tough! are not sympathy but the opposite: 'too bad, deal with it'. Tough rhymes with stuff; do not confuse it with though or through.

Tough has 7 distinct senses.

1. Difficult; full of problems or needing a lot of effort. (трудный, тяжёлый)

  1. It's been a tough year for the business.
  2. The Yankees will be tough to beat.
  3. That was the toughest exam I've ever taken.

2. Strong and able to deal with pain, hard work or trouble. (крепкий, выносливый, стойкий)

  1. She's tougher than she looks.
  2. You have to be tough to survive in this business.

3. Strict and firm; not willing to give way. (строгий, жёсткий)

  1. The senator promised to be tough on crime.
  2. Our math teacher was tough but fair.
  3. The city passed tough new rules on short-term rentals.

4. Of food, especially meat: hard to cut or chew. (жёсткий)

  1. The steak was overcooked and tough.
  2. Older chickens have tougher meat.

5. Of a material: strong and not easily broken or torn. (прочный)

  1. These boots are made of tough leather.
  2. The phone case is made of tough plastic.

6. Of a place or person: rough and likely to be violent. (опасный, неблагополучный; крутой)

  1. He grew up in a tough neighborhood.
  2. He likes to act tough in front of his friends.

7. Unfortunate or unfair; as Tough! or tough luck, said without sympathy. (неудачный; не повезло)

  1. "I don't want to go." "Tough. You're going."
  2. Losing your job right before the holidays is tough.
  3. You missed the deadline? Tough luck.

likely#

Pronunciation
/ˈlaɪkli/ · LYK-lee
Русский
вероятный; подходящий
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — more likely, most likely; likelier, likeliest in writing
Takes
to-infinitive with a person or thing as subject (She's likely to win); that-clause after it (It's likely that she'll win)
Form
-ly from like in its old sense 'similar', so it is an adjective, not a manner adverb
Opposite
unlikely
Contrast
Although it ends in -ly, likely is an adjective: a likely result, Rain is likely. American English also uses it as an adverb meaning 'probably', usually with most, very or quite or right before the verb: They'll most likely be late, She will likely win. Russian вероятно is safest as probably. Likely allows a personal subject (He is likely to call), but probable does not: say It is probable that he will call, never He is probable to call. Not likely! is a spoken way to say 'no chance'.

Likely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Probably going to happen or be true. (вероятный)

  1. Snow is likely later tonight.
  2. She's likely to win the election.
  3. It's more likely that he just forgot.

2. Seeming right for a purpose; promising. (подходящий, многообещающий)

  1. We searched every likely hiding place.
  2. The bend in the river looked like a likely spot for trout.

3. A likely story: used to say you do not believe what someone has told you. (так я и поверил)

  1. He says the dog ate his homework. A likely story!
  2. "I was at the library all night." "A likely story."

unlikely#

Pronunciation
/ʌnˈlaɪkli/ · uhn-LYK-lee
Русский
маловероятный; неожиданный
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — more unlikely, most unlikely; unlikeliest before a noun
Takes
to-infinitive (He's unlikely to call); that-clause after it (It's unlikely that he'll call)
Form
un- + likely
Opposite
likely
Contrast
Unlike likely, unlikely is never an adverb: He probably won't come or He is unlikely to come, not He will unlikely come. Do not confuse it with the preposition unlike (в отличие от): Unlike his brother, he's quiet. Most unlikely can be a true superlative or just mean 'very unlikely' (That's most unlikely).

Unlikely has 2 distinct senses.

1. Not probably going to happen or be true. (маловероятный)

  1. He's unlikely to call back before Monday.
  2. It's unlikely that the store is still open.
  3. A deal now seems more unlikely than ever.

2. Not what you would expect; surprising. (неожиданный, странный)

  1. The two men formed an unlikely friendship.
  2. She became the unlikeliest of heroes.
  3. The idea came from an unlikely source.

able#

Pronunciation
/ˈeɪbəl/ · AY-buhl
Русский
в состоянии; способный, умелый
Type
quality
Position
both — able to + verb only after a verb; before a noun able means 'skilled': an able student
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — abler, ablest, more able; with to + verb, better able
Takes
to-infinitive (required in the 'can' sense: able to swim)
Opposite
unable (can't); incompetent (skilled)
Contrast
Be able to fills the gaps where can has no form: will be able to, have been able to, might be able to (never will can). For one success in the past, use was able to or managed to, not could: I was able to get two tickets. Russian способный на + noun or verb is capable of + noun or -ing: capable of lying, not able of lying. The noun is ability; disabled is a different word, about a lasting physical or mental condition.

Able has 2 distinct senses.

1. able to: having the skill, strength, time or chance to do something. (в состоянии, способный (сделать))

  1. Will you be able to come on Saturday?
  2. I've never been able to whistle.
  3. With the new software, we're better able to track orders.

2. Clever and good at what you do; skilled. (способный, умелый)

  1. She's a very able student.
  2. He was one of the ablest lawyers in the state.

willing#

Pronunciation
/ˈwɪlɪŋ/ · WIL-ing
Русский
готовый, согласный; охотный
Type
quality
Position
both — with to + verb only after a verb; before a noun it means 'glad to help': a willing helper
Comparison
more/most — more willing, most willing
Takes
to-infinitive (willing to help)
Form
-ing from the old verb will 'want'
Opposite
unwilling, reluctant
Contrast
Willing means you will do it if needed, not that you want to: I'm willing to drive is я не против or я готов, while хочу is want and очень хочу is eager or can't wait. Willing is not a verb form and is not followed by for + verb: willing to help, not willing for helping. God willing is a fixed phrase, если Бог даст.

Willing has 2 distinct senses.

1. willing to: ready to do something if it is needed or asked; not objecting. (готовый, согласный (сделать))

  1. Are you willing to work weekends?
  2. How much are you willing to pay?
  3. After the strike, management seemed more willing to talk.

2. Doing something gladly and by choice. (охотный, добровольный)

  1. There was no shortage of willing volunteers.
  2. She was a willing participant in the experiment.

unwilling#

Pronunciation
/ʌnˈwɪlɪŋ/ · uhn-WIL-ing
Русский
не желающий; невольный
Type
quality
Position
both — with to + verb only after a verb; before a noun it means 'forced, against your will': an unwilling witness
Comparison
more/most — more unwilling, most unwilling
Takes
to-infinitive (unwilling to help)
Form
un- + willing
Opposite
willing
Contrast
Unwilling means you refuse and will not do it; reluctant means you do not want to and hesitate, but may do it in the end. He was unwilling to sign suggests he did not sign; He was reluctant to sign leaves it open. In everyday speech Russian не хочу is I don't want to; unwilling is more formal.

Unwilling has 2 distinct senses.

1. unwilling to: not ready to do something; refusing. (не желающий, не готовый)

  1. He was unwilling to talk about what happened.
  2. Many banks are unwilling to lend to new businesses.
  3. Voters seem more unwilling than ever to pay higher taxes.

2. Made to do something or be part of something against your will. (невольный, вынужденный)

  1. She became an unwilling celebrity after the video went viral.
  2. The prosecutor called him as an unwilling witness.

ready#

Pronunciation
/ˈrɛdi/ · RED-ee
Русский
готовый; быстрый
Type
quality
Position
both — in the 'prepared', 'willing' and 'about to' senses usually after a verb; before a noun it can mean 'quick' or 'available': a ready answer, ready cash
Comparison
-er/-est or more/most — readier, readiest, more ready
Takes
for + noun (prepared for: ready for the test; wanting: ready for bed); to-infinitive (prepared, willing or about to: ready to go, ready to help, ready to cry)
Preposition patterns
ready for
Contrast
Ready is not already (уже): Dinner is ready (готов) vs Dinner is already on the table. Russian Готово! about finished work is Done! or All set!. Before a noun the meaning shifts: a ready answer is a quick answer, not a prepared one (that is a canned answer), and ready cash is money you have on hand. The everyday phrase for preparing yourself is get ready: Go get ready for school. Ready-made is a compound: ready-made curtains.

Ready has 5 distinct senses.

1. Prepared for something; or finished and available to use or eat. (готовый)

  1. Dinner's ready!
  2. Are you ready for the test tomorrow?
  3. Some kids are readier for kindergarten than others.

2. ready to: willing and glad to do something. (готовый (охотно))

  1. She's always ready to help a friend.
  2. I'm not ready to forgive him yet.

3. ready to: so upset, tired or excited that you are about to do something. (готовый (вот-вот))

  1. By the end of the meeting I was ready to scream.
  2. He looked ready to cry.

4. ready for: wanting or needing something soon. (которому хочется, пора)

  1. I'm ready for bed.
  2. After six months without a break, we're more than ready for a vacation.

5. Quick; or easy to get and use when needed. (быстрый; имеющийся под рукой)

  1. He always has a ready excuse.
  2. Students have ready access to computers in the library.

reluctant#

Pronunciation
/rɪˈlʌktənt/ · rih-LUHK-tuhnt
Русский
неохотный; вынужденный
Type
quality; also emotion
Position
both
Comparison
more/most — more reluctant, most reluctant
Takes
to-infinitive (reluctant to leave)
Preposition patterns
reluctant to
Opposite
willing, eager
Contrast
Russian неохотно is usually the adverb reluctantly: She agreed reluctantly, not She agreed reluctant. Reluctant means you hesitate and do not want to, but may still do it; unwilling means you will not. It takes a to-infinitive, not about or for + -ing: reluctant to ask, not reluctant for asking.

Reluctant has 2 distinct senses.

1. Not wanting to do something, and slow or hesitant to do it. (неохотно делающий, не желающий)

  1. He was reluctant to admit he'd made a mistake.
  2. Young people are more reluctant to buy houses than their parents were.
  3. I was reluctant to leave the kids with a new sitter.

2. Taking part in something, or given, without real willingness. (вынужденный, неохотный)

  1. He became a reluctant hero after pulling a child from the river.
  2. She gave a reluctant nod.

bound#

Pronunciation
/baʊnd/ · BOWND
Русский
обязательный, непременный; обязанный; направляющийся
Type
quality
Position
predicative only — also after a noun in the 'heading for' sense (a flight bound for Denver); before a noun only in compounds: a Denver-bound flight
Comparison
not gradable
Takes
to-infinitive (certain to; obliged to); by + noun (a law, promise or contract); for + place (traveling toward); up with or up in + noun (closely connected with)
Preposition patterns
bound by
Form
past participle of bind 'tie'
Contrast
Bound to usually means 'certain to', not 'obliged to': He's bound to be late is он наверняка опоздает, not он обязан опоздать. The 'obliged' sense mostly comes with legally, by law, by contract or duty-bound. This bound has nothing to do with the verb and noun bound 'jump' or with out of bounds. The suffix -bound has two meanings: Chicago-bound is heading to Chicago, but snowbound and housebound mean unable to leave. The prisoner was bound is the passive of bind (связан), a verb form.

Bound has 4 distinct senses.

1. bound to: certain or very likely to happen or to do something. (обязательно, непременно, наверняка)

  1. It's bound to rain the day we go to the beach.
  2. You're bound to feel nervous on your first day.
  3. With so many people involved, there are bound to be mistakes.

2. Required to do something by a law, a promise or duty. (обязанный, связанный (обязательством))

  1. The company is legally bound to refund your money.
  2. We're bound by the terms of the contract.
  3. I felt bound to tell her the truth.

3. bound for: traveling toward a place. (направляющийся (в))

  1. The plane was bound for Seattle.
  2. Passengers bound for Newark should change trains here.

4. bound up with: closely connected with something. (тесно связанный (с))

  1. Her sense of self is bound up with her job.
  2. The town's history is bound up with the railroad.

due#

Pronunciation
/duː/ · DOO
Русский
ожидаемый; причитающийся; вызванный; должный
Type
quality
Position
both — the 'proper' sense only before a noun (due care, in due course); the other senses after a verb or noun (The rent is due, the money due to her)
Comparison
not gradable
Takes
to-infinitive (scheduled to: due to arrive); to + noun (caused by: due to the weather; owed to: the credit due to her); for + noun (going to need soon: due for a checkup)
Contrast
Due to has two patterns with different meanings: due to + verb is 'scheduled' (The plane is due to land at six, по расписанию), and due to + noun is 'caused by' (The delay was due to fog, из-за). It does not mean Russian должен in the sense of duty: You are due to pay on Monday means payment is scheduled; for obligation use have to or should. Careful writers keep due to after be and start a sentence with Because of, though Due to the storm, the game was canceled is everywhere in American English. Past due and overdue mean late. The plural noun dues is a membership fee (членские взносы), and in due north the word is an adverb.

Due has 5 distinct senses.

1. Expected or planned to happen or arrive at a certain time. (ожидаемый (по плану, по расписанию))

  1. The baby is due in March.
  2. The next train is due at 6:15.
  3. The report is due to come out next week.

2. Owed; that must be paid, returned or handed in by a certain time. (подлежащий уплате, сдаче; причитающийся)

  1. The rent is due on the first of the month.
  2. Your library books are due back on Friday.
  3. She finally got the credit she was due.

3. due to: caused by. (вызванный (чем-то), из-за)

  1. The delay was due to bad weather.
  2. Most of the damage was due to water, not fire.

4. due for: going to need or get something soon. (которому пора)

  1. I'm due for a haircut.
  2. After three losing seasons, the team is due for some luck.

5. Proper and appropriate; as much as is needed. (должный, надлежащий)

  1. Drive with due care on wet roads.
  2. With all due respect, I think you're wrong.
  3. You'll hear from us in due course.

apt#

Pronunciation
/æpt/ · APT
Русский
склонный; уместный, меткий
Type
quality
Position
both — apt to + verb only after a verb; the 'fitting' sense before or after a noun: an apt description, The name is apt
Comparison
more/most — more apt, most apt
Takes
to-infinitive (apt to forget)
Form
from Latin aptus 'fitted', the root of aptitude
Contrast
Apt to, likely to, liable to and prone to all mean 'tending to'. Apt to describes a habit or natural tendency (He's apt to exaggerate); liable to and prone to point to something bad; likely to is the neutral, most common choice and can be about a single event (It's likely to rain). In American apartment listings apt. is short for apartment, a different word.

Apt has 3 distinct senses.

1. apt to: tending to do something, as a habit or by nature. (склонный)

  1. He's apt to lose his temper when he's tired.
  2. Cheap shoes are apt to fall apart in the rain.
  3. Shoppers are more apt to buy when prices end in 99 cents.

2. Exactly right for the situation; well chosen. (уместный, меткий, подходящий)

  1. 'Chaos' is an apt description of our first week.
  2. I can't think of a more apt word for it.

3. Quick to learn; used mostly in formal or older writing. (способный, смышлёный)

  1. She was an apt pupil and learned to read at four.
  2. He proved an apt student of the game.

liable#

Pronunciation
/ˈlaɪəbəl/ · LY-uh-buhl
Русский
склонный (к плохому); ответственный; подлежащий
Type
quality
Position
predicative only — also after a noun (the driver liable for the damage); rare before a noun outside legal writing
Comparison
more/most — more liable, most liable
Takes
to-infinitive (likely to, usually something bad: liable to crack); for + noun (legally responsible for); to + noun (legally subject to: liable to a fine)
Preposition patterns
liable for · liable to
Contrast
Liable to + verb is for bad or unwanted things (The roof is liable to leak); for neutral or good things use likely to. Liable for is legal responsibility, often with hold (The court held the landlord liable). Do not confuse it with libel (клевета), which sounds almost the same; liable has three syllables, LY-uh-buhl. The noun liability also means a debt in business (обязательство, пассив).

Liable has 3 distinct senses.

1. liable to: likely to do or suffer something bad. (склонный, могущий (о плохом))

  1. The ice is liable to crack if you walk on it.
  2. Older cars are more liable to break down.
  3. He's liable to say something he'll regret.

2. Legally responsible for paying for something or for damage. ((юридически) ответственный)

  1. The landlord is liable for repairs to the building.
  2. You could be held liable if someone gets hurt on your property.

3. liable to + noun: legally subject to a punishment, tax or rule. (подлежащий, облагаемый)

  1. Violators are liable to a fine of up to $500.
  2. Anyone who lies under oath is liable to prosecution.

prone#

Pronunciation
/proʊn/ · PROHN
Русский
склонный, подверженный; лежащий ничком
Type
quality
Position
both — the 'tending to' sense only after a verb, or before a noun in compounds (accident-prone, an injury-prone player); the 'face down' sense after lie or before a noun (a prone position)

both — the side sense and the geometry sense only before a noun (my right hand, a right angle); the sense 'correct in what you think' only after a verb (You're right)
Comparison
not gradable — strengthen with exactly, absolutely or just (You're absolutely right)
Takes
about + noun (correct about); to-infinitive (right to worry); of + person + to-infinitive (morally right: right of you to tell me); for + person or job (suitable: right for you); with + noun (in order: not right with the car)
Opposite
wrong; left (side)
Contrast
Russian ты прав is You're right, with be, not You have right: have the right to uses the noun (иметь право). Right of you to judges an action (It was right of you to call the police); right for you means suitable (This job isn't right for you). Right is also an adverb (turn right, right now), a noun (human rights, on the right) and a verb (right a wrong). It sounds like write. In geometry Russian прямой угол is a right angle, ninety degrees — never a 'straight' one.

Right has 7 distinct senses.

1. Correct or true. (правильный, верный)

  1. Is this the right answer?
  2. "Your name is Sam?" "That's right."
  3. Make sure you have the right address.

2. Of a person: correct in what you think, say or do. (правый (прав))

  1. You were right about the weather.
  2. She was right to worry about him.

3. Morally good or fair. (правильный, справедливый)

  1. It's not right to take credit for someone else's work.
  2. I hope I did the right thing.
  3. It was right of you to tell me the truth.

4. Best or most suitable for a situation. (подходящий, нужный)

  1. He's the right person for the job.
  2. Wait for the right moment to ask.

5. On the side of the body that is toward the east when you face north. (правый)

  1. She writes with her right hand.
  2. Stay in the right lane until the exit.

6. Healthy, or working as it should. (в порядке)

  1. Something isn't right with the car; it's making a strange noise.
  2. I haven't felt right since the flu.

7. In geometry: of an angle, exactly ninety degrees. (прямой (об угле))

  1. Check that the corners form a right angle before you nail them.
  2. The two hallways meet at a right angle.

wrong#

Pronunciation
/rɔːŋ/ · RAWNG
Русский
неправильный; не тот; нехороший
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both — the sense 'not working properly' only after a verb (What's wrong?, Something is wrong with it)
Comparison
not gradable — strengthen with completely, totally or dead (dead wrong)
Takes
about + noun (mistaken about); with + noun (having a problem: wrong with the car); to-infinitive (wrong to lie); of + person + to-infinitive (wrong of him to yell); for + person or job (not suitable)
Preposition patterns
wrong with
Opposite
right, correct
Contrast
Wrong is also an adverb after a verb: go wrong, get it wrong, spell it wrong; the adverb wrongly goes before a past participle (wrongly accused). Russian не тот is the wrong: We took the wrong bus, not We took not that bus, and You have the wrong number is Вы ошиблись номером. What's wrong? is Что случилось?, but What's wrong with you? can sound rude. Judging an action takes of: It was wrong of him to yell.

Wrong has 5 distinct senses.

1. Not correct or true. (неправильный, неверный)

  1. Two of my answers were wrong.
  2. That's the wrong date; the party is on Saturday.

2. Of a person: mistaken in what you think or say. (неправый (не прав), ошибающийся)

  1. You're wrong about her; she's really nice.
  2. I was wrong to doubt you.

3. Morally bad or unfair. (нехороший, дурной, несправедливый)

  1. Stealing is wrong.
  2. It was wrong of him to yell at the kids.

4. Not the one you need or want; not suitable. (не тот, неподходящий)

  1. We got on the wrong bus.
  2. Sorry, you have the wrong number.
  3. It's the wrong time to ask for a raise.

5. Not working properly; causing a problem. (неладный, не в порядке)

  1. What's wrong? You look upset.
  2. Something is wrong with my laptop.
