# Spelling changes in comparison

> *big*, *bigger*; *happy*, *happier*; *simple*, *simpler* — regular comparison with irregular spelling.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

The comparative and superlative are regular, but the spelling is not. One-syllable adjectives ending in consonant–vowel–consonant double the last letter (*big*, *bigger*); a final *-y* becomes *-i-* (*happy*, *happier*); a final *-e* just takes *-r* (*nice*, *nicer*); and a few two-syllable words take *-er* where learners expect *more* (*simpler*, *narrower*, *quieter*). Each entry spells both forms out.

---
### big

**Pronunciation:** /bɪɡ/ &middot; *BIG*
**Русский:** большой; крупный; важный
**Type:** size; also evaluative
**Position:** both — but *big* meaning older (*my big sister*) only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *bigger*, *biggest*
**Takes:** *on* + noun or *-ing* (informal: very enthusiastic about); *of* + person in *That's big of you*
**Opposite:** *small*, *little*
**Contrast:** Double the *g*: *bigger*, *biggest*, never *biger*. *Big* and *large* both mean большой, but *large* is more formal and goes with *amount*, *number*, *quantity* and sizes; only *big* means important (*a big decision*), older (*my big brother*) or popular (*big in Japan*), so *a large mistake* and *my large brother* are wrong. In *think big* and *talk big*, *big* is an adverb.

Big has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Large in size, number or amount.** (большой, крупный)

1. They live in a *big* house outside Denver.
2. Texas is *bigger* than France.
3. That was the *biggest* pizza I've ever seen.

**2. Important, serious or significant.** (важный, серьёзный)

4. Getting married is a *big* decision.
5. It's no *big* deal, so don't worry about it.
6. The *biggest* problem is money.

**3. Older or grown-up; used mainly with children and about brothers and sisters.** (старший; взрослый)

7. My *big* sister taught me to drive.
8. You're a *big* boy now, so you can tie your own shoes.

**4. Very popular or successful.** (популярный, успешный)

9. The band is still *big* in Japan.
10. Podcasts got really *big* during the pandemic.
11. He was one of the *biggest* stars of the '80s.

**5. Doing something a lot or with great enthusiasm; informal *big on*: very enthusiastic about.** (большой (любитель), заядлый; увлечённый)

12. I'm a *big* fan of her early work.
13. He's never been a *big* eater.
14. My dad is *big* on camping.

**6. Generous or kind; often said with irony.** (великодушный, благородный)

15. It was *big* of him to admit he was wrong.
16. You're lending me a dollar? That's really *big* of you.

### fat

**Pronunciation:** /fæt/ &middot; *FAT*
**Русский:** толстый; жирный; крупный
**Type:** size; also quality
**Position:** both — but *fat* about money (*a fat bonus*) and in *fat chance* only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *fatter*, *fattest*
**Opposite:** *thin*
**Contrast:** Double the *t*: *fatter*, *fattest*. Calling a person *fat* is rude in American English; say *overweight*, *heavy* or *big*. For food with a lot of fat, use *fatty* or *rich* (*fatty meat*), not *fat meat*; *low-fat* and *fat-free* use the noun *fat* (жир). *Fat chance* means the opposite of what it seems: almost no chance.

Fat has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Having a lot of extra flesh on the body.** (толстый, полный)

1. The cat got *fat* after we moved to the city.
2. He was a *fat* kid, but he lost the weight in high school.
3. Our dog is *fatter* than the neighbor's.

**2. Thick, full or swollen.** (толстый, пухлый, распухший)

4. She pulled a *fat* envelope out of her bag.
5. He came home from the game with a *fat* lip.
6. It's the *fattest* book on the reading list.

**3. Of money: large.** (крупный, солидный)

7. She signed a *fat* contract with a big law firm.
8. The company lost money, but the CEO still got a *fat* bonus.

**4. Informal and ironic: very little or none, in *fat chance* and *a fat lot of good*.** (никакой, ничтожный)

9. Will he pay you back? *Fat* chance.
10. A *fat* lot of good that did us.

### hot

**Pronunciation:** /hɑːt/ &middot; *HAHT*
**Русский:** горячий; жаркий; острый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *hotter*, *hottest*
**Opposite:** *cold*; *mild* (food)
**Contrast:** Double the *t*: *hotter*, *hottest*. Russian splits heat in two, жаркий for weather and rooms and горячий for things you touch; English uses *hot* for both. *I'm hot* = мне жарко, never *It's hot to me*. Weather that is only pleasant is *warm* (тепло); *hot* is too much. *Hot* food can mean spicy (острый), so say *spicy* when you mean pepper. *Hot* about a person is informal and means sexy, so be careful with it.

Hot has 10 distinct senses.

**1. Of weather, a place or a time: having a high temperature.** (жаркий)

1. It was a *hot* day in July.
2. Phoenix is one of the *hottest* cities in the country.
3. Why is it so *hot* in here?

**2. Of food, drink or an object: at a high temperature.** (горячий)

4. Careful, the pan is *hot*.
5. I'd love a cup of *hot* coffee.
6. The water for tea should be *hotter* than that.

**3. Of a person: feeling too warm.** ((кому-то) жарко)

7. I'm *hot*. Can we turn on the AC?
8. The kids were *hot* and tired after the hike.

**4. Spicy, with a burning taste.** (острый)

9. This salsa is really *hot*.
10. Do you want the mild sauce or the *hot* one?
11. Their wings are the *hottest* in town.

**5. Very popular, successful or in demand at the moment.** (модный, популярный, актуальный)

12. It's the *hottest* restaurant in Brooklyn right now.
13. Immigration was a *hot* topic in the debate.
14. The Celtics are *hot* and have won eight straight games.

**6. Full of strong feeling, especially anger or excitement.** (горячий, бурный, вспыльчивый)

15. The meeting turned into a *hot* argument.
16. He has a *hot* temper.

**7. Informal: sexually attractive.** (сексуальный, привлекательный)

17. All her friends thought the new lifeguard was *hot*.
18. He's even *hotter* in person.

**8. Informal: stolen.** (краденый)

19. He didn't know the watch was *hot*.
20. The police traced the *hot* TVs to a warehouse.

**9. Of an electrical wire: carrying current.** (под напряжением)

21. Don't touch the black wire; it's *hot*.
22. Connect the *hot* wire to the brass screw.

**10. In guessing and hiding games: very close to the answer.** (горячо (в игре))

23. You're getting *hotter*. It's somewhere near the couch.
24. Now you're *hot*! Look under the pillow.

### sad

**Pronunciation:** /sæd/ &middot; *SAD*
**Русский:** грустный; печальный
**Type:** emotion; also evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *sadder*, *saddest*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *-ing*; *for* + person (sad on their behalf); *to*-infinitive (*sad to hear*); *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [sad about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#sad-about)
**Opposite:** *happy*
**Contrast:** Double the *d*: *sadder*, *saddest*. The same word describes the person and the cause: *I'm sad* (мне грустно) and *a sad movie* (грустный фильм), with no *-ing/-ed* pair to choose from. Russian мне грустно is *I'm sad* or *I feel sad*, never *It's sad to me*. *A sad excuse for* is informal and scornful: a very bad example of something.

Sad has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Feeling unhappy.** (грустный, печальный)

1. She was *sad* to leave her friends behind.
2. Don't look so *sad*. It's only a week.
3. I've never felt *sadder* than on the day we sold the farm.

**2. Making people feel unhappy.** (грустный, печальный)

4. It's a *sad* movie, so bring tissues.
5. The *saddest* part is that nobody called her.

**3. Unfortunate and deserving regret or blame.** (прискорбный, плачевный)

6. It's *sad* that so many kids go to school hungry.
7. The old theater is in a *sad* state.

**4. Informal: poor, pathetic or embarrassing.** (жалкий)

8. That's a *sad* excuse for a burger.
9. I spent Saturday night alone with my laptop. How *sad* is that?

### thin

**Pronunciation:** /θɪn/ &middot; *THIN*
**Русский:** тонкий; худой; жидкий; редкий
**Type:** size; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *thinner*, *thinnest*
**Opposite:** *thick*; *fat*
**Contrast:** Double the *n*: *thinner*, *thinnest*. Russian splits what English calls *thin*: тонкий (a slice, a wall), худой (a person), жидкий (soup, hair). About people, *thin* is neutral or slightly negative, *slim* is a compliment, and *skinny* is often negative. Russian тонкий about taste, humor or a difference is *subtle*, not *thin*: *a subtle joke*.

Thin has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Having a small distance between the two sides or surfaces.** (тонкий)

1. Cut the cucumber into *thin* slices.
2. The walls in this apartment are really *thin*.
3. The ice is *thinner* near the middle of the lake.

**2. Of a person or animal: having little fat on the body.** (худой)

4. He was tall and *thin*, with glasses.
5. She looked *thinner* after being sick.

**3. Of a liquid: watery, not thick.** (жидкий)

6. The soup was too *thin* and bland.
7. Add water until the paint is *thin* enough to spray.

**4. Not dense; with few people, hairs or things, or with little oxygen in the air.** (редкий, жидкий, немногочисленный; разреженный)

8. His hair is getting *thin* on top.
9. The crowd was *thin* for a Friday night.
10. The air is *thinner* up in the mountains.

**5. Weak and not convincing.** (неубедительный, слабый)

11. The evidence against him is pretty *thin*.
12. That's a *thin* excuse.

**6. Of a voice or sound: high and weak.** (тонкий, слабый)

13. She answered in a *thin*, shaky voice.
14. The cheap speakers sounded *thin*.

### wet

**Pronunciation:** /wɛt/ &middot; *WET*
**Русский:** мокрый; сырой; дождливый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *wetter*, *wettest*
**Opposite:** *dry*
**Contrast:** Double the *t*: *wetter*, *wettest*. Russian влажный is usually not *wet*: say *damp* for clothes or walls, *humid* for air, *moist* for cake or skin; *wet* means really covered with water (мокрый). *Wet* is also a verb whose past form is usually *wet* (*The baby wet the bed*).

Wet has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Covered with or full of water or another liquid.** (мокрый)

1. Take off those *wet* shoes before you come in.
2. My hair is still *wet*.
3. The grass was *wetter* than it looked.

**2. Of paint, ink or cement: not yet dry.** (невысохший, непросохший)

4. *Wet* paint. Do not touch.
5. Don't walk there; the concrete is still *wet*.

**3. Of weather or a season: rainy.** (дождливый, сырой)

6. It's been a cold, *wet* spring.
7. November is usually the *wettest* month here.

**4. US: of a town or county: allowing the sale of alcohol.** (где разрешена продажа алкоголя)

8. The county voted to go *wet* in 2012.
9. You have to drive to the next *wet* county to buy beer.

### fit

**Pronunciation:** /fɪt/ &middot; *FIT*
**Русский:** в хорошей форме; подходящий; пригодный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — but *fit for* and *fit to* (suitable) mostly after a verb
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *fitter*, *fittest*
**Takes:** *for* + noun (*fit for work*); *to*-infinitive (*fit to drive*)
**Opposite:** *unfit*; *out of shape*
**Contrast:** Double the *t*: *fitter*, *fittest* (*survival of the fittest*). Russian подходить по размеру is the verb *fit* (*The jacket fits*; past *fit* in American English), not the adjective, so *The jacket is fit* is wrong. The adjective means healthy, or good enough in quality (*fit to eat*). In everyday American speech *in shape* is often more natural: *I'm out of shape*. British slang *fit* (attractive) is not American.

Fit has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Healthy and strong, especially from exercise.** (в хорошей форме, спортивный)

1. My grandmother is really *fit* for her age.
2. He's a *fit*, athletic guy who runs every morning.
3. I'm *fitter* now than I was in college.

**2. Good enough or suitable for a purpose.** (подходящий, пригодный)

4. The water isn't *fit* to drink.
5. After the flood, the house was no longer *fit* for people to live in.
6. He'd had three beers and wasn't *fit* to drive.

**3. Right or proper, in *see fit*: to decide that something is right.** ((считать) нужным, уместным)

7. Spend the money however you see *fit*.
8. The judge can give whatever sentence she sees *fit*.

### flat

**Pronunciation:** /flæt/ &middot; *FLAT*
**Русский:** плоский; ровный; спущенный; фиксированный
**Type:** shape; also quality
**Position:** both — but *flat* meaning fixed (*a flat fee*) or complete (*a flat refusal*) only before a noun, and after the note name in *B flat*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *flatter*, *flattest*
**Opposite:** *hilly*, *bumpy*; *sharp* (music)
**Contrast:** Double the *t*: *flatter*, *flattest*, spelled like the verb *flatter* (льстить). *A flat* meaning an apartment is British; Americans say *apartment*, and in American speech *a flat* is usually a flat tire. An empty battery is *dead* in American English, not *flat*. In *lie flat*, *fall flat* and *in ten seconds flat*, *flat* is an adverb.

Flat has 10 distinct senses.

**1. Level and smooth, without hills, bumps or holes.** (ровный, плоский)

1. Kansas is famously *flat*.
2. Lay the map on a *flat* surface.
3. The land gets *flatter* as you drive east.

**2. Wide but not deep or high; of shoes: without a raised heel.** (плоский; без каблука)

4. She keeps her jewelry in a *flat* wooden box.
5. Wear *flat* shoes, because we'll be walking a lot.

**3. Of a tire or ball: without enough air in it.** (спущенный)

6. We got a *flat* tire on the way to the airport.
7. The basketball is *flat* again.

**4. Of a drink: no longer fizzy.** (выдохшийся)

8. The soda went *flat* because someone left the cap off.
9. This beer tastes *flat*.

**5. Of paint or a surface finish: not shiny.** (матовый)

10. We used *flat* paint on the ceiling.
11. A *flat* finish hides small dents in the wall better than a glossy one.

**6. Dull, without feeling, energy or interest.** (монотонный, безжизненный, вялый)

12. He spoke in a *flat*, tired voice.
13. The second half of the movie felt *flat*.

**7. Of sales, prices or growth: not rising.** (без изменений, на одном уровне)

14. Sales were *flat* in the third quarter.
15. Wages have stayed *flat* for years.

**8. Of a price or charge: the same in every case.** (фиксированный)

16. The lawyer charges a *flat* fee of $500.
17. They offer a *flat* rate for shipping.

**9. Complete and firm, with no discussion.** (категорический)

18. Her answer was a *flat* no.
19. The senator issued a *flat* denial.

**10. In music: lower than the correct note; after a note name, a half step lower.** (ниже нужного тона; бемоль)

20. The piece is in B *flat*.
21. The violins sounded a little *flat*.

### slim

**Pronunciation:** /slɪm/ &middot; *SLIM*
**Русский:** стройный; тонкий; небольшой
**Type:** size; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *slimmer*, *slimmest*
**Opposite:** *heavy*; *good* (a good chance)
**Contrast:** Double the *m*: *slimmer*, *slimmest*. About people, *slim* is a compliment, *thin* is neutral and *skinny* often is not. *A slim chance* and *slim hopes* are very small ones (мало шансов) and have nothing to do with shape. *Slim down* is a phrasal verb (худеть).

Slim has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Of a person: thin in an attractive way.** (стройный)

1. She's tall and *slim*, with short dark hair.
2. He looks *slimmer* since he started cycling.

**2. Of an object: thin or narrow.** (тонкий, узкий)

3. The new phone is *slimmer* and lighter.
4. He published a *slim* book of poems.
5. These jeans have a *slim* fit.

**3. Of a chance, amount or difference: very small.** (небольшой, незначительный)

6. There's only a *slim* chance of finding survivors.
7. She won by the *slimmest* of margins.
8. Pickings are *slim* this late in the season.

### grim

**Pronunciation:** /ɡrɪm/ &middot; *GRIM*
**Русский:** мрачный; суровый; удручающий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — but *grim* meaning determined (*grim determination*) only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *grimmer*, *grimmest*
**Contrast:** Double the *m*: *grimmer*, *grimmest*. *Grim* is not simply dark: about a face it means serious and stern (суровый), about news or a situation very bad and worrying (мрачный, удручающий). A room with little light is *dark* or *dim*, not *grim*; a *grim* room is depressing to be in. *The Grim Reaper* is the figure of Death.

Grim has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Of a face or expression: very serious and stern.** (суровый, мрачный)

1. The doctor came out with a *grim* look on her face.
2. His expression grew *grimmer* as he read the letter.

**2. Very bad, worrying or depressing.** (мрачный, удручающий, безрадостный)

3. The economic news is *grim*.
4. Things look *grim* for the home team.
5. It was the *grimmest* winter anyone could remember.

**3. Of a place: unpleasant and depressing to be in.** (унылый, мрачный)

6. They spent the night in a *grim* motel off the highway.
7. The waiting room was gray and *grim*.

**4. Determined and not giving up, even when things are hard.** (упорный, отчаянный)

8. She held on with *grim* determination.
9. The workers fought a *grim* battle to keep the factory open.

### dim

**Pronunciation:** /dɪm/ &middot; *DIM*
**Русский:** тусклый; смутный; туповатый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — but *dim* in *take a dim view* only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *dimmer*, *dimmest*
**Opposite:** *bright*
**Contrast:** Double the *m*: *dimmer*, *dimmest*. *A dimmer* is also a noun, a switch that makes lights less bright (диммер). *Take a dim view of* means disapprove of (относиться неодобрительно), not see badly. Calling a person *dim* is informal and unkind. *Dim* is also a verb: *dim the lights*.

Dim has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Not bright; with little light.** (тусклый, полутёмный)

1. The bar was *dim* and smoky.
2. She read by the *dim* light of a candle.
3. The lights get *dimmer* when the AC turns on.

**2. Hard to see, hear or remember clearly.** (смутный, неясный)

4. I have only a *dim* memory of my grandfather.
5. We saw a *dim* shape moving in the fog.

**3. Of chances or prospects: not hopeful.** (слабый, призрачный)

6. Prospects for a deal look *dim*.
7. His chances of winning grow *dimmer* every day.

**4. Informal: not intelligent.** (туповатый, недалёкий)

8. He's nice, but a little *dim*.
9. He's not *dim*, just a little lazy.

**5. Disapproving, in *take a dim view of*.** (неодобрительный)

10. The school takes a *dim* view of cheating.
11. My parents took a *dim* view of my new boyfriend.

### red

**Pronunciation:** /rɛd/ &middot; *RED*
**Русский:** красный; рыжий
**Type:** color
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *redder*, *reddest*
**Contrast:** Double the *d*: *redder*, *reddest*. Red hair is *red* (рыжий) in American English; *ginger* is mainly British and *orange hair* means dyed. In US politics *red* means Republican and *blue* means Democratic, the opposite of the old link between red and the left, so *a red state* is a conservative one. *In the red* (в минусе) uses the noun.

Red has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Having the color of blood or ripe tomatoes.** (красный)

1. She wore a *red* dress to the party.
2. Stop when the light turns *red*.
3. These tomatoes are *redder* than the ones from the store.

**2. Of hair or fur: orange-brown.** (рыжий)

4. Both of their kids have *red* hair.
5. A *red* fox ran across the road.

**3. Of the face or eyes: red because of anger, embarrassment, crying or tiredness.** (покрасневший, красный)

6. He turned *red* when she mentioned his name.
7. Her eyes were *red* from crying.
8. His face got *redder* and *redder*.

**4. US politics: voting mostly for the Republican Party.** (республиканский (о штате, округе))

9. Wyoming is one of the *reddest* states in the country.
10. The rural counties are deep *red*.

### happy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhæpi/ &middot; *HAP-ee*
**Русский:** счастливый; довольный; радостный
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — but *happy* in greetings (*Happy birthday*) and meaning lucky (*a happy accident*) only before a noun
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *happier*, *happiest*
**Takes:** *with* or *about* + noun (satisfied); *for* + person (glad for their sake); *to*-infinitive (glad, willing); *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [happy about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#happy-about)
**Opposite:** *unhappy*, *sad*
**Contrast:** *y* becomes *i*: *happier*, *happiest*, *happily*, *happiness*; *more happy* is rare. *Happy to* often means willing (*I'm happy to help* = охотно помогу), not счастлив. *Happy for you* = рад за тебя; *happy with* = доволен (*I'm happy with my new car*). Russian счастливый meaning lucky (*счастливый билет*) is *lucky*, not *happy*.

Happy has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Feeling pleasure, joy or contentment.** (счастливый, радостный)

1. The kids looked so *happy* on Christmas morning.
2. We're very *happy* together.
3. I've never been *happier*.

**2. Satisfied with something.** (довольный)

4. Are you *happy* with the service?
5. My boss wasn't *happy* about the delay.

**3. Pleased about something, or willing to do something.** (рад; охотно готов)

6. I'd be *happy* to show you around.
7. We're *happy* that you could come.

**4. Glad because something good has happened to someone else.** (рад (за кого-то))

8. I'm so *happy* for you both!
9. We were *happy* for her when she got the job.

**5. Bringing or showing happiness.** (счастливый, радостный)

10. The movie has a *happy* ending.
11. That was the *happiest* day of my life.

**6. Used in good wishes for a holiday or special day.** (с (праздником, днём рождения))

12. *Happy* birthday, Mom!
13. *Happy* holidays from all of us.

**7. Lucky and fortunate.** (удачный, счастливый)

14. Meeting her there was a *happy* accident.
15. By a *happy* coincidence, we were on the same flight.

### funny

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfʌni/ &middot; *FUHN-ee*
**Русский:** смешной; забавный; странный
**Type:** evaluative; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *funnier*, *funniest*
**Contrast:** *y* becomes *i*: *funnier*, *funniest*. *Funny* is not *fun*: *funny* makes you laugh (смешной) or is strange (странный), while *fun* means enjoyable (весёлый, *a fun party*). *The trip was funny* means something made you laugh, not that you had a good time. Because *funny* can also mean odd, Americans sometimes ask *funny ha-ha or funny weird?*

Funny has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Making you laugh.** (смешной, забавный)

1. He told a really *funny* story about his first job.
2. She's the *funniest* person I know.
3. The sequel is even *funnier* than the original.

**2. Strange or surprising.** (странный, чудной)

4. The car is making a *funny* noise.
