Adjectives that are easily confused#

economic and economical, historic and historical, continual and continuous.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Pairs built on the same root that mean different things: economic is about the economy, economical is about saving money; historic means important in history, historical means about the past. Native speakers mix some of these up too, but in writing they matter, and each entry names its partner and the test that separates them.

economic#

Pronunciation
/ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪk/ · ek-uh-NAH-mik
Русский
экономический; рентабельный
Type
classifying — about the economy; also quality in the profitable sense
Position
both — mostly before a noun (economic growth); after be to name the kind of problem (The reasons were economic) and in the profitable sense (It isn't economic to repair it)
Comparison
not gradable — in the economy sense; more economic occurs only in the profitable sense
Opposite
uneconomic (in the profitable sense)
Contrast
economic is about the economy; economical is about spending little. Test: if you can say cheap to run or saves money, use economical: an economical car (экономичный автомобиль), not an economic car. economic growth, an economic crisis (экономический кризис), never an economical crisis. Russian экономический = economic; экономичный and экономный = economical. The profitable sense (It isn't economic to fix it) is the one place the two come close. Russian экономика is two English nouns: the economy (экономика страны) and economics (экономика как наука). Both first syllables are heard in American speech: ek-uh- and ee-kuh-.

Economic has 2 distinct senses.

1. Relating to the economy of a country or region — trade, industry, jobs and money — or to economics as a subject. (экономический)

  1. The country is going through a period of slow economic growth.
  2. Many families left the town for economic reasons.
  3. Their problems are economic, not political.

2. Making enough money to be worth doing; profitable — mostly in business and energy writing. (рентабельный, выгодный)

  1. The mine closed because it was no longer economic to run.
  2. At today's prices, it isn't economic to repair an old TV.
  3. Solar power is getting more economic every year.

economical#

Pronunciation
/ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪkəl/ · ek-uh-NAH-mih-kuhl
Русский
экономичный; экономный, бережливый
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both — an economical car, The car is economical
Comparison
more/most — more economical, most economical
Takes
with + noun = using little of (economical with words); it's economical to + verb
Form
-al from economic
Opposite
uneconomical, wasteful
Contrast
See economic. economical never means about the economy: economic policy, not economical policy; economic reforms (экономические реформы), not economical reforms. In everyday American speech people often say cheap to run or fuel-efficient about machines and thrifty or careful with money about people. economical with the truth (не договаривающий, лукавящий) is a polite, originally British way to say someone is lying by leaving things out.

Economical has 3 distinct senses.

1. Cheap to use or run, because it uses little money, fuel or other resources. (экономичный, выгодный)

  1. Small cars are usually more economical to run.
  2. Buying rice in bulk is the most economical way to shop.
  3. The new furnace is quiet and economical.

2. Of a person: careful not to spend or waste money. (экономный, бережливый)

  1. My grandmother was an economical cook who wasted nothing.
  2. We have to be economical until I find a new job.

3. Using no more words, movements or effort than are needed. (сжатый, лаконичный; скупой (о движениях))

  1. He writes in a clean, economical style.
  2. The pitcher has a smooth, economical delivery.
  3. Her essay is short and economical: every sentence does a job.

historic#

Pronunciation
/hɪˈstɔːrɪk/ · hih-STOR-ik
Русский
исторический, знаменательный; старинный
Type
evaluative; also classifying
Position
both — mostly before a noun (a historic day); after be too (This moment is historic)
Comparison
more/most — more historic, most historic
Contrast
historic = important in history, or famous for its past: a historic victory (историческая победа), a historic building. historical = about the past or the study of it: historical records (исторические документы), a historical novel (исторический роман). Test: if you can say famous or memorable, use historic; if you can say from the past or about the past, use historical. a historical event simply happened; a historic event changed things. Russian исторический covers both, so decide each time. Americans say a historic with the h pronounced; an historic sounds old-fashioned or British. In historic lows and historic times the two words overlap, and both are used.

Historic has 4 distinct senses.

1. Important in history, or likely to be remembered as important. (исторический, знаменательный)

  1. The two leaders signed a historic peace agreement.
  2. It was a historic day for the city when the team finally won.
  3. Fans called it the most historic game they had ever seen.

2. Old and interesting because of its past, and worth preserving. (старинный, исторический (о зданиях, районах))

  1. We stayed at a historic inn built in 1790.
  2. The historic district is full of brick row houses.
  3. Charleston is famous for its historic homes.

3. Of a level or amount: the highest or lowest ever recorded. (рекордный)

  1. Mortgage rates fell to historic lows that spring.
  2. Home prices reached historic highs last year.

4. Belonging to the time of written history, as opposed to prehistoric times — mostly in historic times. (исторический (в отличие от доисторического))

  1. The lake has never dried up in historic times.
  2. Only two volcanoes in the region have erupted in historic times.

historical#

Pronunciation
/hɪˈstɔːrɪkəl/ · hih-STOR-ih-kuhl
Русский
исторический (относящийся к прошлому)
Type
classifying
Position
both — almost always before a noun (historical records); after be mainly in the really existed sense (Is the story historical or made up?)
Comparison
not gradable
Contrast
See historic. historical is the neutral word for anything connected with the past or with history as a subject; it never means famous or memorable: a historic moment (исторический момент), not a historical moment. historical before data, prices or trends just means from past years. Russian исторический covers both words.

Historical has 4 distinct senses.

1. Relating to history or the study of history; about the past. (исторический)

  1. The museum has a large collection of historical documents.
  2. She's doing historical research on the Civil War.
  3. The town's historical society runs tours every Saturday.

2. Of a book, movie or painting: telling a story set in the past. (исторический (о жанре))

  1. My mom loves historical novels set in the Old West.
  2. The show is a historical drama about the first English settlers.

3. Really having lived or happened in the past, not only in stories. (реально существовавший, исторически достоверный)

  1. Scholars still argue about whether King Arthur was a historical figure.
  2. The movie mixes historical fact with fiction.
  3. Is the story historical, or did the author make it up?

4. Based on figures from past years, as opposed to current ones. (прошлый, за прошлые периоды; ретроспективный)

  1. Historical data shows that prices rise every December.
  2. The model predicts sales from historical trends.

classic#

Pronunciation
/ˈklæsɪk/ · KLAS-ik
Русский
классический, образцовый; типичный
Type
evaluative; also quality
Position
both — mostly before a noun (a classic mistake); after be in the style and informal senses (Her look is classic, That's classic!)
Comparison
more/most — more classic, most classic
Contrast
classic = excellent and lasting, typical, or traditional in style: a classic movie, a classic mistake (типичная ошибка), a classic suit. classical = of ancient Greece and Rome, of serious Western music, or of an old established system: classical architecture, classical music (классическая музыка), classical ballet. Test: if you can say typical or great and lasting, use classic; if you can say ancient or traditional art form, use classical. classic music is a mistake for classical music, and a classical example for a classic example. Russian классический covers both. a classic (a great book or movie) and the classics (Greek and Latin literature) are nouns.

Classic has 5 distinct senses.

1. Of the highest quality and admired for a long time; one of the best of its kind. (классический, признанный, образцовый)

  1. It's a classic American novel that every teenager reads.
  2. The band played all their classic hits.
  3. We watched a classic Hitchcock movie last night.

2. Very typical; a perfect example of its kind. (типичный, хрестоматийный)

  1. Forgetting to back up your files is a classic mistake.
  2. He showed all the classic signs of a heart attack.
  3. It's a classic case of too little, too late.

3. Simple and traditional in style, so it does not go out of fashion. (классический (о стиле), строгий)

  1. You can't go wrong with a classic white shirt.
  2. Her style is more classic than her sister's.

4. Of cars, music or TV shows: from an earlier period and still loved today. (ретро, винтажный, классический)

  1. He spends every weekend restoring classic cars.
  2. The station plays classic rock all day.
  3. We grew up watching classic cartoons on Saturday mornings.

5. Informal: very funny, or exactly what you would expect of someone. (в своём репертуаре; ну это классика (разг.))

  1. He fell asleep at his own party? That's classic.
  2. Showing up an hour late is classic Mike.

classical#

Pronunciation
/ˈklæsɪkəl/ · KLAS-ih-kuhl
Русский
классический; античный; академический
Type
classifying; also quality
Position
both — almost always before a noun (classical music); after be mostly in the style sense (The lines of the building are classical)
Comparison
not gradable — except in the style sense: more classical
Contrast
See classic. classical music covers everything from Bach to modern concert music, not only the Classical period of Haydn and Mozart. classical never means typical: a classic example, not a classical example. Nor does it mean old and still loved about cars or rock: classic cars, classic rock.

Classical has 4 distinct senses.

1. Relating to ancient Greece and Rome, their languages, art and ideas. (античный, классический)

  1. She studied classical Greek and Latin in college.
  2. The Capitol is built in the classical style, with tall columns.
  3. Many classical writers told stories about the gods of Olympus.

2. Of music: in the serious Western tradition of composers like Bach and Beethoven, not pop, rock or jazz. (классический (о музыке), академический)

  1. He listens to classical music while he works.
  2. She trained as a classical pianist.
  3. The classical station plays Mozart every morning.

3. Traditional and long established, as opposed to newer or modern forms. (классический, традиционный)

  1. She trained in classical ballet before switching to jazz dance.
  2. Classical physics can't explain how atoms behave.
  3. Modern Arabic grew out of Classical Arabic.

4. Simple, balanced and elegant, like the art of ancient Greece. (классический, строгий, гармоничный)

  1. The actress was known for her classical beauty.
  2. The new library is more classical in design than the old one.

electric#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈlɛktrɪk/ · ih-LEK-trik
Русский
электрический; наэлектризованный, волнующий
Type
classifying; also quality
Position
both — before a noun or after be in the powered by electricity sense (an electric stove, Is the stove gas or electric?); mostly after a verb in the exciting sense (The mood was electric)
Comparison
not gradable — except in the exciting sense: more electric, most electric
Contrast
electric is for a thing that runs on electricity or carries it: an electric toothbrush, an electric shock. electrical is the general word for electricity as a system, a subject or a kind of fault: electrical wiring, an electrical engineer, an electrical fire. Test: name one machine and use electric; name a category, a job or a problem and use electrical. Many phrases take only one: electric guitar, electric chair; electrical engineering, electrical storm. Only electric has the exciting sense. Americans say the electric bill and the electric company, where British English says the electricity bill. Neither is electronic (электронный: using chips, as in electronic devices). Russian электрик (the worker) is electrician; the color электрик is electric blue.

Electric has 4 distinct senses.

1. Working by electricity, or carrying or producing it. (электрический, электро-)

  1. We just bought an electric car.
  2. The dog got a shock from the electric fence.
  3. Is your stove gas or electric?

2. Relating to the supply of electricity to homes and businesses. (за электричество; энергетический (о компании))

  1. Our electric bill doubled in July.
  2. The electric company shut off their power.

3. Full of excitement and energy; thrilling. (наэлектризованный, волнующий, захватывающий)

  1. The atmosphere in the stadium was electric.
  2. The crowd was even more electric in the second half.
  3. She gave an electric performance on opening night.

4. Of a color: very bright and strong. (яркий; (цвет) электрик)

  1. She wore an electric blue dress to the prom.
  2. The walls were painted an electric green.

electrical#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈlɛktrɪkəl/ · ih-LEK-trih-kuhl
Русский
электрический; электротехнический
Type
classifying
Position
both — almost always before a noun (electrical wiring); after be to name the kind of fault (The problem is electrical)
Comparison
not gradable
Contrast
See electric. electrical usually names a category (electrical equipment) or a field (electrical engineering); electric names the machine (an electric heater). an electrical heater is not wrong, but an electric heater is what people say, and electrical engineering is fixed: never electric engineering. electrical has no exciting sense: an electric atmosphere, not an electrical atmosphere.

Electrical has 2 distinct senses.

1. Using, carrying or involving electricity; relating to electricity in general. (электрический, электро-)

  1. Unplug all electrical appliances before you leave.
  2. An electrical fire started in the basement.
  3. The problem with the car turned out to be electrical.

2. Relating to the science of electricity or to work with it as a job. (электротехнический)

  1. He has a degree in electrical engineering.
  2. Hire a licensed electrical contractor to move the outlet.

alternate#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːltərnət/ · AWL-tur-nuht
Русский
запасной, другой; каждый второй; чередующийся
Type
classifying
Position
attributive only — on alternate days, an alternate route
Comparison
not gradable
Form
from the verb alternate
Contrast
Pronunciation: the adjective is AWL-tur-nuht; the verb to alternate is AWL-tur-nayt. Only alternate means every other: on alternate days (через день). Only alternative means unconventional: alternative medicine, alternative rock, never alternate medicine. For another choice American English uses both: an alternate route and an alternative route; some editors and British readers accept only alternative, so prefer it in formal writing. an alternate is also a noun: a substitute juror, delegate or player. Russian альтернативный is usually alternative; через день is every other day or on alternate days.

Alternate has 4 distinct senses.

1. Available as another choice, in place of the usual or planned one. (запасной, другой, альтернативный)

  1. Take an alternate route if the bridge is closed.
  2. We need an alternate plan in case it rains.
  3. The judge replaced the sick juror with an alternate juror.

2. Happening on every second day, week or other unit; every other. (каждый второй, через один)

  1. The class meets on alternate Tuesdays.
  2. The kids spend alternate weekends with their dad.
  3. Write your answers on alternate lines.

3. Of two things: coming one after the other, again and again. (чередующийся, перемежающийся)

  1. Arrange alternate layers of pasta and cheese in the dish.
  2. The flag has alternate red and white stripes.

4. Existing as another version of events or of the world. (параллельный, альтернативный)

  1. The show is set in an alternate universe where dinosaurs never died out.
  2. She writes alternate history novels.

alternative#

Pronunciation
/ɔːlˈtɜːrnətɪv/ · awl-TUR-nuh-tiv
Русский
альтернативный, другой; нетрадиционный
Type
classifying
Position
both — almost always before a noun (an alternative plan); after a verb only in the informal unconventional sense (Her taste in music is pretty alternative)
Comparison
not gradable — except in the informal unconventional sense: more alternative
Contrast
See alternate. The noun an alternative (вариант, выбор) is more common than the adjective: There's no alternative, an alternative to surgery; the to belongs to the noun. alternative never means every other: on alternate days, not on alternative days. The stress is on the second syllable: awl-TUR-nuh-tiv.

Alternative has 3 distinct senses.

1. Available as another choice; able to be used instead of something else. (альтернативный, другой, запасной)

  1. If the flight is canceled, the airline will book you on an alternative flight.
  2. We need to find an alternative way to pay for the project.
  3. Police posted alternative routes around the parade.

2. Different from what is usual or traditional, and often chosen as a rejection of it. (альтернативный, нетрадиционный, неформальный)

  1. More patients are trying alternative medicine like acupuncture.
  2. She listened to alternative rock in high school.
  3. Her taste in music is pretty alternative.

3. Of energy or fuel: not coming from oil, coal or natural gas. (альтернативный, возобновляемый)

  1. The state is investing in alternative energy such as wind and solar.
  2. City buses run on alternative fuels.

childish#

Pronunciation
/ˈtʃaɪldɪʃ/ · CHYL-dish
Русский
ребяческий, инфантильный; детский
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both — a childish remark, Don't be childish
Comparison
more/most — more childish, most childish
Form
-ish from child — like a child, in a bad way
Opposite
mature, grown-up
Contrast
childish criticizes: an older person acting like a small child (ребячество). childlike praises or describes: the good qualities of a child, such as trust and wonder (детская непосредственность). Test: Don't be childish is a reproach; childlike wonder is a compliment. Neither is the word for things made for children: Russian детские книги is children's books, детская одежда is kids' clothes, детская площадка is a playground.

Childish has 2 distinct senses.

1. Of an older person or their behavior: silly and immature, like a small child. (ребяческий, инфантильный, детский (неодобр.))

  1. Stop being so childish and apologize to your sister.
  2. It was a childish thing to do.
  3. The argument got more childish by the minute.

2. Typical of a child or of childhood. (детский)

  1. The note was written in big, childish handwriting.
  2. We could hear childish voices coming from the yard.

childlike#

Pronunciation
/ˈtʃaɪldlaɪk/ · CHYLD-lyk
Русский
по-детски непосредственный; похожий на ребёнка
Type
quality
Position
both — childlike wonder, Her voice sounded childlike
Comparison
more/most — more childlike, most childlike
Form
compound — child + -like
Contrast
See childish. childlike is usually positive or neutral; childish is negative. Said about an adult's mind or judgment, childlike can sound patronizing (a childlike understanding of money), so use it with care.

Childlike has 2 distinct senses.

1. Having the good qualities of a child, such as innocence, trust and enthusiasm. (по-детски непосредственный, простодушный, наивный)

  1. My grandfather never lost his childlike sense of wonder.
  2. She has an almost childlike trust in people.
  3. He was more childlike than any of the kids he taught.

2. Looking, sounding or moving like a child. (детский, похожий на ребёнка)

  1. The robot has a childlike face with big round eyes.
  2. She spoke in a soft, childlike voice.

continual#

Pronunciation
/kənˈtɪnjuəl/ · kuhn-TIN-yoo-uhl
Русский
постоянный, беспрестанный (с перерывами)
Type
quality
Position
attributive only — continual delays; after be it sounds odd, so use constant: The noise was constant
Comparison
not gradable
Form
-al from continue
Contrast
continual = happening again and again, with breaks: continual phone calls (звонки один за другим). continuous = without any break: continuous rain (непрерывный дождь). Test: a dripping faucet is continual; a running faucet is continuous. Careful writers keep this line; in general use continual also covers without stopping (sense 2), and constant covers both. Only continuous works for space (a continuous line) and grammar (the present continuous). Russian постоянный is most often constant.

Continual has 2 distinct senses.

1. Happening again and again, often in an annoying way. (постоянный, беспрестанный, повторяющийся)

  1. I'm tired of your continual complaining.
  2. The project suffered from continual delays.
  3. Continual interruptions made it impossible to work.

2. Going on for a long time without stopping. (непрерывный, непрекращающийся)

  1. He lived in continual pain after the accident.
  2. The city was under continual attack for three months.

continuous#

Pronunciation
/kənˈtɪnjuəs/ · kuhn-TIN-yoo-uhs
Русский
непрерывный, сплошной
Type
quality
Position
both — continuous rain, The noise was continuous
Comparison
not gradable
Form
-ous from continue
Opposite
intermittent; broken
Contrast
See continual. continuous never means repeated with breaks: continual interruptions, not continuous interruptions, unless there was no pause at all. In grammar, American textbooks usually say progressive (the present progressive) where British ones say continuous; both mean the be + -ing form.

Continuous has 3 distinct senses.

1. Going on without a pause or break. (непрерывный, беспрерывный)

  1. Patients in the ICU need continuous monitoring.
  2. The noise from the highway is continuous, day and night.
  3. The company is committed to continuous improvement.

2. Without gaps in space; forming an unbroken line or surface. (сплошной, непрерывный)

  1. Draw one continuous line without lifting your pen.
  2. A continuous fence runs along the highway.

3. In grammar: formed with be + -ing to show an action in progress; progressive. (продолженный (о времени))

  1. I am reading is in the present continuous.
  2. The past continuous describes what was happening at a moment in the past.

efficient#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈfɪʃənt/ · ih-FISH-uhnt
Русский
эффективный (без потерь), экономичный; расторопный
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both — an efficient system, The system is efficient
Comparison
more/most — more efficient, most efficient
Form
also in compounds: energy-efficient, fuel-efficient, cost-efficient
Opposite
inefficient
Contrast
efficient = doing something without wasting time, money or energy; effective = getting the result you want. Test: a medicine that cures you is effective; a factory that makes more cars with less power is efficient. A thing can be one without the other: a sledgehammer is an effective way to crack a nut, but not an efficient one. Russian эффективный covers both, so ask whether you mean it works (effective) or it works without waste (efficient).

Efficient has 2 distinct senses.

1. Working well without wasting time, money or energy. (эффективный (без потерь), экономичный, производительный)

  1. LED bulbs are much more efficient than old light bulbs.
  2. We need an efficient way to handle all these orders.
  3. The new trains are fast and efficient.

2. Of a person: organized and able to get work done quickly and well. (расторопный, толковый, деловитый)

  1. Our office manager is incredibly efficient.
  2. An efficient waiter kept our glasses full all night.

effective#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈfɛktɪv/ · ih-FEK-tiv
Русский
эффективный, действенный; фактический; вступающий в силу
Type
evaluative; also classifying
Position
both — an effective drug, The drug is effective; before a noun only in the actual sense (in effective control); after be, or opening a phrase, in the starting sense (effective immediately)
Comparison
more/most — more effective, most effective
Takes
in or at + -ing (effective in reducing pain); against + disease or pest (effective against the flu); for + a problem (effective for back pain); in the starting sense as of + date, or a bare date (effective May 1)
Opposite
ineffective
Contrast
See efficient: effective is about the result, efficient about the waste. Russian эффектный (showy, striking) is usually striking, impressive or eye-catching: an eye-catching dress (эффектное платье); effective in that sense (sense 2) is limited to how something works in a design or a picture. effective immediately, effective May 1, with no from, is the usual American way to give a start date. The adverb effectively often means in effect, actually: He effectively ran the company.

Effective has 4 distinct senses.

1. Producing the result that is wanted; working well. (эффективный, действенный, результативный)

  1. Exercise is one of the most effective ways to reduce stress.
  2. The vaccine is highly effective against the virus.
  3. She's a very effective teacher.

2. Making a strong and pleasing impression; striking. (эффектный, производящий впечатление)

  1. The red door looks very effective against the gray brick.
  2. The dark walls make an effective background for the paintings.

3. Real in practice, though not in name or on paper. (фактический, реальный)

  1. The general was the effective ruler of the country.
  2. Rebels are in effective control of the north.
  3. After deductions, their effective tax rate was 12 percent.

4. Officially in use or in force, starting from a stated time. (вступающий в силу, действующий)

  1. The new rules are effective as of January 1.
  2. She resigned, effective immediately.
  3. The price increase becomes effective next month.

imaginary#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈmædʒənɛri/ · ih-MAJ-uh-nair-ee
Русский
воображаемый, вымышленный, мнимый
Type
classifying
Position
both — an imaginary friend, The danger was imaginary
Comparison
not gradable
Opposite
real
Contrast
imaginary = not real: an imaginary enemy (воображаемый враг). imaginative = good at imagining, creative: an imaginative writer (писатель с богатым воображением). Test: an imaginary story isn't true; an imaginative story is original. imaginable = possible to imagine, and it often follows its noun or a superlative: every color imaginable, the worst thing imaginable. Russian мнимый in the sense supposed can be imaginary (an imaginary threat), but мнимый друг is a false friend.
