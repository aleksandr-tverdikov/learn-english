Extreme and absolute adjectives#

freezing, huge, delicious, unique, dead — adjectives that already mean very, or that are simply true or not.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

Extreme adjectives already contain very — freezing means very cold — so they take absolutely, completely or really, never very: absolutely freezing, not very freezing. Absolute adjectives name a state that is either true or not (dead, unique, pregnant) and resist comparison, although real American usage bends that rule, and the entries say where.

huge#

Pronunciation
/hjuːdʒ/ · HYOOJ
Русский
огромный
Type
size
Position
both
Comparison
not gradable — extreme, it already means very big: absolutely huge, really huge; huger and hugest exist but are rare — say much bigger or the biggest
Contrast
Huge already contains very, so very huge sounds like a learner: say really huge, absolutely huge or simply very big. A huge fan is someone who likes something a lot, not a large person.

Huge has 2 distinct senses.

1. Extremely large in size, amount or degree. (огромный, громадный)

  1. They live in a huge house outside Dallas.
  2. The difference in price was huge.
  3. Thanks — that's a huge help.

2. Very popular, successful or important, informal. (очень популярный; очень важный)

  1. The band is still huge in Japan.
  2. Podcasts got huge in just a few years.
  3. Winning that contract is huge for a small company like ours.

freezing#

Pronunciation
/ˈfriːzɪŋ/ · FREE-zing
Русский
ледяной; очень холодный
Type
quality
Position
both — sense 3 only before a noun (freezing rain, freezing temperatures)
Comparison
not gradable — extreme: absolutely freezing, not very freezing
Form
-ing form of freeze
Contrast
Extreme, so It's absolutely freezing, not very freezing. I'm freezing means I feel very cold; frozen food or a frozen lake has actually turned to ice. In freezing cold the word works like an adverb meaning extremely (freezing cold water). In the United States the freezing point in a forecast is 32°F.

Freezing has 3 distinct senses.

1. Extremely cold, of the weather, a place, water or a part of the body. (ледяной, морозный, очень холодный)

  1. It's freezing out there — wear a hat.
  2. We jumped into the freezing water of Lake Superior.
  3. My hands are freezing.

2. Feeling very cold, of a person. (замёрзший (мне очень холодно))

  1. Can you close the window? I'm freezing.
  2. The kids came in from the snow freezing and hungry.

3. At or below 32°F (0°C), the temperature at which water turns to ice. (минусовой; ледяной (о дожде))

  1. Freezing temperatures are expected in Atlanta tonight.
  2. Drive carefully — there's freezing rain on the roads.

Extreme, so not very boiling. Boiling water is still bubbling (Russian кипяток); boiled water has been boiled and may have cooled (кипячёная вода), and a boiled egg is cooked. In boiling hot and boiling mad the word works like an adverb meaning extremely.

  3. We waited two hours on a boiling sidewalk in Phoenix.

2. Heated to the point where it bubbles and turns to steam. (кипящий)

  1. Drop the pasta into boiling water.
  2. Pour the boiling water over the tea bags and wait three minutes.

3. Wanting something very much because you have not had enough of it. (изголодавшийся (по чему-либо))

  1. Small towns are starving for good jobs.
  2. He was starving for someone to talk to.

awful#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːfəl/ · AW-fuhl
Русский
ужасный
Type
evaluative; also emphasizing
Position
both — the 'very great' sense only before a noun (an awful lot)
Comparison
more/most — more awful, most awful; really awful or absolutely awful rather than very awful
Takes
about + noun or -ing — guilty or sorry (feel awful about it); to a person — unkind (awful to her); that-clause after it (It's awful that he lost his job)
Form
awe + -ful, with the e dropped
Contrast
Awful and awesome both come from awe but now mean opposite things: awful is very bad, awesome is great. Americans also use awful as an adverb in speech (awful tired, awful nice); that is informal — in writing use awfully or really. An awful lot just means a very large amount and is not negative. Spelling: awful, never aweful.

Awful has 4 distinct senses.

1. Very bad or unpleasant. (ужасный, отвратительный)

  1. The traffic on I-95 was awful this morning.
  2. He was awful to his little sister.
  3. It was the most awful week of my life.

2. Ill; not well. (плохо (о самочувствии))

  1. I feel awful — I think I'm getting the flu.
  2. You look awful. Go home and rest.

3. Guilty or sorry about something. (ужасно стыдно, неловко)

  1. I feel awful about forgetting your birthday.
  2. She felt awful that she couldn't help.

4. Very great in amount or degree, used for emphasis before a noun. (ужасно много; страшный)

  1. That's an awful lot of money for a used car.
  2. He's gone to an awful lot of trouble for us.

dead#

Pronunciation
/dɛd/ · DED
Русский
мёртвый
Type
quality; also emphasizing
Position
both — the 'complete, exact' sense only before a noun (dead silence)
Comparison
not gradable — absolute: completely dead, almost dead; deader appears only in jokes
Contrast
Dead is the adjective, died the past of the verb, death the noun: He is dead or He died last year, never He is died. Something that kills is deadly, not dead (a deadly disease). As an adverb dead means completely or exactly — dead tired, dead serious, dead ahead — and has nothing to do with death. For a leg or foot that has lost feeling Americans more often say it fell asleep.

Dead has 8 distinct senses.

1. No longer alive. (мёртвый, умерший)

  1. Her grandfather has been dead for ten years.
  2. We found a dead bird on the porch.
  3. Water the plants or they'll be dead by Friday.

2. Not working, especially because there is no power. (разряженный; не работающий)

  1. My phone's dead — can I borrow your charger?
  2. The car wouldn't start because the battery was dead.
  3. The line suddenly went dead.

3. Very quiet, with no activity, energy or feeling. (вымерший, безжизненный)

  1. Downtown is dead after six o'clock.
  2. Business has been dead all week.
  3. He answered in a flat, dead voice.

4. No longer used, active or likely to happen. (мёртвый, отживший, провалившийся)

  1. Latin is a dead language.
  2. After the vote, the bill is dead.

5. Unable to feel anything, of a part of the body. (онемевший)

  1. I slept on my arm, and now it's completely dead.
  2. My legs went dead from sitting on the floor.

6. Complete or exact, used before a noun. (полный, абсолютный; точный)

  1. There was dead silence in the courtroom.
  2. The arrow hit the dead center of the target.
  3. The train came to a dead stop.

7. In serious trouble, informal. ((тебе) конец, крышка)

  1. If Mom sees this mess, we're dead.
  2. You're dead when Coach finds out.

8. Extremely tired, informal. (смертельно уставший)

  1. After the double shift I was dead.
  2. We were dead on our feet by the time the movers left.

unique#

Pronunciation
/juˈniːk/ · yoo-NEEK
Русский
уникальный, единственный в своём роде
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
not gradable — absolute in careful use: completely unique, almost unique; very unique and more unique are common in speech in sense 2, but editors and exams mark them wrong
Takes
to + noun — found only in or with (unique to Hawaii)
Contrast
Unique begins with a y sound, so it takes a: a unique chance, not an unique chance. In careful writing it means the only one of its kind, which cannot be more or less true, so very unique and rather unique are marked as errors; when you mean special, say unusual, rare or distinctive.

Unique has 3 distinct senses.

1. Being the only one of its kind; unlike anything else. (единственный в своём роде, уникальный)

  1. Every snowflake is unique.
  2. Each user gets a unique password.
  3. This is a unique opportunity to work with NASA scientists.

2. Unusual or special, informal. (необычный, особенный)

  1. The restaurant has a really unique atmosphere.
  2. Her sense of style is pretty unique.

3. Belonging only to one particular person, place, group or thing. (свойственный только (кому-либо, чему-либо))

  1. These birds are unique to the Galápagos Islands.
  2. The problem isn't unique to our school.

perfect#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɜːrfɪkt/ · PUR-fikt
Русский
идеальный; совершенный
Type
evaluative; also emphasizing
Position
both — senses 3 and 4 only before a noun (a perfect stranger, the perfect tenses)
Comparison
not gradable — absolute in careful use: absolutely perfect, almost perfect; more perfect has a long history (a more perfect Union, in the U.S. Constitution)
Takes
for + noun or -ing — exactly right for (perfect for a picnic)
Contrast
The adjective is stressed on the first syllable (PUR-fikt); the verb to perfect is stressed on the second (pur-FEKT). Perfect already means as good as possible, so very perfect is wrong; almost perfect and absolutely perfect are fine. In a perfect stranger and perfect sense it only adds emphasis — a perfect stranger is someone you have never met, not an ideal one.

Perfect has 4 distinct senses.

1. Having no mistakes or faults; as good as it can be. (идеальный, безупречный)

  1. She got a perfect score on the SAT.
  2. Nobody's perfect.
  3. It was a perfect fall day.

2. Exactly right for a particular person, purpose or situation. (идеально подходящий)

  1. This dress is perfect for the wedding.
  2. You'd be perfect for the job.
  3. Six o'clock? That's perfect — thanks!

3. Complete, used for emphasis before a noun. (совершенный, полный)

  1. I told my life story to a perfect stranger on the plane.
  2. What he said makes perfect sense.

4. In grammar, describing a verb tense made with a form of have and a past participle, which shows a completed action. (перфектный)

  1. English has three perfect tenses: past, present and future.
  2. In perfect verb forms the helping verb is always a form of have.

impossible#

Pronunciation
/ɪmˈpɑːsəbəl/ · im-PAH-suh-buhl
Русский
невозможный; невыносимый
Type
quality
Position
both
Comparison
not gradable — absolute: completely impossible, almost impossible, nearly impossible, not very impossible
Takes
to-infinitive (impossible to find); for someone + to-infinitive (impossible for me to leave); that-clause after it (It's impossible that she knew)
Form
im- + possible
Contrast
The situation, not the person, is impossible: It's impossible for me to come, not I am impossible to come. A person can be the subject only when the infinitive is about dealing with them (He's impossible to talk to), or on its own in sense 2 (My brother is impossible). Almost and nearly impossible are the natural way to soften it; very impossible is wrong.

Impossible has 2 distinct senses.

1. Not able to happen, exist or be done. (невозможный)

  1. It's impossible to find parking downtown on Saturdays.
  2. Finishing the whole report by Friday is impossible.
  3. The snow made travel almost impossible.

2. Very difficult to deal with or bear, of a person or situation. (невыносимый, несносный)

  1. The kids have been impossible all day.
  2. Her boss put her in an impossible position.

essential#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈsɛnʃəl/ · ih-SEN-shuhl
Русский
необходимый; существенный
Type
quality
Position
both — sense 2 mostly before a noun; sense 3 only in essential oil
Comparison
not gradable — absolutely essential, not very essential; most essential is common in speech in the sense 'most basic'
Takes
to or for + noun (essential to success, essential for growth); to-infinitive after it (It's essential to book early); that-clause, often with the base form of the verb (It's essential that he be there)
Preposition patterns
essential to
Contrast
Essential is much stronger than important: it means you cannot do without it. After It is essential that, formal American English uses the base verb: It's essential that every student attend, not attends. Essential oil is Russian эфирное масло, and the essentials (a noun) are the basic things you need.

Essential has 3 distinct senses.

1. Completely necessary. (необходимый, крайне важный)

  1. Water is essential for life.
  2. Essential workers like nurses and grocery clerks kept working through the pandemic.
  3. It's essential that you bring your passport.

2. Basic and most important; forming the central part of something. (основной, существенный)

  1. The essential difference between the two plans is cost.
  2. Honesty is an essential part of her character.

3. Taken from a plant and carrying its smell or flavor, of an oil. (эфирный (о масле))

  1. She adds a few drops of lavender essential oil to her bath.
  2. Essential oils should never be swallowed.

correct#

Pronunciation
/kəˈrɛkt/ · kuh-REKT
Русский
правильный; надлежащий
Type
evaluative
Position
both
Comparison
not gradable — absolute in the 'right' sense, but more correct and most correct are common when comparing how accurate something is (the most correct answer)
Takes
in + -ing — right to think so (correct in assuming); to-infinitive (You were correct to call)
Contrast
False friend: Russian корректный usually means polite or tactful — in English say polite, courteous or respectful. Correct has 3 distinct senses.

  1. Is this the correct address?
  2. Your answer is correct.

2. Right in what you think or say, of a person. (прав)

  1. You're absolutely correct.
  2. Am I correct in thinking you two have met?

3. Following the accepted rules of behavior or of a situation. (подобающий, надлежащий)

  1. What's the correct way to address a senator?
  2. His manners were always correct but cold.

complete#

Pronunciation
/kəmˈpliːt/ · kuhm-PLEET
Русский
полный; законченный
Type
quality; also emphasizing
Position
both — sense 3 only before a noun (a complete stranger); complete with follows its noun
Comparison
not gradable — absolute when it means finished, but more complete and most complete are normal for having more of the parts (a more complete list)
Takes
with + noun — including something extra (a kitchen complete with dishwasher)
Contrast
Complete is both an adjective and a verb: The work is complete (done) and They completed the work. A completed form is one someone has filled out. In a complete idiot or complete nonsense it only adds emphasis, like total. Complete with is a description pattern that comes after the noun: a house complete with a pool.

Complete has 4 distinct senses.

1. Having all the parts it should have; whole. (полный, целый, комплектный)

  1. We finally have a complete set of the Harry Potter books.
  2. The list isn't complete — we're missing three names.
  3. This is the most complete collection of her letters.

2. Finished. (законченный, завершённый)

  1. Work on the new bridge is almost complete.
  2. Your registration is not complete until you pay the fee.

3. Total, used for emphasis before a noun. (полный, совершенный, абсолютный)

  1. The meeting was a complete waste of time.
  2. I felt like a complete idiot.
  3. She's a complete stranger to me.

4. Including something as an extra part, after a noun. (в комплекте с, вместе с)

  1. The apartment comes complete with a washer and dryer.
  2. He showed up dressed as Santa, complete with a white beard.

Type

Total goes before its noun: The total cost is $50, not The cost is total $50. After a verb Americans use the noun or the verb: The total is $50, The bill totals $50. Total has 2 distinct senses.

  1. The party was a total disaster.
  2. There was total silence in the room.
  3. I felt like a total fool.

full#

Pronunciation
/fʊl/ · FUUL
Русский
полный; сытый
Type
quality; also quantity
Position
both — with of it can follow the noun (a room full of people)
Comparison
-er/-est — fuller, fullest
Takes
of + noun — containing a lot (full of water, full of ideas); of oneself — too proud
Preposition patterns
full of
Contrast
False friend: Russian полный about a person often means overweight, but English I'm full means I'm not hungry any more: No dessert, thanks — I'm full. For weight say heavy or overweight; a fuller figure is only a polite fashion phrase. Full of follows its noun naturally: a box full of toys. The suffix -ful has one l: careful, spoonful. To the fullest means as much as possible (live life to the fullest).

Full has 6 distinct senses.

1. Containing as much or as many as it can hold, or a lot of something. (полный, заполненный)

  1. The parking lot was full, so we parked on the street.
  2. She handed me a glass full of ice water.
  3. The kids were full of energy.

2. Having eaten enough; not hungry. (сытый)

  1. I'm full — I can't eat another bite.
  2. Eat slowly and you'll feel full sooner.

3. Complete, whole or as great as possible. (полный, целый; максимальный)

  1. Please write your full name.
  2. The train was moving at full speed.
  3. We got a full refund.

4. Busy, with a lot of activities. (насыщенный, загруженный)

  1. I have a really full schedule this week.
  2. She lived a long and full life.

5. Round and large, of the face, lips or body; wide, of clothing. (пухлый, округлый; широкий (об одежде))

  1. She has full lips and dark eyes.
  2. The dress has a full skirt.
  3. The brand makes jeans for a fuller figure.

6. Rich and strong, of a sound, taste or color. (насыщенный, сочный)

  1. The wine has a full, fruity flavor.
  2. The cello has a fuller sound than the viola.

equal#

Pronunciation
/ˈiːkwəl/ · EE-kwuhl
Русский
равный
Type
quantity
Position
both
Comparison
not gradable — absolute; more equal is a famous joke from Orwell's Animal Farm (some animals are more equal than others)
Takes
to + noun — the same as (equal to one mile); to a task — able to do it; in + noun — the way they are the same (equal in size)
Preposition patterns
equal to
Contrast
Use equal to, not equal with, for amounts: One yard is equal to three feet. In math Americans usually use the verb: Two plus two equals four. Equal is about size, value or rights; for things that are exactly alike use the same: We have the same car, not equal cars. As a noun, an equal is a person of the same rank (She treated us as equals).

Equal has 3 distinct senses.

1. The same in size, amount, number or value. (равный, одинаковый)

  1. Cut the cake into eight equal pieces.
  2. One kilometer is equal to about 0.62 miles.
  3. The two bedrooms are equal in size.

2. Having the same rights, status or opportunities. (равноправный, равный)

  1. The Declaration of Independence says that all men are created equal.
  2. Women are still fighting for equal pay.
  3. They're equal partners in the business.

3. Having the ability or strength to deal with something. (способный справиться (с задачей))

  1. She proved equal to the challenge.
  2. I wasn't sure he was equal to the job.
