# More adjectives: V–Z

> Adjectives V–Z beyond the grammar groups — the everyday adjectives that a triage of WordNet’s adjective lemmas showed a learner needs.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Most English adjectives behave: they go before a noun or after *be*, and they compare with *-er* or *more*. These are those — but each still says how it compares, where it can stand, and what it takes. WordNet lists about twenty thousand adjectives; most of the rest are technical, archaic or British-only, and a triage pass left them out on purpose.

---
### v-shaped

**Pronunciation:** /ˈviː ʃeɪpt/ &middot; *VEE-shaypt*
**Русский:** V-образный, клиновидный
**Type:** shape; also compound
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** compound — letter + *-shaped*
**Contrast:** Written with a capital V and a hyphen: *a V-shaped valley*. Economists use it for a downturn that rebounds just as fast as it fell.

Shaped like the letter V, wide at the top and narrow at the bottom.

1. The trail follows a *V-shaped* valley.
2. She wore a sweater with a *V-shaped* neckline.
3. Geese fly in a *V-shaped* formation.

### vacant

**Pronunciation:** /ˈveɪkənt/ &middot; *VAY-kuhnt*
**Русский:** свободный, незанятый; пустующий; отсутствующий (о взгляде)
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more vacant*, *most vacant*
**Opposite:** *occupied*
**Contrast:** *Vacant* means empty and available: a *vacant* room can be taken. *Empty* only says there is nothing in it.

Vacant has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Empty and available; not occupied.** (свободный, незанятый)

1. There's a *vacant* lot behind the store.
2. Two apartments in the building are *vacant*.

**2. Of a job or a position: not filled.** (вакантный, незанятый)

3. The seat has been *vacant* since March.
4. They're advertising a *vacant* teaching position.

**3. Showing no thought or interest.** (отсутствующий, пустой (о взгляде))

5. He gave me a *vacant* stare.
6. Her expression went *vacant* for a second.

### vaccinated

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvæksəneɪtɪd/ &middot; *VAK-suh-nay-tid*
**Русский:** привитый, вакцинированный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *against* + a disease (*vaccinated against measles*)
**Form:** -ed from *vaccinate*
**Opposite:** *unvaccinated*

Having been given a vaccine to protect against a disease.

1. All the kids are fully *vaccinated*.
2. Are you *vaccinated* against hepatitis?
3. *Vaccinated* travelers skip the extra paperwork.

### vacuous

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvækjuəs/ &middot; *VAK-yoo-uhs*
**Русский:** пустой, бессодержательный; бессмысленный (о взгляде)
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more vacuous*, *most vacuous*

Vacuous has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Empty of ideas or meaning; silly.** (пустой, бессодержательный)

1. The speech was polished but *vacuous*.
2. He asked a string of *vacuous* questions.

**2. Showing no thought or feeling, of a look or a smile.** (бессмысленный, пустой)

3. She wore a *vacuous* smile the whole time.
4. His face went *vacuous* when we asked for details.

### vague

**Pronunciation:** /veɪɡ/ &middot; *VAYG*
**Русский:** неясный, расплывчатый; смутный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *vaguer*, *vaguest*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *-ing* when it describes a person (*vague about the details*)
**Opposite:** *clear*, *precise*

Vague has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Not clear or exact; short on detail.** (неясный, расплывчатый)

1. He gave a *vague* answer about the schedule.
2. Their plans are still pretty *vague*.
3. The instructions got *vaguer* the further I read.

**2. Slight; not strong or definite, of a feeling or a memory.** (смутный, неотчётливый)

4. I have a *vague* memory of the house.
5. She had a *vague* sense that something was wrong.

**3. Of a person: not giving details, or not thinking clearly.** (уклончивый; рассеянный)

6. He was *vague* about where the money went.
7. She's a little *vague* before her coffee.

### vain

**Pronunciation:** /veɪn/ &middot; *VAYN*
**Русский:** тщеславный; напрасный, тщетный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — the 'useless' sense only before a noun: *a vain attempt*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *vainer*, *vainest*
**Contrast:** Three homophones: *vain* (тщеславный), *vein* (вена) and *vane* (флюгер). *In vain* means 'without success': *They searched in vain*.

Vain has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Too proud of your looks or your abilities.** (тщеславный, самовлюблённый)

1. He's *vain* about his hair.
2. She's the *vainest* person I know.

**2. Unsuccessful; producing no result.** (напрасный, тщетный)

3. It was a *vain* attempt to save the building.
4. They made one last *vain* effort to reach him by phone.

### valiant

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvæljənt/ &middot; *VAL-yuhnt*
**Русский:** доблестный, отважный; отчаянный (о попытке)
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun
**Comparison:** more/most — *more valiant*, *most valiant*
**Contrast:** Formal or literary; everyday English says *brave*. It is very common in *a valiant effort*, which is usually said of a try that failed.

Very brave and determined, especially against long odds.

1. They made a *valiant* effort to keep the store open.
2. He was *valiant* in the face of a terrible diagnosis.
3. The firefighters got a medal for their *valiant* work.

### valid

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvælɪd/ &middot; *VAL-id*
**Русский:** действительный (о документе); обоснованный, веский
**Type:** quality; also classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more valid*, *most valid*
**Takes:** *for* + a period or a purpose (*valid for six months*)
**Opposite:** *invalid*
**Contrast:** The opposite is stressed in-VAL-id when it means 'not valid'; the noun *invalid*, a person too sick to care for themselves, is stressed IN-vuh-lid.

Valid has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Officially or legally acceptable.** (действительный, имеющий силу)

1. You need a *valid* driver's license.
2. The coupon is *valid* through Sunday.

**2. Based on good reasoning; fair.** (обоснованный, веский)

3. That's a *valid* point.
4. She had a *valid* reason for missing the exam.

### validated

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvælədeɪtɪd/ &middot; *VAL-uh-day-tid*
**Русский:** подтверждённый, проверенный; получивший признание
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *validate*

Validated has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Officially checked and confirmed.** (подтверждённый, проверенный)

1. Only *validated* results go into the report.
2. The lab uses a *validated* testing method.

**2. Feeling that your feelings or your experience have been recognized as real.** (получивший признание, услышанный)

3. She felt *validated* when the doctor finally believed her.
4. It feels good to be *validated* by people you respect.

### valuable

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvæljəbəl/ &middot; *VAL-yuh-buhl*
**Русский:** ценный, дорогой; полезный
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more valuable*, *most valuable*
**Takes:** *to* + a person (*valuable to us*); *for* + noun or *-ing*
**Opposite:** *worthless*
**Contrast:** *Invaluable* is not the opposite of *valuable* — it means so useful that no price fits: *Her advice was invaluable*. The plural noun *valuables* means jewelry, money and the like.

Valuable has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Worth a lot of money.** (ценный, дорогой)

1. The ring is quite *valuable*.
2. Lock your *valuable* equipment in the van.

**2. Very useful or important.** (полезный, важный)

3. That was *valuable* advice.
4. She's the most *valuable* person on the team.

### valued

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvæljuːd/ &middot; *VAL-yood*
**Русский:** ценимый, уважаемый
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — before a noun in *a valued customer*, after a verb in *feel valued*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ed from *value*
**Contrast:** *Valued* means appreciated by people; *valuable* means worth a lot of money. Watch for the verb as well: *a painting valued at $2 million* means someone set that price.

Treated as important and appreciated.

1. Thank you for being a *valued* customer.
2. Employees need to feel *valued*.
3. He was a *valued* member of the department.

### vanilla

**Русский:** обычный, ничем не примечательный, базовый
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both — often in *plain vanilla*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** In *vanilla ice cream* and *vanilla extract* this is the noun, the flavor. As an adjective it is informal and means 'plain, with no special features', usually about products, software or plans.

Ordinary, with no special features.

1. It's a pretty *vanilla* setup — nothing custom.
2. They offered a plain *vanilla* version of the loan.
3. The movie was *vanilla* but pleasant enough.

**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more vapid*, *most vapid*
**Contrast:** Formal and dismissive, close to *vacuous*, but used more about talk, writing and entertainment than about faces.

### variable

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛriəbəl/ &middot; *VAIR-ee-uh-buhl*
**Русский:** изменчивый, непостоянный; переменный, регулируемый
**Type:** quality; also classifying
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more variable*, *most variable*
**Form:** -able from *vary*
**Opposite:** *constant*, *fixed*
**Contrast:** Also a noun in math and science (*solve for the variable*). *Variable* means it changes; *various* means several different ones.

Variable has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Likely to change; not staying the same.** (изменчивый, непостоянный)

1. The weather here is highly *variable*.
2. Service has been *variable* at best.

**2. Able to be set at different levels.** (переменный, регулируемый)

3. The drill has a *variable* speed setting.
4. They offer a *variable* interest rate.

**Русский:** варикозный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only — almost always in *varicose veins*

Of a vein: swollen and twisted, usually in the legs.

### varied

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛrid/ &middot; *VAIR-eed*
**Русский:** разнообразный, разнородный
**Type:** participial; also quality
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** more/most — *more varied*, *most varied*
**Form:** -ed from *vary*
**Contrast:** *Varied* describes a range that holds many kinds (*a varied menu*); *various* means 'several different' and goes straight before a plural noun (*various reasons*).

Made up of many different kinds; not all the same.

1. The menu is more *varied* than it looks.
2. He's had a long and *varied* career.
3. Reviews have been *varied*.

### various

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛriəs/ &middot; *VAIR-ee-uhs*
**Русский:** различные, разные, несколько
**Type:** quantity; also classifying
**Position:** attributive only — straight before a plural noun: *various reasons*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** *Various* replaces the article: *for various reasons*, never *for a various reason*. It does not follow *be* in everyday English — say *The reasons vary*. And it is not *varied*, which is about a rich range.

Several different.

1. We looked at *various* options before deciding.
2. She's lived in *various* cities over the years.
3. The delay was caused by *various* problems at the port.

### varying

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛriɪŋ/ &middot; *VAIR-ee-ing*
**Русский:** разный, различной степени, меняющийся
**Type:** participial
**Position:** attributive only — *to varying degrees*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** -ing from *vary*
**Contrast:** Nearly fixed in *varying degrees*, *varying amounts* and *varying levels*. For something that keeps changing, use *variable*.

Different from one case to the next; changing in amount or degree.

1. The programs work, with *varying* degrees of success.
2. Students arrive with *varying* levels of preparation.
3. The pills come in *varying* strengths.

### vegan

**Pronunciation:** /ˈviːɡən/ &middot; *VEE-guhn*
**Русский:** веганский
**Type:** classifying — without animal products
**Position:** both
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** *Vegan* excludes all animal products; *vegetarian* excludes only meat and fish. It is also a noun (*a vegan*), and Americans say both *she's vegan* and *she's a vegan*. The first syllable is VEE, not VAY.

Vegan has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Containing no meat, fish, dairy, eggs or other animal products.** (веганский)

1. The restaurant has a separate *vegan* menu.
2. Is the chocolate cake *vegan*?

**2. Eating and using no animal products at all.** ((о человеке) веган, придерживающийся веганства)

3. She's been *vegan* for five years.
4. Half the people at the dinner were *vegan*.

### vegetative

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛdʒəteɪtɪv/ &middot; *VEJ-uh-tay-tiv*
**Русский:** вегетативный
**Type:** classifying
**Position:** attributive only
**Comparison:** not gradable

Vegetative has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of a patient: alive but showing no awareness — in *a vegetative state*.** (вегетативный (о состоянии))

**2. Of a plant: growing leaves and stems rather than flowers or seeds.** (вегетативный (о росте растения))

3. The plant is still in its *vegetative* stage.
4. *Vegetative* growth slows once the buds open.

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɜːrbəl/ &middot; *VUR-buhl*

**Type:** classifying
**Position:** both — mostly before a noun
