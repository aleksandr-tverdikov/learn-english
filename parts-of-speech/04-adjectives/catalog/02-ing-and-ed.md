# The -ing and -ed pairs

> *boring* and *bored*, *interesting* and *interested* — what causes a feeling, and who has it.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

The *-ing* adjective describes what causes a feeling; the *-ed* adjective describes the person who has it. *The movie was boring*, and so *I was bored*. Russian often uses one word for both, and *I am boring* — which says something unkind about the speaker — is one of the most common mistakes in learner English. Both members of each pair are here, next to each other, with examples that make the difference impossible to miss.

---
### interesting

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/ &middot; *IN-truh-sting*
**Русский:** интересный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an interesting book*, *the book was interesting*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more interesting*, *most interesting*
**Takes:** often *it's interesting* + *to*-infinitive or *that*-clause: *It's interesting to see how kids learn*, *It's interesting that nobody noticed*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *uninteresting*, *boring*
**Contrast:** *interesting* describes the thing that holds your attention; *interested* describes the person. *I'm interesting* praises yourself; *I'm interested* is almost always what you mean. Russian *мне интересно* is *I'm interested* or *I wonder*, never *it's interesting for me*. Said in a flat voice, *That's interesting* is often a polite way of not saying you disagree.

Interesting has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Holding your attention because it is new, unusual or makes you want to know more.** (интересный)

1. We had a really *interesting* conversation about the election.
2. Her new book is *more interesting* than her first one.
3. The *most interesting* part of the tour was the old jail.

**2. Strange or unexpected; used politely instead of saying something is odd or bad.** (странный; своеобразный)

4. Pineapple on a hot dog? That's an *interesting* choice.
5. Well, that was an *interesting* meeting — nobody agreed on anything.

### interested

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪntrəstɪd/ &middot; *IN-truh-stid*
**Русский:** заинтересованный; интересующийся
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *interested students*, *the students were interested*; in the sense 'personally involved' almost always before a noun: *an interested party*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more interested*, *most interested*
**Takes:** *in* + noun or *-ing*: *interested in history*, *interested in buying*; *to*-infinitive with verbs of learning: *interested to hear*, *interested to know*
**Preposition patterns:** [interested in](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#interested-in)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Opposite:** *uninterested*
**Contrast:** The preposition is *in* — not *interested about*, *for* or *by*. *I'm interested in learning Spanish* means you want to; *I'd be interested to hear what you think* means you would like to find out. *uninterested* is not interested; *disinterested* traditionally means fair, with nothing to gain, although many Americans use it for both. *I'm interesting* means you think you are fascinating — say *I'm interested*.

Interested has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Wanting to know or learn more about something, and giving it your attention.** (интересующийся; заинтересованный)

1. My son is really *interested* in dinosaurs.
2. I'm not *interested* in politics.
3. The kids seemed *more interested* in the snacks than in the movie.

**2. Wanting to do, have or take part in something.** (желающий; заинтересованный (в чём-то))

4. Are you *interested* in joining our team?
5. We already have three *interested* buyers for the house.
6. If you're *interested*, call me before Friday.

**3. Personally involved in a situation and likely to be affected by the result.** (заинтересованный (о стороне))

7. The city will hear from all *interested* parties before it votes.
8. Copies of the report went to every *interested* group.

### boring

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɔːrɪŋ/ &middot; *BOR-ing*
**Русский:** скучный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a boring class*, *the class was boring*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more boring*, *most boring*; never *boringer*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *interesting*
**Contrast:** *boring* is the cause, *bored* is the feeling. *I'm boring* means other people find you dull (я скучный); *I'm bored* is Russian *мне скучно*. In the same way: *The class was boring*, so *the students were bored*.

Not interesting; making you feel tired and impatient.

1. The first hour of the movie was so *boring* that I fell asleep.
2. He's a nice guy, but his stories are kind of *boring*.
3. Doing taxes is the *most boring* job of the year.

---

### bored

**Pronunciation:** /bɔːrd/ &middot; *BORD*
**Русский:** скучающий; (мне) скучно
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *bored kids*, *the kids were bored*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more bored*, *most bored*
**Takes:** *with* + noun or *-ing*: *bored with my job*; *by* + noun: *bored by the lecture*; *bored of* is common in casual speech, but many teachers mark it wrong
**Preposition patterns:** [bored with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#bored-with)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Opposite:** *interested*
**Contrast:** *I'm bored* (мне скучно) is about how you feel; *I'm boring* (я скучный) is about how others see you. *bored with* something that has gone on too long is closer to Russian *надоело*: *I'm bored with my job*. Common intensifiers: *bored to death*, *bored to tears*, *bored stiff*.

Bored has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Tired and impatient because you have nothing interesting to do.** (скучающий; (мне) скучно)

1. The kids get *bored* on long car trips.
2. A *bored* teenager is often a teenager looking for trouble.
3. Nobody at that meeting looked *more bored* than the boss.

**2. Tired of something because it has gone on too long or never changes.** (которому надоело; уставший (от))

4. She was *bored* with her job and started looking for a new one.
5. After ten years in the same town, he was *bored* with small-town life.

### exciting

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈsaɪtɪŋ/ &middot; *ik-SY-ting*
**Русский:** захватывающий; увлекательный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an exciting game*, *the game was exciting*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more exciting*, *most exciting*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *dull*, *boring*
**Contrast:** *exciting* is about the thing, *excited* about the person: *an exciting game*, *excited fans*. It is always positive, never Russian *волнующий* in the sense of worrying: for *я волнуюсь перед экзаменом* say *I'm nervous about the exam*.

Making you feel eager, happy and full of energy.

1. Moving to New York was the *most exciting* thing I've ever done.
2. It was an *exciting* game right up to the last second.
3. We have some *exciting* news — we're having a baby!

### excited

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈsaɪtɪd/ &middot; *ik-SY-tid*
**Русский:** радостно взволнованный; в предвкушении
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *excited fans*, *the fans were excited*; the physics sense mostly before a noun: *an excited atom*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more excited*, *most excited*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *-ing*: *excited about the trip*; *to*-infinitive: *excited to see you*; *that*-clause; *for* + person or event, especially in American speech: *I'm so excited for you*, *excited for the weekend*
**Preposition patterns:** [excited about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#excited-about)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** *I'm excited* is Russian *я жду не дождусь*, *я в предвкушении* or *я так рад*, not *возбуждённый*, which in Russian often sounds agitated or sexual. *I'm exciting* is wrong. *Don't get excited* can mean *calm down*, and *Don't get too excited* means don't expect too much. You are *excited about* a thing and *excited for* a person who has good news.

Excited has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Very happy and eager about something that is happening or will happen.** (радостно взволнованный; в предвкушении)

1. The kids are so *excited* about Christmas.
2. I'm really *excited* to start my new job.
3. I've never seen him *more excited* than the day he got his first car.

**2. Nervous, upset or unable to stay calm.** (возбуждённый; взбудораженный)

4. Calm down — there's no need to get *excited*.
5. The dog gets *excited* and barks whenever someone knocks.

**3. Sexually aroused.** (возбуждённый (сексуально))

6. The study measured heart rate when subjects were sexually *excited*.
7. Doctors say it can take longer for older adults to become sexually *excited*.

**4. In physics, having more energy than the normal, lowest state.** (возбуждённый (о частице))

8. An *excited* electron gives off light as it drops to a lower energy level.
9. The laser works by pumping atoms into an *excited* state.

### surprising

**Pronunciation:** /sərˈpraɪzɪŋ/ &middot; *sur-PRY-zing*
**Русский:** неожиданный; удивительный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a surprising result*, *the result was surprising*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more surprising*, *most surprising*
**Takes:** often *it's surprising* + *that*-clause or *how*-clause: *It's surprising that she said no*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *unsurprising*, *predictable*
**Contrast:** *surprising* only means unexpected, good or bad. Russian *удивительный* in the sense of wonderful is *amazing*: *an amazing view* (удивительный вид), not *a surprising view*. *It's not surprising* and *hardly surprising* are the usual ways to say *неудивительно*. The person is *surprised*.

Unexpected, and so causing surprise.

1. The results of the survey were *surprising*.
2. It's *surprising* how many people still don't wear seat belts.
3. The *most surprising* thing about the trip was how cheap it was.

### surprised

**Pronunciation:** /sərˈpraɪzd/ &middot; *sur-PRYZD*
**Русский:** удивлённый
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — mostly after a verb: *I was surprised*; before a noun mainly with look, face, expression: *a surprised look*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more surprised*, *most surprised*
**Takes:** *at* or *by* + noun: *surprised at the price*, *surprised by the news*; *to*-infinitive: *surprised to see you*; *that*-clause; *at* + person, disapproving: *I'm surprised at you*
**Preposition patterns:** [surprised at](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#surprised-at)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** The person is *surprised*, the cause is *surprising*: *I was surprised*; *The news was surprising*. Not *surprised of*. *I wouldn't be surprised if* is Russian *не удивлюсь, если*. *I'm surprised at you* is not neutral — like *ты меня удивляешь*, it scolds someone for behaving worse than you expected: *Cheating on a test? I'm surprised at you*.

Feeling surprise because something happened that you did not expect.

1. I was *surprised* to see her at the party.
2. Nobody was *more surprised* by the win than the coach.
3. She gave me a *surprised* look.

### confusing

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈfjuːzɪŋ/ &middot; *kuhn-FYOO-zing*
**Русский:** запутанный; непонятный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *confusing instructions*, *the instructions were confusing*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more confusing*, *most confusing*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *clear*
**Contrast:** *confusing* is what makes you lose track; *confused* is how you feel: *The instructions were confusing, and I was confused*. *I'm confusing* means you confuse other people.

Difficult to understand; not clear.

1. The tax forms are really *confusing*.
2. The ending of the movie was *more confusing* than the rest of it.
3. It's *confusing* when two people on the team have the same name.

### confused

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈfjuːzd/ &middot; *kuhn-FYOOZD*
**Русский:** растерянный; сбитый с толку
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *a confused student*, *the student was confused*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more confused*, *most confused*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *wh*-clause: *confused about the rules*, *confused about what to do*; *by* + the cause: *confused by the signs*
**Preposition patterns:** [confused about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#confused-about)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** False friend: *confused* is *растерянный* or *сбитый с толку*, not *сконфуженный* — that is *embarrassed*. The person is *confused*; the cause is *confusing*. *confused with* is usually the verb: *He is often confused with his brother* (его путают с братом).

Confused has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Unable to understand something or to decide what to do.** (растерянный; сбитый с толку)

1. I'm *confused* — which bus do I take?
2. Students were *confused* about the new schedule.
3. I was even *more confused* after he explained it.

**2. Unable to think clearly or know where you are, because of illness, age or an injury.** (в спутанном сознании; дезориентированный)

4. After the fall, the patient was *confused* and couldn't remember his address.
5. Call a doctor if an older person suddenly seems *confused*.

**3. Mixed up and not clear or orderly.** (путаный; сбивчивый; неясный)

6. The witness gave a *confused* account of the accident.
7. The message from the campaign was *confused* and contradictory.

### tiring

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtaɪərɪŋ/ &middot; *TY-uh-ring*
**Русский:** утомительный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a tiring day*, *the day was tiring*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more tiring*, *most tiring*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Contrast:** *tiring* wears out your body; *tiresome* is boring or annoying: *a tiring hike*, *a tiresome neighbor*. *I'm tiring* is the verb and means *I'm getting tired*; for *я устал* say *I'm tired*.

Making you feel tired.

1. It was a long, *tiring* day at work.
2. Driving at night is *more tiring* than driving during the day.
3. Taking care of twins is *tiring*.

### tired

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtaɪərd/ &middot; *TY-urd*
**Русский:** уставший; усталый
**Type:** participial; also quality
**Position:** both — *tired kids*, *the kids were tired*; *tired of* only after a verb
**Comparison:** more/most — *more tired*, *most tired*; *tireder* is rare
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing* — fed up: *tired of waiting*; *from* + noun or *-ing* — the physical cause: *tired from the trip*
**Preposition patterns:** [tired of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#tired-of)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Opposite:** *rested*
**Contrast:** Russian *устал от* splits in English: *I'm tired from work* (устал после работы) but *I'm tired of work* (работа надоела). *sick and tired of* is a strong *надоело*. The person is *tired*; the cause is *tiring*.

Tired has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Needing rest or sleep.** (уставший; усталый)

1. I'm too *tired* to cook tonight.
2. She came home *tired* from a twelve-hour shift.
3. You look *more tired* than usual — are you OK?

**2. No longer interested in or patient with something because it has gone on too long.** (которому надоело)

4. I'm *tired* of hearing the same excuses.
5. People are getting *tired* of all the political ads.

**3. Used too often or too long, and so no longer fresh or interesting.** (избитый; заезженный; потрёпанный)

6. He told the same *tired* old jokes at every party.
7. The hotel lobby looked a little *tired*, with worn carpet and faded chairs.

### terrifying

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɛrəfaɪɪŋ/ &middot; *TER-uh-fy-ing*
**Русский:** ужасающий; жуткий
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a terrifying moment*, *the moment was terrifying*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more terrifying*, *most terrifying*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Contrast:** A strong word: *absolutely* or *really terrifying* sounds more natural than *very terrifying*. Do not confuse it with *terrific*, which means *great*: *You did a terrific job* (отличная работа). The thing is *terrifying*; the person is *terrified*.

So frightening that it fills you with fear.

1. Being stuck in the elevator was *terrifying*.
2. It was the *most terrifying* movie I've ever seen.
3. There was one *terrifying* moment when the plane suddenly dropped.

**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *a terrified child*, *the child was terrified*

**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing*: *terrified of spiders*; *to*-infinitive: *terrified to go outside*; *that*-clause
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** A strong word: *absolutely* or *completely terrified* sounds more natural than *very terrified*. Not *terrified from*. The person is *terrified*; the thing is *terrifying*.

Extremely frightened.

### amazing

**Pronunciation:** /əˈmeɪzɪŋ/ &middot; *uh-MAY-zing*
**Русский:** потрясающий; удивительный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an amazing view*, *the view was amazing*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more amazing*, *most amazing*
**Takes:** often *it's amazing* + *that*-clause or *how*-clause: *It's amazing how fast kids grow*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Contrast:** In everyday American speech *amazing* mostly just means *great* — *The food was amazing* — and it is used so often it can sound empty. A strong word: *absolutely* or *really amazing*, rather than *very amazing*. Russian *удивительный* meaning simply unexpected is *surprising*. The person is *amazed*.

Amazing has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Extremely good; wonderful.** (потрясающий; классный; замечательный)

1. We had an *amazing* time in Hawaii.
2. Your mom's lasagna is *amazing*.
3. That was the *most amazing* concert I've ever been to.

**2. So unusual or unexpected that it causes great surprise.** (удивительный; поразительный)

4. It's *amazing* how fast kids pick up languages.
5. It's *amazing* that nobody was hurt in the crash.

### amazed

**Pronunciation:** /əˈmeɪzd/ &middot; *uh-MAYZD*
**Русский:** поражённый; изумлённый
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — mostly after a verb: *I was amazed*; before a noun mainly with look, face, crowd: *an amazed look*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more amazed*, *most amazed*
**Takes:** *at* or *by* + noun: *amazed at the view*, *amazed by her talent*; *to*-infinitive: *amazed to find*; *that*-clause; *how*-clause
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** *amazed* is stronger than *surprised* and usually pleasant. Not *amazed of*. The person is *amazed*; *I'm amazing* is bragging.

Very surprised, often in a pleasant way.

1. I was *amazed* at how much the city had changed.
2. We were *amazed* to find the store still open at midnight.
3. The *amazed* crowd watched the rocket climb into the sky.

### disappointing

**Русский:** неутешительный; разочаровывающий
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *disappointing results*, *the results were disappointing*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more disappointing*, *most disappointing*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Contrast:** Russian often says *не оправдал ожиданий*; English simply uses the adjective: *The results were disappointing*. Spelling: one *s*, two *p*s. The thing is *disappointing*; the person is *disappointed*.

Not as good as you hoped or expected.

1. The movie got great reviews, but I found it *disappointing*.
2. Sales this quarter were *more disappointing* than expected.
3. It's *disappointing* that the park is closed again.

### disappointed

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdɪsəˈpɔɪntɪd/ &middot; *dis-uh-POYN-tid*
**Русский:** разочарованный
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *disappointed fans*, *the fans were disappointed*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more disappointed*, *most disappointed*
**Takes:** *in* + person: *disappointed in you*; *with* or *by* + thing: *disappointed with the service*; *about* + event: *disappointed about the loss*; *to*-infinitive: *disappointed to hear*; *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [disappointed with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#disappointed-with)
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Contrast:** *disappointed in* someone is personal and it hurts: *I'm disappointed in you* (ты меня разочаровал). For a result or a service use *with* or *by*. Not *disappointed of*. *I'm disappointing* means you let others down — say *I'm disappointed*.

Unhappy because something was not as good as you hoped or did not happen.

1. I was *disappointed* with the hotel.
2. The coach said he was *disappointed* in the team's effort.
3. Thousands of *disappointed* fans left before the game ended.

### relaxing

**Русский:** расслабляющий; успокаивающий
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a relaxing bath*, *the bath was relaxing*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more relaxing*, *most relaxing*
**Form:** -ing — what causes the feeling
**Opposite:** *stressful*
**Contrast:** *relaxing* is the thing that calms you: *a relaxing vacation*. *relaxed* is usually the person, but it can also describe a place or style: *a relaxed atmosphere* (непринуждённая атмосфера). *I'm relaxing* is the verb — *я отдыхаю*.

Making you feel calm and rested.

1. We spent a *relaxing* week at the lake.
2. A hot shower is *relaxing* after a long day.
3. Fishing is *more relaxing* than you might think.

### relaxed

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈlækst/ &middot; *ri-LAKST*
**Русский:** расслабленный; непринуждённый
**Type:** participial; also emotion
**Position:** both — *a relaxed atmosphere*, *she seemed relaxed*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more relaxed*, *most relaxed*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *-ing*: *relaxed about the deadline*
**Form:** -ed — the person who has the feeling
**Opposite:** *tense*
**Contrast:** Unlike most *-ed* adjectives, *relaxed* describes places, events and rules as well as people: *a relaxed restaurant* (непринуждённый), *relaxed rules* (нестрогие). What makes you calm is *relaxing*. *I'm relaxed* is how you feel; *I'm relaxing* is what you are doing.

Relaxed has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Calm and not worried or tense.** (расслабленный; спокойный)

1. You seem much *more relaxed* since you changed jobs.
2. She was *relaxed* and smiling during the interview.
3. Keep your shoulders *relaxed* while you type.

**2. Informal and comfortable, not stiff.** (непринуждённый; неформальный)

4. The restaurant has a *relaxed* atmosphere and a great patio.
5. The dress code at the office is pretty *relaxed*.

**3. Not strict or firmly controlled.** (нестрогий; смягчённый)

6. Some schools have a *relaxed* attitude toward phones in class.
7. Under the *relaxed* guidelines, restaurants could seat more people.

### embarrassing

**Pronunciation:** /ɪmˈbɛrəsɪŋ/ &middot; *im-BER-uh-sing*
**Русский:** неловкий; стыдный
**Type:** participial; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *an embarrassing mistake*, *the mistake was embarrassing*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more embarrassing*, *most embarrassing*

Embarrassing has 2 distinct senses.

1. It was so *embarrassing* when I called my teacher 'Mom.'

3. Forgetting his name was really *embarrassing*.

**2. Causing problems for someone's public image or position.** (компрометирующий; неудобный)

4. The leaked emails were *embarrassing* for the senator.
5. The mistake was politically *embarrassing* for the mayor.

### embarrassed

**Pronunciation:** /ɪmˈbɛrəst/ &middot; *im-BER-uhst*
**Русский:** смущённый; (мне) неловко
**Type:** participial; also emotion
