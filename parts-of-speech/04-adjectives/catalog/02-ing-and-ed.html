The -ing and -ed pairs#

boring and bored, interesting and interested — what causes a feeling, and who has it.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

The -ing adjective describes what causes a feeling; the -ed adjective describes the person who has it. The movie was boring, and so I was bored. Russian often uses one word for both, and I am boring — which says something unkind about the speaker — is one of the most common mistakes in learner English. Both members of each pair are here, next to each other, with examples that make the difference impossible to miss.

interesting#

Pronunciation
/ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/ · IN-truh-sting
Русский
интересный
Type
participial; also evaluative
Position
both — an interesting book, the book was interesting
Comparison
more/most — more interesting, most interesting
Takes
often it's interesting + to-infinitive or that-clause: It's interesting to see how kids learn, It's interesting that nobody noticed
Form
-ing — what causes the feeling
Opposite
uninteresting, boring
Contrast
interesting describes the thing that holds your attention; interested describes the person. I'm interesting praises yourself; I'm interested is almost always what you mean. Russian мне интересно is I'm interested or I wonder, never it's interesting for me. Said in a flat voice, That's interesting is often a polite way of not saying you disagree.

Interesting has 2 distinct senses.

1. Holding your attention because it is new, unusual or makes you want to know more. (интересный)

  1. We had a really interesting conversation about the election.
  2. Her new book is more interesting than her first one.
  3. The most interesting part of the tour was the old jail.

2. Strange or unexpected; used politely instead of saying something is odd or bad. (странный; своеобразный)

  1. Pineapple on a hot dog? That's an interesting choice.
  2. Well, that was an interesting meeting — nobody agreed on anything.

interested#

Pronunciation
/ˈɪntrəstɪd/ · IN-truh-stid
Русский
заинтересованный; интересующийся
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — interested students, the students were interested; in the sense 'personally involved' almost always before a noun: an interested party
Comparison
more/most — more interested, most interested
Takes
in + noun or -ing: interested in history, interested in buying; to-infinitive with verbs of learning: interested to hear, interested to know
Preposition patterns
interested in
Form
-ed — the person who has the feeling
Opposite
uninterested
Contrast
The preposition is in — not interested about, for or by. I'm interested in learning Spanish means you want to; I'd be interested to hear what you think means you would like to find out. uninterested is not interested; disinterested traditionally means fair, with nothing to gain, although many Americans use it for both. I'm interesting means you think you are fascinating — say I'm interested.

Interested has 3 distinct senses.

1. Wanting to know or learn more about something, and giving it your attention. (интересующийся; заинтересованный)

  1. My son is really interested in dinosaurs.
  2. I'm not interested in politics.
  3. The kids seemed more interested in the snacks than in the movie.

2. Wanting to do, have or take part in something. (желающий; заинтересованный (в чём-то))

  1. Are you interested in joining our team?
  2. We already have three interested buyers for the house.
  3. If you're interested, call me before Friday.

3. Personally involved in a situation and likely to be affected by the result. (заинтересованный (о стороне))

  1. The city will hear from all interested parties before it votes.
  2. Copies of the report went to every interested group.

boring#

Pronunciation
/ˈbɔːrɪŋ/ · BOR-ing
Русский
скучный
Type
participial; also evaluative
Position
both — a boring class, the class was boring
Comparison
more/most — more boring, most boring; never boringer
Form
-ing — what causes the feeling
Opposite
interesting
Contrast
boring is the cause, bored is the feeling. I'm boring means other people find you dull (я скучный); I'm bored is Russian мне скучно. In the same way: The class was boring, so the students were bored.

Not interesting; making you feel tired and impatient.

  1. The first hour of the movie was so boring that I fell asleep.
  2. He's a nice guy, but his stories are kind of boring.
  3. Doing taxes is the most boring job of the year.

bored#

Pronunciation
/bɔːrd/ · BORD
Русский
скучающий; (мне) скучно
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — bored kids, the kids were bored
Comparison
more/most — more bored, most bored
Takes
with + noun or -ing: bored with my job; by + noun: bored by the lecture; bored of is common in casual speech, but many teachers mark it wrong
Preposition patterns
bored with
Form
-ed — the person who has the feeling
Opposite
interested
Contrast
I'm bored (мне скучно) is about how you feel; I'm boring (я скучный) is about how others see you. bored with something that has gone on too long is closer to Russian надоело: I'm bored with my job. Common intensifiers: bored to death, bored to tears, bored stiff.

Bored has 2 distinct senses.

1. Tired and impatient because you have nothing interesting to do. (скучающий; (мне) скучно)

  1. The kids get bored on long car trips.
  2. A bored teenager is often a teenager looking for trouble.
  3. Nobody at that meeting looked more bored than the boss.

2. Tired of something because it has gone on too long or never changes. (которому надоело; уставший (от))

  1. She was bored with her job and started looking for a new one.
  2. After ten years in the same town, he was bored with small-town life.

excited#

Pronunciation
/ɪkˈsaɪtɪd/ · ik-SY-tid
Русский
радостно взволнованный; в предвкушении
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — excited fans, the fans were excited; the physics sense mostly before a noun: an excited atom
Comparison
more/most — more excited, most excited
Takes
about + noun or -ing: excited about the trip; to-infinitive: excited to see you; that-clause; for + person or event, especially in American speech: I'm so excited for you, excited for the weekend
Preposition patterns
excited about
Form
-ed — the person who has the feeling
Contrast
I'm excited is Russian я жду не дождусь, я в предвкушении or я так рад, not возбуждённый, which in Russian often sounds agitated or sexual. I'm exciting is wrong. Don't get excited can mean calm down, and Don't get too excited means don't expect too much. You are excited about a thing and excited for a person who has good news.

Excited has 4 distinct senses.

1. Very happy and eager about something that is happening or will happen. (радостно взволнованный; в предвкушении)

  1. The kids are so excited about Christmas.
  2. I'm really excited to start my new job.
  3. I've never seen him more excited than the day he got his first car.

2. Nervous, upset or unable to stay calm. (возбуждённый; взбудораженный)

  1. Calm down — there's no need to get excited.
  2. The dog gets excited and barks whenever someone knocks.

3. Sexually aroused. (возбуждённый (сексуально))

  1. The study measured heart rate when subjects were sexually excited.
  2. Doctors say it can take longer for older adults to become sexually excited.

4. In physics, having more energy than the normal, lowest state. (возбуждённый (о частице))

  1. An excited electron gives off light as it drops to a lower energy level.
  2. The laser works by pumping atoms into an excited state.

surprising#

Pronunciation
/sərˈpraɪzɪŋ/ · sur-PRY-zing
Русский
неожиданный; удивительный
Type
participial; also evaluative
Position
both — a surprising result, the result was surprising
Comparison
more/most — more surprising, most surprising
Takes
often it's surprising + that-clause or how-clause: It's surprising that she said no
Form
-ing — what causes the feeling
Opposite
unsurprising, predictable
Contrast
surprising only means unexpected, good or bad. Russian удивительный in the sense of wonderful is amazing: an amazing view (удивительный вид), not a surprising view. It's not surprising and hardly surprising are the usual ways to say неудивительно. The person is surprised.

Unexpected, and so causing surprise.

  1. The results of the survey were surprising.
  2. It's surprising how many people still don't wear seat belts.
  3. The most surprising thing about the trip was how cheap it was.

Pronunciation
/sərˈpraɪzd/ · sur-PRYZD
Русский
удивлённый
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — mostly after a verb: I was surprised; before a noun mainly with look, face, expression: a surprised look
Comparison
more/most — more surprised, most surprised
Takes
at or by + noun: surprised at the price, surprised by the news; to-infinitive: surprised to see you; that-clause; at + person, disapproving: I'm surprised at you
Preposition patterns

Feeling surprise because something happened that you did not expect.

  1. I was surprised to see her at the party.
  2. Nobody was more surprised by the win than the coach.
  3. She gave me a surprised look.

confused#

Pronunciation
/kənˈfjuːzd/ · kuhn-FYOOZD
Русский
растерянный; сбитый с толку
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — a confused student, the student was confused
Comparison
more/most — more confused, most confused
Takes
about + noun or wh-clause: confused about the rules, confused about what to do; by + the cause: confused by the signs
Preposition patterns
confused about
Form
-ed — the person who has the feeling
Contrast
False friend: confused is растерянный or сбитый с толку, not сконфуженный — that is embarrassed. The person is confused; the cause is confusing. confused with is usually the verb: He is often confused with his brother (его путают с братом).

Confused has 3 distinct senses.

1. Unable to understand something or to decide what to do. (растерянный; сбитый с толку)

  1. I'm confused — which bus do I take?
  2. Students were confused about the new schedule.
  3. I was even more confused after he explained it.

2. Unable to think clearly or know where you are, because of illness, age or an injury. (в спутанном сознании; дезориентированный)

  1. After the fall, the patient was confused and couldn't remember his address.
  2. Call a doctor if an older person suddenly seems confused.

3. Mixed up and not clear or orderly. (путаный; сбивчивый; неясный)

  1. The witness gave a confused account of the accident.
  2. The message from the campaign was confused and contradictory.

tired#

Pronunciation
/ˈtaɪərd/ · TY-urd
Русский
уставший; усталый
Type
participial; also quality
Position
both — tired kids, the kids were tired; tired of only after a verb
Comparison
more/most — more tired, most tired; tireder is rare
Takes
of + noun or -ing — fed up: tired of waiting; from + noun or -ing — the physical cause: tired from the trip
Preposition patterns
tired of
Form
-ed — the person who has the feeling
Opposite
rested
Contrast
Russian устал от splits in English: I'm tired from work (устал после работы) but I'm tired of work (работа надоела). sick and tired of is a strong надоело. The person is tired; the cause is tiring.

Tired has 3 distinct senses.

1. Needing rest or sleep. (уставший; усталый)

  1. I'm too tired to cook tonight.
  2. She came home tired from a twelve-hour shift.
  3. You look more tired than usual — are you OK?

2. No longer interested in or patient with something because it has gone on too long. (которому надоело)

  1. I'm tired of hearing the same excuses.
  2. People are getting tired of all the political ads.

3. Used too often or too long, and so no longer fresh or interesting. (избитый; заезженный; потрёпанный)

  1. He told the same tired old jokes at every party.
  2. The hotel lobby looked a little tired, with worn carpet and faded chairs.

Unlike most -ed adjectives, relaxed describes places, events and rules as well as people: a relaxed restaurant (непринуждённый), relaxed rules (нестрогие). What makes you calm is relaxing. I'm relaxed is how you feel; I'm relaxing is what you are doing.

Relaxed has 3 distinct senses.

2. Informal and comfortable, not stiff. (непринуждённый; неформальный)

  1. The restaurant has a relaxed atmosphere and a great patio.
  2. The dress code at the office is pretty relaxed.

3. Not strict or firmly controlled. (нестрогий; смягчённый)

  1. Some schools have a relaxed attitude toward phones in class.
  2. Under the relaxed guidelines, restaurants could seat more people.

satisfying#

/ˈsætɪsfaɪɪŋ/ · SAT-is-fy-ing

Contrast
Do not mix up satisfying (it feels good) with satisfactory (удовлетворительный — only good enough): a satisfying meal is a good one; a satisfactory grade is just OK. The person is satisfied.

Satisfying has 3 distinct senses.

1. Giving you pleasure because it is what you wanted, or because you did it well. (приносящий удовлетворение; приятный)

2. Of food, filling enough that you are no longer hungry. (сытный)

  1. Oatmeal makes a cheap, satisfying breakfast.
  2. The soup was hot and satisfying.

3. Of an answer or explanation, good enough to accept. (вразумительный; убедительный)

  1. Nobody could give me a satisfying answer.
  2. Scientists still don't have a satisfying explanation for why we dream.

  1. He has an irritating habit of cracking his knuckles.
  2. The most irritating thing about the app is the pop-up ads.
  3. That buzzing noise is so irritating.

2. Making skin, eyes or another part of the body sore or red. (раздражающий (кожу, глаза))

  1. Some soaps are irritating to sensitive skin.
  2. Smoke can be irritating to the eyes and throat.

Inspired has 3 distinct senses.

1. Feeling eager and excited to do something, especially something creative. (вдохновлённый; воодушевлённый)

  1. After the trip, I felt inspired to start painting again.
  2. Kids come out of the science museum totally inspired.
  3. I've never felt more inspired than I did that summer.

2. Extremely good or clever, as if done with special talent. (блестящий; гениальный; вдохновенный)

  1. Hiring her was an inspired choice.
  2. The band gave an inspired performance.

3. Influenced by a particular style, place or idea; usually joined to another word with a hyphen. (в стиле; навеянный; по мотивам)

  1. The restaurant serves Mexican-inspired dishes.
  2. Police believe the attack was politically inspired.
