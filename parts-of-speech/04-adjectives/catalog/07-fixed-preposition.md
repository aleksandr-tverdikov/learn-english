# Adjectives that take a fixed preposition

> *proud of*, *good at*, *interested in*, *similar to* — the adjectives that bring their own preposition.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

The preposition after these adjectives is not chosen for its meaning — it is simply the one the adjective takes: *afraid of*, *good at*, *interested in*, *married to*. Russian uses different prepositions or a case ending, so the English one has to be learned with the word. Every entry names its preposition, says when a different one changes the meaning (*angry at* a person, *angry about* a thing), and links to the full pattern in the preposition dictionary.

### keen

**Pronunciation:** /kiːn/ &middot; *KEEN*
**Русский:** острый; сильный; увлечённый
**Type:** quality; also emotion
**Position:** both — *a keen eye*, *She wasn't keen on it*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *keener*, *keenest*
**Takes:** *on* + noun or *-ing* (liking: *keen on jazz*, mostly *not keen on*); *to*-infinitive (wanting to do: *keen to help*)
**Preposition patterns:** [keen on](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#keen-on)
**Contrast:** *Keen on* + noun or *-ing* means liking something (*I'm not keen on horror movies*); *keen to* + verb means wanting to do it (*She's keen to start*). Never ✗ *keen for doing* or ✗ *keen in*. In American English the 'eager' sense sounds a little British or formal, except in the negative *not too keen on*; Americans more often say *eager to*, *excited about* or *into* (*I'm really into jazz*). The everyday American uses are the 'sharp' and 'strong' ones: *a keen eye*, *a keen sense of smell*, *keen interest*, *keen competition*. *Peachy keen* (great) is old-fashioned, joking slang.

Keen has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Of the senses or the mind: sharp and quick to notice or understand.** (острый, тонкий)

1. Dogs have a much *keener* sense of smell than people.
2. She has a *keen* eye for detail.
3. He was a *keen* observer of small-town life.

**2. Of a feeling or interest: strong and deep.** (сильный, живой, глубокий)

4. He took a *keen* interest in local politics.
5. Competition for the job was *keen*.
6. The company faces *keen* competition from overseas.

**3. Wanting very much to do something, or liking something very much.** (увлечённый, стремящийся; горящий желанием)

7. I'm not too *keen* on the idea of camping in January.
8. The new owners are *keen* to make changes.
9. She was never very *keen* on sports.

**4. Of a blade, having a very sharp edge; of wind, cold and biting — literary.** (острый; пронизывающий (о ветре))

10. The knife had a *keen* edge.
11. A *keen* wind blew off the lake.

### proud

**Pronunciation:** /praʊd/ &middot; *PROWD*
**Русский:** гордый; гордящийся
**Type:** emotion; also quality
**Position:** both — *proud parents*, *We're proud of you*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *prouder*, *proudest*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing* (*proud of you*, *proud of finishing*); *to*-infinitive (*proud to announce*); *that*-clause (*proud that she won*)
**Preposition patterns:** [proud of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#proud-of)
**Opposite:** *ashamed*
**Contrast:** Russian *гордиться* + a noun in the instrumental case becomes *proud of* in English: *I'm proud of my son*, never ✗ *proud with my son*, ✗ *proud for my son* or ✗ *proud by*. *Proud to* + verb is about the honor of doing something now (*proud to be here*); *proud of* + *-ing* is about something done (*proud of winning*). *Proud* can be good (pleased about success, self-respect) or bad (arrogant): context decides, as with *гордый*. The noun is *pride*, and *take pride in* is the verb phrase (*She takes pride in her work*). *Do someone proud* (make them proud) is an adverb idiom.

Proud has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Pleased and happy about something you, or someone close to you, did or has.** (гордящийся, гордый (чем-то))

1. Your mom and I are so *proud* of you.
2. I've never been *prouder* of this team.
3. We're *proud* to announce the opening of our second store.

**2. Having self-respect and not wanting help or pity from others.** (гордый, с чувством собственного достоинства)

4. He was too *proud* to ask for money.
5. They're a *proud* people with a long history.
6. She's a *proud* woman who hates to complain.

**3. Thinking that you are better or more important than other people.** (высокомерный, надменный)

7. He's too *proud* to admit he was wrong.
8. She was a cold, *proud* woman who looked down on everyone.

**4. Of a moment, day or thing: causing a feeling of pride.** (торжественный; предмет гордости)

9. Graduation was the *proudest* day of my father's life.
10. It was a *proud* moment for the whole town.
11. The new stadium is the school's *proudest* achievement.

### jealous

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɛləs/ &middot; *JEL-uhs*
**Русский:** завидующий; ревнивый
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — *a jealous husband*, *He's jealous of her*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more jealous*, *most jealous*
**Takes:** *of* + person or thing (*jealous of her*, *jealous of his success*)
**Preposition patterns:** [jealous of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#jealous-of)
**Form:** noun *jealousy*
**Contrast:** Russian splits *зависть* and *ревность*; everyday American English uses *jealous* for both: *I'm jealous of your vacation* (завидую) and *He gets jealous when she talks to other guys* (ревнует). *Envious* covers only wanting what someone else has, and is more formal. Russian *завидовать кому-то* takes the dative, but English needs *of*: *jealous of him*, never ✗ *jealous to him* or ✗ *jealous on him*. *I'm so jealous!* is often friendly and light, like *везёт же!*. The verb for the envy sense is *envy* with no preposition (*I envy you*); there is no verb *to jealous*.

Jealous has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Unhappy because someone has something you want.** (завидующий, завистливый)

1. Everyone at work is *jealous* of her new office.
2. I'm so *jealous* — I've always wanted to go to Hawaii.
3. He was *jealous* of his brother's success.

**2. Afraid that someone you love is attracted to, or loves, someone else.** (ревнивый, ревнующий)

4. Her boyfriend gets *jealous* when she goes out without him.
5. He was the *most jealous* husband I've ever met.
6. The dog gets *jealous* whenever we hold the baby.

**3. Protecting carefully something you have or value.** (ревностно оберегающий)

7. The family is *jealous* of its privacy.
8. She kept a *jealous* watch over her little brother.

### capable

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkeɪpəbəl/ &middot; *KAY-puh-buhl*
**Русский:** способный; умелый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a capable manager*, *She's capable of more*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more capable*, *most capable*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing* (*capable of anything*, *capable of running a marathon*), never a *to*-infinitive
**Preposition patterns:** [capable of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#capable-of)
**Form:** -able; noun *capability*
**Opposite:** *incapable*
**Contrast:** *Capable of* + *-ing*, never ✗ *capable to do*: *She's capable of doing it*, but *She's able to do it*. The two words take different patterns, and learners mix them. Russian *способный ученик* (talented, quick) is usually *a bright student* or *a gifted student*; *a capable student* means competent and reliable, not especially gifted. *Способен на всё* is *capable of anything*, often with a hint that the person might do something bad.

Capable has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Having the ability, power or capacity to do something.** (способный (сделать), в состоянии)

1. The truck is *capable* of pulling a small boat.
2. You're *capable* of much better work than this.
3. The new stadium is *capable* of holding 60,000 fans.

**2. Able to do things well; competent and skilled.** (умелый, толковый, компетентный)

4. Don't worry — the kids are in very *capable* hands.
5. She's the *most capable* manager we've ever had.
6. He seemed *capable* and calm in a crisis.

**3. Willing or likely to do something bad or surprising.** (способный (на что-то плохое))

7. I didn't think he was *capable* of lying to me.
8. When she's angry, she's *capable* of anything.

### similar

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪmələr/ &middot; *SIM-uh-lur*
**Русский:** похожий, сходный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a similar problem*, *The two houses are similar*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more similar*, *most similar*
**Takes:** *to* + noun (*similar to mine*); *in* + the feature that is alike (*similar in size*)
**Preposition patterns:** [similar to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#similar-to)
**Form:** noun *similarity*
**Opposite:** *different*, *dissimilar*
**Contrast:** *Similar to*, never ✗ *similar with*, ✗ *similar as* or ✗ *similar on*. Russian *похож на* suggests *on*, but English uses *to*. For the feature, use *in*: *similar in color*, not ✗ *similar by color*. *Similar* means alike, not identical: for identical use *the same as* (*My phone is the same as yours*) or *identical to*. For people's faces, *look like* is more natural: *She looks like her mother* rather than *She is similar to her mother*. The comparative is *more similar*, never ✗ *similarer*.

Similar has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Almost the same as someone or something, but not exactly.** (похожий, сходный, аналогичный)

1. Your car is very *similar* to mine.
2. The two sisters are *similar* in size, so they share clothes.
3. The new version is *more similar* to the original than I expected.

**2. In geometry: having the same shape but not the same size.** (подобный (о фигурах))

4. The two triangles are *similar* because their angles are equal.
5. Show that the triangles are *similar*.

### different

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɪfərənt/ &middot; *DIF-ur-uhnt*
**Русский:** другой; разный; отличающийся
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a different color*, *Her plan is different*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more different*, *most different*
**Takes:** *from* + noun (standard); *than* + noun or clause (common American, best before a clause); *to* is British
**Preposition patterns:** [different from](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#different-from)
**Form:** noun *difference*; verb *differ*
**Opposite:** *same*, *similar*
**Contrast:** *Different from* is always correct in American English (*Her account is different from his*). *Different than* is common and natural before a clause (*It was different than I expected*); before a noun some editors still prefer *from*. *Different to* is British. Russian *другой* has three English matches: *different* (not the same: *a different answer*), *another* (one more or a replacement, singular: *another cup*), and *other* (*the other side*). Before a plural noun, *different* often means *разные* (*different colors*). The adverb is *differently*: *She thinks differently*, not *She thinks different* in careful English. Grade it with *very*, *completely* or *a little*; *no different* means exactly the same (*Today was no different*).

Different has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Not the same as someone or something else.** (отличающийся, непохожий, другой)

1. My brother is completely *different* from me.
2. The job turned out to be *different* than I expected.
3. Life in the city couldn't be *more different* from life on the farm.

**2. Before a plural noun: separate and of several kinds.** (разный, различный)

4. The shirt comes in five *different* colors.
5. I've called three *different* times and nobody answers.
6. People have *different* reasons for moving here.

**3. Not the one already mentioned or usual; another.** (другой, иной)

7. Can I try a *different* size?
8. She moved to a *different* school in the fall.
9. Let's talk about something *different*.

**4. Unusual or new, sometimes as a polite way to say you do not like something.** (необычный, оригинальный)

10. Well, the color is certainly *different*.
11. I wanted something *different* for my birthday this year.

### typical

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɪpɪkəl/ &middot; *TIP-ih-kuhl*
**Русский:** типичный; обычный
**Type:** quality; also evaluative
**Position:** both — *a typical day*, *That's typical of him*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more typical*, *most typical*
**Takes:** *of* + person or group (*typical of him*); *for* + place, time or group, common in American English (*typical for July*); *of* + person + *to*-infinitive (*It's typical of her to be late*)
**Preposition patterns:** [typical of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#typical-of)
**Form:** -al from *type*
**Opposite:** *atypical*, *unusual*
**Contrast:** For behavior, *It's typical of him to* + verb is the standard pattern; *typical for him* is common in speech but less careful. For places and times, American English uses both: *typical of* and *typical for this time of year*. Never ✗ *typical to him*. *Typical!* on its own is a complaint: something annoying has happened again, as expected — *как всегда!*. Russian *обычный* matches *typical* only before a noun (*on a typical day* — в обычный день); after a verb use *normal* or *usual* (*That's normal*).

Typical has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Having the usual qualities of a particular kind of person, thing or group.** (типичный, характерный)

1. It was a *typical* American breakfast: eggs, bacon and pancakes.
2. This is *typical* of houses built in the 1950s.
3. Heavy rain is *typical* for this time of year.

**2. Normal and average; happening in the usual way.** (обычный, средний)

4. On a *typical* day, I get to the office at eight.
5. The *most typical* customer is a woman in her thirties.
6. A *typical* family here has two cars.

**3. Behaving in the annoying way you expect of someone or something.** (в духе (кого-то), как всегда)

7. It's *typical* of him to leave the dishes for me.
8. The bus is late again. *Typical*!
9. That's so *typical* of this company.

### related

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈleɪtɪd/ &middot; *rih-LAY-tid*
**Русский:** связанный; родственный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — *related issues*, *The two problems are related*
**Comparison:** more/most — usually with *closely*: *more closely related*, *most closely related*
**Takes:** *to* + noun (connection or family: *related to the case*, *related to me*)
**Preposition patterns:** [related to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#related-to)
**Form:** -ed from *relate*; also in compounds: *work-related*, *stress-related*
**Opposite:** *unrelated*
**Contrast:** *Related to*, never ✗ *related with*: Russian *связан с* pulls toward *with*, but English uses *to*. *Are you two related?* asks whether people are family; the noun for the person is *a relative*, not *a related*. *Relative* as an adjective means compared with something else (*relative safety*) and is a different word. In compounds, *-related* means caused by or connected with: *a work-related injury*, *alcohol-related crashes*.

Related has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Connected with something, often by cause.** (связанный, имеющий отношение (к))

1. Police think the two robberies are *related*.
2. She asked a few questions *related* to the budget.
3. He takes a lot of sick days for stress-*related* health problems.

**2. In the same family, by birth or marriage.** (родственный, состоящий в родстве)

4. Are you *related* to the Johnsons down the street?
5. We have the same last name, but we're not *related*.
6. She's *related* to me by marriage.

**3. Of languages, animals or plants: coming from the same origin.** (родственный (о языках, видах))

7. Russian and Polish are closely *related* languages.
8. Humans are *more closely related* to chimps than to gorillas.

### used

**Pronunciation:** /juːst; juːzd/ &middot; *YOOST; YOOZD*
**Русский:** привыкший; подержанный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — *used to* after a verb (*I'm used to it*); *used* meaning secondhand before a noun (*a used car*)
**Comparison:** more/most — in the *accustomed* sense: *more used to*, *so used to*; not gradable in the *secondhand* sense
**Takes:** *to* + noun or *-ing* after *be*, *get* or *become* (*used to the noise*, *used to working nights*)
**Preposition patterns:** [used to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#used-to)
**Form:** past participle of *use*
**Contrast:** Two different things are spelled *used to*. The adjective, after *be* or *get*, means accustomed and needs a noun or *-ing*: *I'm used to getting up early*, never ✗ *I'm used to get up early*. The habit-in-the-past form has no *be* and takes a bare verb: *I used to get up early* (раньше вставал рано, а теперь нет). *Be used to* is the state (привык); *get used to* is the process (привыкнуть): *You'll get used to it*. In *used to* both are said /juːst/, with the *s* of *loose*; *a used car* has /juːzd/, with a *z*. For a verb meaning 'employed', *used* is a passive verb, not this adjective: *This room is used for storage*.

Used has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Familiar with something, so that it no longer seems strange or difficult.** (привыкший (к))

1. I'm not *used* to this heat yet.
2. You'll get *used* to working nights after a week or two.
3. After a year in Chicago, she's *more used* to the cold.

**2. Owned by someone else before; not new.** (подержанный, б/у)

4. We bought a *used* car for our daughter.
5. The store sells *used* books and records.

**3. Already used once, so not clean or not able to be used again.** (использованный)

6. Put the *used* towels in the basket.
7. Throw *used* needles into the red container.

### accustomed

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkʌstəmd/ &middot; *uh-KUHS-tuhmd*
**Русский:** привыкший; привычный
**Type:** participial
**Position:** both — *accustomed to* after a verb (*She's accustomed to it*); before a noun it means usual (*his accustomed seat*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more accustomed*, *most accustomed*
**Takes:** *to* + noun or *-ing* after *be*, *get*, *become* or *grow* (*accustomed to the noise*, *accustomed to working late*)
**Preposition patterns:** [accustomed to](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#accustomed-to)
**Form:** -ed from *accustom*
**Opposite:** *unaccustomed*
**Contrast:** The *to* is a preposition, so a verb after it takes *-ing*: *accustomed to driving*, not ✗ *accustomed to drive* (that older pattern survives only in very formal writing). *Accustomed to* means the same as *used to* but is more formal; in conversation Americans say *I'm used to it*. *Be accustomed* is the state; *get*, *become* or *grow accustomed* is the change. Before a noun, *accustomed* means usual and is literary: *in her accustomed place*.

Accustomed has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Familiar with something because you have experienced it often.** (привыкший (к))

1. She's *accustomed* to working long hours.
2. My eyes slowly grew *accustomed* to the dark.
3. The kids became *more accustomed* to the new school by spring.

**2. Before a noun: usual or habitual — formal.** (привычный, обычный)

4. He sat in his *accustomed* seat by the window.
5. She answered with her *accustomed* calm.

### familiar

**Pronunciation:** /fəˈmɪljər/ &middot; *fuh-MIL-yur*
**Русский:** знакомый; осведомлённый; фамильярный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a familiar face*, *The name sounds familiar*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more familiar*, *most familiar*
**Takes:** *with* + thing (the person knows it: *familiar with the software*); *to* + person (it is known to them: *familiar to most Americans*)
**Preposition patterns:** [familiar with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#familiar-with)
**Form:** noun *familiarity*
**Opposite:** *unfamiliar*, *strange*
**Contrast:** The preposition swaps the subject. *I'm familiar with the song* means I know it; *The song is familiar to me* means the same thing from the other side. Never ✗ *I'm familiar to the song*. Russian *знаком с* matches *familiar with* for things, but for people you have met, English prefers *know* or *have met*: *I know him*, not *I'm familiar with him*, which sounds like you only know about him. *Фамильярный* is only one sense (too informal), and the Russian noun *знакомый* (a person you know) is *an acquaintance*, not *a familiar*.

Familiar has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Known to you, or easy to recognize because you have seen or heard it before.** (знакомый)

1. It was nice to see a *familiar* face at the party.
2. Your name sounds *familiar* — have we met?
3. The story will be *familiar* to anyone who grew up in a small town.

**2. Knowing something well.** (знакомый (с), хорошо знающий, осведомлённый)

4. Are you *familiar* with this software?
5. I'm not very *familiar* with the area.
6. After a month, she was *more familiar* with the rules.

**3. Friendly and informal, sometimes too informal with someone you do not know well.** (фамильярный; непринуждённый)

7. He was a little too *familiar* with the waitress.
8. The two men were on *familiar* terms after years of working together.

### famous

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfeɪməs/ &middot; *FAY-muhs*
**Русский:** знаменитый, известный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — *a famous singer*, *She became famous*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more famous*, *most famous*
**Takes:** *for* + what makes it known (*famous for its pizza*); *as* + role (*famous as a singer*)
**Preposition patterns:** [famous for](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#famous-for)
**Form:** noun *fame*
**Opposite:** *unknown*, *obscure*
**Contrast:** *Famous for*, never ✗ *famous by*, ✗ *famous with* or ✗ *famous of*: *The city is famous for its jazz* (славится джазом). *Famous as* names the role: *famous as an actor*. For fame for something bad, use *notorious* or *infamous*; *infamous* is not the opposite of *famous* but its dark side, and it is stressed IN-fuh-muhs. Russian *известный* is often just *well-known* (*a well-known doctor in town*); *famous* is stronger, known to very many people.

Known about by very many people.

1. New Orleans is *famous* for its food and music.
2. He was *famous* as a boxer before he went into politics.
3. The Grand Canyon is one of the *most famous* sights in America.

### anxious

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæŋkʃəs/ &middot; *ANGK-shuhs*
**Русский:** тревожный, встревоженный; стремящийся
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — *an anxious mother*, *She was anxious about it*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more anxious*, *most anxious*
**Takes:** *about* + noun or *-ing* (worry: *anxious about the test*); *for* + person (worry for them: *anxious for his safety*) or + thing (wanting it: *anxious for news*); *to*-infinitive (eager: *anxious to leave*); *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [anxious about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#anxious-about)
**Form:** noun *anxiety*
**Contrast:** The preposition changes the meaning. *Anxious about* is worry (*anxious about money*). *Anxious to* + verb and *anxious for* + thing usually mean eager, often with a little impatience (*anxious to get home*, *anxious for news*). Some American usage guides object to *anxious* for 'eager' and prefer *eager*, but it is very common. *Anxious* is stronger and more lasting than *nervous*: you are *nervous* before a speech, *anxious* about your health.

Anxious has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Worried and nervous about something that might happen.** (встревоженный, обеспокоенный)

1. She's *anxious* about her job interview tomorrow.
2. The longer we waited, the *more anxious* I got.
3. Parents are *anxious* for the safety of their kids.

**2. Of a time or a look: full of worry, or causing it.** (тревожный)

4. There were a few *anxious* moments before the plane landed.
5. After an *anxious* wait, the doctor came out.

**3. Wanting very much to do or have something, often impatiently.** (стремящийся, жаждущий, с нетерпением ждущий)

6. We're *anxious* to get home before dark.
7. The company is *anxious* for a quick deal.
8. He was *anxious* that nobody should find out.

### eager

**Pronunciation:** /ˈiːɡər/ &middot; *EE-gur*
**Русский:** жаждущий, стремящийся; полный энтузиазма
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — *eager fans*, *She was eager to help*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more eager*, *most eager*
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (*eager to help*); *for* + noun (*eager for news*)
**Preposition patterns:** [eager for](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#eager-for)

Spelling: two *c*s, two *s*s, one *l*. Don't confuse it with *successive* (последовательный, one after another): *three successive wins*.

Successful has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Achieving what was intended.** (успешный, удачный)

1. The surgery was completely *successful*.
2. Were you *successful* in getting a refund?
3. This year's fundraiser was *more successful* than last year's.

**2. Having achieved money, status or fame in your work.** (преуспевающий, успешный)

4. Her father is a *successful* lawyer in Boston.
5. He became very *successful* as a songwriter.

**3. Of a business, product or work: popular and making money.** (успешный, прибыльный, популярный)

6. They own a *successful* chain of restaurants.
7. It was the *most successful* movie of the year.

**Русский:** недостающий; лишённый (чего-то)
**Type:** participial
**Position:** predicative only — *Support was lacking*, *He's lacking in confidence*

1. Real leadership has been *lacking* in this company for years.
2. Evidence against him is *lacking*.
3. I found the service sadly *lacking*.

**2. lacking in: not having enough of a quality.** (лишённый (чего-то), испытывающий недостаток)

4. The food was *lacking* in flavor.
5. She's smart but *lacking* in experience.

**Русский:** короткий; низкий; недостающий
**Type:** size; also quality
**Position:** both — *short hair*, *The meeting was short*; *short of* and *short with* after a verb
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *shorter*, *shortest*
**Takes:** *of* + noun (not having enough: *short of money*); *on* + noun, informal (*short on time*); *with* + person (rude: *short with me*); *for* + full name (*Liz is short for Elizabeth*)

The adverb *shortly* means soon, not briefly: *I'll be there shortly* (скоро буду). *In short* means *in a few words*. *Shorts* (the clothes) is a plural noun.

4. He's *shorter* than his little brother.

**3. Lasting only a little time, or having few words or pages.** (короткий, краткий)

6. We took a *short* break for lunch.
7. Life is too *short* to waste on people you don't like.
8. Keep your answer *short*.

**4. Not having enough of something, or less than the amount needed.** (испытывающий нехватку; недостающий, неполный)

9. We're a little *short* of cash this month.
10. I'm *short* on time — can we talk later?
11. The cash register was twenty dollars *short*.

**5. Speaking in a rude, impatient way; quick to lose patience.** (резкий, грубоватый; вспыльчивый)

12. Sorry I was *short* with you on the phone.
13. He gave a *short* answer and walked away.
14. Dad has a *short* temper, so don't bring it up tonight.

**6. short for: being a shorter form of a name or word.** (сокращение (от))

15. Bob is *short* for Robert.
16. *Ad* is *short* for *advertisement*.

**7. Of a vowel sound: said more briefly, as in *cat* or *sit*.** (краткий (о гласном))

17. The *a* in *hat* is a *short* vowel.

### sure

**Pronunciation:** /ʃʊr/ &middot; *SHUUR*
**Русский:** уверенный; несомненный; верный
**Type:** quality
**Position:** both — mostly after a verb (*I'm sure*); before a noun in the *certain* and *reliable* senses (*a sure sign*, *a sure thing*)
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *surer*, *surest*, *more sure*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing* (*sure of the answer*, *sure of winning*); *about* + noun (*sure about that*); *that*-clause (*sure that he's right*); *wh*-clause (*not sure why*); *to*-infinitive (certain to happen: *sure to win*)
**Preposition patterns:** [sure of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#sure-of)
**Form:** adverb *surely*
**Opposite:** *unsure*, *uncertain*
**Contrast:** *I'm sure of it* and *I'm sure that...* describe the speaker's belief. *She's sure to win* describes what will happen: others are sure, not necessarily she. *Be sure to* + verb and *make sure* are instructions: *Be sure to lock the door* (обязательно запри дверь). *Sure!* meaning 'yes, of course' is an adverb, and so is informal American *It sure is cold* (ну и холодно же). *Surely* in a question expresses surprise or hope (*Surely you're not leaving?*), not *certainly*. *For sure* means definitely.

Sure has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Certain; having no doubt about something.** (уверенный)

1. Are you *sure* you locked the car?
2. I'm not *sure* why she left so early.
3. I've never been *more sure* of anything.

**2. Certain to happen or to be true.** (несомненный, обязательно (будет))

4. The new phone is *sure* to sell out quickly.
5. Betting on the Yankees is never a *sure* thing.

**3. Reliable and proven to work.** (верный, надёжный)

6. Dark clouds are a *sure* sign of rain.
7. The *surest* way to lose weight is to eat less.

**4. Confident and steady.** (уверенный (в себе), твёрдый)

8. She's very *sure* of herself for someone so young.
9. He guided the boat with a *sure* hand.

**5. be sure to, make sure: used to tell someone to do or check something.** (обязательно; убедиться)

10. Be *sure* to call me when you land.
11. Make *sure* the stove is off before you leave.
