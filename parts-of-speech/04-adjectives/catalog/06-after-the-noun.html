Adjectives that change meaning after the noun#

the members present, the people involved, the city proper — adjectives that can follow a noun, and what that does to them.

← The grammar of adjectives · All groups

A small set of adjectives can follow the noun they describe, and several of them mean something different there. The present members are the current ones; the members present are the ones in the room. A concerned parent is worried; the parents concerned are the ones involved. Each entry gives both positions and both meanings, side by side.

present#

Pronunciation
/ˈprɛzənt/ · PREZ-uhnt
Русский
присутствующий; нынешний, настоящий
Type
classifying — time (current) and place (in attendance)
Position
both — before a noun = current (the present owner); after the noun or after be = in attendance (the people present, Was he present?)
Comparison
not gradable — except in the attentive sense: more present
Takes
at + event (present at the meeting); in + place or substance = found in (present in the water)
Opposite
absent (in attendance); past, future (in time)
Contrast
Position changes the meaning. Before the noun present = current: the present members (нынешние члены). After the noun or after be = in the room: the members present (присутствующие члены), Ten members were present. Russian настоящий in the sense real is real or genuine, not present: a real friend (настоящий друг), not a present friend. The noun a present (подарок) has the same sound; the verb to present is stressed on the second syllable (prih-ZENT). at present (в настоящее время) is a fixed phrase.

Present has 6 distinct senses.

1. Existing or happening now; current — before the noun. (нынешний, настоящий, теперешний)

  1. The present owners bought the house in 2015.
  2. We can't hire anyone in the present economy.
  3. The present system just isn't working.

2. In a particular place or at an event; there — after the noun or after a verb. (присутствующий)

  1. Everyone present voted for the plan.
  2. Were you present at the meeting on Monday?
  3. A parent must be present during the interview.

3. Existing or found in something, such as a substance, a place or a group. (имеющийся, содержащийся)

  1. Traces of lead were present in the drinking water.
  2. The virus may be present in the blood for weeks.
  3. Racism is still present in many parts of society.

4. Being dealt with or discussed now; this one — formal, before the noun. (данный, настоящий)

  1. The present study looks at teenagers in rural Ohio.
  2. In the present case, the court found no evidence of fraud.

5. Giving your full attention to what is happening now, not distracted. (включённый в происходящее, живущий «здесь и сейчас»)

  1. Put your phone away and be present with your kids.
  2. She tries to stay present instead of worrying about next week.
  3. I want to be more present with my family.

6. In grammar, expressing the time now — in present tense, present participle. (настоящее (время))

  1. She goes is in the present tense; she went is in the past.
  2. The present participle always ends in -ing.

concerned#

Pronunciation
/kənˈsɜːrnd/ · kuhn-SURND
Русский
обеспокоенный; причастный, имеющий отношение
Type
participial; also emotion
Position
both — before a noun or after be = worried (a concerned parent, We're concerned); right after the noun = involved, affected (the parents concerned)
Comparison
more/most — in the worried sense: more concerned, most concerned
Takes
about or for + noun = worried (concerned about the cost, concerned for her safety); with + noun or -ing = dealing with, or caring about (concerned with facts); that-clause = worried that; as far as … is concerned
Preposition patterns
concerned about
Form
-ed participle of concern
Opposite
unconcerned
Contrast
Position and preposition change the meaning. Before the noun concerned = worried: a concerned parent (обеспокоенный родитель). Right after the noun = involved: the parent concerned (тот родитель, о котором идёт речь). concerned about/for = worried; concerned with = about a subject, or caring about something: The book is concerned with the Civil War (посвящена). concerning is not the adjective of worry in careful writing: it is a preposition (concerning your letter = о вашем письме); in American speech a concerning trend (тревожная тенденция) is common but informal.

Concerned has 5 distinct senses.

1. Worried about something or someone. (обеспокоенный, встревоженный)

  1. Doctors are concerned about the rise in measles cases.
  2. Concerned parents packed the school board meeting.
  3. I'm more concerned about the cost than the time.

2. Involved in or affected by something — right after the noun, or after be. (причастный, имеющий отношение; о котором идёт речь)

  1. The students concerned will be contacted by email.
  2. It was a hard decision for everyone concerned.
  3. Talk to the people who are directly concerned.

3. About a particular subject; dealing with it — in be concerned with. (посвящённый (чему-либо), касающийся)

  1. The first chapter is concerned with life in 1930s Chicago.
  2. Economics is concerned with how people use limited resources.

4. Caring about something and thinking it important — in be concerned with. (озабоченный (чем-либо), которого волнует)

  1. He was more concerned with winning than with playing fair.
  2. She's not concerned with what other people think.

5. In as far as … is concerned: in someone's opinion, or when talking about something. (что касается; по мнению (кого-либо))

  1. As far as I'm concerned, the matter is closed.
  2. As far as money is concerned, we're doing fine.

responsible#

Pronunciation
/rɪˈspɑːnsəbəl/ · rih-SPAHN-suh-buhl
Русский
ответственный; виновный
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both — before a noun = sensible, trustworthy (a responsible adult) or with important duties (a responsible job); right after the noun = the one who caused it or is in charge (the person responsible); after be, any of these (She's responsible for sales, He's very responsible)
Comparison
more/most — more responsible, most responsible
Takes
for + noun or -ing = in charge of, or the cause of (responsible for the accident); to + person or group = answering to (responsible to the board)
Preposition patterns
responsible for
Opposite
irresponsible (in the sensible sense)
Contrast
Position changes the meaning. Before the noun responsible usually = sensible, reliable: a responsible person (ответственный человек). Right after the noun = the one who did it: the person responsible (виновник, тот, кто это сделал). The preposition is for: responsible for the budget, not responsible of or responsible about. responsible to is different: it names the boss you answer to. responsible for can be duty (responsible for sales) or blame (responsible for the accident); context decides. For a job, Americans also often say in charge of: She's in charge of security. Don't confuse with responsive (quick to react: a responsive website).

Responsible has 5 distinct senses.

1. Having the job or duty of dealing with something, and having to answer for it. (ответственный (за что-либо))

  1. Who's responsible for the kids this weekend?
  2. She's responsible for hiring new staff.
  3. Parents are legally responsible for their children's safety.

2. Being the cause of something, especially something bad; to blame. (виновный, виноватый; ставший причиной)

  1. Police are still looking for the people responsible.
  2. Smoking is responsible for most cases of lung cancer.
  3. The landlord was held responsible for the damage.

3. Sensible and able to be trusted to do the right thing, or done in a sensible, careful way. (ответственный, надёжный, благоразумный)

  1. She's a very responsible kid for her age.
  2. My brother is more responsible than I was at sixteen.
  3. The company promotes responsible drinking.

4. Of a job or position: involving important duties and decisions. (ответственный (о должности, работе))

  1. She holds a responsible position at the bank.
  2. It's a very responsible job for someone so young.

5. Having to report to a person or group that has authority over you — in responsible to. (подотчётный, отвечающий (перед кем-либо))

  1. The police chief is responsible to the city council.
  2. The inspector general is responsible only to Congress.

involved#

Pronunciation
/ɪnˈvɑːlvd/ · in-VAHLVD
Русский
причастный, вовлечённый; сложный, запутанный
Type
participial; also quality
Position
both — before a noun = complicated (a long, involved story) or active, engaged (involved parents); right after the noun = taking part, or needed as part of it (the people involved, the costs involved); after a verb, any of these (She got involved, The process is very involved)
Comparison
more/most — more involved, most involved
Takes
in + noun or -ing = taking part in; with + noun = working closely with, or in a relationship with (involved with a coworker)
Preposition patterns
involved in
Form
-ed participle of involve
Opposite
uninvolved
Contrast
Position changes the meaning. Before the noun involved usually = complicated: an involved explanation (запутанное объяснение); with people it = active, engaged: an involved father (отец, который много занимается детьми). After the noun = taking part or connected: the people involved (участники, причастные), the costs involved (связанные с этим расходы). involved in a thing = taking part in it; involved with a person = working closely with them, or having a romantic relationship. Russian участвовать, вмешиваться often comes out as get involved: I don't want to get involved (не хочу в это ввязываться).

Involved has 5 distinct senses.

1. Taking part in something, or connected with it in some way. (причастный, участвующий, вовлечённый)

  1. Everyone involved in the project got a bonus.
  2. Police want to talk to anyone who was involved.
  3. Two cars were involved in the accident.

2. Needed as a necessary part or result of something — after the noun. (связанный (с чем-либо), сопряжённый)

  1. Do you understand the risks involved?
  2. There's a lot of paperwork involved in adopting a child.
  3. The work involved was more than we expected.

3. Giving a lot of time, attention and energy to something. (увлечённый, активно участвующий)

  1. Kids with involved parents tend to do better in school.
  2. She's more involved in the community than anyone I know.
  3. I got so involved in the book that I missed my stop.

4. Complicated and having many parts, so hard to follow — usually before the noun. (сложный, запутанный)

  1. Getting a visa is a long, involved process.
  2. The plot is too involved to explain in a few words.

5. Having a romantic or sexual relationship with someone. (состоящий в отношениях, связанный (с кем-либо))

  1. She's involved with someone she met at work.
  2. Were they romantically involved?

proper#

Pronunciation
/ˈprɑːpər/ · PRAH-pur
Русский
надлежащий, правильный; приличный; собственно
Type
evaluative; also classifying
Position
both — before a noun = right, suitable (the proper tools); right after the noun = in the strict sense, the thing itself (the city proper)
Comparison
more/most — in the polite sense: more proper, most proper
Takes
for + noun = suitable for (proper for a wedding); to + noun = belonging to (formal: customs proper to the region); it is proper to / that
Opposite
improper
Contrast
Position changes the meaning. Before the noun proper = correct, suitable: the proper place (подходящее место). Right after the noun = strictly speaking, the thing itself: the city proper (собственно город, без пригородов), the concert proper (сам концерт, без разогрева). Russian собственный (own) is not proper: my own room, not my proper room. British a proper mess (= total) is not American; Americans say a real mess. The adverb is properly: Do it properly, not Do it proper.

Proper has 6 distinct senses.

1. Right, correct or suitable for the situation or the job. (правильный, надлежащий, подходящий)

  1. Make sure you use the proper tools.
  2. Put everything back in its proper place.
  3. The form wasn't filled out in the proper way.

2. Real and complete; of the kind it should be. (настоящий, нормальный, полноценный)

  1. I haven't had a proper meal all day.
  2. The town doesn't even have a proper grocery store, just a gas station.

3. Following the accepted rules of polite or formal behavior. (приличный, пристойный, благовоспитанный)

  1. It isn't proper to talk with your mouth full.
  2. Her grandmother was a very proper lady.
  3. He was always more proper than his brothers.

4. In its strict or main sense; the thing itself and not what is around it — after the noun. (собственно, в узком смысле)

  1. The city proper has about 600,000 people, but the metro area has two million.
  2. We didn't reach the town proper until noon.
  3. The meeting proper doesn't start until ten.

5. Belonging to or typical of a particular person, thing or place — formal, in proper to. (свойственный, присущий)

  1. The judge acted within the powers proper to her office.
  2. They studied the plants proper to the desert Southwest.

6. In grammar, naming one particular person, place or thing — in proper noun, proper name. (собственное (имя))

  1. Chicago is a proper noun, so it starts with a capital letter.
  2. Most proper names don't take the.

available#

Pronunciation
/əˈveɪləbəl/ · uh-VAY-luh-buhl
Русский
доступный, имеющийся (в наличии); свободный
Type
quality
Position
both — also right after the noun, especially after a superlative, only or every: the best seats available, the only room available
Comparison
more/most — more available, most available, usually with an adverb: more widely available
Takes
to + person or group (available to all members); for + noun (available for comment, available for rent); to-infinitive (available to help)
Preposition patterns
available to
Form
-able from avail
Opposite
unavailable
Contrast
After the noun, available follows a superlative, the only or every: the cheapest flight available, every seat available; the available seats is also fine. With a person it means free, not busy: Are you available Thursday? = Are you free Thursday? Russian доступный is not always available: доступные цены are affordable prices, доступное объяснение is an accessible or easy-to-understand explanation, and a building open to wheelchairs is accessible.

Available has 4 distinct senses.

1. Able to be bought, used or got. (доступный, имеющийся (в наличии, в продаже))

  1. The new model is available in three colors.
  2. We booked the only room available.
  3. Fresh seafood is more widely available on the coast.

2. Of a person: free to meet, talk or do something; not busy. (свободный, не занятый)

  1. Is Dr. Lee available on Thursday afternoon?
  2. The senator was not available for comment.
  3. I'm available to help after five.

3. Not in a romantic relationship, and so free to start one. (свободный, одинокий (не в отношениях))

  1. Is he available, or is he seeing someone?
  2. All the available guys at the party were ten years younger than me.

4. Able and willing to share your feelings and give emotional support — often emotionally available. (эмоционально открытый)

  1. Her father was never emotionally available to her.
  2. After the divorce, he wasn't emotionally available to anyone.

possible#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɑːsəbəl/ · PAH-suh-buhl
Русский
возможный
Type
evaluative — whether something can happen or be done
Position
both — also right after the noun, especially after a superlative or every, all, only: the lowest price possible = the lowest possible price
Comparison
not gradable — but it often strengthens a superlative: the best possible, as soon as possible
Takes
to-infinitive after it (It's possible to park here); for + person + to-infinitive (possible for us to meet); that-clause after it (It's possible that she forgot)
Form
-ible, from Latin posse (to be able)
Opposite
impossible
Contrast
With a to-infinitive, the subject of possible is it, never the person: It's possible for me to come or I can come, not I am possible to come. After the noun it follows a superlative or every/all: the best result possible and the best possible result are both right; every effort possible = every possible effort. possible = может быть; likely or probable = скорее всего. Russian Возможно, он прав is Maybe he's right or He may be right, not Possible he is right. The adverb is possibly: Could you possibly help?

Possible has 4 distinct senses.

1. Able to be done or achieved. (возможный, осуществимый)

  1. Is it possible to get there by train?
  2. Thanks to donors, the new library was made possible.
  3. The doctors did everything possible to save him.

2. That may happen or be true, but is not certain. (возможный, вероятный)

  1. It's possible that she just forgot.
  2. Thunderstorms are possible later tonight.
  3. There's another possible explanation.

3. The most or least that can exist or be done — used with superlatives and with as … as. (максимально возможный, наилучший (наихудший) из возможных)

  1. We want the best possible result for our clients.
  2. Please reply as soon as possible.
  3. It happened at the worst time possible.

4. Suitable for a purpose, and so worth considering or choosing. (потенциальный, подходящий)

  1. She's being talked about as a possible candidate for mayor.
  2. We looked at three possible locations for the new store.

imaginable#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈmædʒənəbəl/ · ih-MAJ-uh-nuh-buhl
Русский
мыслимый, вообразимый; всевозможный
Type
emphasizing — strengthens a superlative or every, all, no
Position
both — before a noun (every imaginable color) and after be (Is it even imaginable?); also right after the noun, usually after a superlative or every, all, no: every color imaginable
Comparison
not gradable
Form
-able from imagine
Opposite
unimaginable
Contrast
imaginable mostly strengthens a superlative or every/all/no, and then it sits naturally after the noun: the best food imaginable (лучшая еда, какую только можно представить), every problem imaginable (все мыслимые проблемы). Don't confuse it with imaginary = not real (an imaginary friend, воображаемый друг) or imaginative = full of new ideas (an imaginative writer, писатель с богатым воображением).

Imaginable has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used after a superlative or every, all, no to say that something includes everything or is the most that can be thought of. (всевозможный, какой только можно представить)

  1. The shop sells ice cream in every flavor imaginable.
  2. It was the worst weather imaginable for a wedding.
  3. We tried every imaginable way to fix it.

2. Possible to imagine or think of. (мыслимый, вообразимый)

  1. Is a world without cars even imaginable?
  2. Twenty years ago, a phone like this was hardly imaginable.

elect#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈlɛkt/ · ih-LEKT
Русский
избранный (но ещё не вступивший в должность)
Type
classifying
Position
after the noun — joined to a title, usually with a hyphen: the president-elect, the governor-elect; before the noun only in the formal chosen sense (the elect few)
Comparison
not gradable
Form
joined to a title with a hyphen; plural on the title: presidents-elect
Contrast
The adjective elect comes after a title: the president-elect (избранный президент, ещё не вступивший в должность), not the elect president. Once the person takes office, the title stands alone: the president. For someone already in office, Russian избранный is elected: the newly elected mayor, an elected official. to elect is the verb (They elected her mayor); the elect is a noun (избранные).

Elect has 2 distinct senses.

1. Chosen in an election but not yet in office — after a title. (избранный (до вступления в должность))

  1. The president-elect will take office on January 20.
  2. She met with the mayor-elect on Tuesday.
  3. The governor-elect named her new chief of staff.

2. Specially chosen or selected, especially by God — formal or religious, before the noun. (избранный)

  1. The preacher warned that only the elect few would be saved.
  2. The Puritans saw themselves as an elect people.

galore#

Pronunciation
/ɡəˈlɔːr/ · guh-LOR
Русский
в изобилии, хоть отбавляй
Type
quantity
Position
after the noun — only: bargains galore, never galore bargains or The bargains were galore
Comparison
not gradable
Form
from Irish go leor (enough)
Contrast
galore comes only after the noun, and the noun is plural or uncountable: restaurants galore (ресторанов хоть отбавляй), fun galore. It never goes before the noun and never after be: not galore restaurants, not The restaurants are galore. It is informal and cheerful, common in ads; in neutral writing use plenty of or lots of.

In large numbers or amounts; plenty.

  1. The outlet mall has bargains galore this weekend.
  2. There were prizes galore for the kids.
  3. The movie has car chases and explosions galore.

/əˈplɛnti/ · uh-PLEN-tee
Русский
в изобилии, вдоволь
Type
quantity
Position
after the noun — mainly (food aplenty); sometimes after be in literary style
Comparison
not gradable
Form
a- + plenty
Contrast
aplenty goes after the noun, like galore: food aplenty (еды вдоволь), not aplenty food. It sounds literary or journalistic; in speech Americans say plenty of food or lots of food. plenty comes before of + noun; aplenty comes after the noun, with no of.

In large amounts; more than enough.

  1. There was food aplenty at the church picnic.
  2. The team has talent aplenty but no real leader.
  3. In a good summer, there are tomatoes aplenty.

incarnate#

Pronunciation
/ɪnˈkɑːrnət/ · in-KAHR-nuht
Русский
воплощённый, во плоти
Type
emphasizing
Position
after the noun — evil incarnate, the devil incarnate
Comparison
not gradable
Form
from Latin incarnatus (made flesh)
Contrast
incarnate goes after the noun, usually an abstract noun with no article: evil incarnate, greed incarnate (воплощённое зло, сама жадность). Not incarnate evil. The adjective ends in a weak -nuht; the rare verb to incarnate ends in -nayt. In everyday American English, personified or the embodiment of is more common: She is kindness personified.

Incarnate has 2 distinct senses.

  1. To the kids, the new principal was evil incarnate.
  2. In the movie he plays the devil incarnate.
  3. She was elegance incarnate in that black dress.

2. In human form; having a human body — religious. (воплотившийся, во плоти)

  1. Christians believe that Jesus is God incarnate.
  2. In the story, a god incarnate walks among ordinary people.

apparent#

Pronunciation
/əˈpɛrənt/ · uh-PAIR-uhnt
Русский
очевидный, явный; кажущийся
Type
evaluative
Position
both — after be = clear, obvious (It was apparent that…); before a noun often = seeming (his apparent calm); after heir = next in line (the heir apparent)
Comparison
more/most — more apparent, most apparent
Takes
to + person (apparent to everyone); from + noun (apparent from her face); that-clause after it (It was apparent that he was lying)
Form
-ent, related to appear
Contrast
Position shifts the meaning. After be, and in it is apparent that, apparent = obvious (очевидный). Before the noun it usually means seeming, and the truth may be different: his apparent success (кажущийся успех), an apparent heart attack (судя по всему, сердечный приступ). for no apparent reason = for no visible reason. The adverb apparently follows the second meaning: Apparently he quit = I heard he quit (похоже, говорят), not Obviously he quit. After heir it follows the noun: the heir apparent.

Apparent has 3 distinct senses.

1. Easy to see or understand; obvious — usually after a verb. (очевидный, явный)

  1. It soon became apparent that we were lost.
  2. The dog started barking for no apparent reason.
  3. The problems became more apparent as the year went on.

2. Seeming to be true or real, although it may not be — before the noun. (кажущийся, видимый; предполагаемый)

  1. Police say the death was an apparent suicide.
  2. Despite her apparent calm, she was terrified.
  3. The apparent winner lost after a recount.

3. Certain to take a title or position in the future — in heir apparent, after the noun. (прямой (наследник); признанный преемник)

  1. The prince is heir apparent to the throne.
  2. She's seen as the heir apparent to the current CEO.

certain#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɜːrtən/ · SUR-tuhn
Русский
уверенный; несомненный; определённый, некий
Type
evaluative; also classifying (certain people = particular)
Position
both — after be = sure (I'm certain); before a noun = definite (certain death) or particular, some (certain people, a certain Mr. Jones)
Comparison
more/most — in the sure senses only: more certain, most certain
Takes
of or about + noun or -ing (certain of the facts); that-clause (certain that she's right); to-infinitive = sure to happen (She's certain to win)
Preposition patterns
certain of
Opposite
uncertain
Contrast
Position and article change the meaning. After be certain = sure: I'm certain (я уверен). Before a noun without an article it can mean sure to happen, certain death (верная смерть), or some, not named, certain people (некоторые люди). a certain = one particular: a certain Mr. Smith (некий мистер Смит), a certain charm (некоторое обаяние). She is certain to win = she will surely win; She is certain that she'll win = she believes it. When Russian определённый means exact, use specific: a specific date.

Certain has 5 distinct senses.

1. Completely sure; having no doubt — after a verb. (уверенный)

  1. Are you certain you locked the door?
  2. I'm not certain of the exact date.
  3. I've never been more certain of anything in my life.

2. Sure to happen or be true; definite. (несомненный, неизбежный, верный)

  1. The bill is certain to pass the Senate.
  2. It's certain that prices will go up next year.
  3. Without a helmet, he faced certain death.

3. Particular, but not named or described exactly — before the noun. (определённый, некоторые)

  1. Certain foods can trigger migraines.
  2. The store is closed on certain holidays.
  3. You have to be a certain age to rent a car.

4. In a certain + name: a person the listener doesn't know, known only by that name. (некий, некто)

  1. A certain Mr. Jones called while you were out.
  2. The letter was signed by a certain Dr. Alvarez.

5. Some, but not a large amount; real but hard to describe exactly — in a certain. (некоторый, известный (в какой-то мере))

  1. To a certain extent, she's right.
  2. The old house has a certain charm.
  3. There's always a certain amount of risk.

particular#

Pronunciation
/pərˈtɪkjələr/ · pur-TIK-yuh-lur
Русский
конкретный, определённый; особый; разборчивый
Type
classifying — this one and not another; also quality (fussy)
Position
both — before a noun = specific or special (this particular case); after be = fussy (She's particular about food); after the noun with to = found only in (a dish particular to Louisiana)
Comparison
more/most — in the fussy sense only: more particular, most particular
Takes
about + noun or -ing = fussy (particular about his coffee); to + noun = found only in (particular to the region)
Contrast
Position changes the meaning. Before the noun particular = this specific one: a particular hotel (определённый, конкретный отель), or special: of particular importance (особой важности). After be, about a person, it = fussy: She's very particular (очень разборчивая). After the noun with to = found only in: a custom particular to New Orleans (свойственный). Russian частный is usually private (a private school) or individual; частный случай is a special case. in particular (в частности, особенно) is a fixed phrase.

Particular has 4 distinct senses.

1. Used to show that you mean one specific person or thing and not others. (конкретный, определённый, именно этот)

  1. Is there a particular book you're looking for?
  2. I love that particular shade of blue.
  3. He left for no particular reason.

2. Greater than usual; special. (особый, особенный)

  1. Pay particular attention to the last question.
  2. This is a matter of particular importance to veterans.

3. Very careful to choose only what you like, and hard to please; fussy. (разборчивый, привередливый)

  1. He's very particular about how his coffee is made.
  2. My cat is particular about what she eats.
  3. She's more particular about hotels than I am.

4. Belonging to or found in only one person, place or thing — in particular to. (свойственный, присущий (только чему-либо))

  1. It's a style of cooking particular to southern Louisiana.
  2. The problem isn't particular to our school; every district has it.

poor#

Pronunciation
/pʊr/ · PUUR
Русский
бедный; плохой, слабый; несчастный
Type
quality; also evaluative
Position
both — before a noun can mean to be pitied (the poor guy); after be never to be pitied, only without money, of low quality or lacking (They're poor, The service was poor); after the noun with in = lacking (food poor in vitamins)
Comparison
-er/-est — poorer, poorest
Takes
in + noun = lacking (poor in natural resources); at + noun or -ing = bad at (poor at math)
Preposition patterns
poor at
Opposite
rich (money); good, excellent (quality)
Contrast
Before the noun poor often means unlucky, to be pitied, with nothing to do with money: the poor woman can be бедняжка. After be it never means that: The woman is poor = у неё нет денег. After the noun with in = lacking: a diet poor in fiber. the poor = poor people (plural verb, no -s): The poor pay more. For low quality, poor is the natural word with health, results and performance: poor health, poor grades. In many American accents poor sounds like pour.

Poor has 5 distinct senses.

1. Having very little money and few possessions. (бедный, небогатый)

  1. They grew up poor in rural Mississippi.
  2. It's one of the poorest counties in the country.
  3. The program helps poor families pay for heat.

2. Not good enough in quality, amount or condition; bad. (плохой, низкого качества, слабый)

  1. The food was great, but the service was poor.
  2. My grandfather is in poor health.
  3. Poor sales forced the store to close.

3. Deserving pity; unlucky — before the noun only. (бедный, несчастный, бедняжка)

  1. Poor Sam — he's been sick all week.
  2. The poor dog was shaking in the rain.
  3. Oh, you poor thing!

4. Not skilled or not good at doing something. (слабый, неумелый)

  1. I've always been a poor swimmer.
  2. He's a poor judge of character.
  3. She's poor at keeping in touch with old friends.

5. Having very little of something that is needed — often after the noun, in poor in. (бедный (чем-либо), с низким содержанием)

  1. Diets poor in fiber can cause health problems.
  2. The soil here is poor, so not much grows.
  3. It's a country poor in natural resources.
