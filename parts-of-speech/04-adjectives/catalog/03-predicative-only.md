# Adjectives that only follow a verb

> *afraid*, *asleep*, *alive*, *alone* — adjectives that can come after *be* but never before a noun.

[← The grammar of adjectives](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These adjectives can follow a verb — *the baby is asleep* — but cannot stand before a noun: never *an asleep baby*. Most of them begin with *a-* because they grew out of old prepositional phrases (*asleep* was once *on sleep*), and each entry gives the word that does the job before a noun instead: *a sleeping baby*, *a living animal*, *a frightened child*.

---
### afraid

**Pronunciation:** /əˈfreɪd/ &middot; *uh-FRAYD*
**Русский:** испуганный; боящийся
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — also after a noun when it has its own phrase (*a kid afraid of the dark*); before a noun use *frightened* or *scared*: *a frightened child*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more afraid*, *most afraid*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing* (what you fear); *for* + person (fear for their safety); *to*-infinitive; *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [afraid of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#afraid-of)
**Form:** a- adjective, from an old participle
**Opposite:** *unafraid*
**Contrast:** Never directly before a noun: *a frightened child*, *a scared dog*, not *an afraid child*. *Afraid of* + *-ing* is fear that something will happen (*afraid of falling*); *afraid to* + verb is being too scared to do it (*afraid to jump*). *Afraid for* is fear on someone else's behalf (*afraid for her son*). *I'm afraid* often has nothing to do with fear: it softens bad news, like Russian *боюсь, что* or *к сожалению*, and the short answers are *I'm afraid so* and *I'm afraid not*. Strengthen it with *very*, *really* or *terribly*, not *much*.

Afraid has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Feeling fear of someone or something.** (испуганный, боящийся)

1. The kids were *afraid* of the dog next door.
2. He was too *afraid* to move.
3. As a kid, I was more *afraid* of the dark than of anything else.

**2. Worried that something bad will happen, or worried about someone's safety.** (опасающийся, беспокоящийся)

4. She was *afraid* that nobody would show up.
5. He's *afraid* of losing his job.
6. We were *afraid* for our daughter when the storm hit.

**3. I'm afraid: used to say politely that something is unwelcome or disappointing.** (боюсь, что; к сожалению)

7. I'm *afraid* we're out of the large size.
8. "Is it serious?" "I'm *afraid* so."
9. I'm *afraid* I can't make it on Friday.

**4. not afraid of, not afraid to: willing to do or face something hard, unpleasant or unpopular.** (не боящийся (трудностей), готовый (на))

10. He's not *afraid* of hard work.
11. Don't be *afraid* to ask questions.
12. She's never been *afraid* to speak her mind.

### alive

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlaɪv/ &middot; *uh-LYV*
**Русский:** живой
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also right after a noun (*the happiest man alive*) or an object (*keep hope alive*); before a noun use *living* or *live*: *living things*, *live lobsters*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more alive*, *most alive*, in the 'full of energy' sense
**Takes:** *with* + noun (full of); *to* + noun (aware of)
**Form:** a- adjective, from Old English *on life*
**Opposite:** *dead*
**Contrast:** Never before a noun: *a live fish* (/laɪv/) or *a living animal*, not *an alive fish*. Russian *живой* splits three ways: *alive* after a verb (*The fish is still alive*), *live* before a noun for animals, music and broadcasts (*live music*, *a live broadcast*), and *lively* for a person or place full of energy (*a lively kid*, *a lively bar*). *Living* describes people and languages that exist now (*living relatives*, *a living language*). *Alive with* means full of (*alive with birds*); *alive to* means aware of.

Alive has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Living; not dead.** (живой)

1. Is the fish still *alive*?
2. He was lucky to be *alive* after the crash.
3. At 97, she's the oldest woman *alive* in the county.

**2. Still existing or in use; not forgotten or lost.** (живой, сохраняющийся)

4. The family keeps the old traditions *alive*.
5. The team's playoff hopes are still *alive*.

**3. Full of energy, interest and feeling.** (полный жизни, оживлённый)

6. I've never felt more *alive* than on that trip.
7. She really comes *alive* on stage.
8. The neighborhood is most *alive* after dark.

**4. alive with: full of moving things, sounds or activity.** (полный (чего-то), кишащий)

9. The pond was *alive* with frogs.
10. By nine o'clock the streets were *alive* with music.

**5. alive to: aware of something and ready to act on it.** (осознающий, чуткий (к))

11. The mayor seems fully *alive* to the risks.
12. Good teachers are *alive* to every student's needs.

### alone

**Pronunciation:** /əˈloʊn/ &middot; *uh-LOHN*
**Русский:** один; одинокий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also right after a noun or pronoun in the sense 'only' (*you alone*, *the fee alone*); before a noun use *lone* or *solitary*: *a lone rider*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more alone*, *most alone*, in the 'lonely' sense; for emphasis *all alone*
**Takes:** *with* + person (nobody else there); *in* + noun or *-ing* (the only one who thinks or feels so)
**Contrast:** Never before a noun: *a lone traveler* or *a woman on her own*, not *an alone woman*. *Alone* means with nobody else and can be neutral or even pleasant (*I like being alone*); *lonely* means sad about it and goes in both positions (*a lonely old man*). In *She lives alone* and *He did it alone* the word is an adverb. *Let alone* is a fixed phrase meaning 'and certainly not' (*I can't boil an egg, let alone cook dinner*), Russian *не говоря уже о*. *Leave me alone* is *оставь меня в покое*, not literally *оставь меня одного*.

Alone has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Without other people; by yourself.** (один, в одиночестве)

1. Were you *alone* in the house last night?
2. I need to be *alone* for a while.
3. The two of them were *alone* in the elevator.

**2. Feeling lonely and without support.** (одинокий)

4. After the divorce, she felt completely *alone*.
5. I've never felt more *alone* than in that huge city.

**3. Being the only person who has a certain feeling, view or problem — often in *not alone*.** (единственный (кто); не одинок)

6. If you're struggling with this, you're not *alone*.
7. She was *alone* in thinking the plan would fail.

**4. Right after a noun or pronoun: only; and nobody or nothing else.** (только, лишь; один (только))

8. You *alone* can make that decision.
9. The shipping *alone* costs fifty dollars.

**5. leave alone: not bothered, touched or changed by anyone.** (в покое)

10. Leave your brother *alone*!
11. Leave the thermostat *alone* — I just set it.

### asleep

**Pronunciation:** /əˈsliːp/ &middot; *uh-SLEEP*
**Русский:** спящий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — before a noun use *sleeping*: *a sleeping baby*
**Comparison:** not gradable — strengthen with *fast*, *sound* or *half*: *fast asleep*, *half asleep*
**Form:** a- adjective, from *on sleep*
**Opposite:** *awake*
**Contrast:** Never before a noun, and not *very asleep*: *a sleeping child*, *sound asleep*. Russian *заснуть* is *fall asleep* (the moment), *спать* is *be asleep* (the state), and *ложиться спать* is *go to bed* or *go to sleep*: *I fell asleep at ten*, *At ten I was already asleep*. *Sleepy* is *сонный* — wanting to sleep, not sleeping. *My foot is asleep* is the everyday way to say a leg or arm has gone numb (*затекла*).

Asleep has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Sleeping.** (спящий)

1. Don't make any noise — the baby's *asleep*.
2. I fell *asleep* on the couch watching TV.
3. By the time we got home, the kids were *fast asleep*.

**2. Of an arm, leg, hand or foot: numb from staying in one position.** (затёкший, онемевший)

4. I can't stand up yet — my foot is *asleep*.
5. My arm fell *asleep* during the movie.

**3. Not paying attention when you should be; not reacting — often *asleep at the wheel* or *asleep at the switch*.** (невнимательный, бездействующий)

6. The regulators were *asleep* at the switch while the bank took huge risks.
7. Our defense was *asleep* on that play.

### awake

**Pronunciation:** /əˈweɪk/ &middot; *uh-WAYK*
**Русский:** не спящий, бодрствующий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — before a noun use a relative clause: *children who are still awake*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more awake*, *most awake*
**Takes:** *to* + noun (aware of)
**Form:** a- adjective, related to the verb *wake*
**Opposite:** *asleep*
**Contrast:** *Awake* is the state; *wake up* is the change: *I woke up at six* (*проснулся*), but *I was awake at six* (*не спал*). *Awake* (*awoke*, *awoken*) and *awaken* (*awakened*) are also formal verbs, but everyday American English uses *wake up*. Strengthen the adjective with *wide* or *fully* (*wide awake*). *Stay awake* is *не спать*, and *lie awake* is *лежать без сна*.

Awake has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Not sleeping.** (не спящий, бодрствующий)

1. Are you still *awake*?
2. I lay *awake* half the night worrying about the test.
3. Two cups of coffee and I'm *wide awake*.

**2. Alert and able to think clearly; not sleepy.** (бодрый)

4. I feel much more *awake* after a shower.
5. Nobody is fully *awake* at a 7 a.m. meeting.

**3. awake to: aware of something and ready to deal with it.** (осознающий)

6. Few people were *awake* to the risks back then.
7. Parents are finally *awake* to the dangers of vaping.

### aware

**Pronunciation:** /əˈwɛr/ &middot; *uh-WAIR*
**Русский:** знающий, осведомлённый
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also after a noun with its own phrase (*people aware of the risks*); before a noun only with an adverb in front: *a politically aware student*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more aware*, *most aware*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing*; *that*-clause; *how*, *what*, *who* and other wh-clauses
**Preposition patterns:** [aware of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#aware-of)
**Opposite:** *unaware*
**Contrast:** The preposition is *of*, not *about*: *aware of the problem*. *Be aware* is the state (*знать, быть в курсе*); *become aware* or *realize* is the moment you notice. *Well aware* stresses that someone knows perfectly well (*I'm well aware of that*). Before a noun *aware* needs an adverb (*an environmentally aware company*); *an aware person* sounds odd — say *an informed person*. *Conscious* overlaps in the 'knowing' sense but also means *в сознании*, which *aware* never does.

Aware has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Knowing that something exists or is happening.** (знающий, осведомлённый; в курсе)

1. Are you *aware* of the new parking rules?
2. I'm *aware* that the deadline is tight.
3. People are much more *aware* of the risks than they used to be.

**2. Noticing something through your senses at the moment it happens.** (замечающий, ощущающий)

4. I suddenly became *aware* of someone standing behind me.
5. She was *aware* of a strange smell in the kitchen.

**3. Informed about and interested in a subject — usually after an adverb such as *politically* or *environmentally*.** (сознательный, хорошо разбирающийся)

6. Young voters today are very politically *aware*.
7. We want to raise an environmentally *aware* generation.

### ashamed

**Pronunciation:** /əˈʃeɪmd/ &middot; *uh-SHAYMD*
**Русский:** пристыжённый; (мне) стыдно
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — before a noun use *shamefaced* or *embarrassed*: *a shamefaced grin*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more ashamed*, *most ashamed*
**Takes:** *of* + noun, *-ing* or *yourself*; *to*-infinitive; *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [ashamed of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#ashamed-of)
**Form:** old past participle of the verb *ashame*; the *-ed* is /d/, not /ɪd/
**Opposite:** *proud*; *unashamed*
**Contrast:** *Ashamed* is guilt about something wrong or bad (*ashamed of lying*); *embarrassed* is awkwardness about something silly or public (*embarrassed when I tripped*). Russian *стыдно* covers both, so choose carefully: *I'm ashamed* after spilling coffee on your boss sounds far too strong. *Ashamed to* + verb means you do not want to do it because of shame (*too ashamed to ask*). *Shameful* describes the act (*shameful behavior*), and *It's a shame* means *жаль*, not *стыдно*.

Ashamed has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Feeling guilty or embarrassed because of something you did, or because of someone or something connected with you.** (пристыжённый; (мне) стыдно)

1. You should be *ashamed* of yourself.
2. He was *ashamed* that he had lied to his parents.
3. I've never been more *ashamed* of my own behavior.

**2. Unwilling to do something because you feel shame.** (стыдящийся (сделать что-то))

4. I'm *ashamed* to admit that I've never read it.
5. She was too *ashamed* to ask for help.

### alike

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlaɪk/ &middot; *uh-LYK*
**Русский:** похожие, одинаковые
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — needs a plural subject or *look alike*; before a noun use *similar* or *identical*: *similar houses*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more alike*, *most alike*
**Form:** a- + *like*
**Opposite:** *different*
**Contrast:** *Alike* joins two or more things and takes no preposition: *The sisters are alike*, not *She is alike her sister*. For one thing compared with another use *like* or *similar to* (*She is like her sister*). Never before a noun: *similar cars*, not *alike cars*. In *teachers and students alike* and *treat everyone alike* the word is an adverb meaning 'equally'. *A lookalike* is a noun (*двойник*).

Very similar; like each other.

1. My brother and I are nothing *alike*.
2. No two snowflakes are exactly *alike*.
3. The twins look more *alike* every year.

### ablaze

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbleɪz/ &middot; *uh-BLAYZ*
**Русский:** пылающий, охваченный огнём
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also after an object (*set the barn ablaze*); before a noun use *blazing* or *burning*: *a burning building*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *with* + light, color or feeling
**Form:** a- + *blaze*
**Contrast:** Mostly written and news English; in speech Americans say *on fire*: *The car was on fire*. Never before a noun: *a burning car*, not *an ablaze car*. *Set something ablaze* is the same as *set fire to something*. The figurative senses take *with*: *ablaze with lights*, *eyes ablaze with anger*.

Ablaze has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Burning strongly.** (пылающий, охваченный огнём)

1. Within minutes the whole warehouse was *ablaze*.
2. Rioters set two police cars *ablaze*.

**2. Very bright with lights or colors.** (сияющий, сверкающий)

3. The hills were *ablaze* with fall colors.
4. The stadium was *ablaze* with lights.

**3. Full of strong feeling or excitement.** (горящий (гневом, страстью))

5. Her eyes were *ablaze* with anger.
6. The whole town was *ablaze* with excitement before the big game.

### adrift

**Pronunciation:** /əˈdrɪft/ &middot; *uh-DRIFT*
**Русский:** дрейфующий; без цели
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also after an object (*cast adrift*, *set adrift*); before a noun use *drifting*: *a drifting boat*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** a- + *drift*
**Contrast:** Never before a noun: *a drifting raft*, not *an adrift raft*. *Cast adrift* and *set adrift* mean to leave someone or something with no control or support. In British sports reports *adrift* means behind (*five points adrift*); Americans say *behind*. Russian *дрейфовать* is the verb *drift*: *The boat drifted*, or *The boat was adrift*.

Adrift has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Of a boat or a person at sea: floating without being steered or tied to anything.** (дрейфующий, носимый по волнам)

1. The fishermen were *adrift* for three days before a Coast Guard helicopter found them.
2. The storm snapped the rope, and the canoe was *adrift*.

**2. Without direction, purpose or support.** (потерянный, без цели и опоры)

3. After college, I felt completely *adrift*.
4. Laid-off workers were left *adrift* with no plan for retraining.

### afloat

**Pronunciation:** /əˈfloʊt/ &middot; *uh-FLOHT*
**Русский:** на плаву
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also after an object (*keep the boat afloat*); before a noun use *floating*: *a floating dock*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Form:** a- + *float*
**Contrast:** Never before a noun: *a floating log*, not *an afloat log*. Russian *на плаву* fits both main senses (*keep a company afloat*). In the money sense the usual verbs are *stay*, *keep* and *remain*. *Float* is the verb (*Wood floats*); *afloat* describes the state.

Afloat has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Floating on water; not sinking.** (на плаву, держащийся на воде)

1. The boat was damaged but still *afloat*.
2. She grabbed a cooler to keep herself *afloat*.

**2. Having enough money to keep operating or to pay your bills.** (на плаву, платёжеспособный)

3. The restaurant barely stayed *afloat* during the pandemic.
4. A second job kept us *afloat* that year.

**3. Of a rumor or idea: going around; being talked about.** (ходящий (о слухах))

5. Rumors were *afloat* that the coach would quit.
6. There are a lot of wild theories *afloat* right now.

### aghast

**Pronunciation:** /əˈɡæst/ &middot; *uh-GAST*
**Русский:** потрясённый, в ужасе
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — also set off by commas (*She stared, aghast*); before a noun use *horrified* or *shocked*: *a horrified look*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *at* + noun
**Form:** old past participle of *agast* 'to frighten'; the *h* is silent
**Contrast:** Formal and written; in conversation say *horrified* or *shocked*. The preposition is *at*: *aghast at the news*, not *aghast of*. Never before a noun: *a shocked crowd*, not *an aghast crowd*.

Shocked and horrified.

1. Her parents were *aghast* at the size of the bill.
2. We watched, *aghast*, as the bridge collapsed.
3. Fans were *aghast* when the team traded its best player.

### akin

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkɪn/ &middot; *uh-KIN*
**Русский:** сродни, похожий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also right after a noun (*something akin to panic*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more akin*, *most akin*
**Takes:** *to* + noun or *-ing*
**Form:** a- 'of' + *kin* 'family'
**Contrast:** Formal; in speech say *like* or *similar to*. The preposition is always *to*: *akin to*, not *akin with*. Never before a noun. Russian *сродни* is a close match in both meaning and register.

Similar to something; of the same kind.

1. What she felt was *akin* to panic.
2. Learning a language is *akin* to learning a musical instrument.
3. The meeting was more *akin* to a pep rally than a planning session.

### averse

**Pronunciation:** /əˈvɜːrs/ &middot; *uh-VURS*
**Русский:** не склонный, питающий неприязнь (к)
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — before a noun only in compounds: *risk-averse investors*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more averse*, *most averse*
**Takes:** *to* + noun or *-ing*
**Contrast:** Do not confuse with *adverse* (harmful, both positions): *adverse effects*, *adverse weather*. *Averse* describes a person who dislikes something: *averse to risk*. It takes *to* + *-ing*, not an infinitive: *not averse to working late*. It most often appears as *not averse to*, a polite way of saying *не прочь*: *I'm not averse to a second helping*. Before a noun use the compound: *a risk-averse company*.

Strongly disliking or opposed to something.

1. My dad is *averse* to paying for anything he can fix himself.
2. Older investors tend to be more *averse* to risk.
3. I'm not *averse* to a little hard work.

### content

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɛnt/ &middot; *kuhn-TENT*
**Русский:** довольный
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — before a noun use *contented*: *a contented smile*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more content*, *most content*
**Takes:** *with* + noun (satisfied with); *to*-infinitive (willing to)
**Preposition patterns:** [content with](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#content-with)
**Opposite:** *discontented*; *dissatisfied*
**Contrast:** The adjective is stressed on the second syllable, /kənˈtɛnt/, like the verb (*content yourself with*). The noun *content* (*содержание*, *контент*) is /ˈkɑːntɛnt/, stressed on the first. Before a noun use *contented*: *a contented cat*, not *a content cat*. *Content* is quieter than *happy*: it means you want nothing more, Russian *довольный*. *Content to* + verb means willing to accept something, not delighted by it.

Content has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Happy and satisfied with what you have, and not wanting more.** (довольный)

1. He's perfectly *content* with his small apartment.
2. After years of moving around, she finally seems *content*.
3. I've never been more *content* than I am now.

**2. Willing to accept or do something, even if it is not ideal.** (готовый, согласный (довольствоваться))

4. I'm *content* to let the lawyers handle it.
5. She was *content* to stay in the background.

### glad

**Pronunciation:** /ɡlæd/ &middot; *GLAD*
**Русский:** рад, довольный
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** predicative only — except in a few literary set phrases (*glad tidings*); before a noun use *happy* or *pleased*: *a happy face*
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *gladder*, *gladdest*; also *more glad*
**Takes:** *about* + thing; *for* + person (glad for someone); *that*-clause; *to*-infinitive; *of* + noun (grateful for)
**Preposition patterns:** [glad about](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#glad-about)
**Opposite:** *sorry*
**Contrast:** *Glad* reacts to a particular event (*I'm glad you came*); *happy* also describes a lasting state and goes before a noun (*a happy kid*, not *a glad kid*). *I'd be glad to* is a polite yes, like Russian *с радостью*. *Glad for someone* is *рад за кого-то*; *glad of something* means grateful to have it. *Gladder* and *gladdest* exist, but *more glad* is common too.

Glad has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Pleased and happy about something.** (рад, довольный)

1. I'm so *glad* you could make it.
2. I'm really *glad* for you two.
3. I've never been *gladder* to see anyone in my life.

**2. Very willing to do something.** (охотно готовый, с радостью)

4. I'd be *glad* to help you move.
5. If you need a ride, I'm *glad* to drive.

**3. Grateful for something.** (благодарный (за), рад (чему-то))

6. I was *glad* of the extra blanket that night.
7. She was *glad* of the company on the long drive.

**4. Before a noun, in old or literary set phrases: bringing or showing joy.** (радостный)

8. The Christmas card promised *glad* tidings to all.
9. The old hymn tells of a *glad* morning.

### unable

**Pronunciation:** /ʌnˈeɪbəl/ &middot; *uhn-AY-buhl*
**Русский:** неспособный; не в состоянии
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — always followed by a *to*-infinitive; before a noun use a relative clause: *people who are unable to work*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Takes:** *to*-infinitive (required)
**Form:** un- + *able*
**Opposite:** *able*
**Contrast:** *Unable* always needs *to* + verb: *unable to sleep*, not *unable of sleeping* — that pattern belongs to *incapable of* + *-ing*. It is more formal than *can't* or *couldn't* and is the usual word in notices and emails (*We are unable to process your request*). The noun is *inability*, not *unability*. For a lasting physical or mental condition American English says *disabled*, not *unable*.

Not able to do something, because you lack the power, skill, time or opportunity.

1. I was *unable* to sleep after the phone call.
2. The airline was *unable* to find my bag.
3. We regret that we are *unable* to offer refunds.

### unaware

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʌnəˈwɛr/ &middot; *uhn-uh-WAIR*
**Русский:** не знающий, не подозревающий
**Type:** quality
**Position:** predicative only — also set off by commas (*She kept dancing, unaware*); before a noun use *unsuspecting*: *unsuspecting tourists*
**Comparison:** not gradable — strengthen with *completely*, *totally* or *blissfully*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing*; *that*-clause
**Form:** un- + *aware*
**Opposite:** *aware*
**Contrast:** The preposition is *of*, not *about*: *unaware of the change*. Do not confuse it with the adverb *unawares*, used in *catch* or *take someone unawares* (*застать врасплох*). *Blissfully unaware* is a common phrase for someone happily ignorant of a problem.

Not knowing about something, or not noticing it.

1. Most customers are *unaware* of the fee.
2. He was *unaware* that anyone was listening.
3. She kept dancing, blissfully *unaware* of the stain on her dress.

### fond

**Pronunciation:** /fɑːnd/ &middot; *FAHND*
**Русский:** любящий; нежный
**Type:** emotion
**Position:** both — but in the 'liking' sense only after a verb (*She's fond of cats*); before a noun it means affectionate (*a fond smile*) or, of a hope, unlikely (*a fond hope*)
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *fonder*, *fondest*; also *more fond*
**Takes:** *of* + noun or *-ing*
**Preposition patterns:** [fond of](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#fond-of)
**Contrast:** *Be fond of* is softer than *love* and warmer than *like*, and it takes *of* + noun or *-ing*, never an infinitive: *fond of cooking*, not *fond to cook*. The meaning changes with position: *She's fond of her grandfather* means she likes him, but *a fond look* is a loving one, and *a fond hope* is a cherished and often naive one. *Grow fond of* is the usual way to say you came to like someone. The noun is *fondness*.

Fond has 4 distinct senses.

**1. fond of: liking someone or something, often after knowing them for a long time.** (любящий, питающий симпатию (к))

1. I've grown very *fond* of my neighbors.
2. She's especially *fond* of old movies.
3. Absence makes the heart grow *fonder*.

**2. fond of doing: in the habit of doing something, often something other people find annoying.** (любящий (делать что-то), имеющий привычку)

4. My uncle is *fond* of telling everyone how he once met the president.
5. Politicians are *fond* of quoting that line.

**3. Before a noun: loving and affectionate, or pleasant to remember.** (нежный, ласковый; тёплый (о воспоминаниях))

6. She gave her son a *fond* smile.
7. I have *fond* memories of those summers at the lake.
8. Some of my *fondest* memories are from that camp.

**4. Of a hope, wish or belief: deeply felt and long held, often naively, when it is unlikely to come true.** (заветный; наивный, несбыточный)

9. Her *fondest* wish was to see all her grandkids graduate.
10. He had the *fond* hope that the problem would just go away.
11. Their *fond* belief that prices would keep rising proved wrong.

### sorry

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɑːri/ &middot; *SAH-ree*
**Русский:** (мне) жаль; извините; жалкий
**Type:** emotion; also evaluative
**Position:** both — after a verb for the feelings (*I'm sorry*); before a noun it means pitiful or poor (*a sorry state*)
**Comparison:** -er/-est or more/most — *sorrier*, *sorriest*; also *more sorry*
**Takes:** *about* + thing; *for* + what you did, or + person you pity; *to*-infinitive; *that*-clause
**Preposition patterns:** [sorry for](../../06-prepositions/catalog/15-dependent-adjectives.md#sorry-for)
**Opposite:** *glad*
**Contrast:** *Sorry about* goes with a situation (*sorry about the mess*, *sorry about your dad*); *sorry for* goes with your own action (*sorry for yelling*) or, after *feel*, with a person you pity (*I feel sorry for him*, *мне его жалко*). *I'm sorry to hear* is sympathy; *I'm sorry to say* introduces bad news. Before a noun the meaning changes: *a sorry excuse* is a poor one, not an apologetic one. To get a stranger's attention use *Excuse me*, not *Sorry* — Russian *извините* covers both. A standalone *Sorry!* or *Sorry?* is an interjection.

Sorry has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Feeling regret or guilt about something you did; apologizing.** (виноватый; простите, (мне) жаль)

1. I'm really *sorry* I'm late.
2. She said she was *sorry* for what she'd said.
3. Nobody was *sorrier* than Mike when the vase broke.

**2. Feeling sad or sympathetic about something bad that happened to someone else.** ((мне) жаль, сочувствую)

4. I'm so *sorry* about your dad.
5. We were *sorry* to hear that you'd been sick.

**3. Disappointed about a situation or regretting a choice, when no apology is involved — often *you'll be sorry*.** ((мне) жаль; пожалеть)

6. I'm *sorry* I didn't take that job when I had the chance.
7. Don't buy that car — you'll be *sorry*.

**4. Used politely to refuse, disagree, give bad news or ask someone to repeat something.** (простите; к сожалению)

8. I'm *sorry*, but you can't park here.
9. I'm *sorry*, I didn't catch your name.
10. I'm *sorry* to say the store is closing.

**5. feel sorry for: feeling pity for someone.** (жалеть (кого-то))

11. I feel really *sorry* for the new kid.
12. Stop feeling *sorry* for yourself.

**6. Before a noun: poor, pitiful or in bad condition.** (жалкий, плачевный)

13. The old house was in a *sorry* state.
14. That's the *sorriest* excuse I've ever heard.
15. After the frost, the garden was a *sorry* sight.

### worth

**Pronunciation:** /wɜːrθ/ &middot; *WURTH*
**Русский:** стоящий; стоит
**Type:** evaluative
**Position:** predicative only — also right after a noun with its complement (*a book worth reading*, *a ring worth $5,000*); before a noun use *worthwhile* or *valuable*: *a worthwhile trip*
**Comparison:** not gradable — strengthen with *well*, *really* or *definitely*: *well worth it*
**Takes:** a noun, amount or pronoun (*worth $50*, *worth it*); *-ing* (*worth seeing*) — never a *to*-infinitive
**Contrast:** *Worth* must have something after it: *The book is worth reading*, not *worth to read* and not *worth being read* — the active *-ing* carries the passive meaning. Russian *стоит* + infinitive becomes *it's worth* + *-ing*: *It's worth trying* (*стоит попробовать*). *Worth it* stands in for the rest: *Was the trip worth it?* Before a noun use *worthwhile* (*a worthwhile project*); *worthy of* means deserving (*worthy of respect*). Because it takes an object, Merriam-Webster and some grammars call *worth* a preposition. The noun *worth* is a separate word: *a dollar's worth*, *self-worth*.

Worth has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Having a certain value in money.** (стоящий (столько-то), стоит)

1. The ring is *worth* about $5,000.
2. How much is your car *worth*?
3. Our house is *worth* twice what we paid for it.

**2. Good, useful or enjoyable enough to justify the time, money or effort.** (стоящий, заслуживающий (того, чтобы))

4. The museum is well *worth* a visit.
5. Is it *worth* fixing, or should we just buy a new one?
6. It was a long drive, but it was totally *worth* it.

**3. Of a person: owning money and property of a certain value.** (обладающий состоянием (в столько-то))

7. By forty she was *worth* over a billion dollars.
8. Nobody knows exactly how much he's *worth*.
