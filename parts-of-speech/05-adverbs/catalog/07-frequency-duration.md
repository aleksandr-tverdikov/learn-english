# Adverbs of frequency and duration

> How often and how long? — *always*, *usually*, *sometimes*, *never*, *daily*, *twice*, *forever*, *briefly*.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Indefinite frequency adverbs sit on a scale from *always* to *never* and take mid-position: after *be*, before the main verb, after the first auxiliary. Definite frequency adverbs (*daily*, *weekly*, *twice*) go at the end instead. Duration adverbs answer *how long?*. Getting the position right matters more here than anywhere else in the dictionary, because *I go always* is the error every teacher hears.

---
### always

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːlweɪz/ &middot; *AWL-wayz*
**Русский:** всегда
**Type:** frequency — the top of the scale; also duration (*I'll always remember*)
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; front only in commands (*Always lock the door*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** old compound of *all* + *way* with adverbial *-s*
**Contrast:** Position: *I always walk to work*, not *I walk always to work*; after *be* it follows the verb: *She is always late*. *Not always* is 'не всегда' (*He's not always right*); *always not* is wrong — say *never*. With a continuous tense it turns into a complaint: *He's always complaining* (вечно жалуется), not a neutral habit. 'Всегда' reaching up to now takes the present perfect: *I've always lived here*, not *I always live here since childhood*.

Always has 4 distinct senses.

**1. At all times; on every occasion.** (всегда)

1. She *always* takes the bus to work.
2. The store is *always* busy on Saturdays.
3. Do you *always* eat lunch at your desk?

**2. For all time, as far back as one remembers or into the future.** (всегда, вечно)

4. I'll *always* remember that trip.
5. I've *always* wanted to see the Grand Canyon.
6. No matter what happens, we'll *always* be friends.

**3. Again and again, in an annoying way — with a continuous tense.** (вечно, постоянно)

7. He's *always* borrowing money and never paying it back.
8. Why are you *always* interrupting me?

**4. As a possible choice if nothing else works — usually with *can* or *could*.** (всегда можно, в крайнем случае)

9. If you miss the bus, you can *always* call a cab.
10. We can *always* try again tomorrow.
11. Too expensive? Well, you could *always* rent one.

### usually

**Pronunciation:** /ˈjuːʒuəli/ &middot; *YOO-zhoo-uh-lee*
**Русский:** обычно
**Type:** frequency — high on the scale, just below *always*
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; also front or end for emphasis
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *usual*
**Contrast:** A habit that has stopped is *used to*, not *usually* + past: *I used to walk to school* (раньше ходил), while *I usually walked* only describes the typical case back then. 'Как обычно' is *as usual*, never *as usually*. Do not mix it up with *be used to* (привыкнуть): *I'm used to the noise*.

In most cases; as a rule.

1. I *usually* get up at seven.
2. Traffic is *usually* light on Sundays.
3. *Usually*, the meeting ends by noon.

### normally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɔːrməli/ &middot; *NOR-muh-lee*
**Русский:** обычно; нормально
**Type:** frequency; also manner
**Position:** mid — also front in the sense 'usually'; end in the sense 'in a normal way'
**Comparison:** not gradable — in the sense 'usually'; *more normally* is possible in the sense 'in a normal way'
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *normal*
**Contrast:** Russian *нормально* meaning 'fine, OK' is not *normally*: *How are you? — Fine* (or *OK*), not *Normally*; *Everything's fine*, not *Everything is normally*. In American English *normally* means 'обычно' far more often than 'нормально'.

Normally has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Usually; under ordinary conditions.** (обычно)

1. I *normally* walk to work, but it's raining today.
2. The drive *normally* takes about an hour.
3. *Normally*, we'd be closed by now.

**2. In a normal way; as expected, with nothing wrong.** (нормально, как положено)

4. The patient is breathing *normally* again.
5. Just act *normally* and nobody will notice.
6. Is the engine running *normally* now?

### generally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɛnərəli/ &middot; *JEN-ur-uh-lee*
**Русский:** обычно; в целом
**Type:** frequency; also viewpoint (*generally speaking*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be*; also front, especially in *generally speaking*
**Comparison:** not gradable — *more generally* means 'in broader terms'
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, participles and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *general*
**Contrast:** *Generally* is 'обычно' or 'в целом', not the emphatic 'вообще' of negatives and questions: *Я вообще не пью* is *I don't drink at all*; *А ты вообще кто?* is *Who are you, anyway?* The set phrase *generally speaking* is 'вообще говоря'.

Generally has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Usually; in most cases.** (обычно, как правило)

1. I *generally* skip breakfast.
2. Flights are *generally* cheaper on Tuesdays.
3. The weather here is *generally* mild in the fall.

**2. By most people; widely.** (повсеместно, широко, всеми)

4. It is *generally* accepted that smoking causes cancer.
5. The new mayor is *generally* well liked.
6. The drug is not yet *generally* available.

**3. In broad terms, without going into details.** (в целом, в общем)

7. *Generally* speaking, the reviews were positive.
8. She talked about the economy *generally*, not about any one company.

### frequently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfriːkwəntli/ &middot; *FREE-kwuhnt-lee*
**Русский:** часто
**Type:** frequency — high on the scale
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most — *more frequently*, *most frequently*
**Modifies:** verbs and participles (*frequently asked*)
**Form:** -ly from *frequent*
**Contrast:** *Frequently* is more formal than *often*; the everyday question is *How often…?*, not *How frequently…?* It sits comfortably at the end: *It happens frequently*.

Often; many times.

1. He travels *frequently* for work.
2. Check the list of *frequently* asked questions first.
3. Buses run *more frequently* during rush hour.

### often

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːfən/ &middot; *AW-fuhn*
**Русский:** часто
**Type:** frequency — high on the scale
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; also end, especially with *very*, *quite* or *too*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more often*, *most often*; *oftener* is rare
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Contrast:** Position: *I often go there*, not *I go often there*; at the end it is most natural with a partner: *We don't eat out very often*. The t is normally silent (AW-fuhn), though AWF-tuhn is also heard. *Every so often* means 'время от времени' (not often), and *more often than not* means 'обычно'.

Many times; in many cases.

1. We *often* eat dinner on the porch in the summer.
2. How *often* do you go to the gym?
3. I wish we could visit *more often*.

### sometimes

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌmtaɪmz/ &middot; *SUHM-tymz*
**Русский:** иногда
**Type:** frequency — the middle of the scale
**Position:** any position — front, mid and end are all natural
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** *sometime* + adverbial *-s*
**Contrast:** Three different words: *sometimes* (иногда — *I sometimes work late*), *sometime* (когда-нибудь, в какой-то момент — *Let's have lunch sometime*) and *some time* (некоторое время — *It took some time*).

On some occasions, but not always.

1. *Sometimes* I just need a quiet day at home.
2. My sister *sometimes* works from home.
3. The subway gets really crowded *sometimes*.

### occasionally

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkeɪʒənəli/ &middot; *uh-KAY-zhuh-nuh-lee*
**Русский:** иногда, время от времени
**Type:** frequency — below *sometimes* on the scale
**Position:** mid or end — also front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *occasional*
**Contrast:** It means 'now and then', not 'by chance': 'случайно' is *by chance* or *accidentally*. Spelled with double c and single s. It suggests fewer times than *sometimes* — roughly *once in a while*.

Now and then; not often.

1. We *occasionally* go out for Mexican food.
2. I run into him at the gym *occasionally*.
3. The trail is *occasionally* closed after heavy rain.

---

### seldom

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɛldəm/ &middot; *SEL-duhm*
**Русский:** редко
**Type:** frequency; also negative
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (*Seldom do we…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Contrast:** It is already negative, so no *not*: *I seldom go*, not *I don't seldom go*; the question tag is positive: *He seldom calls, does he?* Fronted for emphasis, it inverts: *Seldom have I seen such a mess*, not *Seldom I have seen*. In everyday American speech *rarely* or *not often* is more usual; *seldom* sounds formal or bookish.

Not often; almost never.

1. We *seldom* see snow this far south.
2. He *seldom* talks about his time in the Army.
3. *Seldom* have I heard such a moving speech.

### rarely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrɛrli/ &middot; *RAIR-lee*
**Русский:** редко
**Type:** frequency; also negative
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (*Rarely does it…*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more rarely*, *most rarely*
**Modifies:** verbs, participles and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *rare*
**Contrast:** Already negative, so no *not*: *I rarely eat meat*; the question tag is positive: *She rarely complains, does she?* Fronted, it inverts: *Rarely does it snow here*, not *Rarely it snows here*. *Rarely if ever* and *hardly ever* are standard; *rarely ever* is informal.

Not often; hardly ever.

1. I *rarely* watch TV anymore.
2. She is *rarely* late for work.
3. *Rarely* does a rookie play this well.

### never

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɛvər/ &middot; *NEV-ur*
**Русский:** никогда
**Type:** frequency — the bottom of the scale; also negative
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (*Never have I…*) or in commands (*Never give up*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** old *ne* 'not' + *ever* — negative in itself
**Contrast:** English allows one negative: *I never eat meat*, not *I don't never eat meat* (Russian *никогда не* doubles it). Fronted, it inverts: *Never have I felt so tired*. For experience use the present perfect: *I've never been to Chicago*. In questions use *ever*: *Have you ever…?*, not *Have you never…?* unless you are surprised. Informal *I never took it* is about one occasion and means 'I didn't take it'.

Never has 2 distinct senses.

**1. At no time; not once.** (никогда)

1. I've *never* been to Alaska.
2. She *never* drinks coffee after lunch.
3. *Never* have I felt so welcome.

**2. Not, said with emphasis — often about a single occasion.** (так и не, вовсе не)

4. He *never* even said goodbye.
5. I *never* touched your phone, I swear.
6. The package *never* arrived.

### ever

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛvər/ &middot; *EV-ur*
**Русский:** когда-нибудь, когда-либо
**Type:** frequency; also time and degree
**Position:** mid — after the subject in questions (*Have you ever…?*) and after the negative word (*nobody ever*); after its word after a superlative (*the best ever*); before its word in *ever higher* and *ever so*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, superlatives, comparatives, *so* and whole clauses
**Contrast:** Not in a plain affirmative statement: 'я когда-то был в Париже' is *I've been to Paris* or *I was once in Paris*, never *I have ever been to Paris*. Use it in questions (*Have you ever…?*), negatives (*nobody ever*, *hardly ever*), conditions (*if you ever…*) and after superlatives (*the best ever*). 'Как никогда' after a comparative is *than ever*: *I need you more than ever*, not *like never*. *Ever so* (*ever so slightly*) is informal and more British, but common in American writing. *Ever since* is 'с тех пор'; *forever* is written as one word in American English.

Ever has 6 distinct senses.

**1. At any time — in questions, negatives and conditions.** (когда-нибудь, когда-либо)

1. Have you *ever* tried sushi?
2. Nobody *ever* calls the landline.
3. If you're *ever* in Denver, look us up.

**2. Of all time — after a superlative, after *first* or *only*, and in *than ever*.** (когда-либо, за всю историю; как никогда)

4. This is the best pizza I've *ever* had.
5. She was the first woman *ever* to run the company.
6. Gas prices are higher than *ever* this summer.

**3. Always; all the time — in fixed phrases such as *ever since*, *ever after* and *as ever*.** (всегда, неизменно; с тех пор)

7. He's been sad *ever* since his dog died.
8. And they lived happily *ever* after.
9. She was, as *ever*, the last one to leave.

**4. More and more — before a comparative.** (всё (больше, выше))

10. Prices keep climbing to *ever* higher levels.
11. The suburbs are spreading *ever* farther from the city.

**5. Used for emphasis — in informal American exclamations with inversion, and after *how* or *why* in questions.** (ну и, ещё как; же)

12. Boy, was she *ever* mad!
13. Did we *ever* have fun at that concert!
14. How can I *ever* thank you?

**6. Very; in the smallest or gentlest way — in *ever so* before an adverb or adjective.** (очень, совсем (чуть-чуть))

15. She opened the door *ever* so slightly.
16. He lifted the sleeping baby *ever* so gently.

### daily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdeɪli/ &middot; *DAY-lee*
**Русский:** ежедневно
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end — not mid (*The paper comes out daily*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *day*; the same form as the adjective *daily*
**Contrast:** The same spelling is an adjective (*a daily newspaper*) and a noun (*a daily* — ежедневная газета); only 'every day' after a verb is the adverb. Keep it at the end: *We exercise daily*, not *We daily exercise*. Also do not confuse the phrase *every day* (каждый день) with the adjective *everyday* (повседневный).

Every day; once a day.

1. The museum is open *daily* from 9 to 5.
2. Take this pill twice *daily* with food.
3. The list of volunteers is growing *daily*.

### weekly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwiːkli/ &middot; *WEEK-lee*
**Русский:** еженедельно
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *week*; the same form as the adjective *weekly*
**Contrast:** Also an adjective (*a weekly meeting*) and a noun (*a weekly* — еженедельник). *Biweekly* is ambiguous — it can mean twice a week or every two weeks — so Americans often say *every other week* to be clear.

Once a week; every week.

1. The team meets *weekly* to review progress.
2. The newsletter goes out *weekly*.
3. At this job we get paid *weekly*.

### monthly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmʌnθli/ &middot; *MUHNTH-lee*
**Русский:** ежемесячно
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *month*; the same form as the adjective *monthly*
**Contrast:** Also an adjective (*a monthly payment*) and a noun (*a monthly* — ежемесячный журнал). *Bimonthly* can mean twice a month or every two months; *every other month* avoids the doubt.

Once a month; every month.

1. Rent is due *monthly*, on the first.
2. The book club meets *monthly* at the library.
3. Interest on the account is paid *monthly*.

### quarterly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkwɔːrtərli/ &middot; *KWOR-tur-lee*
**Русский:** ежеквартально
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *quarter*; the same form as the adjective *quarterly*
**Contrast:** It means every three months — a quarter of a year — not every quarter of an hour. Also an adjective (*quarterly earnings*) and a noun (*a quarterly* — ежеквартальный журнал).

Once every three months.

1. The company reports its earnings *quarterly*.
2. Freelancers pay estimated taxes *quarterly*.
3. The board meets *quarterly*.

### annually

**Pronunciation:** /ˈænjuəli/ &middot; *AN-yoo-uh-lee*
**Русский:** ежегодно
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *annual*
**Contrast:** Formal and financial; everyday speech says *every year* or *once a year*. *Biannually* means twice a year, *biennially* every two years. Spelled with double n and double l.

Once a year; every year.

1. The festival is held *annually* in June.
2. About two million people visit the park *annually*.
3. Salaries are reviewed *annually*.

---

### yearly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈjɪrli/ &middot; *YEER-lee*
**Русский:** ежегодно
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *year*; the same form as the adjective *yearly*
**Contrast:** The everyday counterpart of *annually*; also an adjective (*a yearly checkup*). The vowel is the one in *year* (YEER-lee), not the one in *early* (UR-lee).

Once a year; every year.

1. We see the dentist twice *yearly*.
2. The membership fee is paid *yearly*.
3. The town holds the parade *yearly* on the Fourth of July.

### hourly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈaʊərli/ &middot; *OW-ur-lee*
**Русский:** ежечасно; почасово
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *hour*; the same form as the adjective *hourly*
**Contrast:** The h is silent, so the adjective takes *an*: *an hourly bus*, *an hourly wage*. American *hourly* workers are paid by the hour, as opposed to *salaried* ones.

Hourly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Once an hour; every hour.** (ежечасно, каждый час)

1. Trains leave for the city *hourly*.
2. The nurse checks his temperature *hourly*.

**2. By the hour; for each hour worked.** (почасово)

3. Most of the store staff are paid *hourly*.
4. Lawyers usually bill *hourly*.

### nightly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnaɪtli/ &middot; *NYT-lee*
**Русский:** каждый вечер, каждую ночь
**Type:** frequency — definite
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *night*; the same form as the adjective *nightly*
**Contrast:** It usually means every evening — shows and TV news run *nightly* — and never simply 'ночью': for that say *at night* (*Owls hunt at night*). Also an adjective (*the nightly news*).

Every night or every evening.

1. The show runs *nightly* at eight.
2. The kitchen is open *nightly* until eleven.
3. She checks the locks *nightly* before bed.

### twice

**Pronunciation:** /twaɪs/ &middot; *TWYS*
**Русский:** дважды, два раза
**Type:** frequency — definite; also degree (*twice as big*)
**Position:** end — before its word in *twice as … as*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; *as* + adjective or adverb
**Form:** *two* + old adverbial *-ce*, like *once*; the series goes *once*, *twice*, then *three times*
**Contrast:** 'В два раза больше' is *twice as big (as)* or *twice the size (of)*; *two times bigger* is heard but criticized. Frequency takes no preposition: *twice a week*, not *twice in a week* (unless you mean 'within one week'). *Thrice* is archaic — say *three times*.

Twice has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Two times; on two occasions.** (дважды, два раза)

1. I've been to Chicago *twice*.
2. Brush your teeth *twice* a day.
3. The phone rang *twice* and then stopped.

**2. Two times as much — in comparisons.** (вдвое, в два раза)

4. Their house is *twice* as big as ours.
5. The repairs took *twice* as long as we planned.

### again

**Pronunciation:** /əˈɡɛn/ &middot; *uh-GEN*
**Русский:** снова, опять, ещё раз
**Type:** frequency; also linking
**Position:** end — mid in formal writing; front in *Again, …* and *Then again, …*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Contrast:** English *again* is neutral; Russian *опять* can sound annoyed, and English shows that with *yet again* or *Not again!* *What's his name again?* is not 'ещё раз' in the sense of doing something over — it means 'напомни, как его зовут'. 'Повторно' is *again* or *a second time*, not *repeatedly* (много раз). Most Americans say uh-GEN; uh-GAYN is also heard.

Again has 5 distinct senses.

**1. One more time; once more.** (снова, ещё раз, опять)

1. Could you say that *again*?
2. I failed the test, so I'll take it *again* in May.
3. Oh no, it's raining *again*.

**2. Back to an earlier place or condition.** (снова, обратно)

4. It's good to be home *again*.
5. She's feeling well *again* after the surgery.

**3. Used to ask for information that you were told but have forgotten.** (напомни(те), ещё раз)

6. What was your name *again*?
7. Where are we meeting *again*?

**4. Used to repeat or stress a point already made.** (опять же, ещё раз)

8. *Again*, I want to thank everyone for coming.
9. *Again*, the problem is money, not time.

**5. On the other hand — in *then again* and *but then again*.** (с другой стороны)

10. It's expensive. *Then again*, it'll last for years.
11. I might go. *But then again*, I might not.

### constantly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːnstəntli/ &middot; *KAHN-stuhnt-lee*
**Русский:** постоянно, всё время
**Type:** frequency; also duration
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, participles and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *constant*
**Contrast:** *Constantly* (постоянно, всё время) is not *consistently* (неизменно, стабильно): *He's constantly late* means he is late all the time; *Her work is consistently good* means it is reliably good. With a continuous tense it signals irritation: *You're constantly checking your phone*.

All the time, or so often that it seems like all the time.

1. The baby cried *constantly* the first week.
2. Technology is *constantly* changing.
3. He's *constantly* checking his phone during dinner.

### regularly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrɛɡjələrli/ &middot; *REG-yuh-lur-lee*
**Русский:** регулярно
**Type:** frequency; also manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most — *more regularly*, *most regularly*
**Modifies:** verbs, participles and prices
**Form:** -ly from *regular*
**Contrast:** *Regularly* stresses a steady pattern, so it is not always *often*: *I see him regularly* suggests a routine (every Tuesday); *I see him often* just means many times. On American price tags *regularly* means 'at the usual price': *regularly $50, now $35*.

Regularly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Often and as a habit, or at fixed intervals.** (регулярно)

1. She exercises *regularly*.
2. Change your passwords *regularly*.
3. I wish the buses ran *more regularly* out here.

**2. Evenly; in an even pattern or rhythm.** (равномерно, ровно)

4. The fence posts are *regularly* spaced, ten feet apart.
5. His heart is beating *regularly* now.

**3. At the usual price — in American stores and ads.** (по обычной цене, обычно)

6. These jeans are *regularly* $60, now on sale for $40.
7. All *regularly* priced items are 20% off today.

### repeatedly

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈpiːtɪdli/ &middot; *rih-PEE-tid-lee*
**Русский:** неоднократно, много раз
**Type:** frequency
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *repeated*
**Contrast:** *Repeatedly* means many times, not one more time: *I asked him again* (ещё раз) vs *I asked him repeatedly* (много раз). Russian *повторно* (a second time) is *again* or *a second time*.

Many times; again and again.

1. I *repeatedly* asked him to turn the music down.
2. The company had been warned *repeatedly*.
3. She pressed the button *repeatedly*, but nothing happened.

### continually

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɪnjuəli/ &middot; *kuhn-TIN-yoo-uh-lee*
**Русский:** постоянно, то и дело
**Type:** frequency; also duration
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *continual*
**Contrast:** Careful writers split the pair: *continually* is 'again and again, with breaks' (*The phone rang continually*), *continuously* is 'with no break at all' (*The engine ran continuously for ten hours*). Everyday American usage also lets *continually* mean 'without stopping', so choose *continuously* when the absence of breaks matters.

Continually has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Again and again over a period of time, often annoyingly.** (постоянно, то и дело)

1. He *continually* interrupts when other people are talking.
2. The office printer *continually* jams.

**2. Without stopping; all the time.** (непрерывно, всё время)

3. The city's population has grown *continually* since 1950.
4. Our software is *continually* being improved.

### forever

**Pronunciation:** /fərˈɛvər/ &middot; *fur-EV-ur*
**Русский:** навсегда, вечно
**Type:** duration; also frequency (*forever complaining*)
**Position:** end — mid in *be forever doing*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** compound of *for* + *ever*, written as one word in American English
**Contrast:** American English writes *forever* as one word in every sense; *for ever* is British. With a continuous tense it means 'constantly' and sounds annoyed: *He's forever losing his keys* (вечно теряет). *Take forever* is informal exaggeration, not literal. *For good* is a common informal synonym of 'навсегда'.

Forever has 3 distinct senses.

**1. For all future time; permanently.** (навсегда)

1. I wish this summer could last *forever*.
2. Once you post it online, it's there *forever*.
3. The accident changed their lives *forever*.

**2. For a very long time — an informal exaggeration.** (целую вечность, очень долго)

4. The line at the DMV took *forever*.
5. I've known her *forever*.

**3. Constantly; all the time — with a continuous tense.** (вечно, постоянно)

6. He's *forever* losing his glasses.
7. My neighbors are *forever* complaining about something.

### momentarily

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmoʊmənˈtɛrəli/ &middot; *moh-muhn-TAIR-uh-lee*
**Русский:** на мгновение; сейчас, через минуту
**Type:** duration; also time
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, participles and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *momentary*

**1. For a moment; very briefly.** (на мгновение, на миг)

1. I was *momentarily* blinded by the headlights.
2. She paused *momentarily* before answering.

**2. In a moment; very soon — mainly American.** (сейчас, через минуту, вот-вот)

3. The doctor will be with you *momentarily*.
4. We will be landing *momentarily*.
