# More adverbs: A–C

> Adverbs A–C beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### a bit

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˈbɪt/ &middot; *uh BIT*
**Русский:** немного, чуть-чуть
**Type:** degree — a downtoner, informal
**Position:** before its word — or end, after a verb (*rest a bit*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, comparatives, adverbs and verbs
**Contrast:** Before a plain adjective it prefers unwelcome qualities: *a bit late*, *a bit tired*, not *a bit wonderful* — use *a little* or *pretty* there. Before a noun it needs *of*: *a bit of luck*.

To a small degree; slightly, or for a short time.

1. I'm *a bit* worried about the weather.
2. Could you turn the music down *a bit*?
3. Let's wait *a bit* before we call him.

### a cappella

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː kəˈpɛlə/ &middot; *ah kuh-PEL-uh*
**Русский:** а капелла, без музыкального сопровождения
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of singing and performing
**Form:** Italian, 'in the chapel style'
**Contrast:** Also an adjective before a noun (*an a cappella group*). Spelled with two *p*'s and two *l*'s.

With voices only, without instruments.

1. The choir sang the last hymn *a cappella*.
2. When the sound system failed, she finished the song *a cappella*.
3. The group records all its music *a cappella*.

### a good deal

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˌɡʊd ˈdiːl/ &middot; *uh guud DEEL*
**Русский:** намного; много
**Type:** degree
**Position:** before its word — before a comparative (*a good deal better*); end after a verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparatives, *too*, and verbs
**Contrast:** Not the noun *a good deal* (выгодная сделка): *I got a good deal on the car*. Before a noun it needs *of*: *a good deal of time*.

A good deal has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Much; considerably, with a comparative or *too*.** (намного, гораздо)

1. She looks *a good deal* better than she did last week.
2. The new route is *a good deal* shorter.

**2. A lot; much or often, with a verb.** (много, часто)

3. He travels *a good deal* for work.
4. We've talked about it *a good deal* at home.

### a great deal

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˌɡreɪt ˈdiːl/ &middot; *uh grayt DEEL*
**Русский:** намного; очень много
**Type:** degree
**Position:** before its word — before a comparative (*a great deal easier*); end after a verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparatives, *too*, and verbs
**Contrast:** More formal than *a lot*, and the natural choice in affirmative sentences where *much* sounds stiff: *He worries a great deal*, not *He worries much*.

A great deal has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Much; far, with a comparative or *too*.** (намного, гораздо)

1. Things are *a great deal* easier now.
2. The repair cost *a great deal* more than we expected.

**2. Very much or very often, with a verb.** (очень много, часто)

3. She has traveled *a great deal* since she retired.
4. I've thought about your offer *a great deal*.

### a la carte

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɑː lə ˈkɑːrt/ &middot; *ah luh KAHRT*
**Русский:** по меню, отдельно (не комплексом)
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of ordering, buying and pricing
**Form:** French *à la carte*, 'by the menu'; usually written without the accent in American English
**Contrast:** Also an adjective (*an a la carte menu*). It has spread from restaurants to anything sold item by item.

Ordered or priced item by item, not as part of a set meal or package.

1. We skipped the buffet and ordered *a la carte*.
2. Side dishes are sold *a la carte*.
3. The app lets you buy extra features *a la carte*.

### a little

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˈlɪtəl/ &middot; *uh LIT-uhl*
**Русский:** немного, слегка
**Type:** degree — a downtoner
**Position:** before its word — or end, after a verb (*relax a little*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, comparatives, adverbs and verbs
**Contrast:** *A little* is positive (немного); *little* without *a* is negative (мало): *He sleeps a little in the afternoon* vs *He sleeps little*. Unlike *a bit*, it sits comfortably before pleasant adjectives: *a little happier*.

To a small degree; slightly.

1. I'm *a little* tired after the flight.
2. Could you speak *a little* louder?
3. Let's rest *a little* before we go on.

### a lot

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˈlɑːt/ &middot; *uh LAHT*
**Русский:** намного; много, часто
**Type:** degree; also frequency
**Position:** end — after the verb and object; before its word with a comparative (*a lot better*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and comparatives
**Contrast:** Always two words: *alot* is a misspelling. It cannot go before a plain adjective: *a lot better*, but not *a lot good* — say *very good*. Informal; in formal writing prefer *much* or *a great deal*.

A lot has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Much; far, with a comparative.** (намного, гораздо)

1. I feel *a lot* better today.
2. This phone is *a lot* cheaper online.

**2. Very much or very often, with a verb.** (много, часто)

3. We go to the beach *a lot* in the summer.
4. Thanks *a lot* for your help.
5. He talks *a lot* when he's nervous.

### a trifle

**Pronunciation:** /ə ˈtraɪfəl/ &middot; *uh TRY-fuhl*
**Русский:** слегка, чуточку
**Type:** degree — a downtoner, bookish
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs
**Contrast:** Mostly written; in speech Americans say *a little* or *a bit*. The noun *trifle* means a small unimportant thing.

Slightly; a little.

1. She seemed *a trifle* annoyed by the question.
2. The soup was *a trifle* too salty.
3. He arrived *a trifle* late, as usual.

### aback

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbæk/ &middot; *uh-BAK*
**Русский:** врасплох (опешить)
**Type:** manner — only in the fixed phrase *taken aback*
**Position:** end — right after *taken*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** the passive *be taken* in *be taken aback*

Surprised and briefly thrown off balance, in *be taken aback*.

1. I was taken *aback* by how rude he was.
2. She seemed a little taken *aback* when I mentioned the price.
3. We were all taken *aback* by the news.

### abjectly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæbdʒɛktli/ &middot; *AB-jekt-lee*
**Русский:** униженно; полностью, крайне
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — or before its word (*abjectly poor*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of apologizing and failing; adjectives like *poor*
**Form:** -ly from *abject*

Abjectly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a humble, ashamed way.** (униженно, покорно)

1. He apologized *abjectly* for the mistake.
2. She begged *abjectly* for one more chance.

**2. Completely and hopelessly, of failure or poverty.** (полностью, крайне)

3. The plan failed *abjectly*.
4. Many families there live in *abjectly* poor conditions.

### ably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈeɪbli/ &middot; *AY-blee*
**Русский:** умело, искусно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid, before a participle (*ably assisted*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *able*: the -le becomes -ly

With skill; competently.

1. The team was *ably* led by a young captain.
2. She handled the reporters' questions *ably*.
3. He was *ably* assisted by two graduate students.

### abnormally

**Pronunciation:** /æbˈnɔːrməli/ &middot; *ab-NOR-muh-lee*
**Русский:** ненормально, аномально
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives (*abnormally high*); end with verbs
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *abnormal*

In a way that is not normal or usual.

1. Her blood pressure was *abnormally* high.
2. It's been an *abnormally* warm winter.
3. The cells were growing *abnormally*.

### abominably

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbɑːmənəbli/ &middot; *uh-BAHM-uh-nuh-blee*
**Русский:** отвратительно, ужасно
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — or before its word (*abominably rude*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of behaving and treating; adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *abominable*: the -le becomes -ly

Very badly, in a shocking or offensive way.

1. The workers were treated *abominably* by the new owners.
2. He behaved *abominably* at his sister's wedding.
3. The hotel staff were *abominably* rude to us.

### about

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbaʊt/ &middot; *uh-BOWT*
**Русский:** около, примерно; почти
**Type:** degree — an approximator
**Position:** before its word — before numbers and amounts, and before *ready*, *done*, *finished*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** numbers, amounts and times; adjectives like *ready* and *done*
**Contrast:** Before a noun, *about* is usually a preposition meaning 'о, про' (*a book about dogs*), and *be about to* means 'вот-вот, собираться'. For 'here and there' Americans say *around* (*walk around*) where British English says *walk about*.

About has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Approximately; a little more or less than.** (около, примерно)

1. The drive takes *about* three hours.
2. There were *about* fifty people at the party.
3. I'll be there at *about* six.

**2. Almost; nearly, often in *just about*.** (почти)

4. I'm *about* ready to leave.
5. We're just *about* done with the kitchen.
6. He's *about* the only person who knows the password.

### above all

**Pronunciation:** /əˌbʌv ˈɔːl/ &middot; *uh-buv AWL*
**Русский:** прежде всего, самое главное
**Type:** focusing — marks the most important item
**Position:** front or end — often front with a comma, or right before the item it stresses
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause, or the phrase it singles out
**Contrast:** Not *first of all*: *first of all* orders points (во-первых), *above all* ranks them (самое главное).

Most importantly; more than anything else.

1. *Above all*, don't panic.
2. She wanted, *above all*, to be taken seriously.
3. Be kind, be honest, and *above all*, be yourself.

### abreast

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbrɛst/ &middot; *uh-BREST*
**Русский:** в ряд, бок о бок; в курсе
**Type:** place; also manner in *keep abreast of*
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of moving and standing; *keep* and *stay* + *of*

Abreast has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Side by side, facing the same way.** (в ряд, бок о бок)

1. The path was too narrow to walk two *abreast*.
2. Three police cars drove *abreast* down the highway.

**2. Up to date with news or developments, in *keep abreast of* or *stay abreast of*.** (в курсе)

3. I read the news every morning to keep *abreast* of what's happening.
4. Doctors have to stay *abreast* of new research.

### abruptly

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbrʌptli/ &middot; *uh-BRUPT-lee*
**Русский:** внезапно, резко
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid, before the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs, especially *stop*, *end*, *leave*, *turn*
**Form:** -ly from *abrupt*

Suddenly and unexpectedly; also in a short, curt way.

1. The car stopped *abruptly*.
2. The meeting ended *abruptly* when the fire alarm went off.
3. “I have to go,” she said *abruptly*, and hung up.

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid, before the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *absent*

Without paying attention to what you are doing.

1. He nodded *absently*, still looking at his phone.

### absentmindedly

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæbsəntˈmaɪndɪdli/ &middot; *ab-suhnt-MYN-did-lee*
**Русский:** рассеянно, по рассеянности
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *absentminded*; also spelled *absent-mindedly*

Forgetfully, because your mind is on something else.

1. I *absentmindedly* left my keys in the fridge.

### abstractly

**Pronunciation:** /æbˈstræktli/ &middot; *ab-STRAKT-lee*
**Русский:** абстрактно, отвлечённо
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of thinking and talking
**Form:** -ly from *abstract*

In terms of general ideas rather than real examples.

1. It's hard for young children to think *abstractly*.
2. He talked *abstractly* about justice but never gave an example.
3. The problem gets easier once you stop thinking about it so *abstractly*.

### absurdly

**Pronunciation:** /əbˈsɜːrdli/ &middot; *uhb-SURD-lee*
**Русский:** нелепо; до абсурда
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives (*absurdly expensive*); end with verbs
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *absurd*

To a ridiculous degree; in a ridiculous way.

1. Rent in this city is *absurdly* high.

### abundantly

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbʌndəntli/ &middot; *uh-BUN-duhnt-lee*
**Русский:** совершенно (ясно); обильно
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — mostly in *abundantly clear*; end in the 'plentifully' sense
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives like *clear* and *obvious*; verbs of growing and supplying
**Form:** -ly from *abundant*

Abundantly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very; completely, mostly in *abundantly clear*.** (совершенно, вполне)

1. She made it *abundantly* clear that she wasn't interested.
2. It's *abundantly* obvious that the plan won't work.

**2. In large amounts; plentifully.** (обильно, в изобилии)

3. Wildflowers grow *abundantly* in these hills.
4. The region is *abundantly* supplied with fresh water.

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of speaking and treating
**Form:** -ly from *abusive*

In an insulting or cruel way.

### academically

**Pronunciation:** /ˌækəˈdɛmɪkli/ &middot; *ak-uh-DEM-ik-lee*
**Русский:** в учёбе, академически
**Type:** viewpoint — 'as far as studies are concerned'
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives (*academically gifted*); end with verbs; front with a comma
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, verbs, and whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *academic*: -ic adds -ally, pronounced /ɪkli/

In terms of school, studies or scholarship.

1. Our son is doing well *academically* but struggles to make friends.
2. The school admits only *academically* gifted students.
3. *Academically*, it was her best year.

### acceptably

**Pronunciation:** /əkˈsɛptəbli/ &middot; *uhk-SEP-tuh-blee*
**Русский:** приемлемо, удовлетворительно
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — or before its word (*acceptably safe*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *acceptable*: the -le becomes -ly

Well enough to be accepted; satisfactorily.

1. The car ran *acceptably* after the repair.
2. Is the water *acceptably* clean?
3. Students must perform *acceptably* on the final exam to pass.

### accidentally

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæksəˈdɛntəli/ &middot; *ak-suh-DEN-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** случайно, нечаянно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid — before the main verb; also end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *accidental* — spelled *-ally*, often said ak-suh-DENT-lee
**Contrast:** *Accidentally* means 'by mistake, without meaning to'. In questions, Russian *случайно* ('do you happen to…') is *by any chance* (*Do you have a pen, by any chance?*), never *accidentally*.

By mistake; without meaning to.

1. I *accidentally* deleted the whole file.
2. She *accidentally* locked herself out of the car.
3. The fire was started *accidentally* by a cigarette.

**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *accurate*

Correctly and exactly, without mistakes.

### acoustically

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkuːstɪkli/ &middot; *uh-KOOS-tik-lee*
**Русский:** акустически; без усилителя
**Type:** viewpoint; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives (*acoustically perfect*); end in the 'unplugged' sense
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and verbs of playing and recording
**Form:** -ly from *acoustic*: -ic adds -ally

Acoustically has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In terms of sound.** (акустически, по звуку)

1. The new concert hall is *acoustically* excellent.
2. *Acoustically*, the room was a disaster.

**2. On instruments that are not electrically amplified.** (без усилителя, в акустике)

3. The band played the last three songs *acoustically*.
4. She recorded the whole album *acoustically* in her kitchen.

### across the board

**Type:** manner — applying equally to every member of a group
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of cutting, raising and applying

Equally for everyone or everything in a group; without exception.

1. The company cut salaries *across the board*.

### ad infinitum

**Type:** duration — without end
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of going on and repeating; the end of a list

Forever; again and again without end.

### ad nauseam

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæd ˈnɔːziəm/ &middot; *ad NAW-zee-uhm*
**Русский:** до тошноты, до одури
**Type:** frequency — so often that it becomes tiresome
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of repeating, discussing and playing

Repeated so often or so long that people are sick of it.

1. The ad played *ad nauseam* during the game.

**Русский:** постоянно; всё это время
**Type:** frequency; also duration
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs

All the time has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very often; constantly.** (постоянно, всё время)

1. She complains *all the time*.
2. This kind of thing happens *all the time* in big cities.
3. We eat at that diner *all the time*.

**2. During the whole of a period, often without someone knowing.** (всё это время)

4. I searched everywhere, and the ticket was in my wallet *all the time*.
5. He was listening *all the time* we were talking.
