# More adverbs: D–F

> Adverbs D–F beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### daintily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdeɪntəli/ &middot; *DAYN-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** изящно, деликатно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *dainty*

In a small, delicate, graceful way.

1. She sipped her tea *daintily*.
2. The cat stepped *daintily* over the puddle.
3. He *daintily* dabbed his mouth with a napkin.

### damn

**Pronunciation:** /dæm/ &middot; *DAM*
**Русский:** чертовски
**Type:** degree — informal and mildly rude
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs (*damn good*, *damn well*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *damn*
**Contrast:** Informal and mildly offensive; keep it out of formal writing. *Damn well* adds force to a verb (*You know damn well*). *Darn* is the softer version.

Very; extremely (informal).

1. That was a *damn* good meal.
2. You know *damn* well what I mean.
3. It's *damn* cold out there.

### dangerously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdeɪndʒərəsli/ &middot; *DAYN-jur-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** опасно
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end; also before an adjective
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives and adverbs (*dangerously low*, *dangerously fast*)
**Form:** -ly from *dangerous*

In a way that could cause harm or put someone at risk.

1. He was driving *dangerously* fast.
2. The patient's blood pressure was *dangerously* low.
3. The crowd surged *dangerously* toward the stage.

### darkly

4. The car had *darkly* tinted windows.

### day after day

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdeɪ æftər ˈdeɪ/ &middot; *day af-tur DAY*
**Русский:** день за днём, изо дня в день
**Type:** frequency
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Contrast:** *Day after day* stresses tiring repetition; *day by day* stresses gradual change. *The day after tomorrow* (послезавтра) is unrelated.

Every day for a long time, often tiresomely.

1. He wore the same shirt *day after day*.
2. *Day after day*, she waited for a letter.
3. It rained *day after day*.

### day by day

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdeɪ baɪ ˈdeɪ/ &middot; *day by DAY*
**Русский:** с каждым днём; одним днём
**Type:** manner
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs

Day by day has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Gradually, a little more each day.** (с каждым днём, постепенно)

1. *Day by day*, he's getting stronger.
2. Her English improved *day by day*.

**2. One day at a time, without planning far ahead.** (одним днём)

3. Since the diagnosis, we take things *day by day*.
4. With no steady job, he's living *day by day*.

### day in, day out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdeɪ ɪn ˌdeɪ ˈaʊt/ &middot; *DAY in day OWT*
**Русский:** изо дня в день
**Type:** frequency — every day, without change
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; a whole clause

Every day, continuously and without change, often to the point of boredom.

1. She rides the same crowded train *day in, day out*.
2. *Day in, day out*, he answers the same customer complaints.
3. Farmers work the land *day in, day out*, whatever the weather.

### de facto

**Pronunciation:** /deɪ ˈfæktoʊ/ &middot; *day FAK-toh*
**Русский:** фактически, де-факто
**Type:** viewpoint — in actual practice, whatever the law says
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; a whole clause
**Form:** Latin 'from the fact'
**Contrast:** Much more often an adjective before a noun: *the de facto leader*, *a de facto ban*. Its legal opposite is *de jure* (по закону).

In fact or in practice, though not officially.

1. English functions *de facto* as the official language of the United States.
2. After the governor fell ill, his chief of staff was *de facto* in charge.
3. The rule was never repealed, but it has been *de facto* abandoned.

### dead ahead

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdɛd əˈhɛd/ &middot; *ded uh-HED*
**Русский:** прямо впереди, прямо по курсу
**Type:** place — directly in front
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, especially *be*, *lie*, *see*
**Form:** *dead* 'exactly' + *ahead*

Straight ahead; directly in front.

1. There's a gas station *dead ahead*.
2. The captain spotted an iceberg *dead ahead*.
3. Keep driving and you'll see the lake *dead ahead*.

### deadly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɛdli/ &middot; *DED-lee*
**Русский:** смертельно, крайне
**Type:** degree
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a few adjectives: *serious*, *dull*, *quiet*, *pale*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *deadly*
**Contrast:** Usually an adjective (*a deadly virus*). As an adverb it only intensifies a few words: *deadly serious* means *completely serious*, not *dangerously serious*.

Extremely; completely.

1. I'm *deadly* serious about this.
2. The lecture was *deadly* dull.
3. The room went *deadly* quiet.

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end

### deceptively

**Pronunciation:** /dɪˈsɛptɪvli/ &middot; *di-SEP-tiv-lee*
**Русский:** обманчиво
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and participles
**Form:** -ly from *deceptive*
**Contrast:** *Deceptively simple* means it only looks simple and is really hard. With some adjectives the meaning is unclear (*deceptively large* — bigger or smaller than it looks?), so careful writers avoid those.

In a way that gives a false impression.

1. The puzzle looks *deceptively* simple.
2. The current is *deceptively* strong near the shore.
3. The drink was *deceptively* labeled as juice.

### decidedly

**Pronunciation:** /dɪˈsaɪdɪdli/ &middot; *di-SY-did-lee*
**Русский:** определённо, явно
**Type:** degree
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, comparatives and adverbs
**Form:** -ly from *decided*
**Contrast:** *Decidedly* means *clearly, definitely* (*decidedly colder*). *Decisively* means *firmly, without hesitating* or *by a clear margin* (*act decisively*, *win decisively*).

Clearly; without doubt.

1. The weather turned *decidedly* colder.
2. The mood in the room was *decidedly* hostile.
3. Her new album is *decidedly* better than the last one.

### decisively

**Position:** end; also mid
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *decisive*

Decisively has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Quickly and firmly, without hesitating.** (решительно)

1. The governor acted *decisively* to stop the outbreak.

**2. In a way that settles something clearly.** (убедительно, окончательно)

3. The home team won *decisively*.

### futilely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfjuːtəli/ &middot; *FYOO-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** тщетно, напрасно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *futile*; the -e stays

Without any chance of success; in vain.

1. He tried *futilely* to start the car in the freezing cold.
2. She searched *futilely* for her ring in the sand.
3. Neighbors *futilely* argued that the park should be saved.
