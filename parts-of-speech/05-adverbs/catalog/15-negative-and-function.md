# Negative and function adverbs

> *not*, *no*, *any*, *as*, *else*, *together* — the small grammatical adverbs that everything else leans on.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

*Not* is an adverb, and the most frequent one in the language. With it here are the function adverbs that do grammatical work rather than adding meaning: *no* and *any* before a comparative (*no better*, *any faster*), *as* in comparisons of equality (*as tall as*), *else* after a question word or indefinite (*who else*, *something else*), and *together* and *all* in their adverb uses (*all alone*).

---
### not

**Pronunciation:** /nɑːt/ &middot; *NAHT*
**Русский:** не
**Type:** negative; also focusing in *not only*
**Position:** mid — after *be* and the first auxiliary; before *to* and -ing forms; before its word when it negates one word or phrase
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses, infinitives and -ing forms, adjectives, adverbs, noun phrases, quantifiers; stands for a whole clause in *I hope not*
**Form:** contracts to *n't* after *be*, *have*, *do* and the modals: *isn't*, *doesn't*, *can't*, *won't* (from *will*)
**Contrast:** *Not* negates verbs, adjectives and adverbs; before a noun use the determiner *no* or *not any*: *I have no time* or *I don't have any time*, not *I have not time*. A main verb needs *do*: *I don't know*, not *I not know* or the archaic *I know not*. One negative per clause: Russian *Я никогда ничего не видел* is *I have never seen anything*, not *I haven't never seen nothing*. English moves the negative up to *think*, *believe*, *expect*: *Я думаю, он не прав* is naturally *I don't think he's right*. Position changes the meaning with infinitives: *I decided not to go* (I stayed home) vs. *I didn't decide to go* (I made no decision). *Not all the students passed* (some failed) is clear; *All the students didn't pass* is ambiguous. A fronted *not* phrase inverts: *Not once did he call*; *Not until Friday will we know*. In short replies say *I hope not*, *I guess not*, *probably not* — but *I don't think so*; *I think not* is formal.

Not has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Used to make a verb or a whole clause negative.** (не)

1. I do *not* agree with you.
2. She's *not* coming tonight.
3. Try *not* to wake the baby.

**2. Used before a single word or phrase to make only that part negative.** (не (перед словом или фразой))

4. *Not* everyone likes jazz.
5. Call me, *not* my assistant.
6. She's a colleague, *not* a friend.

**3. Used instead of a whole negative clause — after *hope*, *guess*, *be afraid*, and after *if*, *or* and *probably*.** (нет; не)

7. Is it going to rain? — I hope *not*.
8. Take the job if you like it; if *not*, keep looking.
9. Ready or *not*, here I come.

**4. Not a single one; none at all — emphatic, in *not a*, *not one*, *not a single*.** (ни (один), ни (слова))

10. *Not* one person showed up.
11. There wasn't a cloud in the sky — *not* a single one.
12. *Not* a word to anyone about this.

**5. Not just this, and that as well — in *not only … but (also)*.** (не только… но и)

13. She *not* only wrote the song but also sang it.
14. *Not* only is it cheaper, it's faster too.

**6. Used before a negative word to say something positive in a modest, understated way.** (не- (неплохо, нередко — смягчённое утверждение))

15. Honestly, the pizza was *not* bad.
16. It's a *not* unreasonable request.
17. Delays like this are *not* uncommon in winter.

**7. In *not that*, used to deny a reason or meaning someone might read into what you just said.** (не то чтобы)

18. He's moving to Denver — *not* that I care.
19. *Not* that it matters now, but I was right.

### no

**Pronunciation:** /noʊ/ &middot; *NOH*
**Русский:** больше не; ничуть не
**Type:** negative — before comparatives and a few adjectives; also degree
**Position:** before its word — before a comparative, *longer*, *sooner*, *more*, *less*, *different* or *good*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparative adjectives and adverbs, *more*, *less*, *longer*, *sooner*; the adjectives *different* and *good*
**Contrast:** The adverb *no* goes only before a comparative and a few words like *different* and *good*: *no better*, *no longer*, *no different*. Before an ordinary adjective use *not*: *He's not tall*, not *He's no tall*. Before a noun *no* is a determiner (*no money*), and *No.* as an answer is an interjection — both are in those dictionaries. *No more than* means 'only' (*It costs no more than $10* — всего лишь), while *not more than* means 'at most' (не больше); *no less than* stresses a large amount (целых). Russian *больше не* is *no longer* or *not … anymore*: *I no longer work there* or *I don't work there anymore*, not *I don't work there more* — and never both in one clause. *No longer* goes mid, *anymore* at the end. Fronted *no sooner* inverts and takes *than*, not *when*: *No sooner had we sat down than the phone rang*. *He's no more a doctor than I am* means neither of us is a doctor (он такой же врач, как я), not that he is a better one.

No has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Not now, though it was before — in *no longer* and, in formal or literary use, *no more*.** (больше не, уже не)

1. He *no* longer works here.
2. We can *no* longer afford to wait.
3. The old theater is *no* more; it closed in 2019.

**2. Not at all; in no way — before a comparative, *different* or *good*, to say there is no difference in degree.** (ничуть не, нисколько не)

4. The new model is *no* faster than the old one.
5. Her second book is *no* different from her first.
6. This old phone charger is *no* good anymore.

**3. Only, or as much as — before *more than* or *less than* and an amount, to stress how small or how large it is.** (не более, всего лишь; не менее, целых)

7. The walk takes *no* more than ten minutes.
8. He paid *no* less than $2,000 for the ticket.
9. The storm dumped *no* less than two feet of snow on the city.

**4. Used after a name or fact to stress that it is surprising or impressive — in *no less* at the end of a phrase.** (не кто-нибудь, а; ни много ни мало)

10. She got a thank-you note from the governor, *no* less.
11. He showed up in a tuxedo, *no* less.

**5. Immediately after; as soon as — in *no sooner … than*.** (едва… как; не успел… как)

12. *No* sooner had I left than it started to pour.
13. I had *no* sooner paid than I saw a better deal online.

**6. Used to take back a word you just said and replace it with a stronger or more exact one.** (нет, даже; вернее)

14. Hundreds, *no*, thousands of fans lined the street.
15. It was a good year — *no*, a great year.

### any

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛni/ &middot; *EN-ee*
**Русский:** хоть немного; ничуть не
**Type:** degree — in questions, negatives and *if* clauses
**Position:** before its word — before a comparative, *good* or *different*; end in informal American *didn't help any*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparative adjectives and adverbs, *longer*, *more*, and the adjectives *good* and *different*; a verb phrase in informal speech
**Contrast:** The adverb *any* works only in questions, negatives and *if* clauses, and only before a comparative, *good* or *different*: *Is it any cheaper?* is fine, *Is it any cheap?* is not — say *Is it cheap at all?* In an affirmative sentence use *a little* or *a bit*: *It's a little better*, not *It's any better*. *Not any* + comparative equals *no* + comparative: *It's not any better* = *It's no better*. Russian *любой* is the determiner *any* (*any size*, *any time*), which is in the determiner dictionary, not this adverb. American spelling splits *anymore* (time: *I don't live there anymore* — больше не) from *any more* (amount: *I don't want any more* — determiner). *It didn't help any* is informal American; in writing use *at all*.

Any has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At all; to any degree — before a comparative, *good* or *different*.** (хоть немного, хоть сколько-нибудь; (в отрицании) ничуть не)

1. Do you feel *any* better today?
2. Is the sequel *any* good?
3. The traffic isn't *any* worse than usual.

**2. For more time; still — in *any longer*, in negatives, questions and *if* clauses.** (больше (не), дольше)

4. I can't wait *any* longer.
5. If this takes *any* longer, we'll miss the train.

**3. At all — at the end of a negative sentence or a question; informal American.** (нисколько, вообще, хоть сколько-то)

6. The medicine didn't help *any*.
7. Did you sleep *any* last night?
8. That kid hasn't grown *any* since June.

### none

**Pronunciation:** /nʌn/ &middot; *NUHN*
**Русский:** ничуть не, не слишком
**Type:** degree — negative, in *none too* and *none the* + comparative
**Position:** before its word — before *too* or *the* + a comparative
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** *too* + an adjective or adverb; *the* + a comparative
**Form:** fixed patterns only: *none too* + adjective or adverb, *none the* + comparative
**Contrast:** The pronoun *none* (*none of us*) is in the pronoun dictionary; the adverb lives only in *none too* and *none the* + comparative. *None the* needs *the*: *none the wiser*, not *none wiser*. A Russian reader can take *She was none too pleased* as 'никто не был доволен' — it means she was not pleased at all, said as understatement, usually about annoyance. *And none too soon* means 'and not a moment too early' (давно пора), not 'too early'. *No wiser than* compares with someone else; *none the wiser* compares with before — you learned nothing. *Nonetheless* (однако) is one word in American spelling and is in the linking group.

None has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not at all; not very — in *none too*, usually as understatement.** (не слишком, не очень-то)

1. The clerk looked *none* too happy to see us.
2. The motel was *none* too clean.
3. The ambulance finally arrived, and *none* too soon.

**2. Not at all; in no way — in *none the* + comparative, often with *for*.** (ничуть не, нисколько не)

4. I read the manual twice and was *none* the wiser.
5. The car was *none* the worse for the long trip.
6. She was *none* the happier for all her money.

### as

**Pronunciation:** /æz/ &middot; *AZ*
**Русский:** так же, столь же
**Type:** degree — equality, the first *as* in *as … as*
**Position:** before its word — before the adjective, adverb, *much* or *many* being compared
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, *much*, *many*, *little*, *few*; an adjective with *a* + noun in *as good a cook*
**Form:** the same spelling as the conjunction and preposition *as* (the second *as* in *as … as*); both are usually weak, əz, in connected speech and full æz only when stressed
**Contrast:** Only the first *as* in *as … as* is this adverb; the second *as*, and *as* meaning 'like', 'because', 'while' or 'in the role of', are in the conjunction and preposition dictionaries. Use the plain form, not the comparative: *as tall as*, not *as taller as*. For multiples say *twice as expensive as*, not *twice expensive as*. The everyday American negative is *not as … as*; *not so … as* is formal. Russian *такой же высокий, как* is *as tall as*, but *такого же роста, как* is *the same height as* — never *the same tall as*. After the second *as*, speech uses *me* and careful writing *I am*: *She's as tall as me* / *as tall as I am*. With a singular noun the article moves: *as good a cook as*, not *as a good cook as*. *As well* ('too' — тоже) is a separate phrase.

As has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To the same degree; equally — the first *as* in *as … as*, or alone when the comparison is understood.** (так же, такой же (… как); столь же)

1. Nobody works *as* hard as Dana.
2. It's not *as* expensive as I thought.
3. I used to run every day, but I'm not *as* fit now.

**2. After *twice*, *three times*, *half* and similar words: that many times the size, amount or degree.** (в (два, три) раза; вдвое)

4. The new apartment is twice *as* big.
5. Tickets cost three times *as* much as last year.
6. My commute is half *as* long now.

**3. Before *many*, *much*, *long*, *little* or *few* and a number: to stress how large or how small an amount is.** (целых, аж, до; всего)

7. *As* many as 300 homes lost power.
8. A good hearing aid can cost *as* much as $6,000.
9. You can get there in *as* little as an hour.

**4. In *as much*: the same thing, just that — when something confirms what you already suspected.** (так (я и думал); то же самое)

10. The store's closed? I thought *as* much.
11. Everyone suspected *as* much, but nobody said it.

### else

**Pronunciation:** /ɛls/ &middot; *ELS*
**Русский:** ещё; другой, иначе
**Type:** focusing — additive (*what else*) or excluding (*someone else*); also linking in *or else*
**Position:** after its word — after *who*, *what*, *where*, *how*, *why*, the *some-*, *any-*, *no-*, *every-* words, and *little* and *much*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** question words; indefinite pronouns and adverbs (*someone*, *anything*, *nowhere*, *everybody*); *little*, *much*
**Form:** the possessive goes on *else*: *someone else's*, *nobody else's*
**Contrast:** *Else* never stands before a noun and never after *which* or an ordinary noun: *another person* or *other people*, not *else person*; *which other book*, not *which else book*. Russian *ещё* splits several ways: *What else do you need?* (after a question word), *Do you want more coffee?* (with a noun), *one more time* or *another day*; and *ещё не* is *not yet*. The possessive *'s* goes on *else*: *someone else's phone*, not *someone's else phone*. *Somewhere else* and *elsewhere* mean the same; *elsewhere* is in the place group. *Or else* as a threat can end the sentence: *Pay up, or else!*

Else has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Other; different — a person, thing, place or way instead of the one mentioned.** (другой; (кто-то) другой, (где-то) ещё, иначе)

1. This isn't my jacket — it must be someone *else*'s.
2. Let's eat somewhere *else* tonight.
3. How *else* could we have done it?

**2. In addition; more — besides what was already mentioned.** (ещё)

4. What *else* do you need from the store?
5. Is anyone *else* coming?
6. There's not much *else* we can do.

**3. Otherwise; if not — in *or else*, often as a warning or a threat.** (иначе, а то)

7. Hurry up, or *else* we'll miss the bus.
8. You'd better return it by Friday — or *else*!

### together

**Pronunciation:** /təˈɡɛðər/ &middot; *tuh-GETH-ur*
**Русский:** вместе
**Type:** manner — with each other or into one; also time
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object; after the particle in phrasal verbs (*put it together*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Contrast:** *All together* (everyone at once, in one group — все вместе) is not *altogether* (completely; in total — совсем, всего): *Let's sing it all together* / *That's $40 altogether*. Russian *вместе* for the total of a bill or a count is *altogether* or *in all*, not *together*; *together* adds things up only as *put together* or *taken together* (вместе взятые): *He earns more than the two of us put together*. *Together with* does not make a singular subject plural: *The manager, together with two assistants, is on the way*. Russian *Мы с Анной пошли вместе* is *Anna and I went together* or *I went with Anna*, not *We with Anna went together*. The informal adjective *together* (*She's so together* — собранная) is in the adjective dictionary.

Together has 6 distinct senses.

**1. With each other; in a group or as a team.** (вместе, сообща)

1. We grew up *together* in Ohio.
2. Let's work on this *together*.
3. The whole family had dinner *together*.

**2. Into one place, piece or whole; so that things are joined or combined.** (вместе, воедино; (соединить) друг с другом)

4. Mix the flour and sugar *together*.
5. Tape the two pieces *together*.
6. Add these numbers *together*.

**3. In a romantic relationship; as a couple.** (вместе (как пара))

7. They've been *together* for twelve years.
8. Are Mike and Jen still *together*?

**4. Combined; counted or considered as a whole — often in *put together* and *taken together*.** (вместе взятые, в сумме)

9. He earns more than the two of us put *together*.
10. *Together*, the three stores employ about 200 people.
11. Taken *together*, these studies point to the same cause.

**5. So that they suit or match each other — mainly with *go* and *fit*.** ((сочетаться) друг с другом)

12. Those colors don't really go *together*.
13. Wine and chocolate go *together* surprisingly well.

**6. At the same time; at once.** (одновременно, разом)

14. The two runners crossed the finish line *together*.
15. The two alarms went off *together*.

### all

**Pronunciation:** /ɔːl/ &middot; *AWL*
**Русский:** совсем, совершенно
**Type:** degree — completely; also in tied scores
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective, adverb, prepositional phrase, *the* + comparative, *too* or *but*; after the number in scores
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, prepositional phrases (*all over*, *all through*), *the* + comparative, *too*; numbers in scores
**Contrast:** The determiner and pronoun *all* (*all the students*, *We all agree*) are in those dictionaries; the adverb modifies adjectives and phrases. *All but* has two readings: before an adjective or verb it means 'almost' (*all but impossible* — почти невозможно); before a noun or number it means 'everyone except' (*all but one* — все, кроме одного). *All the better* needs *the*. Watch the one-word look-alikes: *all ready* (everyone ready) vs. *already* (уже); *all together* vs. *altogether* (совсем, всего); *all right* is standard in American writing, *alright* informal. Russian *всё* meaning 'still' is *still*, not *all*: *Он всё ждёт* is *He's still waiting*; *всё равно* is *anyway*. In informal American speech *be all* introduces a quote (*And he's all, 'No way'*) — keep it out of writing.

All has 7 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; entirely — and, informally, very.** (совсем, совершенно, весь)

1. She lives *all* alone in that big house.
2. You're *all* wet — did you walk here?
3. The kids got *all* excited about the snow.

**2. Throughout; in every part of a place or a period of time — before *over*, *around*, *through* and *along*.** (по всему, повсюду; всё (время))

4. There were toys *all* over the floor.
5. The baby cried *all* through the night.
6. I knew it *all* along.

**3. Very; so — in *not all that* + adjective or adverb, to say something is less than people might expect.** (не так уж, не очень-то)

7. The movie wasn't *all* that good.
8. It's not *all* that far — maybe ten minutes on foot.
9. Nobody was *all* that surprised when he quit.

**4. Almost; very nearly — in *all but* before an adjective, participle or verb.** (почти, практически)

10. The project is *all* but finished.
11. By noon the snow had *all* but melted.
12. It was *all* but impossible to find parking.

**5. Even more, for a reason just given — in *all the* + comparative.** (тем более, ещё (лучше))

13. If you can come early, *all* the better.
14. The win was *all* the sweeter after last year's loss.
15. That's *all* the more reason to call her.

**6. Much more than is good or wanted — in *all too*.** (слишком (уж))

16. Accidents like this happen *all* too often.
17. The ending was *all* too predictable.

**7. Each; for each side — in a tied score, as in tennis or baseball.** (по, поровну (о равном счёте: 3:3))

18. It's three-*all* going into the ninth inning.
19. At thirty-*all*, she served an ace.
