# Stance and comment adverbs

> What the speaker thinks of it — *probably*, *maybe*, *obviously*, *luckily*, *frankly*, *actually*, *of course*.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Stance adverbs comment on the whole statement rather than on the verb: how certain it is (*probably*, *definitely*), how the speaker feels about it (*unfortunately*, *luckily*), or how it is being said (*frankly*, *honestly*). Most go at the front with a comma or in mid-position. Two are false friends for Russian speakers — *actually* means *in fact*, not *актуально*, and *eventually* means *in the end*, not *возможно*.

---
### certainly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɜːrtnli/ &middot; *SURT-n-lee*
**Русский:** конечно, несомненно
**Type:** stance — certainty
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also alone as an answer
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives (*certainly true*)
**Form:** -ly from *certain*
**Contrast:** *Surely* is not a synonym: *Certainly he knows* states a fact, but *Surely you know him* means “I'd be amazed if you didn't” and asks for agreement. As a reply, *Certainly* is polite and a little formal; everyday American speech says *Sure*.

Certainly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Without any doubt; definitely.** (несомненно, безусловно)

1. She *certainly* knows how to throw a party.
2. It's *certainly* not the cheapest option, but it's the best.
3. Things will *certainly* get busier after the holidays.

**2. Used as a polite answer to agree or give permission: yes, of course; *certainly not* is a firm no.** (конечно, разумеется)

4. “Could you help me with this box?” “*Certainly*.”
5. “Can I pay by card?” “*Certainly* — right over here.”
6. “May I borrow your car tonight?” “*Certainly* not.”

### definitely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɛfənətli/ &middot; *DEF-uh-nuht-lee*
**Русский:** определённо, точно
**Type:** stance — certainty
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also alone as an answer
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *definite*; spelled -ite-, never *definately*
**Contrast:** Order with *not* changes the meaning: *It's definitely not true* = точно неправда, but *It's not definitely true* = не факт, что правда. Do not use it for Russian *определённый* = “certain, particular”: *в определённой степени* is *to a certain extent*, not *definitely to some degree*.

Definitely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Without any doubt; for sure.** (определённо, точно)

1. I'm *definitely* coming to your party.
2. That was *definitely* the best pizza I've ever had.
3. We *definitely* need more chairs for Saturday.

**2. Used as an answer to say a strong yes or no.** (конечно, безусловно)

4. “Are you going to vote?” “*Definitely*.”
5. “Would you go back to that hotel?” “*Definitely* not.”

**3. In a fixed, final way, so that there is no more doubt or change.** (окончательно, точно)

6. Nothing has been *definitely* decided yet.
7. We can't say *definitely* when the store will reopen.

### obviously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɑːbviəsli/ &middot; *AHB-vee-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** очевидно, конечно; явно
**Type:** stance — what everyone should know; also manner
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *obvious*
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *очевидно* often means “probably, it seems” (*Очевидно, он опоздал на поезд*) — that is *apparently* or *evidently* in English. *Obviously* means “as anyone can see” and can sound rude if the listener did not know: *Obviously, the meeting is at three.*

Obviously has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Used to say that something is clear or expected and should need no explanation.** (конечно, само собой, очевидно)

1. *Obviously*, we can't leave the kids home alone.
2. “Did you tell her?” “*Obviously* not.”
3. We'll *obviously* pay you back as soon as we can.

**2. In a way that is easy to see or notice.** (явно, заметно)

4. She was *obviously* upset about something.
5. He'd *obviously* never cooked a turkey before.
6. The dog was *obviously* hungry.

---

### evidently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛvədəntli/ &middot; *EV-uh-duhnt-lee*
**Русский:** по-видимому, очевидно
**Type:** stance — a conclusion from evidence
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *evident*
**Contrast:** *Evidently* is closer to Russian *очевидно, по-видимому* (a conclusion) than *obviously* is. It is a little more formal than *apparently*, and like *apparently* it can also pass on what the speaker has heard: *Evidently, she quit last week* = судя по всему (говорят), она уволилась.

Evidently has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Based on what you can see or have found out; apparently.** (по-видимому, судя по всему)

1. The owners had *evidently* left in a hurry.
2. *Evidently*, nobody told him about the schedule change.
3. “Did the package come?” “*Evidently* not.”

**2. In a way that is plain to see; clearly.** (явно)

4. He was *evidently* nervous — his hands were shaking.
5. She was *evidently* pleased with the results.

### apparently

**Pronunciation:** /əˈpɛrəntli/ &middot; *uh-PAIR-uhnt-lee*
**Русский:** по-видимому, говорят, судя по всему
**Type:** stance — what the speaker has heard or what seems true
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma, and end in speech
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives (*apparently harmless*)
**Form:** -ly from *apparent*
**Contrast:** False friend: *apparently* does not mean *явно, очевидно*. The adjective can (*for no apparent reason* = без видимой причины), but the adverb almost always means “I heard” or “it seems”: *Apparently he's rich* = говорят, он богат.

Apparently has 2 distinct senses.

**1. According to what you have heard or read, though you cannot be sure.** (говорят, судя по всему)

1. *Apparently*, the restaurant is closing next month.
2. He's *apparently* moving to Denver.
3. The meeting got canceled, *apparently*.

**2. Seeming to be true from what you can see, though it may not be.** (по-видимому, на первый взгляд)

4. The back door had *apparently* been forced open.
5. It started as an *apparently* harmless joke.

### presumably

**Pronunciation:** /prɪˈzuːməbli/ &middot; *prih-ZOO-muh-blee*
**Русский:** надо полагать, вероятно
**Type:** stance — a reasonable assumption
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** -ably from *presume*
**Contrast:** *Presumably* is the speaker's own reasonable guess, which they expect to be right. Russian news style *предположительно* (*предположительно, преступник*) is *allegedly* or *suspected* in English, not *presumably*.

Used to say that you think something is very likely true, based on what you know.

1. You're *presumably* tired after such a long flight.
2. *Presumably*, the package will arrive tomorrow.
3. He left early, *presumably* to beat the traffic.

---

### probably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɑːbəbli/ &middot; *PRAHB-uh-blee*
**Русский:** вероятно, наверное
**Type:** stance — likelihood
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary, and before a negative auxiliary (*probably won't*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** -ably from *probable*
**Contrast:** Position with a negative: *He probably won't come*, not *He won't probably come*. Russian *наверное* is often a polite hedge; *probably* really means “more likely than not”, so *I'll probably come* is a weak promise.

Very likely; more likely than not.

1. It'll *probably* rain this afternoon.
2. She *probably* won't remember me.
3. “Are you coming tonight?” “*Probably*.”

### possibly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɑːsəbli/ &middot; *PAHS-uh-blee*
**Русский:** возможно; никак (не)
**Type:** stance — possibility; also degree
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after the modal (*can't possibly*); also alone as an answer
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives, numbers
**Form:** -ibly from *possible*
**Contrast:** *Can't possibly* is not “maybe can't” — it means “there is no way”: *I can't possibly finish by Friday* = я никак не успею к пятнице. In a request, *Could you possibly…?* is extra polite, not doubtful.

Possibly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Perhaps; used when something could be true but is not certain.** (возможно)

1. The drive could take three hours, *possibly* more.
2. It was *possibly* the worst movie I've ever seen.
3. “Will you finish today?” “*Possibly*.”

**2. Used with *can* or *could* for emphasis: in any way at all.** (никак; всё, что возможно)

4. I can't *possibly* eat all that.
5. How could anyone *possibly* believe him?
6. We did everything we *possibly* could.

**3. Used to make a request more polite.** (не могли бы вы)

7. Could you *possibly* turn the music down a little?
8. Could I *possibly* borrow your notes from Tuesday?

---

### maybe

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪbi/ &middot; *MAY-bee*
**Русский:** может быть, возможно
**Type:** stance — possibility
**Position:** front — usually at the start of the clause; also before a number, and alone as an answer
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, numbers (*maybe fifty*)
**Form:** one word, from *may* + *be*
**Contrast:** *Maybe* (one word) is the adverb; *may be* (two words) is a modal and a verb: *Maybe he is right* = *He may be right*. Never *He maybe right*. *Maybe* is informal; in writing *perhaps* is more common.

Maybe has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Perhaps; possibly.** (может быть, возможно)

1. *Maybe* she's stuck in traffic.
2. We could go to the beach, or *maybe* the lake.
3. “Are you coming with us?” “*Maybe*.”

**2. Used to make a suggestion politely.** (может, (давай))

4. *Maybe* we should call a plumber.
5. *Maybe* you could ask your dad for help.

**3. Used before a number: about, approximately.** (примерно, где-то)

6. There were *maybe* fifty people at the meeting.
7. It's *maybe* a ten-minute walk from here.

---

### perhaps

**Pronunciation:** /pərˈhæps/ &middot; *pur-HAPS*
**Русский:** возможно, может быть
**Type:** stance — possibility
**Position:** front — at the start of the clause; also mid, especially before an adjective or noun phrase
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives, numbers
**Contrast:** *Perhaps* is more formal than *maybe*; in everyday American conversation *maybe* is far more common. Unlike *maybe*, *perhaps* sits easily in mid position: *It was perhaps her best book*.

Perhaps has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Possibly; maybe.** (возможно, может быть)

1. *Perhaps* the letter got lost in the mail.
2. It was *perhaps* the most important decision of her career.
3. “Will he agree?” “*Perhaps*.”

**2. Used to make a suggestion, offer or request more polite.** (может быть, не хотите ли)

4. *Perhaps* you'd like to wait in the lobby.
5. *Perhaps* we could discuss this after lunch?

**3. Used before a number: about, approximately.** (примерно, около)

6. *Perhaps* two hundred people showed up.
7. The house is *perhaps* a mile from the highway.

### undoubtedly

**Pronunciation:** /ʌnˈdaʊtɪdli/ &middot; *uhn-DOW-tid-lee*
**Русский:** несомненно, бесспорно
**Type:** stance — certainty
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives
**Form:** un- + *doubted* + -ly; the *b* is silent and the -ed is a full syllable
**Contrast:** *Undoubtedly* means full certainty; *doubtless*, despite its look, often means only “probably”.

Without any doubt; certainly.

1. She is *undoubtedly* the best player on the team.
2. Prices will *undoubtedly* go up again next year.
3. *Undoubtedly*, the new law will be challenged in court.

---

### arguably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɑːrɡjuəbli/ &middot; *AHR-gyoo-uh-blee*
**Русский:** пожалуй, можно сказать
**Type:** stance — a claim the speaker supports
**Position:** mid — usually right before the adjective or noun phrase it comments on; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, superlatives, noun phrases, clauses
**Form:** -ably from *argue*
**Contrast:** False friend: the adjective *arguable* often means “спорный”, but *arguably* supports the claim — *He is arguably the best* = пожалуй, он лучший (and the speaker thinks so). It is not doubt and not *доказуемо*.

Used to say that something can reasonably be claimed, and the speaker believes it, though others may disagree.

1. He is *arguably* the greatest basketball player of all time.
2. It was *arguably* the most important invention of the century.
3. *Arguably*, the city's best food is found in its smallest restaurants.

---

### allegedly

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlɛdʒɪdli/ &middot; *uh-LEJ-id-lee*
**Русский:** якобы, предположительно
**Type:** stance — a claim that has not been proved
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *alleged*; the -ed is a full syllable
**Contrast:** In news reports *allegedly* protects the writer: someone has made the claim, usually an accusation, but it is not proved. It is not the speaker's own guess — that is *presumably*.

According to what someone has said, especially in an accusation that has not been proved.

1. The suspect *allegedly* stole more than $10,000 from his employer.
2. He was *allegedly* driving over 90 miles an hour.
3. The company *allegedly* hid the test results from regulators.

---

### supposedly

**Pronunciation:** /səˈpoʊzɪdli/ &middot; *suh-POH-zid-lee*
**Русский:** якобы, будто бы
**Type:** stance — what people say, with doubt
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *supposed*; the -ed is a full syllable: /zɪd/
**Contrast:** *Supposedly* signals that the speaker doubts the claim (якобы), unlike *presumably* (the speaker believes it). The form *supposably* is heard but counts as an error.

According to what people say or believe, though the speaker doubts it is true.

1. The old house is *supposedly* haunted.
2. This cream *supposedly* makes you look ten years younger.
3. He was *supposedly* sick, but I saw him at the game.

---

### reportedly

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈpɔːrtɪdli/ &middot; *rih-POR-tid-lee*
**Русский:** по сообщениям, как сообщается
**Type:** stance — information from reports
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *reported*; the -ed is a full syllable
**Contrast:** *Reportedly* only says the news comes from reports and is not confirmed; *allegedly* says someone has been accused of something.

According to what has been reported, though it is not confirmed.

1. The singer is *reportedly* planning a world tour.
2. *Reportedly*, the two companies have been in talks for months.
3. The storm *reportedly* left thousands without power.

---

### seemingly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsiːmɪŋli/ &middot; *SEE-ming-lee*
**Русский:** казалось бы, на вид; по-видимому
**Type:** stance — how things appear
**Position:** before its word — usually before an adjective; also front with a comma, or mid
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives (*seemingly endless*), verbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from the participle *seeming*
**Contrast:** Before an adjective, *seemingly* hints that the look is false: *a seemingly simple question* = казалось бы, простой вопрос. At the front it is closer to *apparently*: *Seemingly, nobody noticed* = судя по всему, никто не заметил.

Seemingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Appearing to be something, especially when this may not be true.** (казалось бы, на вид)

1. It was a *seemingly* simple question, but nobody could answer it.
2. We waited in a *seemingly* endless line of cars.
3. The *seemingly* quiet town had a lot of secrets.

**2. As far as anyone can tell; apparently.** (по-видимому, судя по всему)

4. *Seemingly*, nobody noticed the mistake.
5. The two men were *seemingly* unaware of each other.

### fortunately

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːrtʃənətli/ &middot; *FOR-chuh-nuht-lee*
**Русский:** к счастью
**Type:** stance — the speaker's feeling about it
**Position:** front — with a comma; also mid, or end after a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses
**Form:** -ly from *fortunate*

Used to say that something good happened, or something bad was avoided, by good luck.

1. *Fortunately*, no one was hurt in the accident.
2. We *fortunately* had an umbrella in the car.
3. *Fortunately* for us, the store was still open.

### unfortunately

**Pronunciation:** /ʌnˈfɔːrtʃənətli/ &middot; *uhn-FOR-chuh-nuht-lee*
**Русский:** к сожалению
**Type:** stance — the speaker's feeling about it
**Position:** front — with a comma; also mid, or end after a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses
**Form:** un- + *fortunate* + -ly
**Contrast:** *Unfortunately* is the everyday English for *к сожалению* — in emails, refusals and bad news. *Regrettably* is formal and official, *sadly* adds real emotion, and *to my regret* sounds stiff.

Used to say that something is bad news or not what you wanted.

1. *Unfortunately*, the concert is sold out.
2. I *unfortunately* won't be able to make it on Friday.
3. The package went to the wrong address, *unfortunately*.

### luckily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlʌkəli/ &middot; *LUHK-uh-lee*
**Русский:** к счастью, по счастью
**Type:** stance — the speaker's feeling about it
**Position:** front — with a comma; also mid
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses
**Form:** -ly from *lucky*, with *y* changed to *i*

Used to say that something good happened because of good luck.

1. *Luckily*, I had my phone with me.
2. We *luckily* found a parking spot right outside.
3. *Luckily* for him, the police officer let him go with a warning.

### sadly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsædli/ &middot; *SAD-lee*
**Русский:** к сожалению; грустно, печально
**Type:** stance — the speaker's feeling about it; also manner and degree
**Position:** front — with a comma as a stance adverb; end or mid in the manner sense; before its word in the degree sense
**Comparison:** more/most — in the manner sense only (*more sadly*); not gradable as a stance adverb
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs, adjectives and participles (*sadly mistaken*)
**Form:** -ly from *sad*
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: *Sadly, she left* = к сожалению, она ушла; *She left sadly* = она ушла грустная. *Sadly mistaken* is degree: “very wrong”, with no sadness at all.

Sadly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Used to say that something is sad or regrettable; unfortunately.** (к сожалению, увы)

1. *Sadly*, the old theater was torn down in 2015.
2. Her grandfather *sadly* passed away last spring.
3. *Sadly*, the museum is closed on Mondays.

**2. In a sad way.** (грустно, печально)

4. She smiled *sadly* and waved goodbye.
5. He shook his head *sadly*.

**3. Very much or completely, in a bad way — before a few adjectives and participles.** (глубоко, совсем, прискорбно)

6. If you think I'll pay for that, you're *sadly* mistaken.
7. The old park has been *sadly* neglected.

---

### regrettably

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈɡrɛtəbli/ &middot; *rih-GRET-uh-blee*
**Русский:** к сожалению, прискорбно
**Type:** stance — the speaker's feeling about it; also degree
**Position:** front — with a comma; mid; before its word in the degree sense
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, adjectives (*regrettably slow*), quantifiers (*regrettably few*)
**Form:** -ably from *regret*, with the *t* doubled
**Contrast:** *Regrettably* means “it is a pity”; *regretfully* means “with regret, feeling sorry” (*She regretfully declined*). Many Americans use *regretfully* for both, but careful writing keeps them apart. *Regrettably* is formal — in conversation say *unfortunately*.

Regrettably has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Used in formal speech or writing to say that something is unfortunate.** (к сожалению)

1. *Regrettably*, we are unable to offer you a position at this time.
2. The event has *regrettably* been canceled.

**2. To a degree that is a pity — before an adjective or quantifier.** (прискорбно, досадно)

3. *Regrettably* few students signed up for the program.
4. The city's response to the flooding was *regrettably* slow.

---

### amazingly

**Pronunciation:** /əˈmeɪzɪŋli/ &middot; *uh-MAY-zing-lee*
**Русский:** удивительно, поразительно
**Type:** stance — surprise; also degree
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or adverb; front with a comma as a stance adverb
**Comparison:** more/most — *even more amazingly*, at the front
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *amazing*
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: *Amazingly, she sang well* = удивительно, что она хорошо спела; *She sang amazingly well* = она пела потрясающе.

Amazingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To a very great or surprising degree; extremely.** (удивительно, невероятно)

1. The food was *amazingly* good.
2. She handled the bad news *amazingly* well.
3. The tickets were *amazingly* cheap.

**2. Used to say that something is very surprising.** (как ни удивительно, поразительно, но)

4. *Amazingly*, nobody was hurt in the crash.
5. The old car *amazingly* still runs.
6. Even *more amazingly*, the dog found its way home.

---

### surprisingly

**Pronunciation:** /sərˈpraɪzɪŋli/ &middot; *sur-PRY-zing-lee*
**Русский:** на удивление, удивительно
**Type:** stance — surprise; also degree
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or adverb; front with a comma as a stance adverb
**Comparison:** more/most — *even more surprisingly*, *most surprisingly*, at the front
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *surprising*
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *неожиданно* often means “suddenly” (*Неожиданно он встал*) — that is *suddenly*, not *surprisingly*. *Surprisingly* only says the fact was not expected. *Not surprisingly* at the front means “as expected”.

Surprisingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To a degree that you would not expect.** (на удивление)

1. The test was *surprisingly* easy.
2. The hotel was *surprisingly* cheap for Manhattan.
3. He took the news *surprisingly* well.

**2. Used to say that a fact or event is not what you expected.** (как ни странно, удивительно, но)

4. *Surprisingly*, the youngest runner won the race.
5. *Not surprisingly*, the plan failed.
6. *Most surprisingly*, nobody complained about the price.

---

### thankfully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈθæŋkfəli/ &middot; *THANGK-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** к счастью, слава богу; с благодарностью
**Type:** stance — relief; also manner
**Position:** front — with a comma as a stance adverb; mid or end in the manner sense
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, verbs
**Form:** -ly from *thankful*
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: *Thankfully, he took the money* = слава богу, он взял деньги; *He took the money thankfully* = взял деньги с благодарностью. The stance use is standard American English.

Thankfully has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Used to say you are glad and relieved that something happened or did not happen.** (к счастью, слава богу)

1. *Thankfully*, the rain stopped before the wedding.
2. The surgery was *thankfully* a success.
3. *Thankfully*, nobody was home when the tree fell.

**2. In a grateful way.** (с благодарностью)

4. He smiled *thankfully* at the nurse.
5. She sank *thankfully* into the hot bath.

---

### ironically

**Pronunciation:** /aɪˈrɑːnɪkli/ &middot; *eye-RAHN-ik-lee*
**Русский:** по иронии судьбы; иронично
**Type:** stance — an unexpected reversal; also manner
**Position:** front — with a comma as a stance adverb; end in the manner sense
**Comparison:** more/most — *even more ironically*, *most ironically*, at the front; *more ironically* in the manner sense
