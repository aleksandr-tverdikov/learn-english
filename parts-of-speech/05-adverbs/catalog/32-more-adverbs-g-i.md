# More adverbs: G–I

> Adverbs G–I beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### gaily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡeɪli/ &middot; *GAY-lee*
**Русский:** весело, радостно; ярко, пёстро
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end — before a past participle (*gaily decorated*) or after the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and past participles
**Form:** -ly from *gay*, spelled with *i*
**Contrast:** Because *gay* now usually means 'homosexual', *gaily* sounds old-fashioned and is mostly met in writing. In speech Americans say *cheerfully* or *brightly*.

Gaily has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Cheerfully and happily.** (весело, радостно)

1. The children sang *gaily* as they walked.
2. She waved *gaily* from the deck of the ship.

**2. In bright colors.** (ярко, пёстро)

3. The streets were *gaily* decorated for the festival.
4. Fishing boats were *gaily* painted in red and blue.

### gainfully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡeɪnfəli/ &middot; *GAYN-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** с доходом, на оплачиваемой работе
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid — almost only in *gainfully employed*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** the participle *employed*
**Form:** -ly from *gainful*

gainfully employed: having a paid job.

1. All three of her kids are now *gainfully* employed.
2. He hasn't been *gainfully* employed since the factory closed.
3. The program helps veterans become *gainfully* employed.

### gallantly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæləntli/ &middot; *GAL-uhnt-lee*
**Русский:** доблестно; галантно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also mid (*gallantly offered*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *gallant*

Gallantly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Bravely and with spirit, especially in a hard fight.** (доблестно, мужественно)

1. The team fought *gallantly* but lost in overtime.
2. The firefighters worked *gallantly* through the night.

**2. Politely and attentively, especially in an old-fashioned way toward a woman.** (галантно, учтиво)

3. He *gallantly* offered her his coat.
4. The old man *gallantly* held the door for us.

### gamely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡeɪmli/ &middot; *GAYM-lee*
**Русский:** храбро, не сдаваясь
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *game* 'willing to try'

Bravely and willingly, especially when doing something hard or unpleasant.

1. She *gamely* tried the spicy soup, then reached for water.

3. He smiled *gamely* through the whole awkward dinner.

### garishly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɛrɪʃli/ &middot; *GAIR-ish-lee*
**Русский:** кричаще, аляповато
**Type:** manner
**Position:** before its word — before a participle (*garishly painted*); end after a verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *garish*

In a way that is too bright, colorful, or showy.

1. The casino was *garishly* lit with neon.
2. She wore a *garishly* patterned shirt to the funeral.

### gaudily

**Русский:** кричаще, аляповато
**Type:** manner
**Position:** before its word — before a participle (*gaudily painted*); end after a verb
**Comparison:** more/most

### generically

**Pronunciation:** /dʒəˈnɛrɪkli/ &middot; *juh-NAIR-ik-lee*
**Русский:** обобщённо; без торговой марки
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also before its word (*generically named*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *generic*

Generically has 2 distinct senses.

**1. As a whole class or type, not as a particular one.** (обобщённо, в общем виде)

1. In some states, all soft drinks are *generically* called "coke."
2. The report refers to them *generically* as "contractors."

**2. Under a common, non-brand name, and usually more cheaply.** (как дженерик, без торговой марки)

3. The drug is now available *generically* at a much lower price.
4. Ask your pharmacist whether the medicine is sold *generically*.

Freely and in large amounts; giving more than is needed or expected.

### genetically

**Pronunciation:** /dʒəˈnɛtɪkli/ &middot; *juh-NET-ik-lee*
**Русский:** генетически
**Type:** viewpoint; also manner (*genetically modified*)
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or participle; front as a viewpoint adverb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *genetic*

In terms of genes, or by changing genes.

1. Most of the corn grown in the US is *genetically* modified.

### gently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɛntli/ &middot; *JENT-lee*
**Русский:** мягко, осторожно; полого
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles (*gently rolling hills*)
**Form:** -ly from *gentle*, with *-le* becoming *-ly*

Gently has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Softly and carefully, without force or roughness.** (мягко, нежно, осторожно)

1. She *gently* picked up the sleeping baby.
2. Stir the sauce *gently* so it doesn't break.
3. Could you tell him the news more *gently*?

**2. Gradually, not steeply or suddenly.** (полого, плавно)

4. The path slopes *gently* down to the lake.
5. Let the soup simmer *gently* for an hour.

### geologically

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒiːəˈlɑːdʒɪkli/ &middot; *jee-uh-LAH-jik-lee*
**Русский:** геологически
**Type:** viewpoint
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective (*geologically active*); front as a viewpoint adverb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *geological*

In terms of geology, the study of rocks and the earth's structure.

1. Yellowstone is one of the most *geologically* active places on earth.
2. *Geologically*, the Appalachians are much older than the Rockies.
3. The site is *geologically* unstable, so nobody can build there.

### geometrically

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒiːəˈmɛtrɪkli/ &middot; *jee-uh-MET-rik-lee*
**Русский:** геометрически; в геометрической прогрессии
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also before its word (*geometrically precise*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *geometric*

Geometrically has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In terms of shapes, lines, and angles.** (геометрически)

1. The garden is laid out in *geometrically* perfect squares.
2. The two triangles are *geometrically* similar.

**2. By repeated multiplication, and so very fast.** (в геометрической прогрессии)

3. The number of cases grew *geometrically* in the first month.
4. Costs rise *geometrically* as the project gets bigger.

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɪv ɔːr ˈteɪk/ &middot; *GIV or TAYK*

### goddamn

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɑːdæm/ &middot; *GAHD-dam*
**Русский:** чертовски (грубо)
**Type:** degree — a rude, informal intensifier
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs
**Contrast:** Offensive to many people, especially religious ones, and much stronger than *damn* or *really* — avoid it at work, in class, and with strangers. Also spelled *goddam* and *goddamned*. The same word works as an adjective (*the goddamn car*) and an interjection; those uses belong elsewhere.

Very; extremely — a strong, rude way to add force.

1. You're *goddamn* right I'm angry.
2. It's *goddamn* cold out here.
3. That was a *goddamn* stupid thing to say.

### good

**Pronunciation:** /ɡʊd/ &middot; *GUUD*
**Русский:** хорошо (разг.); как следует
**Type:** manner — informal and nonstandard in writing
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable — standard English uses *better* and *best*, the forms of *well*
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *good*
**Contrast:** The standard adverb is *well*: *She did well on the test*. Americans say *did good* all the time, but teachers and editors mark it wrong. After linking verbs *good* is an adjective and correct: *You look good*, *It smells good*. *I feel good* means happy; *I feel well* means healthy. *Do good* (help others) uses the noun.

Good has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Well (informal, not accepted in careful speech or writing).** (хорошо (разг.))

1. He played real *good* last night.
2. The car runs *good* now that it's been fixed.
3. She did *good* on her driving test.

**2. Thoroughly; completely (informal).** (как следует, основательно)

4. They beat us *good* in the final.
5. He got fooled *good* by that email.

### good and

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡʊd ən/ &middot; *GUUD uhn*
**Русский:** совсем, как следует
**Type:** degree — informal: completely, thoroughly
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, especially *ready*, *tired*, *mad*, *hot*
**Contrast:** Informal American. It is not *good* plus *and*: *I'll leave when I'm good and ready* means *when I'm completely ready, not before*.

Very; completely.

1. I'll leave when I'm *good and* ready.
2. By the time the pizza came, we were *good and* hungry.
3. Let the pan get *good and* hot before you add the oil.
