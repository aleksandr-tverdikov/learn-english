# Linking adverbs

> How this sentence relates to the last one — *however*, *therefore*, *moreover*, *instead*, *otherwise*, *anyway*.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Linking (conjunctive) adverbs connect a sentence to the one before it, but grammatically they are adverbs, not conjunctions — which is why *It rained, however we went* is a punctuation error: two sentences joined only by a comma. Use a period or a semicolon before them. The conjunction dictionary covers them as connectors; these entries cover them as adverbs, with the positions they can take inside a clause.

---
### however

**Pronunciation:** /haʊˈɛvər/ &middot; *how-EV-ur*
**Русский:** однако; как бы ни
**Type:** linking; also degree and manner
**Position:** any position — front with a comma (*However, …*), mid between commas (*The plan, however, failed*) or end; as *no matter how*, at the front of its clause
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause as a link; an adjective, adverb or *much*/*many* in *however hard*, *however much*
**Form:** how + ever, written as one word
**Contrast:** *However* is an adverb, not a conjunction like *but*: *It rained, however we went* is a comma splice. Write *It rained; however, we went* or *It rained. However, we went.* Russian *однако* can follow a comma, so this is the classic learner error. The comma also changes the meaning: *However, hard work pays off* (однако) vs *However hard you work, …* (как бы ни), with no comma after *however* when it means *no matter how*. *However did you find us?* (= how on earth) is mainly British; Americans say *How on earth did you find us?*

However has 3 distinct senses.

**1. But; in spite of what was just said.** (однако, тем не менее)

1. The hotel was expensive. *However*, the service was excellent.
2. Most of the team agreed; the manager, *however*, had doubts.
3. I'd love to come. I have to work that night, *however*.

**2. To whatever degree or extent; no matter how.** (как бы ни, сколько бы ни)

4. *However* hard I try, I can't get up before seven.
5. We'll buy it, *however* much it costs.
6. Call me when you land, *however* late it is.

**3. In whatever way; no matter how something is done.** (как бы ни, каким бы образом ни)

7. *However* you look at it, the numbers don't add up.
8. *However* we get there, we need to arrive by noon.

### nevertheless

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnɛvərðəˈlɛs/ &middot; *nev-ur-thuh-LES*
**Русский:** тем не менее, всё же
**Type:** linking — contrast: despite what was just said
**Position:** any position — mostly front (*Nevertheless, …*) or mid (*was nevertheless*); end is less common
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** never + the + less, written as one word
**Contrast:** Stronger and more formal than *however* or *still*; in conversation Americans more often say *still* or *even so*. Like *however*, it cannot join two sentences with only a comma: *It was late, nevertheless we kept working* needs a semicolon or a period. It often follows *but* or *and*: *risky but nevertheless worth it*.

In spite of that; even so.

1. The test was hard. *Nevertheless*, almost everyone passed.
2. She was exhausted but *nevertheless* finished the race.
3. The plan had flaws; it was, *nevertheless*, the best we had.

### nonetheless

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnʌnðəˈlɛs/ &middot; *nun-thuh-LES*
**Русский:** тем не менее, всё же
**Type:** linking — contrast: despite what was just said
**Position:** any position — front, mid (*was nonetheless*) or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** none + the + less; the American spelling is one word
**Contrast:** Means the same as *nevertheless*; *nonetheless* is at least as common in current American English and sits more easily at the end of a clause (*It was a long shot, but we tried nonetheless*). The spaced *none the less* is older and mostly British. It is not a conjunction: when it opens a new sentence, put a period or a semicolon before it, not just a comma.

In spite of that; even so.

1. The movie got bad reviews. *Nonetheless*, it made a lot of money.
2. It's a small gesture, but it's *nonetheless* important.
3. We knew the odds were against us, but we tried *nonetheless*.

### conversely

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈvɜːrsli/ &middot; *kuhn-VURS-lee*
**Русский:** наоборот, и наоборот
**Type:** linking — the reverse relation
**Position:** front — also mid between commas (*and, conversely, …*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** -ly from *converse* (the reverse)
**Contrast:** *Conversely* states the reverse of a relation that is also true (*Cold weather raises heating bills; conversely, warm weather lowers them*). It does not reject what someone said; that is *on the contrary*: *You're not bothering me. On the contrary, I'm glad you came.* Russian *наоборот* covers both jobs, so learners use *conversely* where *on the contrary* is needed. For *и наоборот* at the end of a sentence, say *and vice versa*.

In the opposite way; used to introduce the reverse of what was just said.

1. Some people work better in the morning; *conversely*, others do their best work at night.
2. When interest rates rise, bond prices fall. *Conversely*, when rates fall, bond prices rise.
3. A small company moves fast but has few resources. *Conversely*, a large company has plenty of resources but moves slowly.

### instead

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈstɛd/ &middot; *in-STED*
**Русский:** вместо этого
**Type:** linking — replacement: one thing in place of another
**Position:** front or end — end is the usual place (*Let's walk instead*); front with a comma when what happened replaces what was expected (*Instead, he laughed*); also mid (*She instead chose law*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb phrase or a whole clause
**Form:** in + stead (place)
**Contrast:** Alone, *instead* is an adverb and takes no object: *I had tea instead*, not *instead of it*. With an object you need the preposition *instead of*: *tea instead of coffee*, *instead of waiting*. Russian *вместо* always takes a noun, so learners write *instead coffee*; the *of* is required. *Instead of* + verb uses *-ing*: *instead of calling*, never *instead to call*.

In place of that; as a substitute or an alternative.

1. We didn't have any rice, so I made pasta *instead*.
2. Don't text him. Call him *instead*.
3. He didn't apologize. *Instead*, he blamed everyone else.

### otherwise

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʌðərwaɪz/ &middot; *UTH-ur-wyz*
**Русский:** иначе; в остальном; по-другому
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** any position — front or mid as *if not*, mid for *apart from that* (*an otherwise quiet day*), end in *think otherwise*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a clause; an adjective (*an otherwise good film*); a verb (*think otherwise*)
**Form:** other + -wise (way)
**Contrast:** As a link, *otherwise* is an adverb, so careful writing does not join two clauses with it and a comma alone: *Hurry, otherwise we'll miss the bus* is common in speech, but write *Hurry, or we'll miss the bus* or *Hurry; otherwise, we'll miss the bus*. Russian *иначе* covers both *if not* and *differently*; *otherwise* means *differently* mainly after *think*, *say*, *decide*, *suggest* and in *unless told otherwise*. For *сделай иначе*, say *do it differently*, not *do it otherwise*.

Otherwise has 3 distinct senses.

**1. If not; or else.** (иначе, а то, в противном случае)

1. Take an umbrella; *otherwise* you'll get soaked.
2. I'm glad you called. *Otherwise*, I would have waited all day.
3. You have to register by Friday. *Otherwise*, you can't take the class.

**2. Apart from that; in other respects.** (в остальном, а так)

4. The room was a bit small, but *otherwise* it was perfect.
5. It was an *otherwise* quiet afternoon.
6. Her arm was broken, but she was *otherwise* unhurt.

**3. In a different way; differently.** (по-другому, иначе)

7. Everyone says it's a good idea, but I think *otherwise*.
8. Unless you hear *otherwise*, the meeting is at ten.
9. He claims he was home, but the evidence suggests *otherwise*.

### though

**Pronunciation:** /ðoʊ/ &middot; *THOH*
**Русский:** впрочем, однако, всё же
**Type:** linking — contrast, informal
**Position:** end — also mid after the first phrase (*The ending, though, was great*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Contrast:** *Though* is both a conjunction and an adverb. As a conjunction it opens a clause (*Though it was late, we stayed*); as an adverb it comes at the end or in the middle and means *however*: *It was late. We stayed, though.* The adverb never opens its sentence, and *although* cannot be used this way: *We stayed, although* is wrong. In speech it is the usual way to say *однако*; *however* sounds formal in conversation.

However; used to add a contrast to what was just said.

1. The apartment is small. I love the location, *though*.
2. Thanks for the offer. I think I'll pass, *though*.
3. The first half was slow. The ending, *though*, was fantastic.

### moreover

**Pronunciation:** /mɔːrˈoʊvər/ &middot; *mor-OH-vur*
**Русский:** более того, кроме того
**Type:** linking — adding a stronger point
**Position:** front — also mid between commas (*It is, moreover, …*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** more + over, written as one word
**Contrast:** Formal and written; in conversation Americans say *also*, *plus* or *what's more*. It adds a point that supports the one before, usually a stronger one; it never introduces a contrast. It is an adverb, not a conjunction, so it needs a period or a semicolon before it, not just a comma.

In addition to what has been said, and more importantly.

1. The plan is too expensive. *Moreover*, it would take years to complete.
2. The drug is effective; *moreover*, it has few side effects.
3. The witness was far away and, *moreover*, it was dark.

### furthermore

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɜːrðərmɔːr/ &middot; *FUR-thur-mor*
**Русский:** кроме того, более того
**Type:** linking — adding a further point
**Position:** front — also mid between commas
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** further + more, written as one word
**Contrast:** Formal, like *moreover*; in speech use *also*, *besides* or *plus*. It has nothing to do with distance: *We can't go any further* (дальше) uses the comparative *further*, not *furthermore*. It needs a period or a semicolon before it, not a comma alone.

In addition; used to add another point to an argument.

1. The report was late. *Furthermore*, it was full of errors.
2. Exercise improves your mood; *furthermore*, it helps you sleep.
3. Applicants must be 18 or older. *Furthermore*, they need a valid driver's license.

### additionally

**Pronunciation:** /əˈdɪʃənəli/ &middot; *uh-DISH-uh-nuh-lee*
**Русский:** кроме того; дополнительно
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** front — as a link; mid before a verb or participle in the *as an extra* sense (*were additionally treated*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause; a verb or participle
**Form:** -ly from *additional*
**Contrast:** *Additionally* is formal and overused in learner writing; in conversation *also* or *plus* is more natural. It does not join two clauses with only a comma. Russian *дополнительно* in *оплачивается дополнительно* is usually *extra* or *separately* in everyday American English: *Parking costs extra*, *Drinks are sold separately*.

Additionally has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In addition; also.** (кроме того, также)

1. The job pays well. *Additionally*, it offers good health insurance.
2. *Additionally*, all guests will receive a free breakfast.
3. You'll need a passport; *additionally*, some countries require a visa.

**2. As an extra; on top of what was already done or given.** (дополнительно)

4. Some patients were *additionally* treated with antibiotics.
5. Clearance items are *additionally* discounted at checkout.

### besides

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈsaɪdz/ &middot; *bih-SYDZ*
**Русский:** кроме того, к тому же
**Type:** linking — adding another reason
**Position:** front or end — front for another reason (*Besides, …*), end for *as well* (*and more besides*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** beside + -s
**Contrast:** *Besides* (кроме того) is not *beside* (рядом с): *Sit beside me*, but *Besides, it's too late*. As a preposition, *besides* means *in addition to* and *except* means *not including*: *Who came besides Tom?* (Tom came too) vs *Everyone came except Tom* (Tom did not). Russian *кроме* covers both, so learners confuse them. The adverb takes no object and usually adds a casual, often decisive extra reason.

Besides has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Also, and more importantly; used to add another reason.** (к тому же, да и, кроме того)

1. I don't feel like going out. *Besides*, it's supposed to rain.
2. We can't afford a new car, and *besides*, the old one still runs fine.
3. Don't worry about the money. *Besides*, you'd do the same for me.

**2. In addition; as well.** (помимо этого, ещё)

4. She speaks French and Spanish and a little Italian *besides*.
5. He lost his job, his car, and a lot more *besides*.

### similarly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪmələrli/ &middot; *SIM-uh-lur-lee*
**Русский:** аналогично, точно так же
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** front — as a link (*Similarly, …*); in the manner sense before an adjective or participle (*similarly priced*) or at the end (*answered similarly*)
**Comparison:** more/most — only in the manner sense (*more similarly*, *most similarly*); the linking sense is not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause; an adjective, participle or verb
**Form:** -ly from *similar*
**Contrast:** At the front, *similarly* links a second case that works the same way as the first; before an adjective or participle it means *in a similar way* (*similarly dressed*). Russian colloquial *Аналогично!* as a reply (= me too, same here) is not *Similarly!* in English: say *Me too*, *Same here* or, to return a wish, *You too*.

Similarly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In the same way; used to show that a second point is like the first.** (аналогично, точно так же)

1. Children need structure. *Similarly*, pets do better with a routine.
2. Home prices rose sharply in Texas. *Similarly*, Arizona saw big increases.
3. Doctors must report every case to the state. *Similarly*, schools must tell parents about any outbreak.

**2. In a similar manner; almost the same.** (схожим образом, одинаково)

4. The two brothers were *similarly* dressed in jeans and white shirts.
5. The two phones are *similarly* priced.
6. Identical twins raised apart often behave more *similarly* than ordinary siblings.

### therefore

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðɛrfɔːr/ &middot; *THAIR-for*
**Русский:** поэтому, следовательно
**Type:** linking — result or conclusion
**Position:** mid — after *be* or the first auxiliary (*It is therefore…*); also front after a period, a semicolon or *and*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause or a verb phrase
**Form:** there + fore, written as one word
**Contrast:** *Therefore* is formal; in conversation Americans say *so*. It is an adverb, not a conjunction, so *He was sick, therefore he stayed home* is a comma splice: write *He was sick; therefore, he stayed home* or *He was sick, so he stayed home*. Russian *поэтому* is neutral and follows a comma freely; its everyday English match is *so*, and *therefore* is the written one.

For that reason; as a result.

1. The road is closed. *Therefore*, we'll have to take the long way.
2. She is under 18 and *therefore* cannot sign the contract.
3. The data is incomplete; the results are *therefore* unreliable.

### thus

**Pronunciation:** /ðʌs/ &middot; *THUS*
**Русский:** таким образом; так
**Type:** linking; also manner and degree
**Position:** front — also mid (*and thus avoided*), before an *-ing* phrase (*…, thus saving time*), and end in the *in this way* sense (*began thus*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a clause, a verb phrase or an *-ing* phrase; *far* in *thus far*
**Contrast:** *Thus* is formal and written; in speech Americans say *so* or *like this*. A common written pattern is *thus* + *-ing*: *The new road cuts the trip in half, thus saving drivers time*, with no second subject. *Thusly* exists but is widely considered nonstandard; use *thus*. Like *therefore*, it does not join two full sentences with just a comma.

Thus has 3 distinct senses.

**1. As a result; therefore.** (таким образом, следовательно)

1. The company cut costs and *thus* avoided layoffs.
2. Oil prices went up; *thus*, shipping became more expensive.
3. The bridge was repaired, *thus* reopening the main route into town.

**2. In this way; like this.** (так, таким образом)

4. The letter began *thus*: 'Dear friends, I have bad news.'
5. Arranged *thus*, the chairs leave room for a wheelchair.

**3. To this point; used mainly in *thus far*.** (до сих пор, пока что)

6. *Thus far*, no one has claimed the prize.
7. The project has gone well *thus far*.

### hence

**Pronunciation:** /hɛns/ &middot; *HENS*
**Русский:** поэтому, отсюда
**Type:** linking; also time
**Position:** front — of its clause or verbless phrase (*hence the name*); after its word in the time sense (*two years hence*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a clause or a noun phrase (*hence the delay*); a time expression
**Contrast:** *Hence* often takes no verb at all: *The bus broke down, hence the delay*. It is formal; in speech say *that's why*. It gives the result, not the reason, so it does not mean *since* or *because*. *From hence* is redundant, because *hence* already contains *from*. The old sense *from this place* (*Get thee hence*) survives only in old texts.

Hence has 2 distinct senses.

**1. For this reason; that is why.** (поэтому, отсюда)

1. The town sits on a hot spring; *hence* its name, Hot Springs.
2. The flight was canceled, *hence* the long lines at the desk.
3. The survey used a small sample and is *hence* unreliable.

**2. From now; later than the present by the stated time.** (через (такое-то время), книжн.)

4. Five years *hence*, few people will remember this debate.
5. What will this city look like a century *hence*?

### consequently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːnsəkwɛntli/ &middot; *KAHN-suh-kwent-lee*
**Русский:** следовательно, в результате
**Type:** linking — result
**Position:** front — also mid (*was consequently delayed*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause or a verb phrase
**Form:** -ly from *consequent*
**Contrast:** *Consequently* (as a result) is easy to confuse with *subsequently* (later): *He was injured and consequently missed the game* gives a cause; *He was injured and subsequently retired* only gives an order. It is an adverb, not a conjunction, so it needs a semicolon or a period before it, not a comma alone. In speech *so* or *as a result* is more natural.

As a result; because of that.

1. He missed the bus and *consequently* was late for work.
2. The factory closed. *Consequently*, hundreds of people lost their jobs.
3. Demand has grown; prices have *consequently* gone up.

### accordingly

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkɔːrdɪŋli/ &middot; *uh-KOR-ding-lee*
**Русский:** соответственно; поэтому
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** front or end — end for *in a suitable way* (*act accordingly*), front for *therefore*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb phrase; a whole clause
**Form:** -ly from *according*
**Contrast:** *Accordingly* is not *according to*: *according to the report* (по данным) is a preposition, while *accordingly* stands alone and means *in a way that fits* or *therefore*. Russian *соответственно* also means *respectively*, which English says only with *respectively*: *Tom and Ann are 10 and 12, respectively*, never *accordingly*.

Accordingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a way that suits what was just said; appropriately.** (соответственно, как следует)

1. The dress code is formal, so please dress *accordingly*.
2. We'll look at the results and adjust the plan *accordingly*.
3. You're an adult now, so act *accordingly*.

**2. For that reason; therefore.** (поэтому, следовательно)

4. The store's lease ends in June. *Accordingly*, everything is 50 percent off.
5. The storm is expected to hit tonight; *accordingly*, schools will close early.

### subsequently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌbsəkwəntli/ &middot; *SUB-suh-kwuhnt-lee*
**Русский:** впоследствии, затем
**Type:** linking; also time
**Position:** mid — before the main verb (*was subsequently arrested*); also front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb phrase or a whole clause
**Form:** -ly from *subsequent*
**Contrast:** *Subsequently* means *later, after that*; it does not state a cause, which is *consequently*. Russian *последовательно* (consistently, one after another) is *consistently* or *in sequence*, not *subsequently*. It is formal; in speech say *later* or *after that*. Before a noun, use *after* or the formal *subsequent to*.

Afterward; at a later time.

1. The suspect was *subsequently* arrested in Ohio.
2. She joined the company in 2015 and *subsequently* became its CEO.
3. The first test failed. *Subsequently*, the team redesigned the engine.

### namely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪmli/ &middot; *NAYM-lee*
**Русский:** а именно
**Type:** linking — naming exactly what was just mentioned
**Position:** front — of the words it introduces, usually after a comma (*two cities, namely Boston and Denver*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a noun phrase, list or clause that specifies what came before
**Form:** -ly from *name*
**Contrast:** *Namely* only introduces the exact items or details you just referred to. Russian *именно* in *именно так* or *именно он* means *exactly* or *precisely*, not *namely*: *That's exactly what I meant*, *He's exactly the person we need*. Compare *that is* (*i.e.*), which restates rather than names the items.

That is to say; used to name the specific people or things just referred to.

1. Two of our best players, *namely* Jones and Rivera, are out with injuries.
2. We need to fix the biggest problem, *namely* the lack of funding.
3. The job requires two things, *namely* patience and a good sense of humor.

### anyway

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛniweɪ/ &middot; *EN-ee-way*
**Русский:** всё равно; ну ладно; в любом случае
**Type:** linking — conceding, dismissing or returning to the point
**Position:** front or end — front to change or return to a topic (*Anyway, …*), end for *despite that* (*We went anyway*) and in questions (*Who are you, anyway?*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** any + way, written as one word
**Contrast:** *Anyway* (всё равно, в любом случае) is not *any way* (любой способ): *Is there any way to fix it?* *Anyways* is common in casual American speech but is nonstandard; do not write it. *Anyway* is informal; in formal writing prefer *nevertheless* or *in any case*.

Anyway has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Despite that; all the same.** (всё равно)

1. The tickets were expensive, but we bought them *anyway*.
2. I told him not to call, but he called *anyway*.
3. It probably won't work, but let's try *anyway*.

**2. Used to change the subject, return to the main point, or end a conversation.** (ну ладно, так вот, в общем)

4. *Anyway*, what were we talking about?
5. So that's what happened. *Anyway*, I should get going.

**3. In any case; used to add a fact that makes what was just said matter less.** (да и вообще, всё равно)

6. Don't buy me a gift. I don't need anything *anyway*.
7. It doesn't matter if we're late; the movie always starts late *anyway*.

**4. Used at the end of a question to ask what is really going on, often with impatience.** (вообще, всё-таки)

8. Who are you, *anyway*?
9. What's the point of all this, *anyway*?
10. Why do you care what he thinks *anyway*?

**5. At least; used to correct or limit what was just said.** (по крайней мере, во всяком случае)

11. She's coming tomorrow. That's what she said, *anyway*.
12. No one noticed, or no one said anything, *anyway*.

### anyhow

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛnihaʊ/ &middot; *EN-ee-how*
**Русский:** всё равно; ну ладно; кое-как
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** front or end — front to change the topic, end for *despite that* or *carelessly*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause; a verb (*piled anyhow*)
**Form:** any + how, written as one word
**Contrast:** In the linking senses *anyhow* means the same as *anyway* but is less common and sounds more casual or old-fashioned. The *carelessly* sense (*clothes thrown anyhow*) is mainly British and is met in American reading rather than speech. It is not *somehow* (как-нибудь, каким-то образом): *We'll finish it somehow*.

Anyhow has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Despite that; anyway.** (всё равно)

1. It was raining, but we went for a walk *anyhow*.
2. I knew he'd say no, but I asked him *anyhow*.

**2. Used to change the subject or return to the main point.** (ну ладно, так вот)

3. *Anyhow*, enough about me. How's your new job?
4. *Anyhow*, the point is that we need more time.

**3. Carelessly; without any order.** (кое-как, как попало)

5. Books were piled *anyhow* on the shelves.
6. He tossed his clothes on the chair *anyhow* and went to bed.

### incidentally

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɪnsəˈdɛntəli/ &middot; *in-suh-DEN-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** кстати, между прочим
**Type:** linking; also manner
**Position:** front — as *by the way*, or mid between commas; mid before the verb in the *as a side effect* sense
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause; a verb or participle
**Form:** -ly from *incidental*
**Contrast:** *Incidentally* (кстати) is not *accidentally* (случайно, нечаянно): *I accidentally deleted the file*, never *incidentally*. Russian *кстати* also means *at the right moment* (*Ты пришёл кстати*), which English says with *at just the right time*, not *incidentally*. In conversation *by the way* is more common.

Incidentally has 2 distinct senses.

**1. By the way; used to add something that is not the main topic.** (кстати, между прочим)

1. *Incidentally*, have you seen my keys anywhere?
2. The new manager is, *incidentally*, an old friend of my brother's.
3. I saw Lisa yesterday. *Incidentally*, she's moving to Chicago.

**2. As a minor or unplanned side effect; not as the main purpose.** (попутно, заодно, как побочный результат)

4. The turtles were caught *incidentally* by boats fishing for shrimp.
5. The study was about sleep, but it *incidentally* revealed a link to diet.

### alternatively

**Pronunciation:** /ɔːlˈtɜːrnətɪvli/ &middot; *awl-TUR-nuh-tiv-lee*
**Русский:** как вариант, или же
**Type:** linking — offering another option
**Position:** front — also mid after *or* (*or, alternatively, …*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Form:** -ly from *alternative*
**Contrast:** *Alternatively* (или же, как вариант) is not *alternately* (по очереди, попеременно): *They alternately laughed and cried*. In speech plain *or* is more natural: *We could take a cab, or we could walk*.

As another possibility; used to suggest a second choice.

1. You can pay online. *Alternatively*, you can pay in person at the office.
2. We could drive; *alternatively*, we could take the train.
3. Call the help desk or, *alternatively*, send us an email.

### regardless

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈɡɑːrdləs/ &middot; *rih-GAHRD-luhs*
**Русский:** несмотря ни на что, всё равно
**Type:** linking — despite what was just said
**Position:** end — also front with a comma in informal writing
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb phrase or a whole clause
**Form:** regard + -less
**Contrast:** Alone, *regardless* is an adverb meaning *anyway*: *We went regardless*. With an object it is the preposition *regardless of*: *regardless of the weather*, *regardless of what he says*. *Irregardless* is heard in American speech but is widely treated as nonstandard; use *regardless*.

Despite everything; anyway.

1. The weather was terrible, but we held the wedding outdoors *regardless*.
2. I know it's risky. I'm going to do it *regardless*.
3. Some people will complain *regardless*.
