Adverbs of frequency and duration#

How often and how long? — always, usually, sometimes, never, daily, twice, forever, briefly.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Indefinite frequency adverbs sit on a scale from always to never and take mid-position: after be, before the main verb, after the first auxiliary. Definite frequency adverbs (daily, weekly, twice) go at the end instead. Duration adverbs answer how long?. Getting the position right matters more here than anywhere else in the dictionary, because I go always is the error every teacher hears.

always#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːlweɪz/ · AWL-wayz
Русский
всегда
Type
frequency — the top of the scale; also duration (I'll always remember)
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; front only in commands (Always lock the door)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
old compound of all + way with adverbial -s
Contrast
Position: I always walk to work, not I walk always to work; after be it follows the verb: She is always late. Not always is 'не всегда' (He's not always right); always not is wrong — say never. With a continuous tense it turns into a complaint: He's always complaining (вечно жалуется), not a neutral habit. 'Всегда' reaching up to now takes the present perfect: I've always lived here, not I always live here since childhood.

Always has 4 distinct senses.

1. At all times; on every occasion. (всегда)

  1. She always takes the bus to work.
  2. The store is always busy on Saturdays.
  3. Do you always eat lunch at your desk?

2. For all time, as far back as one remembers or into the future. (всегда, вечно)

  1. I'll always remember that trip.
  2. I've always wanted to see the Grand Canyon.
  3. No matter what happens, we'll always be friends.

3. Again and again, in an annoying way — with a continuous tense. (вечно, постоянно)

  1. He's always borrowing money and never paying it back.
  2. Why are you always interrupting me?

4. As a possible choice if nothing else works — usually with can or could. (всегда можно, в крайнем случае)

  1. If you miss the bus, you can always call a cab.
  2. We can always try again tomorrow.
  3. Too expensive? Well, you could always rent one.

usually#

Pronunciation
/ˈjuːʒuəli/ · YOO-zhoo-uh-lee
Русский
обычно
Type
frequency — high on the scale, just below always
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; also front or end for emphasis
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
-ly from usual
Contrast
A habit that has stopped is used to, not usually + past: I used to walk to school (раньше ходил), while I usually walked only describes the typical case back then. 'Как обычно' is as usual, never as usually. Do not mix it up with be used to (привыкнуть): I'm used to the noise.

In most cases; as a rule.

  1. I usually get up at seven.
  2. Traffic is usually light on Sundays.
  3. Usually, the meeting ends by noon.

normally#

Pronunciation
/ˈnɔːrməli/ · NOR-muh-lee
Русский
обычно; нормально
Type
frequency; also manner
Position
mid — also front in the sense 'usually'; end in the sense 'in a normal way'
Comparison
not gradable — in the sense 'usually'; more normally is possible in the sense 'in a normal way'
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
-ly from normal
Contrast
Russian нормально meaning 'fine, OK' is not normally: How are you? — Fine (or OK), not Normally; Everything's fine, not Everything is normally. In American English normally means 'обычно' far more often than 'нормально'.

Normally has 2 distinct senses.

1. Usually; under ordinary conditions. (обычно)

  1. I normally walk to work, but it's raining today.
  2. The drive normally takes about an hour.
  3. Normally, we'd be closed by now.

2. In a normal way; as expected, with nothing wrong. (нормально, как положено)

  1. The patient is breathing normally again.
  2. Just act normally and nobody will notice.
  3. Is the engine running normally now?

generally#

Pronunciation
/ˈdʒɛnərəli/ · JEN-ur-uh-lee
Русский
обычно; в целом
Type
frequency; also viewpoint (generally speaking)
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be; also front, especially in generally speaking
Comparison
not gradable — more generally means 'in broader terms'
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, participles and whole clauses
Form
-ly from general
Contrast
Generally is 'обычно' or 'в целом', not the emphatic 'вообще' of negatives and questions: Я вообще не пью is I don't drink at all; А ты вообще кто? is Who are you, anyway? The set phrase generally speaking is 'вообще говоря'.

Generally has 3 distinct senses.

1. Usually; in most cases. (обычно, как правило)

  1. I generally skip breakfast.
  2. Flights are generally cheaper on Tuesdays.
  3. The weather here is generally mild in the fall.

2. By most people; widely. (повсеместно, широко, всеми)

  1. It is generally accepted that smoking causes cancer.
  2. The new mayor is generally well liked.
  3. The drug is not yet generally available.

3. In broad terms, without going into details. (в целом, в общем)

  1. Generally speaking, the reviews were positive.
  2. She talked about the economy generally, not about any one company.

frequently#

Pronunciation
/ˈfriːkwəntli/ · FREE-kwuhnt-lee
Русский
часто
Type
frequency — high on the scale
Position
mid or end
Comparison
more/most — more frequently, most frequently
Modifies
verbs and participles (frequently asked)
Form
-ly from frequent
Contrast
Frequently is more formal than often; the everyday question is How often…?, not How frequently…? It sits comfortably at the end: It happens frequently.

Often; many times.

  1. He travels frequently for work.
  2. Check the list of frequently asked questions first.
  3. Buses run more frequently during rush hour.

often#

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːfən/ · AW-fuhn
Русский
часто
Type
frequency — high on the scale
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary, before the main verb; also end, especially with very, quite or too
Comparison
more/most — more often, most often; oftener is rare
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Contrast
Position: I often go there, not I go often there; at the end it is most natural with a partner: We don't eat out very often. The t is normally silent (AW-fuhn), though AWF-tuhn is also heard. Every so often means 'время от времени' (not often), and more often than not means 'обычно'.

Many times; in many cases.

  1. We often eat dinner on the porch in the summer.
  2. How often do you go to the gym?
  3. I wish we could visit more often.

sometimes#

Pronunciation
/ˈsʌmtaɪmz/ · SUHM-tymz
Русский
иногда
Type
frequency — the middle of the scale
Position
any position — front, mid and end are all natural
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
sometime + adverbial -s
Contrast
Three different words: sometimes (иногда — I sometimes work late), sometime (когда-нибудь, в какой-то момент — Let's have lunch sometime) and some time (некоторое время — It took some time).

On some occasions, but not always.

  1. Sometimes I just need a quiet day at home.
  2. My sister sometimes works from home.
  3. The subway gets really crowded sometimes.

occasionally#

Pronunciation
/əˈkeɪʒənəli/ · uh-KAY-zhuh-nuh-lee
Русский
иногда, время от времени
Type
frequency — below sometimes on the scale
Position
mid or end — also front
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
-ly from occasional
Contrast
It means 'now and then', not 'by chance': 'случайно' is by chance or accidentally. Spelled with double c and single s. It suggests fewer times than sometimes — roughly once in a while.

Now and then; not often.

  1. We occasionally go out for Mexican food.
  2. I run into him at the gym occasionally.
  3. The trail is occasionally closed after heavy rain.

seldom#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɛldəm/ · SEL-duhm
Русский
редко
Type
frequency; also negative
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (Seldom do we…)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Contrast
It is already negative, so no not: I seldom go, not I don't seldom go; the question tag is positive: He seldom calls, does he? Fronted for emphasis, it inverts: Seldom have I seen such a mess, not Seldom I have seen. In everyday American speech rarely or not often is more usual; seldom sounds formal or bookish.

Not often; almost never.

  1. We seldom see snow this far south.
  2. He seldom talks about his time in the Army.
  3. Seldom have I heard such a moving speech.

rarely#

Pronunciation
/ˈrɛrli/ · RAIR-lee
Русский
редко
Type
frequency; also negative
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (Rarely does it…)
Comparison
more/most — more rarely, most rarely
Modifies
verbs, participles and whole clauses
Form
-ly from rare
Contrast
Already negative, so no not: I rarely eat meat; the question tag is positive: She rarely complains, does she? Fronted, it inverts: Rarely does it snow here, not Rarely it snows here. Rarely if ever and hardly ever are standard; rarely ever is informal.

Not often; hardly ever.

  1. I rarely watch TV anymore.
  2. She is rarely late for work.
  3. Rarely does a rookie play this well.

never#

Pronunciation
/ˈnɛvər/ · NEV-ur
Русский
никогда
Type
frequency — the bottom of the scale; also negative
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary; front only with inversion (Never have I…) or in commands (Never give up)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
old ne 'not' + ever — negative in itself
Contrast
English allows one negative: I never eat meat, not I don't never eat meat (Russian никогда не doubles it). Fronted, it inverts: Never have I felt so tired. For experience use the present perfect: I've never been to Chicago. In questions use ever: Have you ever…?, not Have you never…? unless you are surprised. Informal I never took it is about one occasion and means 'I didn't take it'.

Never has 2 distinct senses.

1. At no time; not once. (никогда)

  1. I've never been to Alaska.
  2. She never drinks coffee after lunch.
  3. Never have I felt so welcome.

2. Not, said with emphasis — often about a single occasion. (так и не, вовсе не)

  1. He never even said goodbye.
  2. I never touched your phone, I swear.
  3. The package never arrived.

ever#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛvər/ · EV-ur
Русский
когда-нибудь, когда-либо
Type
frequency; also time and degree
Position
mid — after the subject in questions (Have you ever…?) and after the negative word (nobody ever); after its word after a superlative (the best ever); before its word in ever higher and ever so
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, superlatives, comparatives, so and whole clauses
Contrast
Not in a plain affirmative statement: 'я когда-то был в Париже' is I've been to Paris or I was once in Paris, never I have ever been to Paris. Use it in questions (Have you ever…?), negatives (nobody ever, hardly ever), conditions (if you ever…) and after superlatives (the best ever). 'Как никогда' after a comparative is than ever: I need you more than ever, not like never. Ever so (ever so slightly) is informal and more British, but common in American writing. Ever since is 'с тех пор'; forever is written as one word in American English.

Ever has 6 distinct senses.

1. At any time — in questions, negatives and conditions. (когда-нибудь, когда-либо)

  1. Have you ever tried sushi?
  2. Nobody ever calls the landline.
  3. If you're ever in Denver, look us up.

2. Of all time — after a superlative, after first or only, and in than ever. (когда-либо, за всю историю; как никогда)

  1. This is the best pizza I've ever had.
  2. She was the first woman ever to run the company.
  3. Gas prices are higher than ever this summer.

3. Always; all the time — in fixed phrases such as ever since, ever after and as ever. (всегда, неизменно; с тех пор)

  1. He's been sad ever since his dog died.
  2. And they lived happily ever after.
  3. She was, as ever, the last one to leave.

4. More and more — before a comparative. (всё (больше, выше))

  1. Prices keep climbing to ever higher levels.
  2. The suburbs are spreading ever farther from the city.

5. Used for emphasis — in informal American exclamations with inversion, and after how or why in questions. (ну и, ещё как; же)

  1. Boy, was she ever mad!
  2. Did we ever have fun at that concert!
  3. How can I ever thank you?

6. Very; in the smallest or gentlest way — in ever so before an adverb or adjective. (очень, совсем (чуть-чуть))

  1. She opened the door ever so slightly.
  2. He lifted the sleeping baby ever so gently.

daily#

Pronunciation
/ˈdeɪli/ · DAY-lee
Русский
ежедневно
Type
frequency — definite
Position
end — not mid (The paper comes out daily)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
-ly from day; the same form as the adjective daily
Contrast
The same spelling is an adjective (a daily newspaper) and a noun (a daily — ежедневная газета); only 'every day' after a verb is the adverb. Keep it at the end: We exercise daily, not We daily exercise. Also do not confuse the phrase every day (каждый день) with the adjective everyday (повседневный).

Every day; once a day.

  1. The museum is open daily from 9 to 5.
  2. Take this pill twice daily with food.
  3. The list of volunteers is growing daily.

hourly#

Contrast
The h is silent, so the adjective takes an: an hourly bus, an hourly wage. American hourly workers are paid by the hour, as opposed to salaried ones.

Hourly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Once an hour; every hour. (ежечасно, каждый час)

  2. The nurse checks his temperature hourly.

2. By the hour; for each hour worked. (почасово)

  1. Most of the store staff are paid hourly.
  2. Lawyers usually bill hourly.

Every night or every evening.

twice#

Pronunciation
/twaɪs/ · TWYS
Русский
дважды, два раза
Type
frequency — definite; also degree (twice as big)
Position
end — before its word in twice as … as
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; as + adjective or adverb
Form
two + old adverbial -ce, like once; the series goes once, twice, then three times
Contrast
'В два раза больше' is twice as big (as) or twice the size (of); two times bigger is heard but criticized. Frequency takes no preposition: twice a week, not twice in a week (unless you mean 'within one week'). Thrice is archaic — say three times.

Twice has 2 distinct senses.

1. Two times; on two occasions. (дважды, два раза)

  1. I've been to Chicago twice.
  2. Brush your teeth twice a day.
  3. The phone rang twice and then stopped.

2. Two times as much — in comparisons. (вдвое, в два раза)

  1. Their house is twice as big as ours.
  2. The repairs took twice as long as we planned.

again#

Pronunciation
/əˈɡɛn/ · uh-GEN
Русский
снова, опять, ещё раз
Type
frequency; also linking
Position
end — mid in formal writing; front in Again, … and Then again, …
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Contrast
English again is neutral; Russian опять can sound annoyed, and English shows that with yet again or Not again! What's his name again? is not 'ещё раз' in the sense of doing something over — it means 'напомни, как его зовут'. 'Повторно' is again or a second time, not repeatedly (много раз). Most Americans say uh-GEN; uh-GAYN is also heard.

Again has 5 distinct senses.

1. One more time; once more. (снова, ещё раз, опять)

  1. Could you say that again?
  2. I failed the test, so I'll take it again in May.
  3. Oh no, it's raining again.

2. Back to an earlier place or condition. (снова, обратно)

  1. It's good to be home again.
  2. She's feeling well again after the surgery.

3. Used to ask for information that you were told but have forgotten. (напомни(те), ещё раз)

  1. What was your name again?
  2. Where are we meeting again?

4. Used to repeat or stress a point already made. (опять же, ещё раз)

  1. Again, I want to thank everyone for coming.
  2. Again, the problem is money, not time.

5. On the other hand — in then again and but then again. (с другой стороны)

  1. It's expensive. Then again, it'll last for years.
  2. I might go. But then again, I might not.

constantly#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɑːnstəntli/ · KAHN-stuhnt-lee
Русский
постоянно, всё время
Type
frequency; also duration
Position
mid or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies

Contrast
Constantly (постоянно, всё время) is not consistently (неизменно, стабильно): He's constantly late means he is late all the time; Her work is consistently good means it is reliably good. With a continuous tense it signals irritation: You're constantly checking your phone.

All the time, or so often that it seems like all the time.

  1. The baby cried constantly the first week.
  2. Technology is constantly changing.
  3. He's constantly checking his phone during dinner.

regularly#

Pronunciation
/ˈrɛɡjələrli/ · REG-yuh-lur-lee
Русский
регулярно
Type
frequency; also manner
Position
mid or end
Comparison
more/most — more regularly, most regularly
Modifies
verbs, participles and prices
Form
-ly from regular
Contrast
Regularly stresses a steady pattern, so it is not always often: I see him regularly suggests a routine (every Tuesday); I see him often just means many times. On American price tags regularly means 'at the usual price': regularly $50, now $35.

Regularly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Often and as a habit, or at fixed intervals. (регулярно)

  1. She exercises regularly.
  2. Change your passwords regularly.
  3. I wish the buses ran more regularly out here.

2. Evenly; in an even pattern or rhythm. (равномерно, ровно)

  1. The fence posts are regularly spaced, ten feet apart.
  2. His heart is beating regularly now.

3. At the usual price — in American stores and ads. (по обычной цене, обычно)

  1. These jeans are regularly $60, now on sale for $40.
  2. All regularly priced items are 20% off today.

continually#

Pronunciation
/kənˈtɪnjuəli/ · kuhn-TIN-yoo-uh-lee
Русский
постоянно, то и дело
Type
frequency; also duration
Position
mid or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and participles
Form
-ly from continual
Contrast
Careful writers split the pair: continually is 'again and again, with breaks' (The phone rang continually), continuously is 'with no break at all' (The engine ran continuously for ten hours). Everyday American usage also lets continually mean 'without stopping', so choose continuously when the absence of breaks matters.

Continually has 2 distinct senses.

1. Again and again over a period of time, often annoyingly. (постоянно, то и дело)

  1. He continually interrupts when other people are talking.
  2. The office printer continually jams.

2. Without stopping; all the time. (непрерывно, всё время)

  1. The city's population has grown continually since 1950.
  2. Our software is continually being improved.

/spəˈrædɪkli/ · spuh-RAD-ik-lee
Русский

Type
frequency
Position

Comparison

Modifies

Form

Contrast
Not the same as periodically: sporadically stresses irregular, scattered occurrences with no pattern, while periodically suggests intervals.

Now and then, irregularly and with no pattern.

  1. Fighting continued sporadically through the night.
  2. He attended class only sporadically.
  3. The Wi-Fi in this hotel works sporadically at best.

forever#

Pronunciation
/fərˈɛvər/ · fur-EV-ur
Русский
навсегда, вечно
Type
duration; also frequency (forever complaining)
Position
end — mid in be forever doing
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
compound of for + ever, written as one word in American English
Contrast
American English writes forever as one word in every sense; for ever is British. With a continuous tense it means 'constantly' and sounds annoyed: He's forever losing his keys (вечно теряет). Take forever is informal exaggeration, not literal. For good is a common informal synonym of 'навсегда'.

Forever has 3 distinct senses.

1. For all future time; permanently. (навсегда)

  1. I wish this summer could last forever.
  2. Once you post it online, it's there forever.
  3. The accident changed their lives forever.

2. For a very long time — an informal exaggeration. (целую вечность, очень долго)

  1. The line at the DMV took forever.
  2. I've known her forever.

3. Constantly; all the time — with a continuous tense. (вечно, постоянно)

  1. He's forever losing his glasses.
  2. My neighbors are forever complaining about something.

briefly#

Pronunciation
/ˈbriːfli/ · BREEF-lee
Русский
ненадолго; кратко
Type
duration; also manner
Position
mid or end — front in Briefly, … (in short)
Comparison

Modifies

Form

Contrast
Briefly is about length (ненадолго, кратко); shortly means 'soon' (вскоре): I'll speak briefly means the speech will be short, I'll speak shortly means it will start soon.

Briefly has 2 distinct senses.

1. For a short time. (ненадолго, на короткое время)

  1. She briefly worked as a waitress in college.
  2. The power went out briefly during the storm.
  3. We stopped briefly in Memphis.

2. In a few words; in short. (кратко, вкратце)

  1. Let me explain briefly how it works.
  2. Briefly, the answer is no.
  3. Could you put that more briefly?

It means 'with no end date fixed', not 'endlessly': a concert postponed indefinitely may still happen. 'Бесконечно' is endlessly or infinitely, and vague 'неопределённо' is vaguely.

For a period of time with no fixed end.

momentarily#

Pronunciation
/ˌmoʊmənˈtɛrəli/ · moh-muhn-TAIR-uh-lee
Русский
на мгновение; сейчас, через минуту
Type
duration; also time
Position
mid or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, participles and adjectives
Form
-ly from momentary
Contrast
US/UK split: in British English it means only 'for a moment'; in American English it very often means 'in a moment, very soon' — the flight attendant's We'll be landing momentarily. Context decides: past events and states (was momentarily confused) mean 'for a moment', future ones (will be here momentarily) mean 'soon'. American stress falls on the third syllable. False friend: Russian моментально (instantly) is instantly or right away, not momentarily.

Momentarily has 2 distinct senses.

1. For a moment; very briefly. (на мгновение, на миг)

  1. I was momentarily blinded by the headlights.
  2. She paused momentarily before answering.

2. In a moment; very soon — mainly American. (сейчас, через минуту, вот-вот)

  1. The doctor will be with you momentarily.
  2. We will be landing momentarily.

overnight#

Pronunciation
/ˌoʊvərˈnaɪt/ · oh-vur-NYT
Русский
за ночь, на ночь; в одночасье
Type
duration; also time
Position
end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
compound of over + night; the same form as the adjective overnight
Contrast
The adverb is stressed on -night (oh-vur-NYT); the adjective before a noun (an overnight bag, an overnight success) often moves the stress to the first syllable and belongs to the adjective dictionary. Stay overnight means 'остаться на ночь', not 'не спать всю ночь' (stay up all night).

Overnight has 2 distinct senses.

1. During the night, or for the whole night. (за ночь, на ночь)

  1. Let the dough rise overnight.
  2. Can I stay overnight at your place?
  3. It snowed overnight, and the roads are icy.

2. Very suddenly or quickly. (в одночасье, мгновенно)

  1. The video made her famous overnight.
  2. You won't learn a language overnight.

permanently#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɜːrmənəntli/ · PUR-muh-nuhnt-lee
Русский
навсегда; постоянно
Type
duration
Position
mid or end
Comparison
more/most — more permanently, most permanently
Modifies
verbs, participles and adjectives
Form
-ly from permanent
Contrast
Russian постоянно meaning 'all the time' is constantly or always, not permanently: He constantly calls me, not He permanently calls me. Permanently is about lasting: permanently closed (закрыто навсегда), live here permanently (жить здесь постоянно).

Permanently has 2 distinct senses.

1. For all time; in a way that will not change or cannot be undone. (навсегда, окончательно)

  1. The store is permanently closed.
  2. The fire permanently damaged the old church.
  3. Those files were permanently deleted.

2. On a lasting basis rather than for a limited time — about where one lives or works. (постоянно, на постоянной основе)

  1. He's now permanently based in our Denver office.
  2. She's permanently employed now, not on a contract.
  3. Is he in town permanently, or just visiting?

temporarily#

Pronunciation
/ˌtɛmpəˈrɛrəli/ · tem-puh-RAIR-uh-lee
Русский
временно
Type
duration
Position
mid or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, participles and adjectives
Form
-ly from temporary, with y changed to i
Contrast
American stress falls on the third syllable (tem-puh-RAIR-uh-lee); British English often stresses the first. Do not confuse it with temporally (во времени, 'relating to time').

For a limited time; not permanently.

  1. The road is temporarily closed for repairs.
  2. I'm temporarily staying with my brother.
  3. The website is temporarily unavailable.
