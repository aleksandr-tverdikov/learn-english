# The -ly form that means something else

> *hardly*, *lately*, *nearly*, *highly* — the *-ly* forms whose meaning drifted away from the flat adverb they look like.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Adding *-ly* to *hard* does not give a more formal way of saying *with effort* — it gives *hardly*, which means *almost not*. The same drift happened to *late*/*lately*, *near*/*nearly*, *high*/*highly* and a dozen more. These are among the most frequent errors in learner English, because the spelling promises a relationship the meaning no longer has. Every entry names its flat partner and says exactly how they differ.

---
### hardly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɑːrdli/ &middot; *HAHRD-lee*
**Русский:** едва, почти не; вряд ли
**Type:** degree — a downtoner with negative force; also stance and time (*hardly … when*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; front with inversion (*Hardly had…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, adverbs, nouns and quantifiers (*hardly anyone*, *hardly ever*)
**Form:** -ly from *hard*, but not its meaning
**Contrast:** *hard* (flat) = with effort or force: *She works hard* (много, усердно работает). *hardly* = almost not: *She hardly works* (почти не работает). *hardly* is already negative — *I can hardly see*, not *I can't hardly see* — and takes *any* and *ever*: *hardly anyone*, *hardly ever*, not *hardly nobody*. Fronted, it inverts and pairs with *when* (not *than*): *Hardly had we left when it started to rain*.

Hardly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Almost not; only a very small amount or degree.** (едва, почти не)

1. I could *hardly* hear her over the music.
2. There's *hardly* any coffee left.
3. We *hardly* ever see each other now that she lives in Denver.

**2. Certainly not — used to say that something is obviously untrue, unreasonable or unsuitable.** (вряд ли, едва ли, никак не)

4. It's *hardly* surprising that he quit.
5. This is *hardly* the time to talk about money.
6. "Did you enjoy the meeting?" "*Hardly*."

**3. Only just, a moment before something else happened — in *hardly … when*.** (едва (как), только… как)

7. We had *hardly* sat down when the lights went out.
8. *Hardly* had the game started when it began to pour.

### lately

**Pronunciation:** /ˈleɪtli/ &middot; *LAYT-lee*
**Русский:** в последнее время
**Type:** time — recently, over a period up to now
**Position:** front or end — usually at the end; mid in formal writing (*has lately been*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *late*, but it means *recently*
**Contrast:** *late* (flat) = after the usual or expected time: *She got up late* (поздно). *lately* = recently, over a stretch of time up to now: *She's been getting up late lately*. Because it covers a period up to now, *lately* goes with the present perfect — *I haven't seen him lately* — and not with one finished event: *I saw him recently*, not *I saw him lately*. Russian *поздно* is always *late*, never *lately*.

Recently; during the period of time up to now.

1. Have you talked to Mike *lately*?
2. I've been feeling really tired *lately*.
3. *Lately*, prices at our grocery store have gone way up.

### nearly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɪrli/ &middot; *NEER-lee*
**Русский:** почти, чуть не
**Type:** degree — almost, but not quite
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; before its word with adjectives, numbers and quantifiers (*nearly empty*, *nearly 300*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — though *very nearly* occurs
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers and quantifiers (*nearly all*, *nearly everyone*)
**Form:** -ly from *near*, but it means *almost*, not *close by*
**Contrast:** *near* (flat) = close in space or time: *Don't sit so near*; *The exam is getting near*. *nearly* = almost: *I nearly fell* (чуть не упал). *nearly the station* is wrong — *near the station*, where *near* is a preposition. *nearly* and *almost* swap freely with numbers and verbs, but only *almost* goes with *no*, *never* and *nobody*: *almost nobody came*, not *nearly nobody*. And *not nearly* (далеко не) has no *almost* version.

Nearly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Almost; not completely, or very close to happening.** (почти; чуть не)

1. It's *nearly* midnight — we should go.
2. I *nearly* missed my flight.
3. *Nearly* 40 percent of the students commute to campus.

**2. In *not nearly*: far from; much less than.** (далеко не, совсем не)

4. There aren't *nearly* enough chairs for everyone.
5. The sequel is *not nearly* as good as the original.

### highly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhaɪli/ &middot; *HY-lee*
**Русский:** весьма, крайне; высоко
**Type:** degree — very, to a high level; also manner (*speak highly of*)
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or participle (*highly unlikely*); mid before *recommend* (*I highly recommend it*); end after verbs of judging (*think highly of*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more highly*, *most highly*
**Modifies:** adjectives and past participles; verbs of judging (*think*, *speak*, *rate*, *regard*)
**Form:** -ly from *high*, but not its meaning of height
**Contrast:** *high* (flat) = at or to a high point in space: *fly high*, *jump high*, *aim high*. *highly* = very, or with a good opinion: *highly intelligent*, *think highly of her*. *The plane flew highly* and *jump highly* are wrong. *highly* keeps to fixed partners — *highly unlikely*, *highly recommended*, *highly qualified*, *highly paid* — and does not replace *very* with everyday adjectives: *very tall*, not *highly tall*.

Highly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Very; to a high degree.** (весьма, крайне, очень)

1. It's *highly* unlikely that the store is open on Thanksgiving.
2. She's a *highly* skilled surgeon.
3. I *highly* recommend the fish tacos.

**2. With approval; with a good opinion of someone or something.** (высоко, хорошо (отзываться, ценить))

4. Everyone at work speaks *highly* of her.
5. I don't think very *highly* of his plan.
6. The book was *highly* praised by critics.

**3. At a high level of pay, rank or importance.** (высоко (оплачиваемый, поставленный))

7. Doctors are *more highly* paid than teachers.
8. The story came from a *highly* placed source in the White House.

### deeply

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdiːpli/ &middot; *DEEP-lee*
**Русский:** глубоко, очень
**Type:** degree — very strongly, of feelings and beliefs; also manner
**Position:** mid or end — before a verb or participle (*deeply regret*, *deeply moved*), after the verb (*breathe deeply*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more deeply*, *most deeply*
**Modifies:** verbs and past participles of feeling and belief; verbs like *breathe*, *sleep*, *think*
**Form:** -ly from *deep*
**Contrast:** *deep* (flat) = far down or far into, mostly with verbs of movement and in fixed phrases: *dig deep*, *go deep into the woods*, *still waters run deep*, and in the comparative *dig deeper*. *deeply* = intensely: *deeply moved*, *deeply regret*; it also goes with *breathe*, *sleep* and *think*. *I deep regret* is wrong — *I deeply regret*. For a real hole, *They dug deep*, not *dug deeply*.

Deeply has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very much; strongly or seriously — of feelings and beliefs, and of states like *flawed*, *unpopular* and *in debt*.** (глубоко, очень, искренне; крайне)

1. We are *deeply* sorry for your loss.
2. The plan is *deeply* flawed.
3. Many Americans feel *deeply* about gun laws.

**2. Far down or far in; to a great depth — also of breathing, sleep and serious thought.** (глубоко; крепко (спать))

4. Close your eyes and breathe *deeply*.
5. A piece of glass cut *deeply* into his hand.
6. I haven't thought *deeply* about it yet.

### closely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkloʊsli/ &middot; *KLOHS-lee*
**Русский:** внимательно; тесно; близко
**Type:** manner — carefully; also degree (*closely related*)
**Position:** end — after the verb and object (*watch closely*); before its word with participles (*closely related*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more closely*, *most closely*
**Modifies:** verbs of watching, working and following; participles like *related*, *linked*, *guarded*
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *close* (near), said with /s/
**Contrast:** *close* (flat) = near: *Come close*, *Stay close to me*, *We live close to the school*. *closely* = carefully, or tightly connected: *Watch closely*, *closely related*. *Stay closely to me* is wrong. Both are said with /s/ like the adjective *close*, not /z/ like the verb *close* (закрывать).

Closely has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Carefully, paying close attention.** (внимательно, пристально, тщательно)

1. Listen *closely* — I'm only going to say this once.
2. Police are watching the situation *closely*.
3. The recipe is a *closely* guarded family secret.

**2. In a close relationship or with a strong connection; working together a lot.** (тесно)

4. We work *closely* with local schools.
5. Stress and poor sleep are *closely* linked.

**3. Near in space or time, with little distance or gap.** (близко, вплотную, плотно)

6. A black SUV was following *closely* behind us.
7. The houses on our street are *closely* packed together.
8. The storm was *closely* followed by a heat wave.

**4. Very nearly; with only a small difference.** (очень (похоже), почти в точности; с небольшим перевесом)

9. The copy *closely* resembles the original.
10. It was one of the *most closely* contested elections in the state's history.

### freely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfriːli/ &middot; *FREE-lee*
**Русский:** свободно; открыто; щедро
**Type:** manner — without limits or control
**Position:** end — after the verb and object; mid before *admit* and *give* (*freely admits*); before its word with participles and adjectives (*freely available*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more freely*, *most freely*
**Modifies:** verbs of movement, speech and giving; adjectives like *available*
**Form:** -ly from *free*, but without its *no cost* meaning
**Contrast:** *free* (flat) = without paying: *Kids under five ride free* (бесплатно). *freely* = without limits: *Kids can move freely around the museum* (свободно). Mixing them changes the meaning. And Russian *свободно говорить по-английски* is *speak English fluently*, not *freely* — *speak freely* means to say openly what you think.

Freely has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Without being controlled, stopped or limited.** (свободно, беспрепятственно)

1. Once the cast came off, I could move my arm *freely*.
2. The information is *freely* available online.
3. Traffic is moving *more freely* now that the accident has been cleared.

**2. Openly and honestly, without fear; willingly.** (открыто, откровенно; охотно)

4. You can speak *freely* — nobody here will repeat it.
5. He *freely* admits he made mistakes.

**3. Generously; in large amounts.** (щедро, обильно)

6. The wine flowed *freely* at the wedding.
7. They gave *freely* to the church and the food bank.

**4. Loosely, not exactly — of translating or adapting a text.** (вольно, нестрого)

8. The movie is *freely* adapted from a nineteenth-century novel.
9. The poem was *freely* translated from Spanish.

### widely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwaɪdli/ &middot; *WYD-lee*
**Русский:** широко, повсеместно; сильно
**Type:** degree — by many people, or by a lot; also place and manner
**Position:** before its word — before a participle (*widely used*); end after verbs (*vary widely*, *travel widely*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more widely*, *most widely*
**Modifies:** past participles (*used*, *known*, *spaced*); verbs like *vary*, *differ*, *travel*, *grin*
**Form:** -ly from *wide*
**Contrast:** *wide* (flat) = fully, to the full extent: *open wide*, *wide awake*, *wide open*, *far and wide*. *widely* = by many people, or a lot: *widely known*, *vary widely*. *Open your mouth widely* is wrong — *open wide*; *a wide known fact* is wrong — *a widely known fact*.

Widely has 4 distinct senses.

**1. By a lot of people or in a lot of places.** (широко, повсеместно)

1. It's *widely* believed that the policy failed.
2. The app is *widely* used by small businesses.
3. Spanish is the *most widely* spoken language in the U.S. after English.

**2. Over a large area; to many different places.** (много, повсюду (путешествовать, распространяться))

4. She has traveled *widely* in Asia.
5. The seeds are *widely* scattered by the wind.

**3. By a large amount; very differently.** (сильно, значительно)

6. Prices vary *widely* from store to store.
7. The two reports come to *widely* different conclusions.

**4. With a wide opening or a big gap — of smiling, and of things set far apart.** (широко; далеко (друг от друга))

8. He grinned *widely* when he saw his test score.
9. The trees are *widely* spaced so the grass gets plenty of sun.

### mostly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmoʊstli/ &middot; *MOHST-lee*
**Русский:** в основном; обычно
**Type:** degree — mainly, for the most part; also frequency
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be*; before its word (*mostly students*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, nouns and phrases (*mostly in the evening*)
**Form:** -ly from *most*, but without its superlative meaning
**Contrast:** *most* (flat) = more than anything else, and the superlative marker: *What I miss most is the food*; *the most expensive*. *mostly* = mainly: *The food was mostly vegetarian*. *What I miss mostly* and *the mostly expensive* are errors. Russian *больше всего* → *most*; *в основном* → *mostly*. Don't confuse it with *most of*: *Most of the guests were teachers* = *The guests were mostly teachers*.

Mostly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Mainly; for the most part.** (в основном, преимущественно)

1. The crowd was *mostly* college students.
2. The sauce is *mostly* butter and garlic.
3. I agree with you — *mostly*.

**2. Usually; most of the time.** (обычно, чаще всего)

4. We *mostly* eat dinner at home.
5. On weekends I *mostly* just sleep in.

### shortly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʃɔːrtli/ &middot; *SHORT-lee*
**Русский:** вскоре, скоро; резко
**Type:** time — soon; also manner
**Position:** mid or end — at the end (*see you shortly*) or after the first auxiliary (*will shortly arrive*); before *after* and *before* (*shortly after noon*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; the words *after* and *before*
**Form:** -ly from *short*, but it means *soon*, not *briefly*
**Contrast:** *short* (flat) = suddenly, or before the end: *stop short*, *cut the meeting short* (прервать), *fall short*, *run short of cash*. *shortly* = soon: *I'll call you shortly*. *cut shortly* is wrong. *shortly* is not *briefly*: *speak briefly* = говорить коротко, but *She'll speak shortly* = она скоро выступит, and only after a quote does it mean резко: *"No," she said shortly*. *shortly ago* is wrong — use *a short time ago* or *recently*.

Shortly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Soon; in a short time, or a short time before or after something.** (вскоре, скоро)

1. The doctor will be with you *shortly*.
2. *Shortly* after we moved to Chicago, I found a job.
3. The last train leaves *shortly* before midnight.

**2. In a short, impatient or unfriendly way.** (резко, отрывисто, сухо)

4. "I'm busy," she said *shortly*.
5. The clerk answered *shortly*, without looking up from her phone.

### fairly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɛrli/ &middot; *FAIR-lee*
**Русский:** довольно; честно, справедливо
**Type:** degree — moderately; also manner (*in a fair way*)
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or adverb (*fairly easy*); end after the verb in the *justly* sense (*treat people fairly*)
**Comparison:** more/most — only in the *justly* sense (*more fairly*, *most fairly*); the degree sense is not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs (degree); verbs (manner)
**Form:** -ly from *fair*; the two main meanings are unrelated
**Contrast:** *fair* (flat) = by the rules: *play fair*, *fight fair*. *fairly* means either that (*treat people fairly*) or *moderately* (*fairly good*) — position tells you which: right before an adjective it is degree. In American speech *fairly good* is a cool, limited compliment, weaker than *pretty good*; *quite good* is stronger in AmE than in BrE. *fairly* does not go before *too* or a comparative: *a bit too big*, *a little bigger*, not *fairly too big* or *fairly bigger*.

Fairly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. More than a little but less than very; to a moderate degree.** (довольно, достаточно)

1. The test was *fairly* easy.
2. I know him *fairly* well.
3. We live in a *fairly* quiet neighborhood.

**2. In a fair, honest or equal way; with good reason.** (честно, справедливо; по праву)

4. All employees must be treated *fairly*.
5. The work was divided *fairly* among the team.
6. It can *fairly* be said that she saved the company.

**3. Actually, almost literally — a literary intensifier before a strong verb.** (буквально, прямо-таки)

7. The whole house *fairly* shook when the train went by.
8. She was *fairly* bursting with pride.

### rightly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈraɪtli/ &middot; *RYT-lee*
**Русский:** справедливо, по праву; верно
**Type:** stance — with good reason; also manner
**Position:** mid — before the main verb or participle, after *be*; also in *and rightly so*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *right*
**Contrast:** *right* (flat) = correctly, after the verb: *You guessed right*, *spell it right*, *Nothing goes right*. *rightly* comes before the verb or participle and judges the action as justified or accurate: *You rightly guessed that…*. *right* also means *exactly*, *directly* and *immediately* (*right here*, *right away*) — *rightly* never does: *rightly now* is wrong. *I don't rightly know* is informal, heard mostly in the South and rural US.

Rightly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. With good reason; justly.** (справедливо, по праву, не зря)

1. Parents are *rightly* worried about screen time.
2. She was angry, and *rightly* so.
3. *Rightly* or wrongly, he blamed himself.

**2. Correctly; accurately.** (верно, правильно)

4. As you *rightly* pointed out, the numbers don't add up.
5. The doctor *rightly* suspected a food allergy.

**3. For sure, with certainty — informal, with a negative.** (толком, точно)

6. I don't *rightly* know where he went.
7. I can't *rightly* say when they'll be back.

### justly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌstli/ &middot; *JUHST-lee*
**Русский:** справедливо; заслуженно
**Type:** manner — fairly; also stance (*justly famous*)
**Position:** end — after the verb (*treated justly*); before its word with adjectives and participles (*justly famous*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more justly*, *most justly*
**Modifies:** verbs; adjectives like *famous*, *proud*, *celebrated*
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *just* (fair), not from the adverb *just*
**Contrast:** The flat *just* is unrelated in meaning: *just* = только что, только, как раз, просто. *justly* comes from the adjective *just* = справедливый. *He was just punished* = его только что наказали; *He was justly punished* = его наказали справедливо. *justly* is formal; everyday speech says *fairly* or *rightly*.

Justly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a fair way; according to what is morally right.** (справедливо)

1. A good judge treats everyone *justly*.
2. The land was *justly* divided among the heirs.
3. People want leaders who will govern *justly*.

**2. Deservedly; with good reason.** (по праву, заслуженно)

4. The restaurant is *justly* famous for its barbecue.
5. She is *justly* proud of her students.

### prettily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɪtəli/ &middot; *PRIT-uh-lee*
**Русский:** мило, изящно
**Type:** manner — in a pretty, charming way
**Position:** end — after the verb (*smiled prettily*); before its word with participles (*prettily decorated*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more prettily*, *most prettily*
**Modifies:** verbs; past participles like *decorated*, *arranged*
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *pretty*, with *y* changed to *i*
**Contrast:** *pretty* (flat) = fairly, quite — one of the most common degree adverbs in American speech: *pretty good*, *pretty fast* (довольно). *prettily* keeps the adjective's meaning, *charmingly* (мило). *She sings pretty well* = она поёт довольно хорошо; *She sings prettily* = она мило поёт. Before another adjective or an adverb, *pretty* almost always means *fairly*, not *attractive*: *pretty ugly* = довольно некрасивый.

In a pretty, charming or pleasantly neat way.

1. The little girl smiled *prettily* for the camera.
2. The cake was *prettily* decorated with fresh flowers.
3. She asked so *prettily* that nobody could say no.

### surely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʃʊrli/ &middot; *SHUUR-lee*
**Русский:** неужели; наверняка; верно
**Type:** stance — the speaker expects agreement or is surprised; also manner (*slowly but surely*)
**Position:** any position — front to ask for agreement (*Surely you…*), mid for *certainly* (*will surely win*), end in *slowly but surely*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** whole clauses; verbs
**Form:** -ly from *sure*; the flat *sure* is the everyday American adverb
**Contrast:** At the front of a sentence, *surely* is not a calm *certainly*: *Surely you know him?* shows surprise or a hope for agreement (неужели не знаешь? / ты же его знаешь?) — to open with a plain fact use *certainly* or *definitely*. Only in mid position does it mean наверняка: *The plan will surely fail*. In everyday American speech the flat *sure* does the rest: *Sure, I'll help* (конечно), *It sure is hot* (ну и жара). As a one-word yes, *Surely.* sounds old-fashioned — say *Sure.*

Surely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Used to show that you believe something is true and want agreement, or are surprised that it might not be.** (неужели; ведь, конечно же)

1. *Surely* you don't expect me to pay for that?
2. *Surely* someone at the front desk can help us.
3. "I lost the tickets." "*Surely* not!"

**2. Certainly; without any doubt.** (наверняка, несомненно)

4. Without more funding, the program will *surely* fail.
5. This is *surely* one of the best pizzas in New York.

**3. Steadily and without failing — mostly in *slowly but surely*.** (верно, уверенно)

6. *Slowly but surely*, the team climbed back into the game.
7. Her English is improving *slowly but surely*.

### scarcely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈskɛrsli/ &middot; *SKAIRS-lee*
**Русский:** едва, почти не; вряд ли
**Type:** degree — almost not, with negative force; also stance and time (*scarcely … when*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; front with inversion (*Scarcely had…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, numbers and quantifiers (*scarcely anyone*)
**Form:** -ly from *scarce*, but it does not mean *rare*
**Contrast:** *scarcely* already carries the negative: *I could scarcely breathe*, not *I couldn't scarcely breathe*; it takes *any* and *ever*: *scarcely anyone*, *scarcely ever*. Fronted, it inverts the subject and verb, and the pair is *scarcely … when* (not *than*): *Scarcely had she sat down when…*. It does not mean *in short supply* — that is the adjective *scarce*: *Jobs are scarce* (рабочих мест мало), not *Jobs are scarcely*. More formal than *hardly*.

Scarcely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Almost not; only just.** (едва, почти не)

1. I could *scarcely* believe my eyes.
2. There was *scarcely* anyone on the beach.
3. He was *scarcely* twenty when he started his first company.

**2. Certainly not — used to say that something is obviously untrue, unreasonable or unsuitable.** (вряд ли, едва ли)

4. It's *scarcely* a secret that they don't get along.
5. A parking ticket is *scarcely* a reason to move to another state.

**3. Only just before something else happened — in *scarcely … when*.** (едва (как), только… как)

6. *Scarcely* had we arrived when it started to snow.
7. She had *scarcely* hung up when the phone rang again.

### evenly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈiːvənli/ &middot; *EE-vuhn-lee*
**Русский:** поровну, равномерно; ровно
**Type:** manner — equally or steadily
**Position:** end — after the verb and object; before its word with participles (*evenly spaced*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more evenly*, *most evenly*
**Modifies:** verbs of spreading, dividing, breathing and speaking; participles like *matched*, *spaced*
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *even* (level, equal), not from the adverb *even*
**Contrast:** *even* (flat) = даже: *She even paid for dinner*. *evenly* = поровну or ровно: *They split the check evenly*. Same spelling, unrelated meanings. The idiom *break even* (выйти в ноль) uses the flat form.

Evenly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. In equal amounts or parts; spread the same everywhere.** (поровну, равномерно)

1. We split the bill *evenly* between the four of us.
2. Spread the frosting *evenly* over the cake.
3. The two teams were *evenly* matched.

**2. In a calm, steady voice, without showing emotion.** (ровно, спокойно)

4. "Put the phone down," he said *evenly*.
5. She answered *evenly*, though she was clearly upset.

**3. Smoothly and regularly, at a steady rate.** (ровно, размеренно)

6. The baby was finally breathing *evenly*.
7. The ceiling fan turned slowly and *evenly*.

### lastly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlæstli/ &middot; *LAST-lee*
**Русский:** наконец, в заключение
**Type:** linking — introduces the last point in a list
**Position:** front — at the start of the sentence, usually with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *last*
**Contrast:** *last* (flat) = at the end, or the most recent time: *He finished last* (последним), *When did you last see her?* (в последний раз). *lastly* only introduces the final item of a list. Russian *наконец* has three English partners: *lastly* (the last point), *finally* (the last point, or after a long time) and *at last* (наконец-то, after waiting): *At last the bus came*, not *Lastly the bus came*. Don't confuse it with *lately* (в последнее время).

As the final point or item in a list.

1. *Lastly*, I'd like to thank my parents.
2. First, preheat the oven; next, mix the dry ingredients; and *lastly*, add the eggs.
3. *Lastly*, remember to sign and date the form.

### directly

**Pronunciation:** /dəˈrɛktli/ &middot; *duh-REKT-lee*
**Русский:** напрямую, непосредственно; прямо; сразу
**Type:** manner — without anything in between; also place and time
**Position:** end — after the verb and object; mid before some verbs (*directly affect*); before its word with prepositions and participles (*directly above*, *directly affected*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more directly*, *most directly*
**Modifies:** verbs; prepositions of place and time (*directly behind*, *directly after*); participles
**Form:** -ly from *direct*
**Contrast:** *direct* (flat) = straight, without stopping or a go-between: *fly direct to Denver*, *buy direct from the farm*. *directly* can say that too, but only *directly* means *exactly* or *immediately*: *directly opposite*, *directly after lunch* — not *direct opposite*. In the US South, *I'll be there directly* means *soon* (скоро), not *this second*. British English also uses *directly* as a conjunction (*Directly he left, we began* = as soon as); that use is rare in American English.

Directly has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Without anyone or anything in between; straight from one person or thing to another.** (напрямую, непосредственно)

1. You can buy tickets *directly* from the theater.
2. The new law will *directly* affect small businesses.
3. I need to speak *directly* to your manager.

**2. Exactly in a particular position, or straight toward something.** (прямо, точно)

4. The bank is *directly* across from the post office.
5. Don't look *directly* at the sun.
6. After school, go *directly* home.

**3. Honestly and clearly, without avoiding the subject.** (прямо, откровенно)

7. She asked me *directly* whether I was quitting.
8. Politicians rarely answer questions *directly*.

**4. Immediately; right after or right before something.** (сразу, тотчас)

9. Call me *directly* after the meeting.
10. The ceremony will be followed *directly* by a reception in the church hall.
11. The patient was taken *directly* into surgery.

**5. Soon; in a little while — informal, mostly Southern American speech.** (скоро, сейчас (приду))

12. Y'all go ahead — I'll be there *directly*.
13. Supper will be ready *directly*, so go wash up.

### sharply

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʃɑːrpli/ &middot; *SHAHRP-lee*
**Русский:** резко; чётко
**Type:** manner — suddenly, severely or clearly; also degree
**Position:** end — after the verb; mid before verbs of criticizing (*sharply criticized*); before its word with participles and adjectives (*sharply different*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more sharply*, *most sharply*
**Modifies:** verbs of change and speaking; participles and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *sharp*
**Contrast:** *sharp* (flat) = exactly at a time, or abruptly: *at six o'clock sharp* (ровно в шесть), *turn sharp left*, *Look sharp!* (живее!). *sharply* is the general adverb: *Prices fell sharply*. *at six sharply* and *prices rose sharp* are both errors.

Sharply has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Suddenly and by a large amount.** (резко)

1. Gas prices rose *sharply* this summer.
2. The road turns *sharply* to the left.
3. Home sales fell *more sharply* than expected.

**2. Severely or angrily; in a critical way.** (резко, строго)

4. The senator *sharply* criticized the plan.
5. "Sit down," the teacher said *sharply*.

**3. Clearly and distinctly, with obvious differences.** (чётко, резко)

6. Their views on taxes differ *sharply*.
7. The photo is *sharply* focused.

**4. Stylishly, in neat, well-fitting clothes.** (элегантно, щегольски)

8. He showed up *sharply* dressed in a navy suit.
9. She wore a *sharply* tailored blazer to the interview.

### largely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlɑːrdʒli/ &middot; *LAHRJ-lee*
**Русский:** в основном, в значительной степени
**Type:** degree — to a great extent
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be*; before its word (*largely because*, *largely ignored*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, participles and phrases (*largely due to*)
**Form:** -ly from *large*, but not about size
**Contrast:** False friend: *largely* has nothing to do with size and does not mean *widely* or *on a large scale* (широко, масштабно). It means *mostly* (в основном): *The movie was largely filmed in Georgia*. The flat *large* is an adverb only in fixed phrases: *loom large*, *writ large*, *by and large* (в целом).

Mostly; to a great extent.

1. The delay was *largely* due to bad weather.
2. His warnings were *largely* ignored.
3. The town's economy depends *largely* on tourism.

### firstly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɜːrstli/ &middot; *FURST-lee*
**Русский:** во-первых
**Type:** linking — introduces the first point in a list
**Position:** front — at the start of the sentence, usually with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *first*; *first* itself is the usual adverb
**Contrast:** American writers usually prefer *first* (*First, … Second, … Finally, …*); *firstly* sounds British or stiff to many Americans, though it is still met. *firstly* only opens a list of points. It never means *for the first time* or *before anything else*: *When I first came to Boston* (впервые), *Wash your hands first* (сначала), not *firstly*. *At first* (сначала, а потом всё изменилось) is different again.

As the first point in a list of reasons or ideas.

1. *Firstly*, the plan is too expensive; secondly, it won't work.
2. There are two problems: *firstly*, we're out of time, and secondly, we're out of money.
3. *Firstly*, I want to thank everyone for coming.

### fully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfʊli/ &middot; *FUU-lee*
**Русский:** полностью, вполне
**Type:** degree — completely
**Position:** mid or end — before the main verb or participle, after *be* (*fully understand*, *is fully aware*); end after verbs (*recover fully*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more fully*, *most fully*
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, participles and numbers (*fully half*)
**Form:** -ly from *full*, with one *l* dropped
**Contrast:** *full* (flat) = directly, straight, and in the set phrase *full well*: *The ball hit him full in the face*, *You know full well what I mean* (прекрасно знаешь). *fully* = completely: *fully charged*, not *full charged*. Before a number *fully* is not *completely* but *as many as*: *fully 40 percent* = целых 40 процентов.

Fully has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; entirely.** (полностью, вполне)

1. I'm *fully* aware of the risks.
2. The hotel is *fully* booked for the holiday weekend.
3. It took her six months to recover *fully*.

**2. In detail; thoroughly.** (подробно, обстоятельно)

4. The side effects are *fully* described on the label.
5. Could you explain that *more fully*?

**3. At least; no less than — before a number, for emphasis.** (целых, не менее)

6. *Fully* half the students failed the test.
7. The project took *fully* ten years to complete.

### cleanly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkliːnli/ &middot; *KLEEN-lee*
**Русский:** ровно, аккуратно; чисто
**Type:** manner — neatly, without mess or pollution
**Position:** end — after the verb and object
**Comparison:** more/most — *more cleanly*, *most cleanly*
**Modifies:** verbs of cutting, breaking, burning and moving
**Form:** -ly from *clean*
**Contrast:** *clean* (flat) = completely: *I clean forgot her birthday* (начисто забыл), *The bullet went clean through the door*; also *come clean* (признаться). *cleanly* = neatly, smoothly or without pollution: *The bone broke cleanly*. Pronunciation trap: the adverb is /ˈkliːnli/; the rare adjective *cleanly* (чистоплотный) is /ˈklɛnli/, like *cleanliness*.

Cleanly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Neatly and smoothly, leaving a straight, clear edge.** (ровно, аккуратно)

1. The bone broke *cleanly*, so it should heal fast.
2. A sharp knife slices the tomato *cleanly*.

**2. Without producing dirt, smoke or pollution.** (чисто, без загрязнения)

3. Natural gas burns *more cleanly* than coal.
4. The new buses run *cleanly* on electricity.

**3. Smoothly and without errors, problems or fouls.** (чисто, безупречно, без помех)

5. The shortstop fielded the ball *cleanly* and threw to first.
6. The update installed *cleanly* on every computer.

### clearly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈklɪrli/ &middot; *KLEER-lee*
**Русский:** ясно, чётко; очевидно
**Type:** stance — obviously; also manner (*speak clearly*, *think clearly*)
**Position:** any position — front or mid in the stance sense (*Clearly, …*; *He was clearly upset*); end in the manner senses (*speak clearly*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more clearly*, *most clearly*, in the manner senses only; the stance sense is not gradable
**Modifies:** whole clauses, adjectives and verbs (stance); verbs and participles (manner)
**Form:** -ly from *clear*
**Contrast:** *clear* (flat) = away, or all the way: *Stand clear of the doors*, *steer clear of him* (держись подальше), *The ball went clear over the fence*. *clearly* = in a clear way, or obviously: *speak clearly* (*loud and clear* is a fixed exception). Position changes the meaning: *He didn't explain clearly* = объяснил нечётко; *He clearly didn't explain* = очевидно, он не объяснил.

Clearly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Obviously; without any doubt.** (очевидно, явно)

1. *Clearly*, something went wrong.
2. He was *clearly* upset about the game.
3. She *clearly* didn't read my email.

**2. In a way that is easy to see, hear or understand.** (ясно, чётко, отчётливо)

4. Please speak *clearly* into the microphone.
5. The exits are *clearly* marked.
6. On a sunny day you can see the mountains *more clearly*.

**3. Sensibly and logically, without confusion.** (ясно, здраво, трезво (мыслить))

7. I was too tired to think *clearly*.
8. She has trouble thinking *clearly* under pressure.

### wrongly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrɔːŋli/ &middot; *RAWNG-lee*
**Русский:** неправильно, ошибочно; несправедливо
**Type:** manner — incorrectly or unfairly; also stance (*rightly or wrongly*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb or participle (*wrongly accused*); end only with a few verbs (*acted wrongly*), since after the verb the flat *wrong* is more usual
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and past participles
**Form:** -ly from *wrong*
**Contrast:** *wrong* (flat) comes after the verb in everyday speech: *You spelled it wrong*, *Everything went wrong*, *I guessed wrong*. *wrongly* comes before a verb or participle: *She was wrongly accused*, *He wrongly assumed…*. *went wrongly* and *a wrong accused man* are both errors.

Wrongly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Incorrectly; by mistake.** (неправильно, ошибочно)

1. He spent ten years in prison after being *wrongly* convicted.
2. Many people *wrongly* believe that bats are blind.
3. My name was *wrongly* spelled on the diploma.

**2. Unfairly, or in a morally wrong way.** (несправедливо, дурно)

4. She knew she had acted *wrongly*, and she apologized.
5. *Rightly or wrongly*, they decided to sell the farm.

### dearly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɪrli/ &middot; *DEER-lee*
**Русский:** нежно, очень; дорого
**Type:** degree — very much; also manner
**Position:** end — after the verb and object (*love her dearly*, *pay dearly*); mid with *would love* and *want* (*would dearly love*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more dearly*, *most dearly*
**Modifies:** verbs of loving and wishing; *pay* and *cost*
**Form:** -ly from *dear*
**Contrast:** *dear* (flat, old-fashioned) = at a high price: *buy cheap and sell dear*. *dearly* is the normal form: *pay dearly*, *cost dearly* — and it is usually about suffering, not money: *He paid dearly for his mistake* = дорого поплатился. *dearly* does not mean *expensively*: *an expensively dressed woman*, not *a dearly dressed woman*.

Dearly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. With deep love and affection.** (нежно, горячо (любить))

1. She loved her grandfather *dearly*.
2. We miss him *dearly*.
3. *Dearly* beloved, we are gathered here today to celebrate this couple.

**2. Very much — with *would love*, *wish*, *want* and similar verbs.** (очень, страстно)

4. I would *dearly* love to see Paris again.
5. He *dearly* wanted to make the team.

**3. At a high cost, with suffering or loss.** (дорого (заплатить, обойтись))

6. The team paid *dearly* for that mistake.
7. Ignoring the warning signs cost them *dearly*.

### flatly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈflætli/ &middot; *FLAT-lee*
**Русский:** наотрез, категорически; бесстрастно
**Type:** manner — firmly, or without emotion; also degree (*flatly refuse*)
**Position:** mid or end — before the main verb (*flatly refused*); end after verbs of saying (*said flatly*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of refusing, denying, contradicting and saying
**Form:** -ly from *flat*
**Contrast:** *flat* (flat adverb) = completely, exactly, or lying level: *flat broke* (без гроша), *in ten seconds flat* (ровно за десять секунд), *fall flat* (провалиться), *lie flat on your back*, *turn someone down flat*. *flatly* does the refusing and denying: *flatly refused*. *Lie flatly on the floor* is wrong — *lie flat*. In music, *sing flat* = петь ниже тона.

Flatly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Completely and firmly, with no room for discussion.** (наотрез, категорически)

1. The mayor *flatly* denied the rumors.
2. She *flatly* refused to apologize.
3. The two reports *flatly* contradict each other.

**2. Without emotion or interest, in a dull voice.** (бесстрастно, монотонно)

4. "We lost," he said *flatly*.
5. She read the list of names *flatly*, one by one.
