# Adverbs of time

> When? — *now*, *soon*, *already*, *yet*, *still*, *recently*, *ago*, and the words that place an event on the timeline.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Time adverbs locate an event relative to now or to another event. Several of them are tied to a tense: *already*, *yet* and *just* pull toward the present perfect in American and British English alike, although American speech also allows the simple past (*Did you eat yet?*). *Ago* is the odd one out — it follows its time phrase (*three days ago*) and cannot be used with the present perfect.

---
### now

**Pronunciation:** /naʊ/ &middot; *NOW*
**Русский:** сейчас, теперь
**Type:** time; also linking (a discourse marker: *Now, listen…*)
**Position:** any position — usually end; front for contrast or as a discourse marker; mid in formal writing (*We now turn to…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses; also *just now*, *right now*, *by now*
**Contrast:** Russian *сейчас* also means 'in a moment' (*сейчас приду*); English does not use *now* for that: *I'll be right there* or *I'm coming*, not *I come now*. *Now that…* (*Now that you're here, we can start*) is a conjunction and belongs to another dictionary. *Right now* is 'at this very moment' or 'immediately', never 'just a moment ago' — that is *just now*. With a length of time, *now* is Russian *уже*: *We've lived here ten years now* (*мы живём здесь уже десять лет*), not *already ten years*.

Now has 9 distinct senses.

**1. At the present moment.** (сейчас)

1. I can't talk *now* — I'm driving.
2. She's in a meeting right *now*.
3. Where are you living *now*?

**2. At this period of time, in contrast with the past.** (теперь, в наше время)

4. I used to hate coffee, but *now* I drink three cups a day.
5. Most people *now* pay with their phones.
6. Prices are much higher *now* than when I was a kid.

**3. In the situation that exists after what has just happened; as a result.** (теперь)

7. You missed the last bus — how are you getting home *now*?
8. She read the letter, so *now* she knows the truth.
9. We've come this far; we can't turn back *now*.

**4. Immediately; without any delay.** (сейчас же, немедленно)

10. Leave *now* or you'll miss the bus.
11. I need that report *now*, not tomorrow.
12. Call her right *now*, before she goes to bed.

**5. With a length of time: up to the present, counting from when something began.** (уже)

13. We've been married twenty years *now*.
14. It's been three weeks *now*, and she still hasn't called.
15. I've lived in Texas for a long time *now*.

**6. At the point in a past story being told; by that time.** (теперь, уже, тут)

16. It was *now* completely dark, and we were lost.
17. The crowd was *now* chanting his name.

**7. Only a moment ago (*just now*).** (только что)

18. Your mom called *just now*.
19. Who was that at the door *just now*?

**8. Occasionally, from time to time (*now and then*, *now and again*).** (время от времени, иногда)

20. We still see each other every *now and then*.
21. *Now and again* he sends me a postcard.

**9. Used to get attention, move to a new point, or soften a warning or comfort.** (итак, ну, так вот; ну-ну (успокаивая))

22. *Now*, let's look at the second question.
23. *Now*, *now*, don't cry — it's only a scratch.
24. *Now* listen to me carefully.

### then

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛn/ &middot; *THEN*
**Русский:** тогда, потом, затем
**Type:** time; also linking (*in that case*, *but then*)
**Position:** any position — 'next' front, after *and*, or mid (*she then left*); 'at that time' usually end; 'in that case' front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and whole clauses
**Contrast:** *then* is not *than*: *She's taller than me*, never *taller then me* — the two sound almost the same in fast speech and native speakers misspell them too. *then* is an adverb, not a conjunction, so careful writing joins two clauses with *and then*: *I finished my work, and then I went home*, not *I finished my work, then I went home*. The adjective use (*the then president*) belongs to another dictionary.

Then has 5 distinct senses.

**1. After that; next in time or in order.** (потом, затем)

1. We had dinner, and *then* we went to a movie.
2. First add the flour, *then* the eggs.
3. Turn left at the light, and *then* it's the second house on the right.

**2. At that time, in the past or the future.** (тогда, в то время)

4. We were living in Chicago *then*.
5. Call me Friday — I'll know more *then*.
6. Back *then*, nobody had a cell phone.

**3. In that case; so; as a result.** (тогда, значит, в таком случае)

7. If you're tired, *then* go to bed.
8. "I don't have a car." "*Then* how did you get here?"
9. Okay *then*, I'll see you tomorrow.

**4. In addition; on top of that (*and then*).** (а ещё, к тому же)

10. The rent is high, and *then* there's parking to pay for.
11. I have to finish this report, and *then* there are all those emails.

**5. On the other hand; considering this (*but then*, *then again*).** (впрочем, с другой стороны)

12. He's very quiet, but *then* his father was the same way.
13. I might go to the party. *Then again*, I might just stay home.

### today

**Pronunciation:** /təˈdeɪ/ &middot; *tuh-DAY*
**Русский:** сегодня; в наши дни
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end — usually end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Contrast:** No preposition: *I'm working today*, not *on today*. Russian *сегодня утром / сегодня вечером* is *this morning / this evening* or *tonight* — never *today morning* or *today evening*. When *today* is the subject (*Today is Monday*) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

Today has 2 distinct senses.

**1. On this day.** (сегодня)

1. I have a dentist appointment *today*.
2. What are you doing *today*?
3. The store closes early *today*.

**2. At the present period; nowadays.** (в наши дни, сегодня)

4. Kids *today* spend hours online.
5. *Today*, most homes in the US have internet access.
6. Few people *today* remember how to use a paper map.

### tomorrow

**Pronunciation:** /təˈmɑːroʊ/ &middot; *tuh-MAHR-oh*
**Русский:** завтра
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end — usually end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Contrast:** No preposition: *See you tomorrow*, not *on tomorrow*. *Завтра утром* is usually *tomorrow morning* (*tomorrow in the morning* sounds clumsy); *послезавтра* is *the day after tomorrow*. A timetable takes the present simple: *The flight leaves at seven tomorrow*. As a subject (*Tomorrow is Friday*) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

On the day after today.

1. I'll call you *tomorrow*.
2. *Tomorrow* we're driving up to the lake.
3. The package should arrive *tomorrow* morning.

### yesterday

**Pronunciation:** /ˈjɛstərdeɪ/ &middot; *YES-tur-day*
**Русский:** вчера
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end — usually end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Contrast:** Past simple, not the present perfect: *I saw her yesterday*, not *I have seen her yesterday*. *Вчера вечером* is *yesterday evening* or *last night*; *вчера ночью* is *last night*, never *yesterday night*. *Позавчера* is *the day before yesterday*. As a subject (*Yesterday was cold*) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

Yesterday has 2 distinct senses.

**1. On the day before today.** (вчера)

1. I ran into your sister *yesterday*.
2. It rained all day *yesterday*.
3. *Yesterday* afternoon we finally finished the project.

**2. Very recently, as it seems (*only yesterday*).** (совсем недавно)

4. It seems like only *yesterday* that we moved in.
5. I remember it as if it happened *yesterday*.

### tonight

**Pronunciation:** /təˈnaɪt/ &middot; *tuh-NYT*
**Русский:** сегодня вечером, сегодня ночью
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end — usually end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Contrast:** Russian splits *сегодня вечером* and *сегодня ночью*; English *tonight* covers both, from about sunset on. The night that has just ended is *last night*, not *tonight* or *this night*. No preposition: *Are you free tonight?*, not *at tonight*.

On the evening or night of today.

1. Are you free *tonight*?
2. It's supposed to snow *tonight*.
3. *Tonight* we're having pizza and watching a movie.

### soon

**Pronunciation:** /suːn/ &middot; *SOON*
**Русский:** скоро, вскоре
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end — end is most common; mid after an auxiliary (*will soon be*); front for a new stage in a story
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *sooner*, *soonest*
**Modifies:** verbs; also *too soon*, *how soon*, *soon after*
**Contrast:** *No sooner* fronted forces inversion and takes *than*, not *when*: *No sooner had we sat down than the phone rang*. *As soon as* takes the present for a future event: *Call me as soon as you arrive*, not *as soon as you will arrive*. *soon* is not *early*: *I got up early*, not *I got up soon*.

Soon has 3 distinct senses.

**1. In a short time from now or from the time mentioned.** (скоро, вскоре)

1. Dinner will be ready *soon*.
2. *Soon* after we arrived, it started to rain.
3. We'll *soon* find out who won.

**2. Early or quickly; before the time expected.** (рано, быстро)

4. How *soon* can you get here?
5. It's too *soon* to say whether the treatment is working.
6. The *sooner* we leave, the *sooner* we'll get there.

**3. Rather; preferably (*would sooner*, *would just as soon*).** (скорее, лучше, охотнее)

7. I'd *sooner* walk than wait an hour for the bus.
8. She would *sooner* quit than apologize to him.
9. I'd just as *soon* stay home tonight.

### later

**Pronunciation:** /ˈleɪtər/ &middot; *LAY-tur*
**Русский:** позже, потом
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — also front (*Later, …*) and mid (*she later became…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — it is already the comparative of *late* (*much later*, *a little later*)
**Modifies:** verbs; follows a time phrase (*two years later*)
**Form:** comparative of *late*, used as a time adverb in its own right
**Contrast:** *two days later* counts forward from a past event (*спустя два дня*); counting forward from now is *in two days*: *I'll be back in two days*, not *two days later*. Standing alone, *later* or *afterward* is safe; *after* at the end (*I'll do it after*) is casual. Do not confuse with *latter* (the second of two).

Later has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At some time after now; afterward.** (позже, потом)

1. I'll call you *later*.
2. Can we talk about this *later*?
3. See you *later*!

**2. After the past time or event mentioned.** (позже, впоследствии, (три года) спустя)

4. Three years *later*, they got married.
5. She *later* became a federal judge.
6. *Later* that night, the power went out.

**3. At a time after the usual, expected or stated time.** (позже, позднее)

7. Can we meet *later* than six?
8. The kids stayed up *later* than usual.

### recently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːsəntli/ &middot; *REE-sunt-lee*
**Русский:** недавно; в последнее время
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end — also front
**Comparison:** more/most — *more recently*, *most recently*
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *recent*
**Contrast:** The tense follows the meaning: one event 'not long ago' takes the past simple (*I recently bought a car*); a period 'lately' takes the present perfect (*I've been busy recently*). *lately* covers only the second meaning: *I recently met her*, not *I lately met her*.

Recently has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not long ago.** (недавно)

1. We *recently* moved to Austin.
2. The bridge was *recently* repainted.
3. *Most recently*, she ran a small bakery in Denver.

**2. During the period up to now.** (в последнее время)

4. Have you seen any good movies *recently*?
5. I haven't been sleeping well *recently*.

### already

**Pronunciation:** /ɔːlˈrɛdi/ &middot; *awl-RED-ee*
**Русский:** уже
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end — mid is neutral (*I've already seen it*); end adds emphasis or surprise
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses; also time expressions (*already midnight*)
**Contrast:** *already* goes in affirmative sentences; negatives and ordinary questions take *yet* (*Have you finished yet?*); *already* in a question shows surprise. The present perfect is standard, but American English freely uses the past simple: *I already ate*. Russian *уже не* is *not … anymore* or *no longer*: *He doesn't work here anymore*, not *He already doesn't work here*. *all ready* is two words meaning 'fully prepared': *We're all ready to go*.

Already has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Before now or before a particular time, often sooner than expected (in a question, with surprise: so soon?).** (уже)

1. I've *already* eaten, thanks.
2. By the time we got there, the movie had *already* started.
3. You're leaving *already*?

**2. Informal American: used after a command or complaint to show impatience.** (уже, ну же (с нетерпением))

4. Enough *already*! We get it.
5. Just make up your mind *already*.

### yet

**Pronunciation:** /jɛt/ &middot; *YET*
**Русский:** ещё (не), уже (в вопросах)
**Type:** time; also degree (*yet another*) and linking (*and yet*)
**Position:** end — in negatives and questions; mid in *have yet to*, *may yet*; before its word in *yet another*, *yet again*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses; comparatives and *another*, *again*
**Contrast:** Russian *ещё* is both *yet* and *still*: *I haven't decided yet* (*ещё не решил*) but *I still live there* (*всё ещё живу*); *still* goes before a negative auxiliary (*He still hasn't called*), *yet* at the end (*He hasn't called yet*). In questions *yet* is Russian *уже*: *Has the mail come yet?* American English allows the past simple: *Did you eat yet?* *have yet to* looks affirmative but is negative. The conjunction *yet* (*simple yet effective*) belongs to another dictionary.

Yet has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Up to now; so far (in negatives and questions, and after a superlative).** (ещё (не); уже (в вопросах); на сегодняшний день)

1. I haven't finished *yet*.
2. "Are we there *yet*?" "Not *yet*."
3. That was her best album *yet*.

**2. Not so far, although it is expected (*have yet to*, *be yet to*).** (ещё не, пока не)

4. The winner has *yet* to be announced.
5. I have *yet* to meet anyone who actually likes that show.

**3. Still; even now; at some time in the future.** (ещё, всё ещё, всё-таки)

6. We may *yet* win this game.
7. There's hope for him *yet*.
8. Don't give up — this plan could *yet* work.

**4. Even more; one more time (*yet another*, *yet again*, before a comparative).** (ещё (один, раз, более))

9. The flight was delayed *yet* again.
10. That's *yet* another reason to stay home.
11. We got *yet* more snow overnight.

**5. Nevertheless; in spite of that (*and yet*).** (и всё же, однако)

12. It's a tiny town, and *yet* it has three bookstores.
13. He knew it was risky, and *yet* he went ahead anyway.

### still

**Pronunciation:** /stɪl/ &middot; *STIL*
**Русский:** всё ещё, до сих пор; всё же
**Type:** time; also linking (*nevertheless*) and degree (*still better*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be*, before a negative auxiliary; front for 'nevertheless'
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses; comparatives (*still more*)
**Contrast:** Before a negative auxiliary: *He still hasn't called* (*всё ещё не*), not *He hasn't still called*. *still* is for something continuing, *not yet* for something not started, *not anymore* for something that stopped. Russian *ещё один* is *another* or *one more*, not *still one*. The adjective 'not moving' (*still water*, and also *sit still*, *hold still*, *keep still*) belongs to another dictionary.

Still has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Continuing up to now or up to the time mentioned.** (всё ещё, до сих пор)

1. She *still* lives with her parents.
2. Are you *still* at work?
3. It was midnight, and he *still* hadn't called.

**2. Nevertheless; even so.** (всё же, тем не менее)

4. It rained all weekend. *Still*, we had a great time.
5. He drives me crazy, but he's *still* my brother.
6. The hotel was expensive, but it was *still* worth it.

**3. Even; to a greater degree (with comparatives and *another*, *more*).** (ещё (более))

7. The next day was hotter *still*.
8. Ten minutes is good; five is *still* better.
9. Better *still*, call ahead and reserve a table.

### immediately

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈmiːdiətli/ &middot; *ih-MEE-dee-ut-lee*
**Русский:** немедленно, сразу
**Type:** time; also place (*immediately behind*)
**Position:** end — also mid (*she immediately called*) and before its word (*immediately after*, *immediately below*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; prepositions and adverbs of time and place (*immediately after*)
**Form:** -ly from *immediate*
**Contrast:** British English also uses *immediately* as a conjunction (*Immediately I saw her, I knew*); American English says *as soon as* or *the moment*: *As soon as I saw her, I knew*.

Immediately has 2 distinct senses.

**1. At once; without any delay.** (немедленно, сразу)

1. Please call 911 *immediately*.
2. She *immediately* recognized his voice.
3. The new rules take effect *immediately*.

**2. Directly next in time, place or relationship, with nothing between.** (сразу, непосредственно)

4. We left *immediately* after the ceremony.
5. The restrooms are *immediately* to your left.
6. Only the people *immediately* affected were told.

### eventually

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈvɛntʃuəli/ &middot; *ih-VEN-choo-uh-lee*
**Русский:** в конце концов, со временем
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end — also front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *eventual*
**Contrast:** False friend: *eventually* never means 'possibly, if need be' (*эвентуально*, German *eventuell*). It says the thing did or will happen in the end, usually after a delay: *He eventually agreed* = *в конце концов согласился*. For 'possibly' use *possibly* or *potentially*. Unlike *finally*, it cannot introduce the last point of a list.

In the end, after a long time, a delay or a series of events.

1. We waited two hours, and the bus *eventually* came.
2. You'll get used to the cold *eventually*.
3. *Eventually*, they sold the house and moved to Florida.

### finally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfaɪnəli/ &middot; *FYN-uh-lee*
**Русский:** наконец; окончательно
**Type:** time; also linking (the last point)
**Position:** mid — front when it introduces the last point
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *final*
**Contrast:** *finally*, *at last* and *eventually* differ: *at last* stresses relief and cannot begin a last point in a list; *eventually* is neutral 'in the end' and also cannot list; *lastly* only lists. Mid position for 'after a long time' (*We finally got home*), front with a comma for the last point (*Finally, …*).

Finally has 3 distinct senses.

**1. After a long time, often with relief or impatience.** (наконец)

1. We *finally* got home at two in the morning.
2. After six years, she *finally* finished her degree.
3. *Finally*! I thought you'd never get here.

**2. As the last in a series of points or actions.** (наконец, в заключение)

4. *Finally*, I'd like to thank my parents.
5. Mix the dry ingredients, add the eggs, and *finally* pour in the milk.

**3. In a way that settles something for good.** (окончательно)

6. The lawsuit was *finally* settled out of court.
7. Nothing has been *finally* decided yet.

### afterward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæftərwərd/ &middot; *AF-tur-wurd*
**Русский:** потом, после этого, впоследствии
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — also front (*Afterward, …*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses; *soon afterward*, *shortly afterward*
**Form:** -ward — from *after*; *afterwards* is the British form, also common in the US
**Contrast:** Russian *после* is both a preposition and an adverb; in English the adverb is *afterward* or *later*, while *after* normally needs an object: *We went for coffee afterward*, but *after the movie*. *afterward* never takes an object: *after dinner*, not *afterward dinner*.

After the time or event mentioned; later.

1. We saw a play and went out for dessert *afterward*.
2. Shortly *afterward*, the police arrived.
3. I felt terrible about it *afterward*.

### beforehand

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈfɔːrhænd/ &middot; *bih-FOR-hand*
**Русский:** заранее
**Type:** time
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** compound — *before* + *hand*
**Contrast:** Russian *заранее* is *beforehand*, *in advance* or *ahead of time*, not *before* alone: *Let me know beforehand*. *beforehand* never takes an object: *before the meeting*, not *beforehand the meeting*.

Before a particular event; in advance.

1. Let me know *beforehand* if you can't come.
2. We bought the tickets online *beforehand*.
3. I wish someone had warned me *beforehand*.

### nowadays

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnaʊədeɪz/ &middot; *NOW-uh-dayz*
**Русский:** в наши дни, сейчас
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses, usually in the present simple
**Form:** compound — *now* + *a* + *days*
**Contrast:** For a general situation compared with the past, not a single moment: *Nowadays people rarely write letters*, but *She's in the shower now*, not *She's in the shower nowadays*. In everyday American speech *these days* is more common.

At the present time, compared with the past.

1. *Nowadays*, almost everyone has a smartphone.
2. Hardly anyone pays by check *nowadays*.
3. Kids *nowadays* don't know what a cassette is.

### currently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɜːrəntli/ &middot; *KUR-unt-lee*
**Русский:** в настоящее время, сейчас
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be*; also front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *current*
**Contrast:** False friend: *actually* looks like Russian *актуально* / *актуальный*, but it means 'in fact' (*на самом деле*), never 'at the moment' or 'relevant'. 'At the moment' is *currently*: *She is currently working in Boston*; 'relevant, topical' is *relevant* or *current*.

At the present time.

1. She's *currently* working on her second novel.
2. The position is *currently* unfilled.
3. *Currently*, about forty people work at the plant.

### formerly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːrmərli/ &middot; *FOR-mur-lee*
**Русский:** раньше, прежде, ранее
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid — also before its word (*formerly known as*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, participles and names (*Myanmar, formerly Burma*)
**Form:** -ly from *former*
**Contrast:** *formerly* (in the past) and *formally* (officially, in a formal way) sound almost the same in American speech. For *бывший* before a noun, English uses the adjective *former* (*a former teacher*); *formerly* goes with a verb or a name.

In the past, but not any longer.

1. The restaurant was *formerly* a bank.
2. He *formerly* worked as a nurse in Ohio.
3. Myanmar, *formerly* known as Burma, is in Southeast Asia.

### previously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpriːviəsli/ &middot; *PREE-vee-us-lee*
**Русский:** ранее, прежде
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid — also front, and after a time phrase (*two days previously*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, participles; follows a time phrase
**Form:** -ly from *previous*
**Contrast:** *two years previously* (or *earlier*, *before*) counts back from a past point; counting back from now is *two years ago*. *previously* is formal — in speech, *before* or *earlier* is more natural.

Before now or before the time mentioned, also after a time phrase (*three months previously*).

1. She had *previously* worked for the city.
2. As *previously* mentioned, the office is closed on Monday.
3. He had left the company three months *previously*.

### once

**Pronunciation:** /wʌns/ &middot; *WUNS*
**Русский:** один раз; когда-то
**Type:** time; also frequency (*once a week*)
**Position:** mid or end — mid for 'at one time in the past' (*I once lived there*); end for 'one time' (*I've been there once*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; *once a week*, *once more*, *at once*
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: *I once lived in Ohio* (*когда-то*) against *I've been to Ohio once* (*один раз*). *at once* is 'immediately' or 'at the same time'; *all at once* is usually 'suddenly'. The conjunction *once* (*Once you start, you can't stop* = *как только*) belongs to another dictionary.

Once has 7 distinct senses.

**1. One time; on one occasion.** (один раз)

1. I've only been to New York *once*.
2. We meet *once* a week.
3. Take one pill *once* a day with food.

**2. At some time in the past, but no longer.** (когда-то, однажды)

4. This town was *once* a busy port.
5. I *once* met him at an airport in Dallas.
6. The house *once* belonged to a famous writer.

**3. Ever; even one time (after a negative).** (ни разу; хоть раз)

7. He never *once* said thank you.
8. Not *once* did she complain about the pain.

**4. Again; one more time (*once more*, *once again*).** (ещё раз, снова)

9. Let's try it *once more* from the top.
10. *Once again*, the team lost at home.

**5. Immediately (*at once*).** (сразу, немедленно)

11. Come here *at once*!
12. The doctor saw me *at once*.

**6. At the same time (*at once*).** (одновременно, сразу)

13. Don't all talk *at once*.
14. I can't do three things *at once*.

**7. Suddenly (*all at once*).** (вдруг, внезапно)

15. *All at once*, the lights went out.
16. *All at once* she started laughing.

### someday

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌmdeɪ/ &middot; *SUM-day*
**Русский:** когда-нибудь
**Type:** time
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses, about the future
**Form:** compound — *some* + *day*, written as one word
**Contrast:** Future only: for the past use *one day* or *once* (*One day last spring, I met him*); *one day* works for both. *someday* is one word; *some day* is two when it means an unnamed particular day: *Let's have lunch some day next week*.

At some unknown time in the future.

1. I want to visit Japan *someday*.
2. *Someday* you'll understand why I did it.
3. Maybe *someday* we'll live by the ocean.

### meanwhile

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmiːnwaɪl/ &middot; *MEEN-wyl*
**Русский:** тем временем, между тем
**Type:** time; also linking (contrast)
**Position:** front — usually followed by a comma; also end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** whole clauses
**Contrast:** *meanwhile* is an adverb, not a conjunction: *I cooked; meanwhile, he set the table*, or *I cooked while he set the table* — not *I cooked meanwhile he set the table*. *in the meantime* can replace it for 'until then', but not for contrast.

Meanwhile has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At the same time as something else is happening.** (тем временем)

1. I'll make the salad. *Meanwhile*, you can set the table.
2. The police searched the house; *meanwhile*, the suspect was already in Mexico.

**2. In the time before something expected happens.** (пока, а пока что)

3. The new office opens in May. *Meanwhile*, we're working from home.
4. Your passport will take six weeks; *meanwhile*, don't book any flights.

**3. Used to contrast two situations.** (между тем, в то же время)

5. Rents keep going up. *Meanwhile*, wages have barely moved.
6. The city spends millions on stadiums; *meanwhile*, schools are falling apart.

### ago

**Pronunciation:** /əˈɡoʊ/ &middot; *uh-GOH*
**Русский:** назад, тому назад
**Type:** time
**Position:** after its word — *three days ago*, never *ago three days*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a preceding time phrase (*two hours ago*, *long ago*, *a while ago*)
**Contrast:** Past simple only: *I saw it two weeks ago*, not *I have seen it two weeks ago*. *ago* counts back from now; from a past point use *before*, *earlier* or *previously*: *She said she had met him two years before*. *ten years ago* answers 'when?' (*десять лет назад*); *for ten years* answers 'how long?' (*уже десять лет*).

Back in time from now.

1. She moved to Seattle three years *ago*.
2. How long *ago* did this happen?
3. I talked to him just a few minutes *ago*.

### before

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈfɔːr/ &middot; *bih-FOR*
**Русский:** раньше, прежде; до этого
**Type:** time
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; follows a time phrase (*the day before*)
**Contrast:** As an adverb *before* stands alone at the end; the preposition (*before lunch*) and the conjunction (*before you go*) belong to other dictionaries. For 'ever in the past' use the present perfect: *Have we met before?* Counting back from now is *ago*: *three days ago*, not *three days before*. *the day before* is *накануне*.

Before has 2 distinct senses.

**1. At an earlier time; in the past.** (раньше, прежде)

1. Have we met *before*?
2. I've never seen anything like it *before*.
3. She had been to Paris twice *before*.

**2. After a time phrase: earlier than a past time.** (до этого, раньше, накануне)

4. I'd seen him the week *before*.
5. The storm hit on Tuesday; the day *before*, the sky was perfectly clear.
6. He had left two hours *before*.

### since

**Pronunciation:** /sɪns/ &middot; *SINS*
**Русский:** с тех пор
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end — end in *ever since*; mid in *has since been*, *long since*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, usually in the present or past perfect
**Contrast:** The preposition (*since Monday*) and the conjunction (*since I was ten*; *since* meaning 'because') belong to other dictionaries. With *since*, the verb goes in the perfect, unlike Russian: *I've known her since college*, not *I know her since college*. *since* marks the start, *for* the length: *for three years*, not *since three years*.

Since has 3 distinct senses.

**1. From that time until now or until the time mentioned (often *ever since*).** (с тех пор)

1. She moved to Denver in 2015 and has lived there ever *since*.
2. I saw him in May, but I haven't heard from him *since*.
3. They met at a party and have been friends ever *since*.

**2. At some time between then and now.** (с тех пор, впоследствии)

4. The house has *since* been sold.
5. He left the band and has *since* started his own label.

**3. A long time before now (*long since*).** (давно (уже))

6. The factory has long *since* closed.
7. Those old rules have long *since* been forgotten.

### instantly

**Русский:** мгновенно, сразу
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and participles (*instantly recognizable*)
**Form:** -ly from *instant*

At once; with no delay at all.

1. The driver was killed *instantly*.
2. I *instantly* regretted saying it.
3. Her voice is *instantly* recognizable.

### presently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɛzəntli/ &middot; *PREZ-unt-lee*
**Русский:** в настоящее время; вскоре
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid — for 'now'; end or front for 'soon'
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *present*
**Contrast:** Two meanings that point in opposite directions. In American English *presently* most often means 'now' (*She is presently in Brazil*); the older 'soon' meaning (*I'll be with you presently*) is still met in fiction and formal speech. Mid position usually means 'now', end position usually means 'soon'.

Presently has 2 distinct senses.

**1. At the present time; now.** (в настоящее время, сейчас)

1. The senator is *presently* on a trip overseas.
2. There are *presently* no plans to close the school.

**2. Soon; after a short time.** (вскоре, скоро)

3. The doctor will be with you *presently*.
4. *Presently* the door opened, and a young man came in.

### next

**Pronunciation:** /nɛkst/ &middot; *NEKST*
**Русский:** потом, затем; в следующий раз
**Type:** time; also degree (*next to impossible*)
**Position:** front or end — front when ordering steps; mid in *when I next see…*; before its word in *next best*, *next to*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; superlatives (*the next largest*); adjectives after *next to*
**Contrast:** *next week*, *next year* use the determiner, with no *the* and no preposition: *See you next week*, not *on the next week*; *the next week* means 'the following week' from a past point. The preposition *next to* 'beside' (*Sit next to me*) belongs to another dictionary; before an adjective the adverb *next to* means 'almost'.

Next has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Immediately after this or that; then.** (потом, затем, дальше)

1. What should we do *next*?
2. *Next*, add the sugar and stir.
3. Guess who walked in *next*?

**2. On the first occasion after this one.** (в следующий раз)

4. When I *next* see him, I'll ask him about it.
5. When are you *next* in town?

**3. Following in order of size or rank (before a superlative).** (следующий (по величине, качеству))

6. Texas is the *next* largest state after Alaska.
7. If you can't get the real thing, this is the *next* best option.

**4. Almost (*next to*, before an adjective or *nothing*).** (почти)

8. Finding parking downtown is *next* to impossible.
9. They sold the car for *next* to nothing.

### originally

**Pronunciation:** /əˈrɪdʒənəli/ &middot; *uh-RIJ-uh-nuh-lee*
**Русский:** изначально; родом
**Type:** time; also manner (the rare 'creatively' sense)
**Position:** mid or end — mid or front for 'at first'; end for 'by origin' (*Where are you from originally?*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — except the rare 'creatively' sense (*more originally*)
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *original*
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *оригинально* usually means 'unusually, creatively', but English *originally* almost always means 'at first' or 'by origin'. *The house was originally designed as a school* = *изначально*. For the Russian sense say *creatively*, *unusually* or *in an original way*.

Originally has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At first; before something changed.** (изначально, первоначально)

1. The building was *originally* a school.
2. *Originally*, we planned to drive, but we flew instead.
3. The song was *originally* recorded in 1965.

**2. As far as someone's or something's origin is concerned.** (родом, по происхождению)

4. Where are you from *originally*?
5. My family is *originally* from Ukraine.

**3. In a new and creative way.** (оригинально, творчески)

6. Few writers think as *originally* as she does.
7. Her dresses are simple but very *originally* designed.

### initially

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈnɪʃəli/ &middot; *ih-NISH-uh-lee*
**Русский:** сначала, первоначально
**Type:** time
**Position:** mid — also front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *initial*
**Contrast:** *initially* and *at first* suggest that things changed later (*Initially I hated it, but…*); *first* and *firstly* put points or steps in order. *initially* is somewhat formal; in speech *at first* is more common.

At the beginning, usually before a change.

1. *Initially*, the project was supposed to take six months.
2. I was *initially* skeptical, but the plan worked.

### suddenly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌdənli/ &middot; *SUD-un-lee*
**Русский:** вдруг, внезапно
**Type:** time; also manner
**Position:** any position — front for a dramatic turn (*Suddenly, …*), mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most — rare (*more suddenly than expected*)
**Modifies:** verbs and clauses
**Form:** -ly from *sudden*
**Contrast:** Russian *а вдруг…?* ('what if') is not *suddenly*: *What if he doesn't come?*, not *And suddenly he doesn't come?*. *вдруг* in 'by chance' is *by any chance*: *Do you by any chance have a charger?*

Quickly and unexpectedly.

1. *Suddenly*, the lights went out.
2. He *suddenly* realized he'd left his wallet at home.
3. The weather changed *more suddenly* than anyone expected.

### anymore

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɛniˈmɔːr/ &middot; *en-ee-MOR*
**Русский:** больше не, уже не
**Type:** time
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** negative or interrogative clauses
**Form:** compound — *any* + *more*, written as one word in American English
**Contrast:** Russian *уже не* / *больше не* is *not … anymore* or *no longer*: *He doesn't work here anymore* or *He no longer works here*, not *He already doesn't work here*. Standard English uses it only with a negative or in a question; the positive 'nowadays' use (*Anymore, gas is so expensive*) is regional Midland American speech and is best not copied. Two words, *any more*, is a quantity in both British and American English: *I don't want any more cake*.

No longer; not now, as before (with a negative or in a question).

1. We don't go to that restaurant *anymore*.
2. She doesn't live here *anymore*.
3. Does anyone even use fax machines *anymore*?

### earlier

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɜːrliər/ &middot; *UR-lee-ur*
**Русский:** раньше, ранее
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — also front (*Earlier, …*) and after a time phrase (*two days earlier*)
**Comparison:** not gradable — it is already the comparative of *early* (*much earlier*)
**Modifies:** verbs; follows a time phrase (*an hour earlier*)
**Form:** comparative of *early*, used as a time adverb in its own right
**Contrast:** *two hours ago* counts back from now; *two hours earlier* counts back from a past point. For an earlier moment today, *earlier* is the natural word: *I called you earlier* (*я тебе сегодня уже звонил*); *before* alone sounds unfinished there.

Earlier has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At a previous time, especially earlier today or in the same text or conversation.** (раньше, ранее)

1. I saw your brother *earlier*.
2. As I mentioned *earlier*, the office is closed on Friday.
3. *Earlier* this year, the company opened a store in Miami.

**2. After a time phrase: before a past point.** (раньше, до этого)

4. He had arrived two days *earlier*.
5. A week *earlier*, nobody had even heard of her.

**3. Sooner than another time or than usual.** (раньше)

6. Can you come in *earlier* tomorrow?
7. I got up *earlier* than usual to beat the traffic.
