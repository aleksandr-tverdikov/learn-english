# Degree adverbs that turn it up

> How much? — *very*, *really*, *so*, *too*, *quite*, *extremely*, *totally*, *absolutely* and the other intensifiers.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Intensifiers strengthen an adjective, an adverb or a verb. The key distinction is gradable versus extreme: *very* goes with gradable adjectives (*very cold*) and *absolutely* with extreme ones (*absolutely freezing*), and mixing them (*very freezing*, *absolutely cold*) sounds wrong to a native ear. *Too* means *more than is wanted*, not *very* — *too expensive* is a complaint. And *quite* means *very* in American English but often only *fairly* in British English.

---
### very

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɛri/ &middot; *VAIR-ee*
**Русский:** очень
**Type:** degree — the basic intensifier for gradable adjectives and adverbs
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** gradable adjectives and adverbs, *much* and *many*, superlatives and *own*
**Contrast:** *Very* cannot modify a verb: say *I like it very much* or *I really like it*, never *I very like it* (Russian *очень нравится*). It does not go with extreme adjectives either — *absolutely freezing*, not *very freezing* — or before a comparative: *much better*, not *very better*. With verb-like past participles English uses *much* or *greatly*: *much appreciated*, not *very appreciated* (but *very interested* and *very tired* are fine). *Not very* is a polite way of saying the opposite: *not very good* means fairly bad. In *at the very end* and *this very moment*, *very* is an adjective meaning *exactly this*.

Very has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To a high degree; extremely.** (очень)

1. It's *very* cold outside today.
2. She speaks English *very* well.
3. Thank you *very* much for coming.

**2. Used to stress a superlative or the words own, same, next, first and last.** (самый; же (на следующий же день))

4. This is the *very* best pizza in town.
5. She finally has her *very* own room.
6. He called me back the *very* next day.

### really

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːəli/ &middot; *REE-uh-lee*
**Русский:** очень; действительно, на самом деле
**Type:** degree — the everyday spoken intensifier; also stance (*in fact*)
**Position:** mid — before the main verb (*I really like it*), and before its word with adjectives (*really good*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, verbs, and whole statements
**Form:** -ly from *real*
**Contrast:** Where *really* goes changes the meaning: *I really don't like him* is strong dislike, while *I don't really like him* is a mild *не особо*. Unlike *very*, *really* can modify a verb: *I really want to go*. *Really?* said alone as a reply (*Правда?*) works as an interjection. In casual American speech *real* replaces it before adjectives (*real good*), but not in writing.

Really has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Very; very much — adding force to an adjective, an adverb or a verb.** (очень, правда)

1. That was a *really* good movie.
2. She drives *really* fast.
3. I *really* like your new haircut.

**2. In fact; in reality, as opposed to what seems true.** (на самом деле, в действительности)

4. What *really* happened that night?
5. He looks forty, but he's *really* almost sixty.
6. Nobody *really* believed her story.

**3. With not: not much or not exactly — a soft way of saying no.** (не особо, не совсем)

7. “Are you hungry?” “Not *really*.”
8. I don't *really* understand how it works.
9. It's not *really* my kind of music.

**4. Used to add force to a protest, reassurance or apology; honestly.** (право, честное слово, ну правда)

10. It's no trouble at all, *really*.
11. *Really*, Tom, you could have called first.

### extremely

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈstriːmli/ &middot; *ik-STREEM-lee*
**Русский:** крайне, чрезвычайно
**Type:** degree — a strong intensifier for gradable adjectives and adverbs
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** gradable adjectives and adverbs
**Form:** -ly from *extreme*
**Contrast:** Like *very*, *extremely* goes with gradable words (*extremely cold*, *extremely difficult*), not with extreme ones: *absolutely freezing*, *absolutely perfect*, not *extremely freezing* or *extremely perfect*. It does not modify a verb on its own: *I liked it very much*, not *I extremely liked it*. It is more formal than *really* or *so*.

To a very high degree; very.

1. The test was *extremely* difficult.
2. He was *extremely* lucky to survive the crash.
3. She handled the situation *extremely* well.

### incredibly

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈkrɛdəbli/ &middot; *in-KRED-uh-blee*
**Русский:** невероятно
**Type:** degree — a strong intensifier; also stance, as a sentence adverb
**Position:** before its word — front when it comments on the whole sentence
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *incredible*
**Contrast:** Before an adjective *incredibly* just means *very, very* (*incredibly tired*) and says nothing about belief. At the front of a sentence, with a comma, it means *it is hard to believe that*: *Incredibly, he won* is *как ни удивительно, он победил*.

Incredibly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Extremely; to a very great degree.** (невероятно, чрезвычайно)

1. The view from the top was *incredibly* beautiful.
2. I'm *incredibly* grateful for your help.
3. She works *incredibly* hard.

**2. In a way that is hard to believe — commenting on a whole fact.** (как ни удивительно, невероятно, но)

4. *Incredibly*, nobody was hurt in the fire.
5. *Incredibly*, the old car still runs.

### terribly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɛrəbli/ &middot; *TAIR-uh-blee*
**Русский:** ужасно, очень
**Type:** degree — an intensifier; also manner (*very badly*)
**Position:** before its word — for *very*; end for *very badly* (*played terribly*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and verbs of feeling (*miss*, *suffer*)
**Form:** -ly from *terrible*
**Contrast:** Position shows the meaning. Before an adjective *terribly* only means *very*, even about good things: *terribly kind*, *terribly important*. After a verb it means *very badly*: *He sang terribly*. *Not terribly* is a polite *not very*: *not terribly interesting* is *не особенно интересный*. *I'm terribly sorry* is a set phrase for a strong apology.

Terribly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very; extremely.** (ужасно, очень)

1. I'm *terribly* sorry to hear about your father.
2. The movie wasn't *terribly* exciting.
3. The kids miss you *terribly*.

**2. Very badly.** (ужасно, отвратительно)

4. Our team played *terribly* last night.
5. I slept *terribly* last night.
6. She treated him *terribly*.

### awfully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːfəli/ &middot; *AW-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** ужасно, очень
**Type:** degree — an informal intensifier; also manner (*very badly*)
**Position:** before its word — for *very*; end for *badly*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs; verbs in the *badly* sense
**Form:** -ly from *awful*
**Contrast:** Before an adjective *awfully* means *very* and carries no bad sense: *That's awfully nice of you* is a compliment. The meaning *badly* comes only after a verb, and even there *terribly* or *badly* is more usual. In casual American speech *awful* itself is used this way (*awful good*), but that is nonstandard in writing.

Awfully has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very; extremely.** (очень, ужасно)

1. That's *awfully* kind of you.
2. You're up *awfully* early.
3. It's *awfully* quiet in here.

**2. Very badly; in an unpleasant way.** (ужасно, скверно)

4. He behaved *awfully* at the wedding.
5. The band played *awfully* that night.

### so

**Pronunciation:** /soʊ/ &middot; *SOH*
**Русский:** так, настолько, очень
**Type:** degree — an intensifier; also manner, linking, and a stand-in for a clause (*I think so*)
**Position:** before its word — for degree; front in *So do I*, end in *I think so*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, *much* and *many*, verbs; replaces a whole clause
**Contrast:** *So* goes before an adjective with no noun; before *a* + adjective + noun use *such*: *so nice*, but *such a nice day*, not *a so nice day*. *So*, not *very*, starts a result: *so tired that I fell asleep*. After *think*, *hope*, *guess* and *be afraid*, English needs *so* where Russian has *так* or nothing: *I think so*, *I hope so*, never *I think it*. *So do I* (with inversion) means *и я тоже*; for a negative use *Neither do I*. *So it is* (no inversion) means *and that is true*. At the start of a clause (*It rained, so we stayed home*) and in *so that* (purpose) it is a conjunction.

So has 10 distinct senses.

**1. Very; to a great degree — often emotional or exclamatory.** (так, очень)

1. I'm *so* happy for you!
2. Thank you *so* much.
3. Why are you driving *so* fast?

**2. Used instead of repeating a clause, after verbs like think, hope and say, and in if so.** (так; да (думаю, да))

4. “Will it rain?” “I don't think *so*.”
5. I told you *so*!
6. Are you coming? If *so*, bring a jacket.

**3. To the degree stated or shown, especially with that for a result, or in only so much.** (настолько, так)

7. She was *so* tired that she fell asleep at her desk.
8. The fish was about *so* big.
9. There's only *so* much I can do.

**4. Also; in the same way — with inversion: so + auxiliary + subject.** (тоже, так же)

10. “I'm starving.” “*So* am I.”
11. Tom passed the exam, and *so* did his sister.

**5. Approximately — in or so, after a number or amount.** (примерно, или около того)

12. I'll be back in an hour or *so*.
13. There were twenty or *so* people at the meeting.

**6. For that reason; therefore — after and.** (поэтому, так что)

14. The store was closed, and *so* we went home.
15. He was sick and *so* missed the game.

**7. In this way; like this — also in and so on.** (так, таким образом)

16. Hold the bat like *so*, with your hands together.
17. It *so* happened that we were in town that week.
18. Bring pens, paper, tape and *so* on.

**8. Informal: definitely, completely — before a verb, not, or a noun phrase.** (совершенно, точно)

19. I am *so* not going to that party.
20. That jacket is *so* last year.
21. You are *so* getting grounded for this.

**9. Used to agree with something you notice or have just been told.** (и правда, действительно)

22. “Look, it's snowing.” “*So* it is!”
23. “The baby's asleep.” “*So* she is.”

**10. Informal: used to contradict a negative statement.** (ещё как, да)

24. “You didn't clean your room.” “I did *so*!”
25. “You can't swim.” “Can *so*!”

### too

**Pronunciation:** /tuː/ &middot; *TOO*
**Русский:** слишком; тоже
**Type:** degree — more than is wanted; also focusing (*also*)
**Position:** before its word — for *more than wanted*; end for *also*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, *much* and *many*; a whole clause in the sense *also*
**Contrast:** *Too* is not *very*: *too expensive* means *more than I can or want to pay* and is a complaint (*слишком*), while *very expensive* only describes. Before a noun the order is *too big a car*, never *a too big car*; and it is *much too hot*, not *too much hot*. In the sense *also* it ends an affirmative clause (in formal writing it can follow the subject between commas: *I, too, was surprised*); after a negative use *either*: *I don't like it either*, not *I don't like it too*. Do not confuse it with *to* and *two*, which sound the same.

Too has 5 distinct senses.

**1. More than is needed, wanted or possible.** (слишком)

1. This coffee is *too* hot to drink.
2. You're driving *too* fast.
3. There were *too* many people at the beach.

**2. Also; as well — at the end of a clause.** (тоже, также)

4. “I love pizza.” “Me *too*.”
5. Bring a jacket, and an umbrella *too*.
6. She speaks French, and German *too*.

**3. Very — in negatives and in polite set phrases.** (очень, не очень)

7. I'm not *too* sure about that.
8. You're *too* kind.
9. We'd be only *too* happy to help.

**4. And what is more — adding a surprising or annoying fact.** (к тому же, да ещё)

10. He lost his wallet, and on his birthday *too*.
11. She got a raise, and in her first year *too*.

**5. Informal: used to contradict a negative statement.** (ещё как, да)

12. “You didn't feed the dog.” “I did *too*!”
13. “That's not true.” “It is *too*!”

### quite

**Pronunciation:** /kwaɪt/ &middot; *KWYT*
**Русский:** довольно, весьма; совсем
**Type:** degree — an intensifier in American English; also a maximizer (*quite right*)
**Position:** before its word — and before *a* or *an* (*quite a party*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, verbs, and *a* + noun
**Contrast:** US/UK split: in American English *quite good* usually means *really good*; in British English, said flatly, it often means only *fairly good* — remember this when reading British texts. With words that cannot be graded (*right*, *sure*, *finished*) it means *completely* in both. *Not quite* means *not completely* (*не совсем*), not *not at all*. The word order is *quite a big house*, and the British one-word reply *Quite.* (*именно*) is rare in the US. Do not confuse it with *quiet* (*KWY-uht*).

Quite has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Very; to a considerable degree — also in quite a few and quite a lot.** (довольно, весьма, очень; немало)

1. The food there is *quite* good.
2. We waited *quite* a long time.
3. *Quite* a few people left early.

**2. Completely; entirely — with words that cannot be graded, and in not quite.** (совсем, вполне, совершенно)

4. You're *quite* right.
5. I'm not *quite* ready yet.
6. Are you *quite* sure you want to leave?

**3. Before a, an or the + noun: a remarkable or unusual example of.** (настоящий, что надо, ну и)

7. That was *quite* a party last night!
8. She's *quite* the cook.
9. It's been *quite* a day.

### totally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtoʊtəli/ &middot; *TOH-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** полностью, совершенно
**Type:** degree — a maximizer; also stance (*definitely*) in informal speech
**Position:** before its word — also mid, before a verb (*I totally forgot*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and verbs; alone as a reply
**Form:** -ly from *total*
**Contrast:** False friend: *totally* never means *в общем, итого*. For a sum use *in total*: *In total, we spent $50*, not *Totally we spent $50*. In casual American speech *totally* is also a strong *yes* and a plain intensifier (*totally awesome*); in writing prefer *completely*.

Totally has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; entirely.** (полностью, совершенно)

1. The two plans are *totally* different.
2. I *totally* forgot about the meeting.
3. The town was *totally* destroyed by the storm.

**2. Informal: definitely — used to agree or to add emphasis.** (точно, конечно, ещё как)

4. “That was a great show.” “*Totally*.”
5. I'm *totally* going to win this time.
6. You should *totally* come with us.

### completely

**Pronunciation:** /kəmˈpliːtli/ &middot; *kuhm-PLEET-lee*
**Русский:** полностью, совершенно
**Type:** degree — a maximizer
**Position:** mid or end — before a verb (*I completely forgot*) or after it; before its word with adjectives
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** non-gradable adjectives, verbs, and past participles
**Form:** -ly from *complete*
**Contrast:** Russian *совсем не* is *not ... at all*, not *completely not*: *I don't understand it at all*, not *I completely don't understand it*. *Not completely* means *partly*: *The work isn't completely finished*. Like other maximizers it sounds wrong with gradable adjectives: *completely empty*, but *very tall*, not *completely tall*.

To the fullest degree; totally.

1. The theater was *completely* empty.
2. I *completely* forgot your birthday.
3. She has recovered *completely* from the surgery.

### absolutely

**Pronunciation:** /ˌæbsəˈluːtli/ &middot; *ab-suh-LOOT-lee*
**Русский:** совершенно, абсолютно; безусловно
**Type:** degree — a maximizer for extreme and non-gradable adjectives; also stance (*Absolutely!* as a reply)
**Position:** before its word — mid before a verb (*I absolutely love it*); alone as a reply
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** extreme and non-gradable adjectives, verbs, *no* and *nothing*; alone as a reply
**Form:** -ly from *absolute*
**Contrast:** The gradable/extreme split: *absolutely* goes with extreme or yes-or-no words — *absolutely freezing*, *absolutely exhausted*, *absolutely right* — and sounds wrong with plain gradable ones: *very cold*, not *absolutely cold*. A few strong positive words take both (*absolutely beautiful*). As a one-word reply *Absolutely* is a warm *yes*, and *Absolutely not* is a firm *no*.

Absolutely has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; totally.** (совершенно, абсолютно)

1. I'm *absolutely* exhausted.
2. You're *absolutely* right.
3. There's *absolutely* nothing to eat in this house.

**2. Without any doubt — used to add strong emphasis to a verb or statement.** (обязательно, безусловно, определённо)

4. You *absolutely* have to see this movie.
5. I *absolutely* love your new apartment.
6. It was *absolutely* the best meal I've ever had.

**3. Used alone as a reply: yes, certainly — or, with not, certainly not.** (конечно, безусловно; ни в коем случае)

7. “Can I borrow your pen?” “*Absolutely*.”
8. “Should we give up?” “*Absolutely* not.”

### utterly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʌtərli/ &middot; *UH-tur-lee*
**Русский:** совершенно, крайне
**Type:** degree — a maximizer, mostly for negative ideas
**Position:** before its word — also end with verbs (*failed utterly*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, and past participles
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *utter*
**Contrast:** *Utterly* leans negative: *utterly exhausted*, *utterly ridiculous*, *failed utterly*. With good qualities it is rarer and literary (*utterly charming*), and like other maximizers it does not fit plain gradable words: not *utterly good*.

Completely; to the highest degree.

1. By midnight we were *utterly* exhausted.
2. The idea is *utterly* ridiculous.
3. The plan failed *utterly*.

### entirely

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈtaɪərli/ &middot; *in-TY-ur-lee*
**Русский:** полностью, целиком; исключительно
**Type:** degree — a maximizer; also focusing (*only*, *solely*)
**Position:** before its word — with adjectives; end with verbs (*I agree entirely*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, and phrases (*entirely by accident*)
**Form:** -ly from *entire*
**Contrast:** *Not entirely* is a polite hedge: *I'm not entirely sure* means *I have doubts* (*не совсем уверен*), not *I'm not sure at all*. With a verb it usually goes at the end (*I agree entirely*) or just before the verb (*I entirely agree*).

Entirely has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; fully.** (полностью, совершенно)

1. The two cases are *entirely* different.
2. I agree with you *entirely*.
3. I'm not *entirely* sure what she meant.

**2. Only; solely — with no other cause or person involved.** (исключительно, целиком)

4. The decision is *entirely* up to you.
5. The accident was *entirely* my fault.
6. The shelter is run *entirely* by volunteers.

### thoroughly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈθɜːroʊli/ &middot; *THUR-oh-lee*
**Русский:** тщательно; совершенно
**Type:** degree — a maximizer (*thoroughly enjoyed*); also manner (*carefully and completely*)
**Position:** mid or end — end for *carefully* (*check it thoroughly*); mid for degree (*I thoroughly enjoyed it*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more thoroughly*, *most thoroughly*
**Modifies:** verbs and past participles; a few adjectives (*thoroughly modern*)
**Form:** -ly from *thorough*
**Contrast:** Spelling trap: *thoroughly* comes from *thorough* — keep it apart from *through* and *though*. As a degree word it goes with a short list — *enjoy*, *deserve*, *disgusted*, *ashamed*, *modern* — and is not a general *very*: not *thoroughly tall*.

Thoroughly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Carefully and completely, paying attention to every detail.** (тщательно, досконально)

1. Wash the vegetables *thoroughly* before cooking.
2. The new drug was *thoroughly* tested.
3. Police searched the house *more thoroughly* the second time.

**2. Completely; very much.** (очень, совершенно, от души)

4. We *thoroughly* enjoyed the concert.
5. I was *thoroughly* disgusted by his behavior.
6. She *thoroughly* deserved the award.

### perfectly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɜːrfɪktli/ &middot; *PUR-fikt-lee*
**Русский:** совершенно, вполне; идеально
**Type:** degree — a maximizer, often insistent; also manner (*without fault*)
**Position:** before its word — for degree; end for manner (*fits perfectly*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, *well*, and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *perfect*
**Contrast:** As a degree word *perfectly* often defends something rather than praising it: *a perfectly good sandwich* is one with nothing wrong with it (*вполне нормальный*), not an excellent one. *You know perfectly well* is a reproach (*ты прекрасно знаешь*). The meaning *without any fault* comes after the verb: *She sang perfectly*.

Perfectly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Completely; quite — often used to insist.** (совершенно, вполне)

1. It's *perfectly* normal to feel nervous.
2. Don't throw it out; it's *perfectly* good.
3. You know *perfectly* well what I mean.

**2. In a perfect way; without any mistakes or faults.** (идеально, безупречно, прекрасно)

4. The dress fits *perfectly*.
5. She played the piece *perfectly*.
6. Everything went *perfectly*.

### remarkably

**Pronunciation:** /rɪˈmɑːrkəbli/ &middot; *rih-MAHR-kuh-blee*
**Русский:** удивительно, поразительно
**Type:** degree — an intensifier with surprise; also stance, as a sentence adverb
**Position:** before its word — front when it comments on the whole sentence
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *remarkable*
**Contrast:** False friend: *remarkably* is not Russian *замечательно*. It means *surprisingly, unusually* (*remarkably calm*) and is not a reply meaning *great* — for *Замечательно!* say *Great!* or *Wonderful!* At the front with a comma it comments on the whole fact: *Remarkably, no one noticed*.

Remarkably has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Unusually; to a surprising degree.** (удивительно, необычайно)

1. She stayed *remarkably* calm during the interview.
2. The twins look *remarkably* alike.
3. The old house is in *remarkably* good condition.

**2. Surprisingly — commenting on a whole fact.** (удивительно, но; как ни странно)

4. *Remarkably*, the vase survived the fall.
5. *Remarkably*, nobody complained.

### exceptionally

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈsɛpʃənəli/ &middot; *ik-SEP-shuh-nuh-lee*
**Русский:** исключительно, необычайно
**Type:** degree — an intensifier meaning *unusually*
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and adverbs
**Form:** -ly from *exceptional*
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *исключительно* also means *only*, but *exceptionally* never does. *Исключительно для членов клуба* is *exclusively for members* or *only for members*, not *exceptionally for members*. *Exceptionally* means *much more than normal*: *exceptionally talented*. In formal British English it can also mean *as an exception* (*Exceptionally, the office will open on Sunday*); American English rarely uses it that way.

Unusually; to a degree much greater than normal.

1. She is an *exceptionally* talented pianist.
2. It was an *exceptionally* cold winter.
3. The team played *exceptionally* well.

### bitterly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɪtərli/ &middot; *BIT-ur-lee*
**Русский:** горько; ужасно, резко
**Type:** degree — with a few words (*cold*, *disappointed*, *opposed*); also manner (*with bitterness*)
**Position:** before its word — for degree; end for manner (*complained bitterly*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more bitterly*, *most bitterly*
**Modifies:** a small set of adjectives and past participles; verbs of speaking and feeling
**Form:** -ly from *bitter*
**Contrast:** As a degree word *bitterly* works only with unpleasant things from a short list — *bitterly cold*, *bitterly disappointed*, *bitterly opposed*, *bitterly regret* — so there is no *bitterly hot* or *bitterly happy*. Russian *горько плакать* is *cry bitterly*, but for taste English uses the adjective: *It tastes bitter*, not *It tastes bitterly*.

Bitterly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very, in a painful or unpleasant way — with cold, disappointment and opposition.** (ужасно, крайне, резко)

1. It was *bitterly* cold in Chicago that week.
2. The fans were *bitterly* disappointed by the loss.
3. The new law is *bitterly* opposed by farmers.

**2. With anger, pain or resentment.** (горько, с горечью)

4. She wept *bitterly* at the news.
5. He complained *bitterly* about the service.
6. The two brothers argued *more bitterly* every year.

### way

**Pronunciation:** /weɪ/ &middot; *WAY*
**Русский:** намного, гораздо; далеко
**Type:** degree — informal, before comparatives and *too*; also place and time (*way back*)
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparatives, *too*, *more* and *less*, words of place and time
**Form:** flat — the same form as the noun *way*
**Contrast:** *Very* cannot go before a comparative: say *way better*, *much better* or *far better*, never *very better*. *Way* is the informal choice; use *much* or *far* in writing. The order is *way too expensive*, not *too way expensive*. The replies *No way!* and *Way!* are interjections, and *away* is a different word: *He lives far away*, but *way back in 1995*.

Way has 3 distinct senses.

1. This phone is *way* better than my old one.
2. The tickets were *way* too expensive.

**2. Far — before words of place or time.** (далеко, задолго, аж)

4. They live *way* out in the country.
5. I met him *way* back in high school.
6. We're *way* behind schedule.

**3. Slang: very — before an adjective.** (очень, реально)

7. That movie was *way* cool.
8. That test was *way* hard.

### strongly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstrɔːŋli/ &middot; *STRAWNG-lee*
**Русский:** решительно, настоятельно; сильно
**Type:** degree — with verbs of opinion and feeling; also manner (*with strength*)
**Position:** mid or end — *I strongly agree*, *I feel strongly*
**Comparison:** more/most — *more strongly*, *most strongly*

**Form:** -ly from *strong*

**2. With strength, force or intensity; powerfully.** (сильно, крепко, мощно)

4. The room smelled *strongly* of paint.
5. He is tall and *strongly* built.

### greatly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡreɪtli/ &middot; *GRAYT-lee*
**Русский:** значительно, очень, сильно
**Type:** degree — a formal intensifier for verbs and participles
**Position:** mid or end — *greatly improved*, *has improved greatly*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and past participles
**Form:** -ly from *great*
**Contrast:** *Greatly* is not a general *very*: it goes with verbs and participles (*greatly appreciated*, *differ greatly*), not with plain adjectives — *very happy*, not *greatly happy*. It is also too formal for liking: *I like it very much*, not *I greatly like it*. In casual American speech *great* itself works as an adverb meaning *very well* (*It works great*), which is a different meaning.

Very much; to a large degree.

1. Your help is *greatly* appreciated.
2. Her English has improved *greatly* since last year.
3. The two versions differ *greatly*.
