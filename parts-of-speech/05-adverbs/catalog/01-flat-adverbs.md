# Flat adverbs: the same form as the adjective

> Adverbs with no *-ly* — *fast*, *hard*, *late*, *straight* — that look like adjectives and are not mistakes.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A flat adverb has the same form as its adjective. These are the older English adverbs that survived the spread of *-ly*, and they are fully standard: *drive fast*, *work hard*, *arrive late*. The trap is the reverse assumption — that adding *-ly* always gives the same meaning. For many of these words it gives a different word entirely, and that partner is in group 3. American speech also uses many informal flat adverbs (*drive slow*, *come quick*, *hold tight*) that formal writing avoids; each entry says which kind it is.

---
### fast

**Pronunciation:** /fæst/ &middot; *FAST*
**Русский:** быстро; крепко
**Type:** manner; also degree (*fast asleep*)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *faster*, *fastest*
**Modifies:** verbs; *asleep* in *fast asleep*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *fast*; there is no *fastly*
**Contrast:** There is no *fastly*: *He drives fast*, never *He drives fastly*. The old sense 'firmly' survives in set phrases and has nothing to do with speed: *hold fast* means hold on tight, *stuck fast* means it will not move, and *fast asleep* means deeply asleep, not that someone fell asleep quickly.

Fast has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Quickly; at high speed.** (быстро)

1. Don't talk so *fast* — I can't follow you.
2. She ran *faster* than anyone else on the team.
3. Prices are rising *fast* this year.

**2. Firmly, so that it cannot move or come loose.** (крепко, прочно, намертво)

4. The truck was stuck *fast* in the mud.
5. Hold *fast* to the railing when the boat turns.
6. The senator stood *fast* and refused to change her vote.

**3. Deeply, in *fast asleep*.** (крепко (спать))

7. By nine o'clock the kids were *fast* asleep.
8. He was so *fast* asleep that he didn't hear the alarm.

**4. Ahead of the correct time, of a clock or watch.** (вперёд (часы спешат))

9. The clock in the kitchen runs five minutes *fast*.
10. My watch has been running *fast* ever since I dropped it.

### hard

**Pronunciation:** /hɑːrd/ &middot; *HAHRD*
**Русский:** усердно; сильно
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *harder*, *hardest*
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *hard*; *hardly* is a different word
**Contrast:** *Hardly* is not the adverb of *hard* in meaning: it means 'almost not' (group 3). *She works hard* = она много работает; *She hardly works* = она почти не работает. Russian *тяжело* about bad news is also *hard*: *He took it hard*.

Hard has 5 distinct senses.

**1. With a lot of effort, energy or attention.** (усердно, упорно; как следует)

1. She worked *hard* to pay off her student loans.
2. Think *hard* before you sign that contract.
3. If you try *harder*, you'll make the team next year.

**2. With force; strongly or heavily.** (сильно)

4. It was raining *hard* when we left.
5. He hit the ball *hard* toward left field.
6. Press *hard* on the cut to stop the bleeding.

**3. With pain or distress, about news or a loss.** (тяжело (переживать))

7. She took her father's death very *hard*.
8. Don't take it so *hard* — everyone fails a test sometimes.

**4. As far as possible in one direction; sharply.** (круто, до упора)

9. Turn the wheel *hard* to the right.
10. The pilot banked *hard* left to avoid the storm.

**5. Closely following; right after, in *hard on the heels of*.** (сразу же, вслед за)

11. The second storm came *hard* on the heels of the first.
12. The announcement came *hard* on the heels of a bad quarter.

### late

**Pronunciation:** /leɪt/ &middot; *LAYT*
**Русский:** поздно, с опозданием
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object; before *in*, *at*, *next* in *late at night*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *later*, *latest*
**Modifies:** verbs; time phrases (*late in the day*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *late*
**Contrast:** *Lately* is a different word: it means 'recently, в последнее время' (group 3). *I got home late* = я поздно пришёл домой; *I haven't slept well lately* = в последнее время я плохо сплю. *Later* meaning 'afterward' (*See you later*) is a time adverb of its own (group 6).

Late has 3 distinct senses.

**1. After the expected, usual or proper time.** (поздно, с опозданием)

1. The train got in forty minutes *late*.
2. Sorry, I'm running *late* — start without me.
3. She stayed *later* than everyone else to finish the report.

**2. Near the end of a period of time.** (в конце; поздно)

4. The accident happened *late* at night.
5. He started painting *late* in life.
6. The package should arrive *late* next week.

**3. Recently, in *as late as* and *of late*.** (ещё (совсем недавно))

7. As *late* as 2010, the town had no traffic lights.
8. The house was lived in until as *late* as the 1980s.
9. The senator has been unusually quiet of *late*.

### early

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɜːrli/ &middot; *UR-lee*
**Русский:** рано, заранее
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object; before *in*, *on* in *early in the morning*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *earlier*, *earliest*
**Modifies:** verbs; time phrases (*early in the year*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *early*; the *-ly* is part of the word, so there is no *earlily*
**Contrast:** *Early* is about the clock or calendar — before the usual time (рано, раньше срока); *soon* is about how long from now (скоро). *Come early* = приходите пораньше; *Come soon* = приходите поскорее. Russian *раньше* meaning 'in the past' is *before* or *used to*, not *earlier*: *I used to smoke*. *Earlier* meaning 'ранее, сегодня уже' (*I called you earlier*) has its own entry (group 6).

Early has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Before the usual, expected or planned time.** (рано, раньше срока, заранее)

1. We got to the airport two hours *early*.
2. The baby was born three weeks *early*.
3. Can you come in *earlier* tomorrow?

**2. Near the beginning of a period of time.** (рано; в начале)

4. I get up *early* in the morning.
5. *Early* in her career, she worked as a waitress.
6. We knew *early* on that the plan wouldn't work.

### straight

**Pronunciation:** /streɪt/ &middot; *STRAYT*
**Русский:** прямо; сразу; подряд
**Type:** manner; also direction, time (*straight home*) and duration (*three days straight*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; before *home*, *ahead*, *to*; after a time phrase in *three days straight*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *straighter*, *straightest*; only in the line and posture sense
**Modifies:** verbs; place words (*straight ahead*); time phrases (*five days straight*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *straight*; *straightly* is almost never used
**Contrast:** Russian *прямо* as an intensifier is *right*, not *straight*: *прямо сейчас* = *right now*, *прямо здесь* = *right here*. *Straight* means in a line, without stopping, or honestly: *go straight home*. *Straight away* (сразу) is heard in the US, but *right away* is far more common.

Straight has 6 distinct senses.

**1. In a straight line or upright position; not curving or bending.** (прямо, ровно)

1. Go *straight* for two blocks and turn left at the light.
2. Sit up *straight* and look at the camera.
3. Can you stand a little *straighter*?

**2. Directly, without stopping or delay.** (сразу, прямиком)

4. I went *straight* home after work.
5. He walked *straight* into a glass door.
6. The kids went *straight* to bed.

**3. Honestly; without hiding anything, or, in *go straight*, without breaking the law.** (прямо, откровенно, начистоту; завязать (с криминалом))

7. Just tell me *straight* — am I getting fired?
8. I'll give it to you *straight*: the car isn't worth fixing.
9. After prison he went *straight* and opened a bakery.

**4. Without a break; in a row, after a number and a time word.** (подряд)

10. It rained for five days *straight*.
11. The Yankees have won six games *straight*.

**5. Clearly, in *think straight* and *see straight*.** (ясно, нормально (соображать))

12. I'm so tired I can't think *straight*.
13. After three drinks he couldn't see *straight*.

**6. Without water, ice or anything mixed in, of a drink.** (неразбавленным, в чистом виде)

14. He drinks his whiskey *straight*.
15. Do you want your bourbon *straight* or on the rocks?

### long

**Pronunciation:** /lɔːŋ/ &middot; *LAWNG*
**Русский:** долго; давно
**Type:** duration; also time (*long ago*)
**Position:** end — *Did you wait long?*; mid in *have long suspected*; before *ago*, *before*, *after*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *longer*, *longest*
**Modifies:** verbs; *ago*, *before*, *after*, *since*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *long*
**Contrast:** In affirmative statements American English says *a long time*, not bare *long*: *I waited a long time*, not *I waited long*. Bare *long* is normal in questions and negatives (*Did you wait long?*, *It won't take long*) and after *too*, *so*, *as* (*I stayed too long*). *Long* is about time; for distance use *far* or *a long way* — sports talk is the exception (*throw long*, *the putt went long*). *As long as* (если только; пока) is a conjunction.

Long has 4 distinct senses.

**1. For a long time.** (долго)

1. Have you been waiting *long*?
2. It won't take *long* to fix.
3. Can you stay a little *longer*?

**2. A long time before or after a point, or for a long time up to now.** (давно; задолго)

4. The house was built *long* ago.
5. We were friends *long* before we started dating.
6. Scientists have *long* suspected a link between the two.

**3. Throughout a whole period, in *all day long*, *all year long*.** (весь (день) напролёт)

7. The baby cried all night *long*.
8. The pool is open all summer *long*.

**4. Any more, in *no longer* and *not any longer*.** (больше не, уже не)

9. She no *longer* works here.
10. I can't wait any *longer*.

### high

**Pronunciation:** /haɪ/ &middot; *HY*
**Русский:** высоко
**Type:** place; also degree (*aim high*)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *higher*, *highest*
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *high*
**Contrast:** *Highly* does not mean 'at a great height': it means 'very' or 'very well' (*highly recommended*, *think highly of* — group 3). *The kite flew high*, never *flew highly*; *a highly successful company*, never *a high successful company*.

High has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At or to a great height.** (высоко)

1. The eagle circled *high* above the canyon.
2. Throw the ball *higher* so I can catch it.
3. Our office is *high* up on the fortieth floor.

**2. At a high level, rank, amount or pitch.** (высоко; на высоком уровне)

4. Aim *high* and don't settle for less.
5. The company ranks *high* on lists of the best places to work.
6. She can sing much *higher* than her sister.

**3. Everywhere, in *high and low*.** (повсюду, везде)

7. We searched *high* and low for the keys.
8. They looked *high* and low but never found the dog.

### low

**Pronunciation:** /loʊ/ &middot; *LOH*
**Русский:** низко
**Type:** place; also degree (*buy low*)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *lower*, *lowest*
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *low*
**Contrast:** *Lowly* is an adjective meaning 'humble, of low rank' (*a lowly clerk*), not the adverb of *low*. *Lower* is also a verb: *Lower your voice* = говорите тише.

Low has 4 distinct senses.

**1. At or to a low position; near the ground.** (низко)

1. The plane flew *low* over the fields.
2. Bend *lower* and keep your back straight.
3. The sun hung *low* in the sky.

**2. At a low level, amount, price or setting.** (низко; дёшево; на малом огне)

4. Buy *low* and sell high.
5. Turn the heat down *low* and let it simmer.
6. Cook the ribs *low* and slow for about six hours.

**3. Quietly, or at a deep pitch.** (тихо; низко (о голосе))

7. They talked *low* so the kids wouldn't hear.
8. He can sing *lower* than anyone in the choir.

**4. Out of sight, so as not to attract attention, in *lie low*.** (затаиться, залечь на дно)

9. The suspect is probably lying *low* until things calm down.
10. Stay home and lie *low* for a couple of days.

### deep

**Pronunciation:** /diːp/ &middot; *DEEP*
**Русский:** глубоко
**Type:** place; also time (*deep into the night*) and degree
**Position:** end — after the verb; before *into*, *under* phrases
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *deeper*, *deepest*
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *deep*
**Contrast:** For feelings and degree use *deeply*: *deeply sorry*, *deeply moved*, *I deeply regret it* — never *deep sorry* (group 3). Flat *deep* is for physical depth or distance and a few set phrases: *dig deep*, *deep into the night*, *run deep*. With *breathe* both work: *breathe deep*, *breathe deeply*.

Deep has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Far down, far in, or far from the front or surface.** (глубоко; далеко)

1. The roots go *deep* into the soil.
2. They hiked *deeper* into the woods.
3. The quarterback threw *deep* to the end zone.

**2. Far into a period of time.** (до поздней (ночи), до самого (конца))

4. We talked *deep* into the night.
5. The game went *deep* into overtime.

**3. At a deep level of feeling or effort, in *dig deep* and *run deep*.** (изо всех сил; глубоко (укоренившись))

6. The team had to dig *deep* to win in the final minutes.
7. Old grudges run *deep* in this town.

### near

**Pronunciation:** /nɪr/ &middot; *NEER*
**Русский:** близко, рядом; почти
**Type:** place; also time and degree (*near perfect*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; before its word in *near perfect*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *nearer*, *nearest*
**Modifies:** verbs; adjectives (*near impossible*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *near*
**Contrast:** *Near* is usually a preposition (*near the station*); it is an adverb only when no noun follows (*come near*). For location, everyday American speech prefers *nearby*: *They live nearby*. For 'almost', formal writing uses *nearly* (group 3): *nearly impossible*, not *near impossible*. *Nowhere near* (далеко не) is informal but standard.

Near has 3 distinct senses.

**1. At or to a short distance in space or time.** (близко, рядом)

1. Don't come any *nearer* — the dog bites.
2. As the wedding drew *near*, she got more nervous.
3. The deer came *near* enough to eat from my hand.

**2. Almost; very nearly, informal, before an adjective.** (почти)

4. It was *near* impossible to find parking.
5. The team played a *near* perfect game.

**3. Not at all, in *nowhere near* and *not anywhere near*.** (далеко не, и близко не)

6. The house is nowhere *near* finished.
7. We don't have anywhere *near* enough chairs.

### far

**Pronunciation:** /fɑːr/ &middot; *FAHR*
**Русский:** далеко; намного
**Type:** place; also degree (*far better*) and time (*so far*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; before its word in *far better*, *far too*
**Comparison:** irregular — *farther*, *farthest*; also *further*, *furthest*
**Modifies:** verbs; comparatives and *too* (*far better*, *far too*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *far*; compares irregularly
**Contrast:** In affirmative statements bare *far* sounds formal; Americans say *a long way*: *We walked a long way*. *Far* is normal in questions and negatives (*Is it far?*, *It's not far*) and with *too*, *so*, *away*, *from*. Before a comparative use *far* or *much*, never *very*: *far better*, not *very better*. *Far from* + adjective means 'not at all': *far from perfect* = далеко не идеальный. For distance American English prefers *farther*; *further* also means 'more, in addition' (group 2).

Far has 5 distinct senses.

**1. At or to a great distance.** (далеко)

1. How *far* is the airport from here?
2. They don't live *far* from us.
3. We drove *farther* than we'd planned.

**2. Much; by a large amount, before a comparative or *too*, and in *by far*.** (намного, гораздо; слишком)

4. This version is *far* better than the last one.
5. The rent is *far* too high.
6. She's by *far* the best player on the team.

**3. To a particular point, degree or stage.** (насколько; далеко (зайти))

7. How *far* did you get with the reading?
8. This time he's gone too *far*.
9. As *far* as I know, the store is still open.

**4. Up to now, in *so far* and *thus far*.** (пока, до сих пор)

10. So *far*, nobody has complained.
11. Thus *far* the plan is working.

**5. A long way in time, before *into*, *back* and *ahead*.** (далеко (во времени); до поздней (ночи); ещё (в))

12. The party went on *far* into the night.
13. Her family's roots go as *far* back as the 1700s.
14. It's too early to plan that *far* ahead.

### wrong

**Pronunciation:** /rɔːŋ/ &middot; *RAWNG*
**Русский:** неправильно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — only after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *wrong*
**Contrast:** Flat *wrong* goes only after the verb or object: *You spelled it wrong*. Before a verb or participle use *wrongly*: *He was wrongly accused*. *Wrongly* also carries the idea of 'unfairly, by mistake' (group 3). *Don't get me wrong* = не поймите меня неправильно.

Wrong has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Incorrectly; in a mistaken way.** (неправильно, не так)

1. You spelled my name *wrong*.
2. I guessed *wrong* about the weather.
3. Don't get me *wrong* — I like her a lot.

**2. Badly, so that a problem happens, in *go wrong*.** (не так (пойти); сломаться)

4. Something went *wrong* with the engine.
5. What could possibly go *wrong*?
6. Where did we go *wrong* with this project?

### right

**Pronunciation:** /raɪt/ &middot; *RYT*
**Русский:** прямо, как раз; сразу; правильно
**Type:** focusing; also time (*right away*), manner (*do it right*), degree (*right to the end*) and direction (*turn right*)
**Position:** before its word — *right here*, *right after lunch*; end in *do it right*, *turn right*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** place and time words and phrases; verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *right*
**Contrast:** Russian *прямо* before a place or time word is *right*, not *straight*: *right now*, *right here*, *right in front of you*. *Right now* means 'at this moment'; *just now* means 'a moment ago'. *Rightly* (group 3) means 'justifiably': *She was rightly proud*; *You guessed right* uses the flat form. *Right?* at the end of a sentence is a question tag, and *Right.* alone is an interjection.

Right has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Exactly; directly, before a place or time word.** (прямо, как раз, точно)

1. The keys were *right* here on the table.
2. She was sitting *right* in front of me.
3. Call me *right* after the meeting.

**2. Immediately; without delay, in *right away*, *right now*, *right back*.** (сразу, сейчас же)

4. I need those files *right* away.
5. Hold on, I'll be *right* back.
6. Stop that *right* now!

**3. Correctly; as it should be.** (правильно, как надо)

7. Did I pronounce that *right*?
8. Nothing is going *right* today.
9. If I remember *right*, the party starts at eight.

**4. All the way; completely.** (до конца, насквозь, совсем)

10. The nail went *right* through the board.
11. Read the contract *right* to the end.
12. He turned *right* around and walked out.

**5. To or toward the right side.** (направо)

13. Turn *right* at the second light.
14. Look left and *right* before you cross.

### direct

**Pronunciation:** /dəˈrɛkt/ &middot; *duh-REKT*
**Русский:** прямо, напрямую
**Type:** manner; also direction
**Position:** end — after the verb (*fly direct*, *buy direct*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of travel, buying and contact
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *direct*
**Contrast:** Flat *direct* is used mainly after verbs of travel and dealing: *fly direct*, *buy direct*, *call direct*. *Directly* covers everything else and is the only choice before an adjective or verb: *directly affected*, *directly opposite*. *Directly* can also mean 'very soon' (*I'll be there directly*, older and Southern US). Both /dəˈrɛkt/ and /daɪˈrɛkt/ are standard American pronunciations.

Direct has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Without stopping or changing along the way.** (прямым рейсом, без пересадок)

1. You can fly *direct* from Chicago to Tokyo.
2. This bus goes *direct* to the airport.

**2. Without anyone or anything in between.** (напрямую, без посредников)

3. We buy our coffee *direct* from the farmers.
4. If there's a problem, contact me *direct*.
5. The payment goes *direct* into your account.

### fair

**Pronunciation:** /fɛr/ &middot; *FAIR*
**Русский:** честно, по правилам
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — after the verb (*play fair*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs (*play*, *fight*, *win*)
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *fair*
**Contrast:** Flat *fair* lives in a few phrases: *play fair*, *fight fair*, *fair and square*. Elsewhere use *fairly*, which usually means 'rather' (*fairly good* = довольно хороший) and only sometimes 'justly' (*treat people fairly*) — group 3. Russian *честно говоря* is *honestly* or *to be fair*, not *fair*.

According to the rules; honestly and without cheating.

1. You have to play *fair*, even when you're losing.
2. They won the election *fair* and square.
3. Couples therapy taught them how to fight *fair*.

### free

**Pronunciation:** /friː/ &middot; *FREE*
**Русский:** бесплатно; на свободу
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *free*
**Contrast:** *Free* after a verb means 'for no money': *Kids eat free*. *Freely* means 'without limits or control': *speak freely* (говорить открыто), *freely available* (group 3). Russian *свободно говорить по-английски* is *speak English fluently*, not *freely*. *For free* is informal but normal in American English; *free of charge* is formal.

Free has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Without payment.** (бесплатно)

1. Children under five ride *free*.
2. I got these tickets *free* from work.
3. Seniors get in *free* on Tuesdays.

**2. Out of a hold or away from a restraint; loose.** (на свободу; свободно)

4. The dog pulled *free* of its leash.
5. She finally broke *free* of the crowd.
6. Wild horses run *free* on the island.

### wide

**Pronunciation:** /waɪd/ &middot; *WYD*
**Русский:** широко; настежь; мимо
**Type:** degree; also place (*far and wide*)
**Position:** end — *open wide*; before its word in *wide open*, *wide awake*
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *wider*, *widest*
**Modifies:** verbs; *open*, *awake*, *apart*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *wide*
**Contrast:** Flat *wide* is physical: *open wide*, *wide open*, *wide awake*. For how many people or how much, use *widely*: *widely known*, *widely used*, *prices vary widely* — never *wide known* (group 3). *Wide open* can also mean 'undecided': *The race is wide open*.

Wide has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Fully; to the full extent.** (широко; настежь; полностью)

1. Open *wider*, please — I can't see the tooth.
2. The front door was *wide* open.
3. At three in the morning I was still *wide* awake.

**2. Away from the target; off to one side.** (мимо (цели))

4. His kick went *wide*, and the game ended in a tie.
5. The shot sailed *wide* of the net.

**5. Below the correct pitch, in music.** (ниже тона, занижая)

11. She sang a little *flat* on the high notes.
12. The trumpets came in *flat* in the second verse.

### full

**Pronunciation:** /fʊl/ &middot; *FUUL*
**Русский:** прекрасно; прямо
**Type:** degree; also manner (*full in the face*)
**Position:** before its word — *full well*; end in *hit him full in the face*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** *well*; *in the face*, *in the eye*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *full*
**Contrast:** The everyday adverb is *fully*: *fully booked*, *I fully agree* — never *I full agree* (group 3). Flat *full* survives only in a few fixed phrases: *know full well*, *full in the face*. *I'm full* (я наелся) is the adjective.

Full has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very well; perfectly, in *full well*.** (прекрасно, отлично (знать))

1. You know *full* well what I mean.
2. She knew *full* well that the check would bounce.

**2. Directly; squarely.** (прямо)

3. The ball hit him *full* in the face.
4. She looked him *full* in the eye and said no.

### alike

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlaɪk/ &middot; *uh-LYK*
**Русский:** одинаково; как…, так и
**Type:** manner; also focusing (*young and old alike*)
**Position:** end — after the verb, or after a pair of nouns
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; pairs of nouns (*kids and adults alike*)
**Form:** a- word — like *alone* and *asleep*, never used before a noun
**Contrast:** *Alike* never stands before a noun: *similar houses*, not *alike houses*. After *look* or *be* it is the adjective: *The twins look alike*. Compare *like*, a preposition that needs an object: *She looks like her mother*, but *She and her mother look alike*.

Alike has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In the same or a very similar way.** (одинаково)

1. The twins always dress *alike*.
2. Great minds think *alike*.
3. The teacher treats all her students *alike*.

**2. Equally; both, after two or more nouns.** (как…, так и; в равной мере)

4. The show is popular with kids and adults *alike*.
5. Rich and poor *alike* were hit by the recession.

### alone

**Pronunciation:** /əˈloʊn/ &middot; *uh-LOHN*
**Русский:** один, в одиночку; только
**Type:** manner; also focusing (*you alone*)
**Position:** end — *live alone*; after its word in *you alone*, *money alone*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; nouns and pronouns (*money alone*)
**Form:** a- word — never used before a noun (*a lone rider*, *a lonely house*)
**Contrast:** *Alone* means 'without other people' with no feeling attached; *lonely* is an adjective meaning 'sad because you are alone': *I live alone, but I'm not lonely*. *Leave me alone* = оставь меня в покое. *Let alone* (не говоря уже о) links two phrases: *He can't boil an egg, let alone cook dinner*.

Alone has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Without other people.** (один, в одиночку)

1. She has lived *alone* since her husband died.
2. I don't like walking home *alone* at night.
3. You shouldn't try to move that couch *alone*.

**2. Only; and nobody or nothing else, after a noun or pronoun.** (только, лишь; одно только)

4. You *alone* can make that decision.
5. Money *alone* won't solve the problem.
6. In July *alone*, the company hired 300 people.
