The -ly form that means something else#

hardly, lately, nearly, highly — the -ly forms whose meaning drifted away from the flat adverb they look like.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Adding -ly to hard does not give a more formal way of saying with effort — it gives hardly, which means almost not. The same drift happened to late/lately, near/nearly, high/highly and a dozen more. These are among the most frequent errors in learner English, because the spelling promises a relationship the meaning no longer has. Every entry names its flat partner and says exactly how they differ.

hardly#

Pronunciation
/ˈhɑːrdli/ · HAHRD-lee
Русский
едва, почти не; вряд ли
Type
degree — a downtoner with negative force; also stance and time (hardly … when)
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; front with inversion (Hardly had…)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, adverbs, nouns and quantifiers (hardly anyone, hardly ever)
Form
-ly from hard, but not its meaning
Contrast
hard (flat) = with effort or force: She works hard (много, усердно работает). hardly = almost not: She hardly works (почти не работает). hardly is already negative — I can hardly see, not I can't hardly see — and takes any and ever: hardly anyone, hardly ever, not hardly nobody. Fronted, it inverts and pairs with when (not than): Hardly had we left when it started to rain.

Hardly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Almost not; only a very small amount or degree. (едва, почти не)

  1. I could hardly hear her over the music.
  2. There's hardly any coffee left.
  3. We hardly ever see each other now that she lives in Denver.

2. Certainly not — used to say that something is obviously untrue, unreasonable or unsuitable. (вряд ли, едва ли, никак не)

  1. It's hardly surprising that he quit.
  2. This is hardly the time to talk about money.
  3. "Did you enjoy the meeting?" "Hardly."

3. Only just, a moment before something else happened — in hardly … when. (едва (как), только… как)

  1. We had hardly sat down when the lights went out.
  2. Hardly had the game started when it began to pour.

lately#

Pronunciation
/ˈleɪtli/ · LAYT-lee
Русский
в последнее время
Type
time — recently, over a period up to now
Position
front or end — usually at the end; mid in formal writing (has lately been)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Form
-ly from late, but it means recently
Contrast
late (flat) = after the usual or expected time: She got up late (поздно). lately = recently, over a stretch of time up to now: She's been getting up late lately. Because it covers a period up to now, lately goes with the present perfect — I haven't seen him lately — and not with one finished event: I saw him recently, not I saw him lately. Russian поздно is always late, never lately.

Recently; during the period of time up to now.

  1. Have you talked to Mike lately?
  2. I've been feeling really tired lately.
  3. Lately, prices at our grocery store have gone way up.

nearly#

Pronunciation
/ˈnɪrli/ · NEER-lee
Русский
почти, чуть не
Type
degree — almost, but not quite
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; before its word with adjectives, numbers and quantifiers (nearly empty, nearly 300)
Comparison
not gradable — though very nearly occurs
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers and quantifiers (nearly all, nearly everyone)
Form
-ly from near, but it means almost, not close by
Contrast
near (flat) = close in space or time: Don't sit so near; The exam is getting near. nearly = almost: I nearly fell (чуть не упал). nearly the station is wrong — near the station, where near is a preposition. nearly and almost swap freely with numbers and verbs, but only almost goes with no, never and nobody: almost nobody came, not nearly nobody. And not nearly (далеко не) has no almost version.

Nearly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Almost; not completely, or very close to happening. (почти; чуть не)

  1. It's nearly midnight — we should go.
  2. I nearly missed my flight.
  3. Nearly 40 percent of the students commute to campus.

2. In not nearly: far from; much less than. (далеко не, совсем не)

  1. There aren't nearly enough chairs for everyone.
  2. The sequel is not nearly as good as the original.

highly#

Pronunciation
/ˈhaɪli/ · HY-lee
Русский
весьма, крайне; высоко
Type
degree — very, to a high level; also manner (speak highly of)
Position
before its word — before an adjective or participle (highly unlikely); mid before recommend (I highly recommend it); end after verbs of judging (think highly of)
Comparison
more/most — more highly, most highly
Modifies
adjectives and past participles; verbs of judging (think, speak, rate, regard)
Form
-ly from high, but not its meaning of height
Contrast
high (flat) = at or to a high point in space: fly high, jump high, aim high. highly = very, or with a good opinion: highly intelligent, think highly of her. The plane flew highly and jump highly are wrong. highly keeps to fixed partners — highly unlikely, highly recommended, highly qualified, highly paid — and does not replace very with everyday adjectives: very tall, not highly tall.

Highly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Very; to a high degree. (весьма, крайне, очень)

  1. It's highly unlikely that the store is open on Thanksgiving.
  2. She's a highly skilled surgeon.
  3. I highly recommend the fish tacos.

2. With approval; with a good opinion of someone or something. (высоко, хорошо (отзываться, ценить))

  1. Everyone at work speaks highly of her.
  2. I don't think very highly of his plan.
  3. The book was highly praised by critics.

3. At a high level of pay, rank or importance. (высоко (оплачиваемый, поставленный))

  1. Doctors are more highly paid than teachers.
  2. The story came from a highly placed source in the White House.

deeply#

Pronunciation
/ˈdiːpli/ · DEEP-lee
Русский
глубоко, очень
Type
degree — very strongly, of feelings and beliefs; also manner
Position
mid or end — before a verb or participle (deeply regret, deeply moved), after the verb (breathe deeply)
Comparison
more/most — more deeply, most deeply
Modifies
verbs and past participles of feeling and belief; verbs like breathe, sleep, think
Form
-ly from deep
Contrast
deep (flat) = far down or far into, mostly with verbs of movement and in fixed phrases: dig deep, go deep into the woods, still waters run deep, and in the comparative dig deeper. deeply = intensely: deeply moved, deeply regret; it also goes with breathe, sleep and think. I deep regret is wrong — I deeply regret. For a real hole, They dug deep, not dug deeply.

Deeply has 2 distinct senses.

1. Very much; strongly or seriously — of feelings and beliefs, and of states like flawed, unpopular and in debt. (глубоко, очень, искренне; крайне)

  1. We are deeply sorry for your loss.
  2. The plan is deeply flawed.
  3. Many Americans feel deeply about gun laws.

2. Far down or far in; to a great depth — also of breathing, sleep and serious thought. (глубоко; крепко (спать))

  1. Close your eyes and breathe deeply.
  2. A piece of glass cut deeply into his hand.
  3. I haven't thought deeply about it yet.

closely#

Pronunciation
/ˈkloʊsli/ · KLOHS-lee
Русский
внимательно; тесно; близко
Type
manner — carefully; also degree (closely related)
Position
end — after the verb and object (watch closely); before its word with participles (closely related)
Comparison
more/most — more closely, most closely
Modifies
verbs of watching, working and following; participles like related, linked, guarded
Form
-ly from the adjective close (near), said with /s/
Contrast
close (flat) = near: Come close, Stay close to me, We live close to the school. closely = carefully, or tightly connected: Watch closely, closely related. Stay closely to me is wrong. Both are said with /s/ like the adjective close, not /z/ like the verb close (закрывать).

Closely has 4 distinct senses.

1. Carefully, paying close attention. (внимательно, пристально, тщательно)

  1. Listen closely — I'm only going to say this once.
  2. Police are watching the situation closely.
  3. The recipe is a closely guarded family secret.

2. In a close relationship or with a strong connection; working together a lot. (тесно)

  1. We work closely with local schools.
  2. Stress and poor sleep are closely linked.

3. Near in space or time, with little distance or gap. (близко, вплотную, плотно)

  1. A black SUV was following closely behind us.
  2. The houses on our street are closely packed together.
  3. The storm was closely followed by a heat wave.

4. Very nearly; with only a small difference. (очень (похоже), почти в точности; с небольшим перевесом)

  1. The copy closely resembles the original.
  2. It was one of the most closely contested elections in the state's history.

freely#

Pronunciation
/ˈfriːli/ · FREE-lee
Русский
свободно; открыто; щедро
Type
manner — without limits or control
Position
end — after the verb and object; mid before admit and give (freely admits); before its word with participles and adjectives (freely available)
Comparison
more/most — more freely, most freely
Modifies
verbs of movement, speech and giving; adjectives like available
Form
-ly from free, but without its no cost meaning
Contrast
free (flat) = without paying: Kids under five ride free (бесплатно). freely = without limits: Kids can move freely around the museum (свободно). Mixing them changes the meaning. And Russian свободно говорить по-английски is speak English fluently, not freely — speak freely means to say openly what you think.

Freely has 4 distinct senses.

1. Without being controlled, stopped or limited. (свободно, беспрепятственно)

  1. Once the cast came off, I could move my arm freely.
  2. The information is freely available online.
  3. Traffic is moving more freely now that the accident has been cleared.

2. Openly and honestly, without fear; willingly. (открыто, откровенно; охотно)

  1. You can speak freely — nobody here will repeat it.
  2. He freely admits he made mistakes.

3. Generously; in large amounts. (щедро, обильно)

  1. The wine flowed freely at the wedding.
  2. They gave freely to the church and the food bank.

4. Loosely, not exactly — of translating or adapting a text. (вольно, нестрого)

  1. The movie is freely adapted from a nineteenth-century novel.
  2. The poem was freely translated from Spanish.

widely#

Pronunciation
/ˈwaɪdli/ · WYD-lee
Русский
широко, повсеместно; сильно
Type
degree — by many people, or by a lot; also place and manner
Position
before its word — before a participle (widely used); end after verbs (vary widely, travel widely)
Comparison
more/most — more widely, most widely
Modifies
past participles (used, known, spaced); verbs like vary, differ, travel, grin
Form
-ly from wide
Contrast
wide (flat) = fully, to the full extent: open wide, wide awake, wide open, far and wide. widely = by many people, or a lot: widely known, vary widely. Open your mouth widely is wrong — open wide; a wide known fact is wrong — a widely known fact.

Widely has 4 distinct senses.

1. By a lot of people or in a lot of places. (широко, повсеместно)

  1. It's widely believed that the policy failed.
  2. The app is widely used by small businesses.
  3. Spanish is the most widely spoken language in the U.S. after English.

2. Over a large area; to many different places. (много, повсюду (путешествовать, распространяться))

  1. She has traveled widely in Asia.
  2. The seeds are widely scattered by the wind.

3. By a large amount; very differently. (сильно, значительно)

  1. Prices vary widely from store to store.
  2. The two reports come to widely different conclusions.

4. With a wide opening or a big gap — of smiling, and of things set far apart. (широко; далеко (друг от друга))

  1. He grinned widely when he saw his test score.
  2. The trees are widely spaced so the grass gets plenty of sun.

mostly#

Pronunciation
/ˈmoʊstli/ · MOHST-lee
Русский
в основном; обычно
Type
degree — mainly, for the most part; also frequency
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be; before its word (mostly students)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, nouns and phrases (mostly in the evening)
Form
-ly from most, but without its superlative meaning
Contrast
most (flat) = more than anything else, and the superlative marker: What I miss most is the food; the most expensive. mostly = mainly: The food was mostly vegetarian. What I miss mostly and the mostly expensive are errors. Russian больше всего → most; в основном → mostly. Don't confuse it with most of: Most of the guests were teachers = The guests were mostly teachers.

Mostly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Mainly; for the most part. (в основном, преимущественно)

  1. The crowd was mostly college students.
  2. The sauce is mostly butter and garlic.
  3. I agree with you — mostly.

2. Usually; most of the time. (обычно, чаще всего)

  1. We mostly eat dinner at home.
  2. On weekends I mostly just sleep in.

shortly#

Pronunciation
/ˈʃɔːrtli/ · SHORT-lee
Русский
вскоре, скоро; резко
Type
time — soon; also manner
Position
mid or end — at the end (see you shortly) or after the first auxiliary (will shortly arrive); before after and before (shortly after noon)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; the words after and before
Form
-ly from short, but it means soon, not briefly
Contrast
short (flat) = suddenly, or before the end: stop short, cut the meeting short (прервать), fall short, run short of cash. shortly = soon: I'll call you shortly. cut shortly is wrong. shortly is not briefly: speak briefly = говорить коротко, but She'll speak shortly = она скоро выступит, and only after a quote does it mean резко: "No," she said shortly. shortly ago is wrong — use a short time ago or recently.

Shortly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Soon; in a short time, or a short time before or after something. (вскоре, скоро)

  1. The doctor will be with you shortly.
  2. Shortly after we moved to Chicago, I found a job.
  3. The last train leaves shortly before midnight.

2. In a short, impatient or unfriendly way. (резко, отрывисто, сухо)

  1. "I'm busy," she said shortly.
  2. The clerk answered shortly, without looking up from her phone.

fairly#

Pronunciation
/ˈfɛrli/ · FAIR-lee
Русский
довольно; честно, справедливо
Type
degree — moderately; also manner (in a fair way)
Position
before its word — before an adjective or adverb (fairly easy); end after the verb in the justly sense (treat people fairly)
Comparison
more/most — only in the justly sense (more fairly, most fairly); the degree sense is not gradable
Modifies
adjectives and adverbs (degree); verbs (manner)
Form
-ly from fair; the two main meanings are unrelated
Contrast
fair (flat) = by the rules: play fair, fight fair. fairly means either that (treat people fairly) or moderately (fairly good) — position tells you which: right before an adjective it is degree. In American speech fairly good is a cool, limited compliment, weaker than pretty good; quite good is stronger in AmE than in BrE. fairly does not go before too or a comparative: a bit too big, a little bigger, not fairly too big or fairly bigger.

Fairly has 3 distinct senses.

1. More than a little but less than very; to a moderate degree. (довольно, достаточно)

  1. The test was fairly easy.
  2. I know him fairly well.
  3. We live in a fairly quiet neighborhood.

2. In a fair, honest or equal way; with good reason. (честно, справедливо; по праву)

  1. All employees must be treated fairly.
  2. The work was divided fairly among the team.
  3. It can fairly be said that she saved the company.

3. Actually, almost literally — a literary intensifier before a strong verb. (буквально, прямо-таки)

  1. The whole house fairly shook when the train went by.
  2. She was fairly bursting with pride.

rightly#

Pronunciation
/ˈraɪtli/ · RYT-lee
Русский
справедливо, по праву; верно
Type
stance — with good reason; also manner
Position
mid — before the main verb or participle, after be; also in and rightly so
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, adjectives and whole clauses
Form
-ly from right
Contrast
right (flat) = correctly, after the verb: You guessed right, spell it right, Nothing goes right. rightly comes before the verb or participle and judges the action as justified or accurate: You rightly guessed that…. right also means exactly, directly and immediately (right here, right away) — rightly never does: rightly now is wrong. I don't rightly know is informal, heard mostly in the South and rural US.

Rightly has 3 distinct senses.

1. With good reason; justly. (справедливо, по праву, не зря)

  1. Parents are rightly worried about screen time.
  2. She was angry, and rightly so.
  3. Rightly or wrongly, he blamed himself.

2. Correctly; accurately. (верно, правильно)

  1. As you rightly pointed out, the numbers don't add up.
  2. The doctor rightly suspected a food allergy.

3. For sure, with certainty — informal, with a negative. (толком, точно)

  1. I don't rightly know where he went.
  2. I can't rightly say when they'll be back.

justly#

Pronunciation
/ˈdʒʌstli/ · JUHST-lee
Русский
справедливо; заслуженно
Type
manner — fairly; also stance (justly famous)
Position
end — after the verb (treated justly); before its word with adjectives and participles (justly famous)
Comparison
more/most — more justly, most justly
Modifies
verbs; adjectives like famous, proud, celebrated
Form
-ly from the adjective just (fair), not from the adverb just
Contrast
The flat just is unrelated in meaning: just = только что, только, как раз, просто. justly comes from the adjective just = справедливый. He was just punished = его только что наказали; He was justly punished = его наказали справедливо. justly is formal; everyday speech says fairly or rightly.

Justly has 2 distinct senses.

1. In a fair way; according to what is morally right. (справедливо)

  1. A good judge treats everyone justly.
  2. The land was justly divided among the heirs.
  3. People want leaders who will govern justly.

2. Deservedly; with good reason. (по праву, заслуженно)

  1. The restaurant is justly famous for its barbecue.
  2. She is justly proud of her students.

prettily#

Pronunciation
/ˈprɪtəli/ · PRIT-uh-lee
Русский
мило, изящно
Type
manner — in a pretty, charming way
Position
end — after the verb (smiled prettily); before its word with participles (prettily decorated)
Comparison
more/most — more prettily, most prettily
Modifies
verbs; past participles like decorated, arranged
Form
-ly from the adjective pretty, with y changed to i
Contrast
pretty (flat) = fairly, quite — one of the most common degree adverbs in American speech: pretty good, pretty fast (довольно). prettily keeps the adjective's meaning, charmingly (мило). She sings pretty well = она поёт довольно хорошо; She sings prettily = она мило поёт. Before another adjective or an adverb, pretty almost always means fairly, not attractive: pretty ugly = довольно некрасивый.

In a pretty, charming or pleasantly neat way.

  1. The little girl smiled prettily for the camera.
  2. The cake was prettily decorated with fresh flowers.
  3. She asked so prettily that nobody could say no.

surely#

Pronunciation
/ˈʃʊrli/ · SHUUR-lee
Русский
неужели; наверняка; верно
Type
stance — the speaker expects agreement or is surprised; also manner (slowly but surely)
Position
any position — front to ask for agreement (Surely you…), mid for certainly (will surely win), end in slowly but surely
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses; verbs
Form
-ly from sure; the flat sure is the everyday American adverb
Contrast
At the front of a sentence, surely is not a calm certainly: Surely you know him? shows surprise or a hope for agreement (неужели не знаешь? / ты же его знаешь?) — to open with a plain fact use certainly or definitely. Only in mid position does it mean наверняка: The plan will surely fail. In everyday American speech the flat sure does the rest: Sure, I'll help (конечно), It sure is hot (ну и жара). As a one-word yes, Surely. sounds old-fashioned — say Sure.

Surely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Used to show that you believe something is true and want agreement, or are surprised that it might not be. (неужели; ведь, конечно же)

  1. Surely you don't expect me to pay for that?
  2. Surely someone at the front desk can help us.
  3. "I lost the tickets." "Surely not!"

2. Certainly; without any doubt. (наверняка, несомненно)

  1. Without more funding, the program will surely fail.
  2. This is surely one of the best pizzas in New York.

3. Steadily and without failing — mostly in slowly but surely. (верно, уверенно)

  1. Slowly but surely, the team climbed back into the game.
  2. Her English is improving slowly but surely.

scarcely#

Pronunciation
/ˈskɛrsli/ · SKAIRS-lee
Русский
едва, почти не; вряд ли
Type
degree — almost not, with negative force; also stance and time (scarcely … when)
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; front with inversion (Scarcely had…)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, numbers and quantifiers (scarcely anyone)
Form
-ly from scarce, but it does not mean rare
Contrast
scarcely already carries the negative: I could scarcely breathe, not I couldn't scarcely breathe; it takes any and ever: scarcely anyone, scarcely ever. Fronted, it inverts the subject and verb, and the pair is scarcely … when (not than): Scarcely had she sat down when…. It does not mean in short supply — that is the adjective scarce: Jobs are scarce (рабочих мест мало), not Jobs are scarcely. More formal than hardly.

Scarcely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Almost not; only just. (едва, почти не)

  1. I could scarcely believe my eyes.
  2. There was scarcely anyone on the beach.
  3. He was scarcely twenty when he started his first company.

2. Certainly not — used to say that something is obviously untrue, unreasonable or unsuitable. (вряд ли, едва ли)

  1. It's scarcely a secret that they don't get along.
  2. A parking ticket is scarcely a reason to move to another state.

3. Only just before something else happened — in scarcely … when. (едва (как), только… как)

  1. Scarcely had we arrived when it started to snow.
  2. She had scarcely hung up when the phone rang again.

evenly#

Pronunciation
/ˈiːvənli/ · EE-vuhn-lee
Русский
поровну, равномерно; ровно
Type
manner — equally or steadily
Position
end — after the verb and object; before its word with participles (evenly spaced)
Comparison
more/most — more evenly, most evenly
Modifies
verbs of spreading, dividing, breathing and speaking; participles like matched, spaced
Form
-ly from the adjective even (level, equal), not from the adverb even
Contrast
even (flat) = даже: She even paid for dinner. evenly = поровну or ровно: They split the check evenly. Same spelling, unrelated meanings. The idiom break even (выйти в ноль) uses the flat form.

Evenly has 3 distinct senses.

1. In equal amounts or parts; spread the same everywhere. (поровну, равномерно)

  1. We split the bill evenly between the four of us.
  2. Spread the frosting evenly over the cake.
  3. The two teams were evenly matched.

2. In a calm, steady voice, without showing emotion. (ровно, спокойно)

  1. "Put the phone down," he said evenly.
  2. She answered evenly, though she was clearly upset.

3. Smoothly and regularly, at a steady rate. (ровно, размеренно)

  1. The baby was finally breathing evenly.
  2. The ceiling fan turned slowly and evenly.

lastly#

Pronunciation
/ˈlæstli/ · LAST-lee
Русский
наконец, в заключение
Type
linking — introduces the last point in a list
Position
front — at the start of the sentence, usually with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole sentences
Form
-ly from last
Contrast
last (flat) = at the end, or the most recent time: He finished last (последним), When did you last see her? (в последний раз). lastly only introduces the final item of a list. Russian наконец has three English partners: lastly (the last point), finally (the last point, or after a long time) and at last (наконец-то, after waiting): At last the bus came, not Lastly the bus came. Don't confuse it with lately (в последнее время).

As the final point or item in a list.

  1. Lastly, I'd like to thank my parents.
  2. First, preheat the oven; next, mix the dry ingredients; and lastly, add the eggs.
  3. Lastly, remember to sign and date the form.

directly#

Pronunciation
/dəˈrɛktli/ · duh-REKT-lee
Русский
напрямую, непосредственно; прямо; сразу
Type
manner — without anything in between; also place and time
Position
end — after the verb and object; mid before some verbs (directly affect); before its word with prepositions and participles (directly above, directly affected)
Comparison
more/most — more directly, most directly
Modifies
verbs; prepositions of place and time (directly behind, directly after); participles
Form
-ly from direct
Contrast
direct (flat) = straight, without stopping or a go-between: fly direct to Denver, buy direct from the farm. directly can say that too, but only directly means exactly or immediately: directly opposite, directly after lunch — not direct opposite. In the US South, I'll be there directly means soon (скоро), not this second. British English also uses directly as a conjunction (Directly he left, we began = as soon as); that use is rare in American English.

Directly has 5 distinct senses.

1. Without anyone or anything in between; straight from one person or thing to another. (напрямую, непосредственно)

  1. You can buy tickets directly from the theater.
  2. The new law will directly affect small businesses.
  3. I need to speak directly to your manager.

2. Exactly in a particular position, or straight toward something. (прямо, точно)

  1. The bank is directly across from the post office.
  2. Don't look directly at the sun.
  3. After school, go directly home.

3. Honestly and clearly, without avoiding the subject. (прямо, откровенно)

  1. She asked me directly whether I was quitting.
  2. Politicians rarely answer questions directly.
