# More adverbs: T–W

> Adverbs T–W beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### tacitly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtæsɪtli/ &middot; *TAS-it-lee*
**Русский:** молча, негласно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of agreeing and accepting
**Form:** -ly from *tacit*

Without saying so directly, but understood.

1. By saying nothing, he *tacitly* agreed to the plan.
2. The government *tacitly* approved the deal.
3. It was *tacitly* understood that nobody would mention the divorce.

### tactfully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtæktfəli/ &middot; *TAKT-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** тактично, деликатно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid, before the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *tactful*

Carefully, so as not to upset or offend anyone.

1. She *tactfully* changed the subject.
2. He *tactfully* pointed out the mistake.
3. The complaint needs to be handled *tactfully*.

### tactically

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtæktɪkli/ &middot; *TAK-tik-lee*
**Русский:** тактически
**Type:** viewpoint; also manner
**Position:** front — as a viewpoint; also before adjectives and at the end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, and whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *tactical*

In terms of the methods used to win a game, battle, or contest.

1. *Tactically*, it was a brilliant move.
2. The coach changed the team *tactically* at halftime.
3. The retreat was *tactically* necessary.

### tamely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈteɪmli/ &middot; *TAYM-lee*
**Русский:** покорно, безропотно; вяло
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid, before the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *tame*

Without fighting back or showing any spirit; in a weak, disappointing way.

1. He *tamely* accepted the pay cut without a word.
2. The home team lost *tamely* in the second half.
3. After a great first hour, the movie ends *tamely*.

### tangentially

**Pronunciation:** /tænˈdʒɛnʃəli/ &middot; *tan-JEN-shuh-lee*
**Русский:** косвенно, вскользь
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — mostly *only tangentially related*; also end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** participles like *related*, *involved*; verbs
**Form:** -ly from *tangential*

Only slightly or indirectly; not about the main point.

1. The question is only *tangentially* related to the topic.
2. The book mentions the war only *tangentially*.
3. He was *tangentially* involved in the scandal.

### tangibly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtændʒəbli/ &middot; *TAN-juh-blee*
**Русский:** ощутимо, заметно
**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — or mid, before the verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of change and improvement
**Form:** -ly from *tangible*: the -le becomes -ly

In a real, noticeable way that can be seen or measured.

1. Voters want to see their lives *tangibly* improve.
2. Crime has fallen *tangibly* since the program started.
3. The new policy has not *tangibly* changed anything yet.

### tantalizingly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtæntəlaɪzɪŋli/ &middot; *TAN-tuh-ly-zing-lee*
**Русский:** дразняще, мучительно (близко)
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — most often *tantalizingly close*
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives like *close*, *near*; verbs
**Form:** -ly from *tantalizing*

In a way that makes you want something you cannot quite have.

1. The team came *tantalizingly* close to winning.
2. The smell of fresh bread drifted *tantalizingly* from the kitchen.
3. The finish line was *tantalizingly* near.

### tartly

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — usually after *said*, *replied*
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of saying
**Form:** -ly from *tart*

In a sharp, slightly unkind way.

**Comparison:** more/most

In an attractive way that shows good judgment about style.

### tastelessly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈteɪstləsli/ &middot; *TAYST-luhs-lee*
**Русский:** безвкусно; бестактно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or before a participle (*tastelessly decorated*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *tasteless*

In a way that shows bad taste or may offend people.

### tautly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɔːtli/ &middot; *TAWT-lee*
**Русский:** туго; сжато, динамично
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or before a participle (*tautly written*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *taut*

Tautly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Stretched tight, with no slack.** (туго, натянуто)

1. The canvas was stretched *tautly* over the frame.
2. Her skin was pulled *tautly* over her cheekbones.

**2. In a tight, tense style with nothing extra, of a book or film.** (сжато, динамично, напряжённо)

3. It's a *tautly* written thriller.
4. The film is *tautly* directed and never drags.

### tentatively

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɛntətɪvli/ &middot; *TEN-tuh-tiv-lee*
**Русский:** предварительно; неуверенно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *tentative*

Tentatively has 2 distinct senses.

**1. As a plan that is not yet final.** (предварительно)

1. The meeting is *tentatively* scheduled for Friday.

**2. In a hesitant, uncertain way.** (неуверенно, робко)

3. She smiled *tentatively* at the new neighbors.
4. He *tentatively* raised his hand.

### terminally

3. The show is *terminally* boring.

### that is to say

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðæt ɪz tə ˈseɪ/ &middot; *THAT iz tuh SAY*
**Русский:** то есть, иначе говоря
**Type:** linking — introduces a more exact restatement
**Position:** front — of the restatement, or mid between commas
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a clause or phrase that explains what came before
**Form:** shortened to *that is*, written *i.e.*

In other words; used to explain or correct what was just said.

1. We'll meet next week, *that is to say*, on Tuesday.
2. He's a vegan, *that is to say*, he eats no animal products.
3. The results were mixed; *that is to say*, not very good.

### the least bit

**Pronunciation:** /ðə ˈliːst ˈbɪt/ &middot; *thuh LEEST BIT*
**Русский:** ни капельки, ничуть; хоть немного
**Type:** degree — at all, in negatives and questions
**Position:** before its word — an adjective, after *not*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives like *worried*, *surprised*, *interested*

At all; even slightly, in negatives and questions.

1. I'm not *the least bit* worried.
2. She didn't seem *the least bit* surprised.
3. Are you *the least bit* interested in what I think?

### the other way around

**Pronunciation:** /ðə ˈʌðər ˌweɪ əˈraʊnd/ &middot; *thuh UHTH-ur way uh-ROWND*
**Русский:** наоборот
**Type:** manner — in the reverse order or relation
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a clause describing an order or relation
**Form:** British *the other way round*
**Contrast:** Russian *наоборот* has three English equivalents: *the other way around* (the reverse is true), *vice versa* (and the reverse too) and *on the contrary* (you are wrong, the opposite is true). *“Are you tired?” “On the contrary!”* not *“The other way around!”*

In the opposite order, direction or relation.

1. I thought she was helping him, but it was *the other way around*.
2. You put the batteries in *the other way around*.
3. The dog doesn't walk me; it's *the other way around*.

### theatrically

**Pronunciation:** /θiˈætrɪkli/ &middot; *thee-AT-rik-lee*
**Русский:** театрально, напоказ
**Type:** manner; also viewpoint
**Position:** end — in the manner sense; front with a comma in the viewpoint sense
**Comparison:** more/most — in the manner sense; not gradable in the viewpoint sense
**Modifies:** verbs; clauses in the viewpoint sense
**Form:** -ly from *theatrical*

Theatrically has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In an exaggerated way that is meant to attract attention.** (театрально, напоказ)

1. He sighed *theatrically* and rolled his eyes.
2. She clutched her chest *theatrically* when she saw the bill.
3. The villain laughed *theatrically* as he left the stage.

**2. In terms of the theater or of staging a play.** (с театральной точки зрения)

4. *Theatrically*, the production is a triumph.
5. The script is strong, but *theatrically* it doesn't quite work.

### then again

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðɛn əˈɡɛn/ &middot; *THEN uh-GEN*
**Русский:** с другой стороны, впрочем
**Type:** linking — introduces an opposite thought
**Position:** front — often after *but*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Contrast:** Despite *again*, it does not mean *one more time*. *But then again, …* weighs a second point against the first.

On the other hand; used to add a point against what was just said.

1. The car is expensive. *Then again*, it'll last for years.
2. He might come, but *then again*, he might not.
3. I didn't love the movie, but *then again*, I'm not a fan of horror.

### therapeutically

### thereabouts

**Pronunciation:** /ˌðɛrəˈbaʊts/ &middot; *thair-uh-BOWTS*
**Русский:** около того, приблизительно
**Type:** degree — approximately; also place
**Position:** end — almost always after *or*: *in 1990 or thereabouts*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a number, time or place just mentioned
**Form:** *there* + *about* + adverbial *-s*; *thereabout* is a rarer variant

Thereabouts has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Approximately — near the number, amount or time just mentioned.** (около того, приблизительно)

1. The house was built in 1920 or *thereabouts*.
2. The repair will cost $500 or *thereabouts*.

**2. Near the place just mentioned.** (где-то поблизости, где-то там)

3. They live in Tulsa or somewhere *thereabouts*.
4. The accident happened on Route 9 or *thereabouts*.

### thereafter

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛrˈæftər/ &middot; *thair-AF-tur*
**Русский:** после этого, впоследствии
**Type:** time
**Position:** end — also front, and after *shortly* or *soon* (*shortly thereafter*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses
**Form:** *there* + *after* — formal

After that time; from then on.

1. He retired in 2010 and died shortly *thereafter*.
2. The first payment is due in May, with monthly payments *thereafter*.
3. *Thereafter*, the town grew quickly.

### thereby

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛrˈbaɪ/ &middot; *thair-BY*
**Русский:** тем самым, таким образом
**Type:** linking — result
**Position:** mid — usually before an *-ing* form (*thereby reducing…*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses — gives the result of the action just described
**Form:** *there* + *by* — formal

As a result of that action.

1. The company cut prices, *thereby* attracting new customers.
2. By signing the contract, she *thereby* accepted its terms.
3. Exercise lowers blood pressure, *thereby* reducing the risk of stroke.

---

### therein

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛrˈɪn/ &middot; *thair-IN*
**Русский:** в этом, там
**Type:** place — in that thing or matter
**Position:** end — front only in *therein lies…*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clauses and nouns just mentioned
**Form:** *there* + *in* — formal

In that place, document or matter.

1. *Therein* lies the problem.
2. Read the contract and the terms contained *therein*.
3. The school's rules, and the penalties described *therein*, apply to every student.

---

### thereof

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛrˈʌv/ &middot; *thair-UV*
**Русский:** этого, такового
**Type:** linking — refers back to something just mentioned (*of it*, *of that*)
**Position:** after its word — *or lack thereof*
**Comparison:** not gradable
