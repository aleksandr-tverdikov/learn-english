# Adverbs of direction and adverbial particles

> Where to? — *up*, *down*, *back*, *forward*, *sideways*, *clockwise*, and the particles that complete phrasal verbs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Direction adverbs answer *where to?* or *which way?*. A large part of this group doubles as the particles of phrasal verbs — *turn **off***, *give **up***, *come **back*** — which is where their meaning stops being about direction at all. Each entry lists the literal directional sense first and then the most important non-literal ones; the phrasal verbs themselves are in the verb dictionary.

---
### up

**Pronunciation:** /ʌp/ &middot; *UHP*
**Русский:** вверх, наверх
**Type:** direction; also particle — the second word of many phrasal verbs (*give up*, *eat up*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but always after a pronoun (*pick it up*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
**Contrast:** With an object after it, *up* is a preposition (*walk up the hill*); with none, it is the adverb (*look up*). As a particle it can come before or after a noun object (*pick up the box*, *pick the box up*) but must follow a pronoun: *pick it up*, never *pick up it*. Very often *up* does not mean вверх at all but ‘completely’, like a Russian perfective prefix: *eat up* = съесть, *use up* = израсходовать, *drink up* = допить. *What's up?* and *Time's up* are adjective uses.

Up has 11 distinct senses.

**1. Toward a higher place or position.** (вверх, наверх)

1. She looked *up* at the sky.
2. Pick your clothes *up* off the floor.
3. Is this elevator going *up*?

**2. Into an upright position, standing or sitting straight.** (прямо; встать)

4. Please stand *up* when the judge comes in.
5. Sit *up* straight at the table.
6. He helped the old man *up* after he slipped.

**3. Out of bed; awake.** (не спать, на ногах)

7. Are the kids *up* yet?
8. We stayed *up* until two watching the game.
9. I got *up* at six this morning.

**4. To a higher amount, level, volume, or intensity.** (выше, больше, громче)

10. Gas prices went *up* again this week.
11. Can you turn the volume *up*?
12. Speak *up*, please; I can't hear you.

**5. Completely, so that nothing is left or the action is finished.** (полностью, до конца)

13. Eat *up* your vegetables.
14. Somebody used *up* all the hot water.
15. Clean *up* your room before dinner.

**6. So as to be closed, fastened, or tied together.** ((застегнуть, завязать, закрыть))

16. Zip *up* your jacket; it's freezing.
17. Don't forget to lock *up* when you leave.
18. Tie *up* the garbage bag before you take it out.

**7. Into pieces or small parts.** (на куски, на части)

19. She tore the letter *up* and threw it away.
20. Cut the chicken *up* into small pieces.

**8. Into existence, view, or attention.** (возникнуть, появиться)

21. Your name came *up* at the meeting.
22. Sorry, something came *up* and I can't make it.
23. He didn't show *up* until nine.

**9. Close to a person or place, approaching.** ((подойти) близко, вплотную)

24. A stranger came *up* and asked for directions.
25. A black car pulled *up* outside the house.
26. Go ahead, I'll catch *up*.

**10. To or in the north, or to a place thought of as more important or central.** (на север; в город, в центр)

27. We're driving *up* to Maine for the weekend.
28. They moved *up* north after they retired.

**11. Ahead of an opponent by a stated score.** (впереди (по счету))

29. We were ten points *up* at halftime.
30. The Yankees were two runs *up* going into the ninth.

### down

**Pronunciation:** /daʊn/ &middot; *DOWN*
**Русский:** вниз
**Type:** direction; also particle — the second word of many phrasal verbs (*calm down*, *break down*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but always after a pronoun (*write it down*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
**Contrast:** *down the street* is the preposition and means ‘along, по улице’, not ‘downhill’. The pronoun goes before the particle: *write it down*, never *write down it*. *I feel down* (мне грустно) is an adjective. *Down to Texas* and *up to Chicago* for south and north are everyday American speech, even across flat land.

Down has 9 distinct senses.

**1. Toward a lower place or position.** (вниз)

1. Sit *down* and make yourself at home.
2. She looked *down* at her shoes.
3. The sun goes *down* around eight in the summer.

**2. To a lower amount, level, volume, speed, or intensity.** (ниже, меньше, тише)

4. Turn the music *down*; the baby's asleep.
5. Prices have finally come *down*.
6. Slow *down*, you're driving too fast.

**3. So as to stop working or operating.** ((сломаться, закрыться))

7. My car broke *down* on the highway.
8. The factory shut *down* last year.
9. The website went *down* for an hour this morning.

**4. On paper; in writing.** (записать, на бумаге)

10. Write the address *down* before you forget it.
11. Did you get all of that *down*?

**5. Firmly in place, or so as to be found or caught.** (прочно; (выследить, поймать))

12. Tie the tarp *down* so it doesn't blow away.
13. Nail the boards *down* before the storm hits.
14. The police finally tracked him *down*.

**6. To or in the south, or away from the center of things.** (на юг; на юге)

15. We drove *down* to Florida for spring break.
16. My grandparents live *down* south.

**7. From an earlier generation or time to a later one.** (из поколения в поколение)

17. The ring was handed *down* from my grandmother.
18. These stories have been passed *down* for centuries.

**8. As a first payment, with the rest paid later.** (первоначальным взносом)

19. We put ten percent *down* on the house.
20. Drive it home today with no money *down*.

**9. Behind an opponent by a stated score.** (позади (по счету))

21. We were *down* by six at halftime.
22. They came back from three goals *down*.

### in

**Pronunciation:** /ɪn/ &middot; *IN*
**Русский:** внутрь, в
**Type:** direction; also place and particle (*fill in*, *give in*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (*hand it in*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
**Contrast:** Russian *в* covers three English forms: *come in* (adverb, no object), *come into the room* (preposition *into*, movement) and *be in the room* (preposition *in*, location). *Come in the house* is common in American speech, but *into* is the standard for movement. *in* meaning ‘fashionable’ (*Wide jeans are in*) is an adjective.

In has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Into or toward the inside of a place, container, or vehicle.** (внутрь)

1. Come *in* and sit down.
2. Get *in*, I'll give you a ride.
3. The roof caved *in* under the snow.

**2. At home or at one's place of work.** (дома; на месте, на работе)

4. Is Dr. Patel *in* today?
5. I'm not coming *in* tomorrow; I'm sick.
6. We stayed *in* and watched a movie.

**3. Arriving, or received at the place where it is expected.** (прибывать, поступать)

7. What time does your flight get *in*?
8. The election results are starting to come *in*.

**4. To a person or organization in authority, handed over.** (сдать, подать)

9. Please hand your essays *in* by Friday.
10. Viewers sent *in* thousands of photos.

**5. Taking part, included with others.** (участвуя, вместе со всеми)

11. If you're ordering pizza, count me *in*.
12. Everyone joined *in* on the chorus.

**6. Into a space, so as to fill or complete it.** (вписать, заполнить)

13. Fill *in* the blanks with the correct word.
14. The kids colored *in* the pictures.

### out

**Pronunciation:** /aʊt/ &middot; *OWT*
**Русский:** наружу, вон
**Type:** direction; also place and particle (*find out*, *run out*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (*take it out*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
**Contrast:** Before a place, standard English uses *out of*: *get out of the car*. American English drops *of* before *door* and *window* (*look out the window*, *run out the door*), where British keeps it. The pictures can run opposite ways: *the lights went out* = погасли, but *the stars came out* = появились. *out* in baseball (*he's out*) is an adjective.

Out has 11 distinct senses.

**1. Away from the inside of a place or container.** (наружу, вон)

1. The kids ran *out* into the yard.
2. Can you take the trash *out*?
3. Get *out*!

**2. Away from home or work, often for a social activity.** (не дома, вне дома)

4. Sorry, she's *out* for lunch.
5. Let's eat *out* tonight.
6. We went *out* on Friday night.

**3. So as to be found, understood, or solved.** ((выяснить, разобраться))

7. Did you ever find *out* who sent the flowers?
8. I can't figure *out* this remote.
9. Don't worry, we'll work it *out*.

**4. So as to stop burning or shining.** ((погаснуть, потушить))

10. The lights went *out* during the storm.
11. Put your cigarette *out*, please.

**5. Until finished, or until nothing is left.** (до конца; кончиться)

12. Our money ran *out* in the second week.
13. Hear me *out* before you decide.
14. The concert sold *out* in ten minutes.

**6. Aloud, so as to be clearly heard.** (вслух, громко)

15. Read the list *out* loud.
16. She cried *out* in pain.

**7. Into view, or into public knowledge or sale.** (появиться; выйти в свет)

17. The sun came *out* after lunch.
18. Her new album comes *out* next month.
19. Sooner or later, the truth will come *out*.

**8. So as to be removed or left out.** (исключить, вычеркнуть)

20. Cross *out* the wrong answer.
21. Leave the onions *out*, please.

**9. To many people, distributed.** (раздать)

22. The teacher handed *out* the tests.
23. They're giving *out* free samples at the store.

**10. Into unconsciousness.** (без сознания)

24. He passed *out* from the heat.
25. The boxer was knocked *out* in the third round.

**11. Far away from land, a town, or the center.** (далеко (от берега, города))

26. They live way *out* in the country.
27. The boat was a mile *out* when the storm hit.

### back

**Pronunciation:** /bæk/ &middot; *BAK*
**Русский:** назад, обратно
**Type:** direction; also time (*back in 2010*) and particle (*pay back*, *hold back*)
**Position:** end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (*give it back*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; phrasal verbs; time phrases (*back in the day*)
**Contrast:** *back* is not ‘again’: *come back* = вернуться, *come again* = прийти ещё раз. With a pronoun: *give it back*, never *give back it*. *a few years back* is informal American for *ago*. *Push a meeting back* makes it later; *move it forward* usually means earlier — say the date if it matters.

Back has 6 distinct senses.

**1. To the place, person, or condition where someone or something was before.** (обратно, назад)

1. I'll be *back* in five minutes.
2. Put the milk *back* in the fridge.
3. She went *back* to sleep.

**2. In return or in reply.** (в ответ)

4. Can I call you *back* in an hour?
5. He never paid me *back*.
6. She smiled *back* at him.

**3. Toward the rear, away from the front.** (назад)

7. Step *back* from the edge.
8. Lean *back* and relax.
9. Don't look *back*.

**4. In or into the past; ago.** ((тому) назад, в прошлом)

10. *Back* in the 1990s, few people had a cell phone.
11. I met her a few years *back*.

**5. Under control, kept from moving forward or coming out.** (сдерживать)

12. The police held the crowd *back*.
13. She fought *back* tears.

**6. To a later time, or to an earlier setting on a clock.** (на более позднее время; (часы) назад)

14. The meeting was pushed *back* to Friday.
15. Remember to set your clocks *back* on Sunday.

### forward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːrwərd/ &middot; *FOR-wurd*
**Русский:** вперёд
**Type:** direction; also time and particle (*come forward*, *put forward*)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable — *further forward* does the comparing
**Modifies:** verbs, especially verbs of motion
**Form:** -ward from *fore*; British also *forwards*
**Contrast:** American English says *forward*; British also *forwards*. *Move the meeting forward* usually means earlier, but some Americans hear later — name the day. *look forward to* takes a noun or *-ing*: *I look forward to seeing you*, never *to see you*. *forward* is also a verb (*forward an email*).

Forward has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Toward the front, or in the direction one is facing.** (вперёд)

1. Take one step *forward*.
2. She leaned *forward* to hear better.
3. The car jerked *forward* and stalled.

**2. Toward the future or toward progress.** (вперёд, дальше)

4. It's time to move *forward* with the plan.
5. From this day *forward*, things will be different.

**3. Into view or public attention, so as to be offered or known.** (выступить; выдвинуть)

6. Two witnesses have come *forward*.
7. She put *forward* a new proposal.

**4. To an earlier date, or to a later setting on a clock.** (на более ранний срок; (часы) вперёд)

8. Set your clocks *forward* one hour tonight.
9. We moved the deadline *forward* to Monday.

### backward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbækwərd/ &middot; *BAK-wurd*
**Русский:** назад
**Type:** direction; also manner (*count backward*)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ward from *back*; American English uses *backward* and *backwards* alike
**Contrast:** Both *backward* and *backwards* are American; the adjective is only *backward* (*a backward glance*; *a backward country* = отсталая). Three Russian phrases, three English ones: задом наперёд = *backward* (*your shirt is on backward*), наизнанку = *inside out*, вверх ногами = *upside down*.

Backward has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Toward the back, or in the direction behind one.** (назад; навзничь)

1. He took a step *backward*.
2. She fell *backward* into the pool.

**2. In reverse order, from the end to the beginning.** (в обратном порядке)

3. Can you say the alphabet *backward*?
4. Count *backward* from ten.

**3. With the back part in front.** (задом наперёд)

5. You have your shirt on *backward*.
6. He wears his baseball cap *backward*.

**4. Toward the past, or to a worse state.** (назад (в прошлое, к худшему))

7. Critics say the new law takes the country *backward*.
8. Critics called the decision a big step *backward*.

**5. Thoroughly, in every detail (in *backward and forward*).** (вдоль и поперёк)

9. She knows the rules *backward* and forward.
10. I've read that contract *backward* and forward.

### upward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʌpwərd/ &middot; *UHP-wurd*
**Русский:** вверх
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end; *upward of* goes before a number
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; numbers (*upward of 500*)
**Form:** -ward from *up*; British usually *upwards*
**Contrast:** American English prefers *upward*, British *upwards*, though Americans often say *upwards of* too. *upward of 50* means ‘more than 50’ (свыше), not ‘up to 50’. The adjective goes before a noun: *an upward trend*.

Upward has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Toward a higher place.** (вверх)

1. The balloon floated *upward* into the clouds.
2. Point the camera *upward*.

**2. Toward a higher amount, level, or rank.** (в сторону повышения)

3. Home prices are moving *upward* again.
4. The forecast was revised *upward*.

**3. More than a stated number (in *upward of*).** (свыше, более)

5. *Upward of* 500 people showed up.
6. The repairs could cost *upward of* $10,000.

### downward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdaʊnwərd/ &middot; *DOWN-wurd*
**Русский:** вниз
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb, or after *from* + a rank (*from the CEO downward*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; ranks and levels after *from*
**Form:** -ward from *down*; British usually *downwards*
**Contrast:** American *downward*, British *downwards*. The adjective sits before a noun (*a downward spiral*). For лицом вниз the everyday phrase is *face down*, not *face downward*.

Downward has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Toward a lower place.** (вниз)

1. The path slopes *downward* to the beach.
2. She glanced *downward* at her phone.

**2. Toward a lower amount or level.** (в сторону понижения)

3. Sales estimates were revised *downward*.
4. Interest rates are heading *downward*.

**3. From a higher rank or level to all the lower ones.** (и ниже (по должности, уровню))

5. Everyone from the CEO *downward* took a pay cut.
6. The rule applies to all staff, from managers *downward*.

### inward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪnwərd/ &middot; *IN-wurd*
**Русский:** внутрь
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ward from *in*; British also *inwards*
**Contrast:** Don't confuse with *inwardly*, which means ‘privately, in one's mind’: *Inwardly, she was furious* = в душе. The adjective goes before a noun: *inward calm*. American *inward*, British also *inwards*.

Inward has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Toward the inside or the center.** (внутрь)

1. The door opens *inward*.
2. The walls began to bend *inward*.

**2. Toward one's own mind and feelings.** (в себя, внутрь себя)

3. After the loss, he turned *inward* and stopped calling friends.
4. Meditation asks you to look *inward*.

### outward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈaʊtwərd/ &middot; *OWT-wurd*
**Русский:** наружу
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ward from *out*; British also *outwards*
**Contrast:** For внешне use *outwardly*: *He was outwardly calm*, never *He was outward calm*. The adjective goes before a noun: *outward appearance*. American *outward*, British also *outwards*.

Outward has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Toward the outside, or away from the center.** (наружу; в стороны)

1. The door opens *outward*.
2. The city keeps spreading *outward*.

**2. Toward other people or the world outside oneself or one's group.** (вовне, на внешний мир)

3. The company needs to look *outward* for new ideas.
4. Try to focus *outward* instead of worrying about yourself.

### northward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɔːrθwərd/ &middot; *NORTH-wurd*
**Русский:** на север
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of motion and extent
**Form:** -ward from *north*; British also *northwards*
**Contrast:** In everyday American speech the plain compass word is the adverb: *drive north*, *head north*, *up north*. *northward* sounds written or technical. No *to* before the bare word: *go north*, but *to the north*.

Toward the north.

1. The storm is moving *northward* along the coast.
2. Many birds fly *northward* in the spring.
3. The highway runs *northward* to the Canadian border.

### southward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsaʊθwərd/ &middot; *SOWTH-wurd*
**Русский:** на юг
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of motion and extent
**Form:** -ward from *south*; British also *southwards*
**Contrast:** Everyday American speech says *go south*, *head south*, *down south*; *southward* belongs to writing and weather reports. No *to* before the bare word: *go south*, but *to the south*.

Toward the south.

1. The river flows *southward* to the Gulf of Mexico.
2. Every fall, the geese head *southward*.
3. The hurricane turned *southward* overnight.

**Modifies:** verbs of motion and extent
**Form:** -ward from *east*; British also *eastwards*

### homeward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhoʊmwərd/ &middot; *HOHM-wurd*
**Русский:** домой
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of motion
**Form:** -ward from *home*; British also *homewards*
**Contrast:** The everyday adverb is *home* with no *to*: *go home*, *head home*. *homeward* is literary; *homeward bound* (на пути домой) is a fixed phrase.

Toward home.

1. We finally started *homeward* after midnight.
2. The birds flew *homeward* as the sun set.
3. At last the soldiers turned *homeward*.

### sideways

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsaɪdweɪz/ &middot; *SYD-wayz*
**Русский:** вбок, боком
**Type:** direction; also manner (*sideways* = боком)
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *side* + *-ways*; *sidewise* is a rare American variant
**Contrast:** Always with *-s*; there is no adverb *sideway*. In informal American English *go sideways* means ‘go wrong’, not a literal direction. A *sideways move* at work is neither a promotion nor a demotion.

Sideways has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To or toward one side.** (вбок, в сторону)

1. The crab scuttled *sideways* across the sand.
2. She stepped *sideways* to let him pass.
3. The car skidded *sideways* on the ice.

**2. With one side facing forward or up.** (боком)

4. Turn the couch *sideways* to get it through the door.
5. He sat *sideways* in the chair.

**3. To a job or position of about the same rank.** (на равноценную должность)

6. She moved *sideways* into marketing.
7. He didn't get promoted; he was shifted *sideways*.

**4. Informally, into a bad or unexpected state; wrong.** (пойти не так)

8. The deal went *sideways* at the last minute.
9. Things went *sideways* when the power went out.

### clockwise

**Pronunciation:** /ˈklɑːkwaɪz/ &middot; *KLAHK-wyz*
**Русский:** по часовой стрелке
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of turning and motion
**Form:** *clock* + *-wise* (‘in the manner of’)
**Contrast:** The opposite is American *counterclockwise*, British *anticlockwise*. The same form is an adjective before a noun: *a clockwise direction*.

In the direction the hands of a clock move.

1. Turn the lid *clockwise* to close it.
2. The numbers on the dial go *clockwise* from zero to ten.
3. Stir the sauce *clockwise* for two minutes.

### counterclockwise

**Pronunciation:** /ˌkaʊntərˈklɑːkwaɪz/ &middot; *kown-tur-KLAHK-wyz*
**Русский:** против часовой стрелки
**Type:** direction
**Position:** end — after the verb or its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of turning and motion
**Form:** *counter-* (‘against’) + *clockwise*
**Contrast:** American *counterclockwise* is British *anticlockwise*; Americans understand the British word but don't use it. The same form is an adjective: *a counterclockwise turn*.

In the direction opposite to the way the hands of a clock move.

**Contrast:** *go overboard* is everyday informal English for doing too much (перебарщивать), and is far more common than the literal sense.

Overboard has 3 distinct senses.

**1. To excess, further than is sensible (in *go overboard*).** (перебарщивать)

1. Don't go *overboard* with the decorations.
2. I went a little *overboard* on snacks for the party.

**2. Over the side of a boat or ship into the water.** (за борт)

3. He fell *overboard* and had to be rescued.
4. Man *overboard*!

**3. Aside, so as to be abandoned (in *throw overboard*).** (отбросить, отказаться)

5. The plan was thrown *overboard* when the budget was cut.
6. They tossed their principles *overboard* to win.
