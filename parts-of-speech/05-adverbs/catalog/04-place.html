Adverbs of place#

Where? — here, there, everywhere, upstairs, abroad, home, and the words that locate without an object.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Place adverbs answer where? and normally go at the end of the clause. Many of them look like prepositions (inside, above, behind) and become adverbs simply by dropping the object: wait inside the car is a preposition, wait inside is an adverb. The one that catches Russian speakers is home, which takes no preposition after verbs of motion — go home, never go to home.

here#

Pronunciation
/ˈhɪr/ · HEER
Русский
здесь, сюда, вот
Type
place; also direction and time
Position
end — or front with inversion: Here comes the bus
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; also follows a noun (the people here)
Contrast
Russian has two words, здесь (where?) and сюда (where to?); English uses here for both, with no preposition: Come here, never come to here. In the presenting use a noun subject comes after the verb (Here comes the bus, Here are your keys), but a pronoun does not (Here it comes, Here they are).

Here has 5 distinct senses.

1. In or at this place. (здесь, тут)

  1. Sit here next to me.
  2. Is Anna here today?
  3. It's really cold in here.

2. To this place. (сюда)

  1. Come here for a second.
  2. How long did it take you to get here?
  3. We moved here from Ohio in 2019.

3. At this point in a process, discussion or piece of writing; in this matter or situation. (в этом месте, на этом этапе, в данном случае)

  1. Let's stop here for today.
  2. Here the author changes her argument.
  3. The real problem here is the cost.

4. Used to present, offer or point out something, or to say that someone or something has arrived. (вот)

  1. Here are your keys.
  2. Here comes the bus.
  3. Here you go — one coffee, no sugar.

5. Used when raising a glass to someone in a toast. (за (кого-то, что-то))

  1. Here's to the happy couple!
  2. Here's to a great year.

there#

Pronunciation
/ˈðɛr/ · THAIR
Русский
там, туда, вот
Type
place; also direction
Position
end — or front with inversion: There goes the last train
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; also follows a noun (that man there)
Contrast
The there of there is / there are is a dummy subject, not this adverb, and does not mean там: There is a pharmacy there uses both. Like here, it covers both там and туда with no preposition: go there, never go to there. With a noun subject the verb comes first (There goes my bus), with a pronoun it does not (There it goes). Do not confuse it with their and they're, which sound the same.

There has 8 distinct senses.

1. In or at that place. (там)

  1. I left my bag over there.
  2. Were you there when it happened?
  3. Put the boxes down there, by the door.

2. To that place. (туда)

  1. We drove there in two hours.
  2. I've never been there, but I'd love to go.
  3. Wait for me — I'll be there in five minutes.

3. At that point in a process, discussion or story; in that respect. (на этом, в этом (месте, отношении))

  1. I'll stop there and take questions.
  2. There I have to disagree with you.
  3. The story could have ended there, but it didn't.

4. Used to point out or draw attention to someone or something, or to show that you have found them. (вот)

  1. There goes the last train.
  2. There you are — I've been looking everywhere for you.
  3. There she is — right on time.

5. Used after a greeting or to call someone's attention. ((не переводится); эй)

  1. Hi there! How's it going?
  2. Hello there — can I help you find something?

6. Available to help and support someone, especially emotionally. (рядом (быть опорой))

  1. My sister was always there for me.
  2. Whatever happens, I'll be there for you.

7. Existing or available, even if not yet used or noticed. (есть, имеется)

  1. The talent is there — he just needs to work harder.
  2. The money is there if we need it.
  3. The demand is clearly there, so why not open a second store?

8. At the goal or the result that you want; succeeding. (у цели; (добиться) своего)

  1. The design isn't quite there yet.
  2. It took years of practice, but she finally got there.

everywhere#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛvriˌwɛr/ · EV-ree-wair
Русский
везде, всюду
Type
place; also direction
Position
end — or front for emphasis
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
every + where, like somewhere, anywhere, nowhere
Contrast
Not everywhere means не везде (only some places). For нигде after a negative verb use anywhere: I can't find it anywhere, not everywhere. Before a clause it works as a conjunction meaning wherever: Everywhere we went, people were friendly.

In, at or to every place.

  1. I've looked everywhere for my glasses.
  2. There were people everywhere downtown.
  3. My little brother follows me everywhere.

nowhere#

Pronunciation
/ˈnoʊˌwɛr/ · NOH-wair
Русский
нигде, никуда
Type
place; also direction, negative and degree
Position
end — front only with inversion: Nowhere is this clearer than…
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; nowhere near modifies adjectives, numbers and phrases
Form
no + where; the negative of somewhere and anywhere
Contrast
English allows one negative per clause. Russian Я никуда не иду is I'm not going anywhere or I'm going nowhere, never I'm not going nowhere. Fronted, it forces inversion: Nowhere have I seen such beautiful beaches. The middle of nowhere and out of nowhere use nowhere as a noun.

Nowhere has 4 distinct senses.

1. Not in or at any place. (нигде)

  1. My keys were nowhere to be found.
  2. Nowhere in the contract does it mention overtime.
  3. The kids were nowhere in sight.

2. Not to any place. (никуда)

  1. After the fire, the family had nowhere else to go.
  2. I'm going nowhere until you tell me the truth.

3. To no progress, result or success, especially with get, go or be. (ни к чему (не привести), ни с места, ничего не добиться)

  1. We talked for hours and got nowhere.
  2. This relationship is going nowhere.
  3. Without my first coach, I'd be nowhere today.

4. In nowhere near: not at all; far from. (далеко не, совсем не)

  1. The report is nowhere near finished.
  2. The sequel is nowhere near as good as the original.
  3. We have nowhere near enough chairs.

somewhere#

Pronunciation
/ˈsʌmˌwɛr/ · SUM-wair
Русский
где-то, куда-то
Type
place; also direction and degree
Position
end — before a number or time in the approximate sense: somewhere around fifty
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; numbers after around or between
Form
some + where; American informal someplace means the same
Contrast
Use somewhere in statements, and in offers and requests that expect yes (Would you like to go somewhere?); use anywhere in negatives and most questions (I didn't go anywhere). An adjective follows it: somewhere quiet, not quiet somewhere.

Somewhere has 4 distinct senses.

1. In or at a place that is not known or not named. (где-то, где-нибудь)

  1. I left my phone somewhere in the car.
  2. Let's sit somewhere quiet.
  3. He lives somewhere near Boston.

2. To a place that is not known or not named. (куда-то, куда-нибудь)

  1. Are you going somewhere?
  2. Let's go somewhere warm this winter.

3. About; approximately, with a number, a time or a range. (примерно, где-то)

  1. She's somewhere around fifty.
  2. We got home somewhere around midnight.
  3. A good laptop costs somewhere between $800 and $1,200.

4. In get somewhere: to make progress. (сдвинуться с места, добиться чего-то)

  1. Now we're getting somewhere!
  2. After three meetings we finally got somewhere on the budget.

anywhere#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛniˌwɛr/ · EN-ee-wair
Русский
где-нибудь, куда-нибудь, где угодно
Type
place; also direction and degree
Position
end — before a number in the range sense: anywhere from three to five hours
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; numbers after from or between
Form
any + where; American informal anyplace means the same
Contrast
In a statement anywhere means где угодно (any place at all), not где-то: I left it somewhere, not I left it anywhere. After a negative verb it gives нигде / никуда: I can't find it anywhere. Do not add a second negative (not … nowhere).

Anywhere has 5 distinct senses.

1. In negatives and questions: in, at or to any place. (где-нибудь, куда-нибудь; (с отрицанием) нигде, никуда)

  1. I can't find my keys anywhere.
  2. Did you go anywhere last weekend?
  3. Is there a pharmacy anywhere near here?

2. In or to any place at all; it does not matter where. (где угодно, куда угодно)

  1. Sit anywhere you like.
  2. You can buy these batteries anywhere.
  3. I'd go anywhere with you.

3. Any amount within a range, with from … to or between. (где-то (от … до))

  1. The drive takes anywhere from three to five hours.
  2. Tickets cost anywhere between $50 and $500.

4. In not anywhere near: not at all; far from. (далеко не, совсем не)

  1. I'm not anywhere near ready.
  2. The new stadium isn't anywhere near as big as the old one.

5. In negatives and questions with get: to any progress or result. (ни к чему (не привести))

  1. Arguing with him won't get you anywhere.
  2. Are we getting anywhere with this plan?

inside#

Pronunciation
/ˌɪnˈsaɪd/ · in-SYD

Type
place; also direction
Position

Comparison

Contrast
With an object it is a preposition (inside the car); American English also says inside of the car, and inside of an hour means within an hour. Inside out means наизнанку, not изнутри наружу.

Inside has 3 distinct senses.

1. In a building, room or container. (внутри, в доме)

  1. Let's wait inside until the rain stops.

2. Into a building, room or container. (внутрь, в дом)

  1. Come inside — it's getting dark.
  2. She opened the envelope and looked inside.

3. In your mind or feelings, often hidden from others. (в душе, внутри)

  1. She smiled, but inside she was furious.
  2. After the breakup, I felt empty inside.

abroad#

Pronunciation
/əˈbrɔːd/ · uh-BRAWD
Русский

Type
place; also direction
Position
end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; also follows a noun (news from abroad)
Form

Contrast
No preposition before it after a verb: go abroad, live abroad, not go to abroad or in abroad (but from abroad is fine). Do not confuse it with aboard (на борту). In everyday American speech overseas is often used instead, though study abroad is the fixed phrase.

Abroad has 3 distinct senses.

1. In a foreign country. (за границей)

2. To a foreign country. (за границу)

  1. We're going abroad this summer.
  2. He moved abroad right after college.

3. Formal: spreading widely among people; in circulation. (ходить (о слухах), распространяться)

  1. Rumors were abroad that the plant would close.
  2. There is a new mood of anger abroad in the country.

place; also direction and degree
Position
end — or after a noun: the table below
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; follows a noun or a number
Contrast
For later text say below, not lower: see below. In American weather talk ten below means ten degrees below zero Fahrenheit. With an object it is a preposition (below the window).

Below has 4 distinct senses.

1. At or to a lower place. (внизу, вниз)

  1. From the bridge we watched the river below.
  2. The apartment below has been empty for months.
  3. The pot fell to the sidewalk below.

2. Later in a piece of writing. (ниже)

  1. See the table below.
  2. Sign your name in the space below.

3. Less than a stated number or level, including below zero on a thermometer. (и ниже, ниже нуля)

  1. Anyone who scores 60 or below has to retake the test.
  2. It was fifteen below in Minneapolis last night.

4. On or to a lower deck of a ship. (вниз (в каюту, в трюм))

  1. The captain ordered the passengers to go below.
  2. Two sailors were sleeping below.

home#

Pronunciation
/ˈhoʊm/ · HOHM
Русский
домой, дома
Type
place; also direction
Position
end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs of motion, and be and stay
Contrast
After verbs of motion home takes no preposition: go home, get home, arrive home, never go to home or arrive to home. For location American English says both stay home and stay at home, Is Dad home? and Is Dad at home?; British English prefers at home. With a possessive it is a noun and needs the preposition: go to my home.

Home has 5 distinct senses.

1. To the place where you live. (домой)

  1. I'm going home — I'm exhausted.
  2. What time did you get home last night?
  3. Can you drive me home after the party?

2. At the place where you live. (дома)

  1. Is your dad home?
  2. I stayed home all weekend.
  3. Nobody's home right now.

3. To or in your own country or hometown. (на родину, домой)

  1. After ten years in Europe, she came home to Texas.
  2. It's time to bring the troops home.

4. So that something is fully felt or understood. ((дойти) до сознания, в полной мере)

  1. The reality of losing her job finally hit home.
  2. The ad drives home the message that speeding kills.
  3. Seeing his empty room really brought it home to me that he was gone.

5. Fully into the right place, as far as it will go. (до упора, до конца)

  1. He hammered the nail home.
  2. Push the cartridge home until it clicks.

away#

Pronunciation
/əˈweɪ/ · uh-WAY
Русский
прочь, в сторону, на расстоянии
Type
place; also direction, time and particle
Position
end
Comparison

Modifies

Contrast
Distance and time go before away: The station is five minutes away, never five minutes far or far in five minutes. She's out means she has left for a short time; she's away means she is traveling for days. Way in way too expensive is a separate informal degree adverb. In phrasal verbs (throw away, give away, put away) it is a particle whose meaning is part of the verb.

Away has 10 distinct senses.

1. From a place; leaving or moving to a different place. (прочь, (уйти, уехать))

  1. Go away and leave me alone!
  2. He turned and walked away without a word.
  3. The bird flew away.

2. At a distance from someone or something, often a stated one. (на расстоянии, (в двух милях) отсюда)

  1. The beach is only two miles away.
  2. The nearest hospital is an hour away.
  3. Stay away from the edge!

3. Not at home or at work; out of town for a period of time. (в отъезде, нет на месте)

  1. She's away on business this week.
  2. We'll be away until Monday.

4. Turning in another direction. (в сторону)

  1. She looked away when I smiled at her.
  2. He turned away from the window.

5. Into a safe or proper place, especially to store or save something. (на место, (убрать, отложить))

  1. Put your toys away before dinner.
  2. They've been putting money away for college.

6. So that you no longer have something; out of your possession. ((отдать, выбросить, продать))

  1. Don't throw that box away — I need it.
  2. We gave most of our furniture away before the move.
  3. The store is giving away free samples today.

7. So that something gets smaller and disappears. (совсем, до конца (исчезнуть))

  1. The snow melted away by noon.
  2. The music slowly faded away.

8. Continuously and busily, without stopping. (не переставая, вовсю)

  1. She was typing away at her laptop all night.
  2. You have questions? Ask away.

9. In the future, at a stated distance in time. ((осталось) до, через)

  1. Christmas is only two weeks away.
  2. The election is still a year away.

10. In right away: immediately. (сразу, немедленно)

  1. I'll call you back right away.
  2. She knew right away that something was wrong.

Type

verbs; adjectives in the degree sense (halfway decent); phrases with through, up, between

Contrast
For a physical amount English uses half (half empty, half a glass); halfway is for progress through distance or time (halfway home, halfway through the book). Halfway decent is informal for более-менее приличный, not наполовину приличный.

Halfway has 4 distinct senses.

1. At or to the middle point between two places. (на полпути, посередине)

  1. We're halfway there — keep going.
  2. She stopped halfway up the stairs.
  3. The gas station is halfway between the two towns.

2. In the middle of a period of time, activity or process. (на середине, наполовину)

  1. I fell asleep halfway through the movie.
  2. We're about halfway done with the renovation.

3. Informal: partly, or to an acceptable degree. (отчасти; более-менее)

  1. I halfway believed his story.
  2. Anything halfway decent in this neighborhood costs a fortune.

4. In meet someone halfway: to compromise by giving up part of what you want. (пойти навстречу, найти компромисс)

  1. I can't pay $500, but I'll meet you halfway.
  2. If both sides met halfway, the strike could end tomorrow.
