Stance and comment adverbs#

What the speaker thinks of it — probably, maybe, obviously, luckily, frankly, actually, of course.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Stance adverbs comment on the whole statement rather than on the verb: how certain it is (probably, definitely), how the speaker feels about it (unfortunately, luckily), or how it is being said (frankly, honestly). Most go at the front with a comma or in mid-position. Two are false friends for Russian speakers — actually means in fact, not актуально, and eventually means in the end, not возможно.

certainly#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɜːrtnli/ · SURT-n-lee
Русский
конечно, несомненно
Type
stance — certainty
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also alone as an answer
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives (certainly true)
Form
-ly from certain
Contrast
Surely is not a synonym: Certainly he knows states a fact, but Surely you know him means “I'd be amazed if you didn't” and asks for agreement. As a reply, Certainly is polite and a little formal; everyday American speech says Sure.

Certainly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Without any doubt; definitely. (несомненно, безусловно)

  1. She certainly knows how to throw a party.
  2. It's certainly not the cheapest option, but it's the best.
  3. Things will certainly get busier after the holidays.

2. Used as a polite answer to agree or give permission: yes, of course; certainly not is a firm no. (конечно, разумеется)

  1. “Could you help me with this box?” “Certainly.”
  2. “Can I pay by card?” “Certainly — right over here.”
  3. “May I borrow your car tonight?” “Certainly not.”

definitely#

Pronunciation
/ˈdɛfənətli/ · DEF-uh-nuht-lee
Русский
определённо, точно
Type
stance — certainty
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also alone as an answer
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ly from definite; spelled -ite-, never definately
Contrast
Order with not changes the meaning: It's definitely not true = точно неправда, but It's not definitely true = не факт, что правда. Do not use it for Russian определённый = “certain, particular”: в определённой степени is to a certain extent, not definitely to some degree.

Definitely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Without any doubt; for sure. (определённо, точно)

  1. I'm definitely coming to your party.
  2. That was definitely the best pizza I've ever had.
  3. We definitely need more chairs for Saturday.

2. Used as an answer to say a strong yes or no. (конечно, безусловно)

  1. “Are you going to vote?” “Definitely.”
  2. “Would you go back to that hotel?” “Definitely not.”

3. In a fixed, final way, so that there is no more doubt or change. (окончательно, точно)

  1. Nothing has been definitely decided yet.
  2. We can't say definitely when the store will reopen.

obviously#

Pronunciation
/ˈɑːbviəsli/ · AHB-vee-uhs-lee
Русский
очевидно, конечно; явно
Type
stance — what everyone should know; also manner
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ly from obvious
Contrast
False friend: Russian очевидно often means “probably, it seems” (Очевидно, он опоздал на поезд) — that is apparently or evidently in English. Obviously means “as anyone can see” and can sound rude if the listener did not know: Obviously, the meeting is at three.

Obviously has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used to say that something is clear or expected and should need no explanation. (конечно, само собой, очевидно)

  1. Obviously, we can't leave the kids home alone.
  2. “Did you tell her?” “Obviously not.”
  3. We'll obviously pay you back as soon as we can.

2. In a way that is easy to see or notice. (явно, заметно)

  1. She was obviously upset about something.
  2. He'd obviously never cooked a turkey before.
  3. The dog was obviously hungry.

evidently#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛvədəntli/ · EV-uh-duhnt-lee
Русский
по-видимому, очевидно
Type
stance — a conclusion from evidence
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ly from evident
Contrast
Evidently is closer to Russian очевидно, по-видимому (a conclusion) than obviously is. It is a little more formal than apparently, and like apparently it can also pass on what the speaker has heard: Evidently, she quit last week = судя по всему (говорят), она уволилась.

Evidently has 2 distinct senses.

1. Based on what you can see or have found out; apparently. (по-видимому, судя по всему)

  1. The owners had evidently left in a hurry.
  2. Evidently, nobody told him about the schedule change.
  3. “Did the package come?” “Evidently not.”

2. In a way that is plain to see; clearly. (явно)

  1. He was evidently nervous — his hands were shaking.
  2. She was evidently pleased with the results.

apparently#

Pronunciation
/əˈpɛrəntli/ · uh-PAIR-uhnt-lee
Русский
по-видимому, говорят, судя по всему
Type
stance — what the speaker has heard or what seems true
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma, and end in speech
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives (apparently harmless)
Form
-ly from apparent
Contrast
False friend: apparently does not mean явно, очевидно. The adjective can (for no apparent reason = без видимой причины), but the adverb almost always means “I heard” or “it seems”: Apparently he's rich = говорят, он богат.

Apparently has 2 distinct senses.

1. According to what you have heard or read, though you cannot be sure. (говорят, судя по всему)

  1. Apparently, the restaurant is closing next month.
  2. He's apparently moving to Denver.
  3. The meeting got canceled, apparently.

2. Seeming to be true from what you can see, though it may not be. (по-видимому, на первый взгляд)

  1. The back door had apparently been forced open.
  2. It started as an apparently harmless joke.

presumably#

Pronunciation
/prɪˈzuːməbli/ · prih-ZOO-muh-blee
Русский
надо полагать, вероятно
Type
stance — a reasonable assumption
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ably from presume
Contrast
Presumably is the speaker's own reasonable guess, which they expect to be right. Russian news style предположительно (предположительно, преступник) is allegedly or suspected in English, not presumably.

Used to say that you think something is very likely true, based on what you know.

  1. You're presumably tired after such a long flight.
  2. Presumably, the package will arrive tomorrow.
  3. He left early, presumably to beat the traffic.

probably#

Pronunciation
/ˈprɑːbəbli/ · PRAHB-uh-blee
Русский
вероятно, наверное
Type
stance — likelihood
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary, and before a negative auxiliary (probably won't)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ably from probable
Contrast
Position with a negative: He probably won't come, not He won't probably come. Russian наверное is often a polite hedge; probably really means “more likely than not”, so I'll probably come is a weak promise.

Very likely; more likely than not.

  1. It'll probably rain this afternoon.
  2. She probably won't remember me.
  3. “Are you coming tonight?” “Probably.”

possibly#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɑːsəbli/ · PAHS-uh-blee
Русский
возможно; никак (не)
Type
stance — possibility; also degree
Position
mid — before the main verb, after the modal (can't possibly); also alone as an answer
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives, numbers
Form
-ibly from possible
Contrast
Can't possibly is not “maybe can't” — it means “there is no way”: I can't possibly finish by Friday = я никак не успею к пятнице. In a request, Could you possibly…? is extra polite, not doubtful.

Possibly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Perhaps; used when something could be true but is not certain. (возможно)

  1. The drive could take three hours, possibly more.
  2. It was possibly the worst movie I've ever seen.
  3. “Will you finish today?” “Possibly.”

2. Used with can or could for emphasis: in any way at all. (никак; всё, что возможно)

  1. I can't possibly eat all that.
  2. How could anyone possibly believe him?
  3. We did everything we possibly could.

3. Used to make a request more polite. (не могли бы вы)

  1. Could you possibly turn the music down a little?
  2. Could I possibly borrow your notes from Tuesday?

maybe#

Pronunciation
/ˈmeɪbi/ · MAY-bee
Русский
может быть, возможно
Type
stance — possibility
Position
front — usually at the start of the clause; also before a number, and alone as an answer
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, numbers (maybe fifty)
Form
one word, from may + be
Contrast
Maybe (one word) is the adverb; may be (two words) is a modal and a verb: Maybe he is right = He may be right. Never He maybe right. Maybe is informal; in writing perhaps is more common.

Maybe has 3 distinct senses.

1. Perhaps; possibly. (может быть, возможно)

  1. Maybe she's stuck in traffic.
  2. We could go to the beach, or maybe the lake.
  3. “Are you coming with us?” “Maybe.”

2. Used to make a suggestion politely. (может, (давай))

  1. Maybe we should call a plumber.
  2. Maybe you could ask your dad for help.

3. Used before a number: about, approximately. (примерно, где-то)

  1. There were maybe fifty people at the meeting.
  2. It's maybe a ten-minute walk from here.

perhaps#

Pronunciation
/pərˈhæps/ · pur-HAPS
Русский
возможно, может быть
Type
stance — possibility
Position
front — at the start of the clause; also mid, especially before an adjective or noun phrase
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives, numbers
Contrast
Perhaps is more formal than maybe; in everyday American conversation maybe is far more common. Unlike maybe, perhaps sits easily in mid position: It was perhaps her best book.

Perhaps has 3 distinct senses.

1. Possibly; maybe. (возможно, может быть)

  1. Perhaps the letter got lost in the mail.
  2. It was perhaps the most important decision of her career.
  3. “Will he agree?” “Perhaps.”

2. Used to make a suggestion, offer or request more polite. (может быть, не хотите ли)

  1. Perhaps you'd like to wait in the lobby.
  2. Perhaps we could discuss this after lunch?

3. Used before a number: about, approximately. (примерно, около)

  1. Perhaps two hundred people showed up.
  2. The house is perhaps a mile from the highway.

undoubtedly#

Pronunciation
/ʌnˈdaʊtɪdli/ · uhn-DOW-tid-lee
Русский
несомненно, бесспорно
Type
stance — certainty
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
un- + doubted + -ly; the b is silent and the -ed is a full syllable
Contrast
Undoubtedly means full certainty; doubtless, despite its look, often means only “probably”.

Without any doubt; certainly.

  1. She is undoubtedly the best player on the team.
  2. Prices will undoubtedly go up again next year.
  3. Undoubtedly, the new law will be challenged in court.

arguably#

Pronunciation
/ˈɑːrɡjuəbli/ · AHR-gyoo-uh-blee
Русский
пожалуй, можно сказать
Type
stance — a claim the speaker supports
Position
mid — usually right before the adjective or noun phrase it comments on; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, superlatives, noun phrases, clauses
Form
-ably from argue
Contrast
False friend: the adjective arguable often means “спорный”, but arguably supports the claim — He is arguably the best = пожалуй, он лучший (and the speaker thinks so). It is not doubt and not доказуемо.

Used to say that something can reasonably be claimed, and the speaker believes it, though others may disagree.

  1. He is arguably the greatest basketball player of all time.
  2. It was arguably the most important invention of the century.
  3. Arguably, the city's best food is found in its smallest restaurants.

allegedly#

Pronunciation
/əˈlɛdʒɪdli/ · uh-LEJ-id-lee
Русский
якобы, предположительно
Type
stance — a claim that has not been proved
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, clauses
Form
-ly from alleged; the -ed is a full syllable
Contrast
In news reports allegedly protects the writer: someone has made the claim, usually an accusation, but it is not proved. It is not the speaker's own guess — that is presumably.

According to what someone has said, especially in an accusation that has not been proved.

  1. The suspect allegedly stole more than $10,000 from his employer.
  2. He was allegedly driving over 90 miles an hour.
  3. The company allegedly hid the test results from regulators.

supposedly#

Pronunciation
/səˈpoʊzɪdli/ · suh-POH-zid-lee
Русский
якобы, будто бы
Type
stance — what people say, with doubt
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be and the first auxiliary; also front with a comma
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, adjectives, clauses
Form
-ly from supposed; the -ed is a full syllable: /zɪd/
Contrast
Supposedly signals that the speaker doubts the claim (якобы), unlike presumably (the speaker believes it). The form supposably is heard but counts as an error.

According to what people say or believe, though the speaker doubts it is true.

  1. The old house is supposedly haunted.
  2. This cream supposedly makes you look ten years younger.
  3. He was supposedly sick, but I saw him at the game.

According to what has been reported, though it is not confirmed.

seemingly#

Pronunciation
/ˈsiːmɪŋli/ · SEE-ming-lee
Русский
казалось бы, на вид; по-видимому
Type
stance — how things appear
Position
before its word — usually before an adjective; also front with a comma, or mid
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives (seemingly endless), verbs, clauses
Form
-ly from the participle seeming
Contrast
Before an adjective, seemingly hints that the look is false: a seemingly simple question = казалось бы, простой вопрос. At the front it is closer to apparently: Seemingly, nobody noticed = судя по всему, никто не заметил.

Seemingly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Appearing to be something, especially when this may not be true. (казалось бы, на вид)

  1. It was a seemingly simple question, but nobody could answer it.
  2. We waited in a seemingly endless line of cars.
  3. The seemingly quiet town had a lot of secrets.

2. As far as anyone can tell; apparently. (по-видимому, судя по всему)

  1. Seemingly, nobody noticed the mistake.
  2. The two men were seemingly unaware of each other.

Unfortunately is the everyday English for к сожалению — in emails, refusals and bad news. Regrettably is formal and official, sadly adds real emotion, and to my regret sounds stiff.

Used to say that something is bad news or not what you wanted.

  1. Unfortunately, the concert is sold out.

Used to say that something good happened because of good luck.

  1. Luckily, I had my phone with me.

sadly#

Pronunciation
/ˈsædli/ · SAD-lee
Русский
к сожалению; грустно, печально
Type
stance — the speaker's feeling about it; also manner and degree
Position
front — with a comma as a stance adverb; end or mid in the manner sense; before its word in the degree sense
Comparison
more/most — in the manner sense only (more sadly); not gradable as a stance adverb
Modifies
clauses, verbs, adjectives and participles (sadly mistaken)
Form
-ly from sad
Contrast
Position changes the meaning: Sadly, she left = к сожалению, она ушла; She left sadly = она ушла грустная. Sadly mistaken is degree: “very wrong”, with no sadness at all.

Sadly has 3 distinct senses.

1. Used to say that something is sad or regrettable; unfortunately. (к сожалению, увы)

  1. Sadly, the old theater was torn down in 2015.
  2. Her grandfather sadly passed away last spring.
  3. Sadly, the museum is closed on Mondays.

2. In a sad way. (грустно, печально)

  1. She smiled sadly and waved goodbye.
  2. He shook his head sadly.

3. Very much or completely, in a bad way — before a few adjectives and participles. (глубоко, совсем, прискорбно)

  1. If you think I'll pay for that, you're sadly mistaken.
  2. The old park has been sadly neglected.

regrettably#

Pronunciation
/rɪˈɡrɛtəbli/ · rih-GRET-uh-blee
Русский
к сожалению, прискорбно
Type
stance — the speaker's feeling about it; also degree
Position
front — with a comma; mid; before its word in the degree sense
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clauses, adjectives (regrettably slow), quantifiers (regrettably few)
Form
-ably from regret, with the t doubled
Contrast
Regrettably means “it is a pity”; regretfully means “with regret, feeling sorry” (She regretfully declined). Many Americans use regretfully for both, but careful writing keeps them apart. Regrettably is formal — in conversation say unfortunately.

Regrettably has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used in formal speech or writing to say that something is unfortunate. (к сожалению)

  1. Regrettably, we are unable to offer you a position at this time.
  2. The event has regrettably been canceled.

2. To a degree that is a pity — before an adjective or quantifier. (прискорбно, досадно)

  1. Regrettably few students signed up for the program.
  2. The city's response to the flooding was regrettably slow.

удивительно, поразительно

ironically#

Pronunciation
/aɪˈrɑːnɪkli/ · eye-RAHN-ik-lee
Русский
по иронии судьбы; иронично
Type
stance — an unexpected reversal; also manner
Position
front — with a comma as a stance adverb; end in the manner sense
Comparison
more/most — even more ironically, most ironically, at the front; more ironically in the manner sense
Modifies
clauses, verbs
Form
-ally from ironic; spelled -ically, said /-ɪkli/
Contrast
Strictly, ironically needs a result opposite to what you would expect (по иронии судьбы); Americans often use it loosely for any coincidence or bad luck, which careful writers dislike.

Ironically has 2 distinct senses.

1. Used to say that something happened in the opposite way to what you would expect, in a strange or funny way. (по иронии судьбы)

  1. Ironically, the fire station burned down.
  2. The anti-smoking ad was, ironically, paid for by a tobacco company.
  3. Ironically, the more he tried to relax, the more stressed he got.

2. In an ironic way, meaning the opposite of what you say. (иронично, с иронией)

  1. “Great job,” he said ironically, looking at the broken vase.
  2. She smiled ironically when he called it a small problem.

Position changes the meaning: She asked about him curiously = спросила с любопытством; Curiously, she asked about him = странно, что она спросила. It is not курьёзно (funny, absurd).

Curiously has 3 distinct senses.

  1. Curiously, nobody heard the alarm.
  2. Curiously enough, the letter had no return address.

3. Strangely, in a way that is hard to explain — before an adjective. (странно, необычно)

  1. The street was curiously empty for a Saturday night.
  2. He seemed curiously calm after the accident.
