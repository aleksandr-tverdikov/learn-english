Adverbs of time#

When? — now, soon, already, yet, still, recently, ago, and the words that place an event on the timeline.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Time adverbs locate an event relative to now or to another event. Several of them are tied to a tense: already, yet and just pull toward the present perfect in American and British English alike, although American speech also allows the simple past (Did you eat yet?). Ago is the odd one out — it follows its time phrase (three days ago) and cannot be used with the present perfect.

now#

Pronunciation
/naʊ/ · NOW
Русский
сейчас, теперь
Type
time; also linking (a discourse marker: Now, listen…)
Position
any position — usually end; front for contrast or as a discourse marker; mid in formal writing (We now turn to…)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses; also just now, right now, by now
Contrast
Russian сейчас also means 'in a moment' (сейчас приду); English does not use now for that: I'll be right there or I'm coming, not I come now. Now that… (Now that you're here, we can start) is a conjunction and belongs to another dictionary. Right now is 'at this very moment' or 'immediately', never 'just a moment ago' — that is just now. With a length of time, now is Russian уже: We've lived here ten years now (мы живём здесь уже десять лет), not already ten years.

Now has 9 distinct senses.

1. At the present moment. (сейчас)

  1. I can't talk now — I'm driving.
  2. She's in a meeting right now.
  3. Where are you living now?

2. At this period of time, in contrast with the past. (теперь, в наше время)

  1. I used to hate coffee, but now I drink three cups a day.
  2. Most people now pay with their phones.
  3. Prices are much higher now than when I was a kid.

3. In the situation that exists after what has just happened; as a result. (теперь)

  1. You missed the last bus — how are you getting home now?
  2. She read the letter, so now she knows the truth.
  3. We've come this far; we can't turn back now.

4. Immediately; without any delay. (сейчас же, немедленно)

  1. Leave now or you'll miss the bus.
  2. I need that report now, not tomorrow.
  3. Call her right now, before she goes to bed.

5. With a length of time: up to the present, counting from when something began. (уже)

  1. We've been married twenty years now.
  2. It's been three weeks now, and she still hasn't called.
  3. I've lived in Texas for a long time now.

6. At the point in a past story being told; by that time. (теперь, уже, тут)

  1. It was now completely dark, and we were lost.
  2. The crowd was now chanting his name.

7. Only a moment ago (just now). (только что)

  1. Your mom called just now.
  2. Who was that at the door just now?

8. Occasionally, from time to time (now and then, now and again). (время от времени, иногда)

  1. We still see each other every now and then.
  2. Now and again he sends me a postcard.

9. Used to get attention, move to a new point, or soften a warning or comfort. (итак, ну, так вот; ну-ну (успокаивая))

  1. Now, let's look at the second question.
  2. Now, now, don't cry — it's only a scratch.
  3. Now listen to me carefully.

then#

Pronunciation
/ðɛn/ · THEN
Русский
тогда, потом, затем
Type
time; also linking (in that case, but then)
Position
any position — 'next' front, after and, or mid (she then left); 'at that time' usually end; 'in that case' front or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and whole clauses
Contrast
then is not than: She's taller than me, never taller then me — the two sound almost the same in fast speech and native speakers misspell them too. then is an adverb, not a conjunction, so careful writing joins two clauses with and then: I finished my work, and then I went home, not I finished my work, then I went home. The adjective use (the then president) belongs to another dictionary.

Then has 5 distinct senses.

1. After that; next in time or in order. (потом, затем)

  1. We had dinner, and then we went to a movie.
  2. First add the flour, then the eggs.
  3. Turn left at the light, and then it's the second house on the right.

2. At that time, in the past or the future. (тогда, в то время)

  1. We were living in Chicago then.
  2. Call me Friday — I'll know more then.
  3. Back then, nobody had a cell phone.

3. In that case; so; as a result. (тогда, значит, в таком случае)

  1. If you're tired, then go to bed.
  2. "I don't have a car." "Then how did you get here?"
  3. Okay then, I'll see you tomorrow.

4. In addition; on top of that (and then). (а ещё, к тому же)

  1. The rent is high, and then there's parking to pay for.
  2. I have to finish this report, and then there are all those emails.

5. On the other hand; considering this (but then, then again). (впрочем, с другой стороны)

  1. He's very quiet, but then his father was the same way.
  2. I might go to the party. Then again, I might just stay home.

today#

Pronunciation
/təˈdeɪ/ · tuh-DAY
Русский
сегодня; в наши дни
Type
time
Position
front or end — usually end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Contrast
No preposition: I'm working today, not on today. Russian сегодня утром / сегодня вечером is this morning / this evening or tonight — never today morning or today evening. When today is the subject (Today is Monday) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

Today has 2 distinct senses.

1. On this day. (сегодня)

  1. I have a dentist appointment today.
  2. What are you doing today?
  3. The store closes early today.

2. At the present period; nowadays. (в наши дни, сегодня)

  1. Kids today spend hours online.
  2. Today, most homes in the US have internet access.
  3. Few people today remember how to use a paper map.

tomorrow#

Pronunciation
/təˈmɑːroʊ/ · tuh-MAHR-oh
Русский
завтра
Type
time
Position
front or end — usually end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Contrast
No preposition: See you tomorrow, not on tomorrow. Завтра утром is usually tomorrow morning (tomorrow in the morning sounds clumsy); послезавтра is the day after tomorrow. A timetable takes the present simple: The flight leaves at seven tomorrow. As a subject (Tomorrow is Friday) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

On the day after today.

  1. I'll call you tomorrow.
  2. Tomorrow we're driving up to the lake.
  3. The package should arrive tomorrow morning.

yesterday#

Pronunciation
/ˈjɛstərdeɪ/ · YES-tur-day
Русский
вчера
Type
time
Position
front or end — usually end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Contrast
Past simple, not the present perfect: I saw her yesterday, not I have seen her yesterday. Вчера вечером is yesterday evening or last night; вчера ночью is last night, never yesterday night. Позавчера is the day before yesterday. As a subject (Yesterday was cold) it is a noun and belongs to another dictionary.

Yesterday has 2 distinct senses.

1. On the day before today. (вчера)

  1. I ran into your sister yesterday.
  2. It rained all day yesterday.
  3. Yesterday afternoon we finally finished the project.

2. Very recently, as it seems (only yesterday). (совсем недавно)

  1. It seems like only yesterday that we moved in.
  2. I remember it as if it happened yesterday.

tonight#

Pronunciation
/təˈnaɪt/ · tuh-NYT
Русский
сегодня вечером, сегодня ночью
Type
time
Position
front or end — usually end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Contrast
Russian splits сегодня вечером and сегодня ночью; English tonight covers both, from about sunset on. The night that has just ended is last night, not tonight or this night. No preposition: Are you free tonight?, not at tonight.

On the evening or night of today.

  1. Are you free tonight?
  2. It's supposed to snow tonight.
  3. Tonight we're having pizza and watching a movie.

soon#

Pronunciation
/suːn/ · SOON
Русский
скоро, вскоре
Type
time
Position
mid or end — end is most common; mid after an auxiliary (will soon be); front for a new stage in a story
Comparison
-er/-est — sooner, soonest
Modifies
verbs; also too soon, how soon, soon after
Contrast
No sooner fronted forces inversion and takes than, not when: No sooner had we sat down than the phone rang. As soon as takes the present for a future event: Call me as soon as you arrive, not as soon as you will arrive. soon is not early: I got up early, not I got up soon.

Soon has 3 distinct senses.

1. In a short time from now or from the time mentioned. (скоро, вскоре)

  1. Dinner will be ready soon.
  2. Soon after we arrived, it started to rain.
  3. We'll soon find out who won.

2. Early or quickly; before the time expected. (рано, быстро)

  1. How soon can you get here?
  2. It's too soon to say whether the treatment is working.
  3. The sooner we leave, the sooner we'll get there.

3. Rather; preferably (would sooner, would just as soon). (скорее, лучше, охотнее)

  1. I'd sooner walk than wait an hour for the bus.
  2. She would sooner quit than apologize to him.
  3. I'd just as soon stay home tonight.

later#

Pronunciation
/ˈleɪtər/ · LAY-tur
Русский
позже, потом
Type
time
Position
end — also front (Later, …) and mid (she later became…)
Comparison
not gradable — it is already the comparative of late (much later, a little later)
Modifies
verbs; follows a time phrase (two years later)
Form
comparative of late, used as a time adverb in its own right
Contrast
two days later counts forward from a past event (спустя два дня); counting forward from now is in two days: I'll be back in two days, not two days later. Standing alone, later or afterward is safe; after at the end (I'll do it after) is casual. Do not confuse with latter (the second of two).

Later has 3 distinct senses.

1. At some time after now; afterward. (позже, потом)

  1. I'll call you later.
  2. Can we talk about this later?
  3. See you later!

2. After the past time or event mentioned. (позже, впоследствии, (три года) спустя)

  1. Three years later, they got married.
  2. She later became a federal judge.
  3. Later that night, the power went out.

3. At a time after the usual, expected or stated time. (позже, позднее)

  1. Can we meet later than six?
  2. The kids stayed up later than usual.

recently#

Pronunciation
/ˈriːsəntli/ · REE-sunt-lee
Русский
недавно; в последнее время
Type
time
Position
mid or end — also front
Comparison
more/most — more recently, most recently
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Form
-ly from recent
Contrast
The tense follows the meaning: one event 'not long ago' takes the past simple (I recently bought a car); a period 'lately' takes the present perfect (I've been busy recently). lately covers only the second meaning: I recently met her, not I lately met her.

Recently has 2 distinct senses.

1. Not long ago. (недавно)

  1. We recently moved to Austin.
  2. The bridge was recently repainted.
  3. Most recently, she ran a small bakery in Denver.

2. During the period up to now. (в последнее время)

  1. Have you seen any good movies recently?
  2. I haven't been sleeping well recently.

already#

Pronunciation
/ɔːlˈrɛdi/ · awl-RED-ee
Русский
уже
Type
time
Position
mid or end — mid is neutral (I've already seen it); end adds emphasis or surprise
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses; also time expressions (already midnight)
Contrast
already goes in affirmative sentences; negatives and ordinary questions take yet (Have you finished yet?); already in a question shows surprise. The present perfect is standard, but American English freely uses the past simple: I already ate. Russian уже не is not … anymore or no longer: He doesn't work here anymore, not He already doesn't work here. all ready is two words meaning 'fully prepared': We're all ready to go.

Already has 2 distinct senses.

1. Before now or before a particular time, often sooner than expected (in a question, with surprise: so soon?). (уже)

  1. I've already eaten, thanks.
  2. By the time we got there, the movie had already started.
  3. You're leaving already?

2. Informal American: used after a command or complaint to show impatience. (уже, ну же (с нетерпением))

  1. Enough already! We get it.
  2. Just make up your mind already.

yet#

Pronunciation
/jɛt/ · YET
Русский
ещё (не), уже (в вопросах)
Type
time; also degree (yet another) and linking (and yet)
Position
end — in negatives and questions; mid in have yet to, may yet; before its word in yet another, yet again
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses; comparatives and another, again
Contrast
Russian ещё is both yet and still: I haven't decided yet (ещё не решил) but I still live there (всё ещё живу); still goes before a negative auxiliary (He still hasn't called), yet at the end (He hasn't called yet). In questions yet is Russian уже: Has the mail come yet? American English allows the past simple: Did you eat yet? have yet to looks affirmative but is negative. The conjunction yet (simple yet effective) belongs to another dictionary.

Yet has 5 distinct senses.

1. Up to now; so far (in negatives and questions, and after a superlative). (ещё (не); уже (в вопросах); на сегодняшний день)

  1. I haven't finished yet.
  2. "Are we there yet?" "Not yet."
  3. That was her best album yet.

2. Not so far, although it is expected (have yet to, be yet to). (ещё не, пока не)

  1. The winner has yet to be announced.
  2. I have yet to meet anyone who actually likes that show.

3. Still; even now; at some time in the future. (ещё, всё ещё, всё-таки)

  1. We may yet win this game.
  2. There's hope for him yet.
  3. Don't give up — this plan could yet work.

4. Even more; one more time (yet another, yet again, before a comparative). (ещё (один, раз, более))

  1. The flight was delayed yet again.
  2. That's yet another reason to stay home.
  3. We got yet more snow overnight.

5. Nevertheless; in spite of that (and yet). (и всё же, однако)

  1. It's a tiny town, and yet it has three bookstores.
  2. He knew it was risky, and yet he went ahead anyway.

still#

Pronunciation
/stɪl/ · STIL
Русский
всё ещё, до сих пор; всё же
Type
time; also linking (nevertheless) and degree (still better)
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be, before a negative auxiliary; front for 'nevertheless'
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses; comparatives (still more)
Contrast
Before a negative auxiliary: He still hasn't called (всё ещё не), not He hasn't still called. still is for something continuing, not yet for something not started, not anymore for something that stopped. Russian ещё один is another or one more, not still one. The adjective 'not moving' (still water, and also sit still, hold still, keep still) belongs to another dictionary.

Still has 3 distinct senses.

1. Continuing up to now or up to the time mentioned. (всё ещё, до сих пор)

  1. She still lives with her parents.
  2. Are you still at work?
  3. It was midnight, and he still hadn't called.

2. Nevertheless; even so. (всё же, тем не менее)

  1. It rained all weekend. Still, we had a great time.
  2. He drives me crazy, but he's still my brother.
  3. The hotel was expensive, but it was still worth it.

3. Even; to a greater degree (with comparatives and another, more). (ещё (более))

  1. The next day was hotter still.
  2. Ten minutes is good; five is still better.
  3. Better still, call ahead and reserve a table.

immediately#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈmiːdiətli/ · ih-MEE-dee-ut-lee
Русский
немедленно, сразу
Type
time; also place (immediately behind)
Position
end — also mid (she immediately called) and before its word (immediately after, immediately below)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; prepositions and adverbs of time and place (immediately after)
Form
-ly from immediate
Contrast
British English also uses immediately as a conjunction (Immediately I saw her, I knew); American English says as soon as or the moment: As soon as I saw her, I knew.

Immediately has 2 distinct senses.

1. At once; without any delay. (немедленно, сразу)

  1. Please call 911 immediately.
  2. She immediately recognized his voice.
  3. The new rules take effect immediately.

2. Directly next in time, place or relationship, with nothing between. (сразу, непосредственно)

  1. We left immediately after the ceremony.
  2. The restrooms are immediately to your left.
  3. Only the people immediately affected were told.

eventually#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈvɛntʃuəli/ · ih-VEN-choo-uh-lee
Русский
в конце концов, со временем
Type
time
Position
mid or end — also front
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Form
-ly from eventual
Contrast
False friend: eventually never means 'possibly, if need be' (эвентуально, German eventuell). It says the thing did or will happen in the end, usually after a delay: He eventually agreed = в конце концов согласился. For 'possibly' use possibly or potentially. Unlike finally, it cannot introduce the last point of a list.

In the end, after a long time, a delay or a series of events.

  1. We waited two hours, and the bus eventually came.
  2. You'll get used to the cold eventually.
  3. Eventually, they sold the house and moved to Florida.

finally#

Pronunciation
/ˈfaɪnəli/ · FYN-uh-lee
Русский
наконец; окончательно
Type
time; also linking (the last point)
Position
mid — front when it introduces the last point
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses
Form
-ly from final
Contrast
finally, at last and eventually differ: at last stresses relief and cannot begin a last point in a list; eventually is neutral 'in the end' and also cannot list; lastly only lists. Mid position for 'after a long time' (We finally got home), front with a comma for the last point (Finally, …).

Finally has 3 distinct senses.

1. After a long time, often with relief or impatience. (наконец)

  1. We finally got home at two in the morning.
  2. After six years, she finally finished her degree.
  3. Finally! I thought you'd never get here.

2. As the last in a series of points or actions. (наконец, в заключение)

  1. Finally, I'd like to thank my parents.
  2. Mix the dry ingredients, add the eggs, and finally pour in the milk.

3. In a way that settles something for good. (окончательно)

  1. The lawsuit was finally settled out of court.
  2. Nothing has been finally decided yet.

afterward#

Pronunciation
/ˈæftərwərd/ · AF-tur-wurd
Русский
потом, после этого, впоследствии
Type
time
Position
end — also front (Afterward, …)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses; soon afterward, shortly afterward
Form
-ward — from after; afterwards is the British form, also common in the US
Contrast
Russian после is both a preposition and an adverb; in English the adverb is afterward or later, while after normally needs an object: We went for coffee afterward, but after the movie. afterward never takes an object: after dinner, not afterward dinner.

After the time or event mentioned; later.

  1. We saw a play and went out for dessert afterward.
  2. Shortly afterward, the police arrived.
  3. I felt terrible about it afterward.

nowadays#

Pronunciation

time
Position
front or end
Comparison
not gradable

Contrast
For a general situation compared with the past, not a single moment: Nowadays people rarely write letters, but She's in the shower now, not She's in the shower nowadays. In everyday American speech these days is more common.

At the present time, compared with the past.

  1. Nowadays, almost everyone has a smartphone.
  2. Hardly anyone pays by check nowadays.
  3. Kids nowadays don't know what a cassette is.

currently#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɜːrəntli/ · KUR-unt-lee
Русский
в настоящее время, сейчас
Type
time
Position
mid — before the main verb, after be; also front
Comparison
not gradable

-ly from current
Contrast
False friend: actually looks like Russian актуально / актуальный, but it means 'in fact' (на самом деле), never 'at the moment' or 'relevant'. 'At the moment' is currently: She is currently working in Boston; 'relevant, topical' is relevant or current.

At the present time.

  1. She's currently working on her second novel.
  2. The position is currently unfilled.
  3. Currently, about forty people work at the plant.

formerly#

Pronunciation
/ˈfɔːrmərli/ · FOR-mur-lee
Русский
раньше, прежде, ранее
Type
time
Position
mid — also before its word (formerly known as)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, participles and names (Myanmar, formerly Burma)
Form
-ly from former
Contrast
formerly (in the past) and formally (officially, in a formal way) sound almost the same in American speech. For бывший before a noun, English uses the adjective former (a former teacher); formerly goes with a verb or a name.

In the past, but not any longer.

  1. The restaurant was formerly a bank.
  2. He formerly worked as a nurse in Ohio.
  3. Myanmar, formerly known as Burma, is in Southeast Asia.

previously#

Pronunciation
/ˈpriːviəsli/ · PREE-vee-us-lee
Русский
ранее, прежде
Type
time
Position
mid — also front, and after a time phrase (two days previously)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, participles; follows a time phrase
Form
-ly from previous
Contrast
two years previously (or earlier, before) counts back from a past point; counting back from now is two years ago. previously is formal — in speech, before or earlier is more natural.

Before now or before the time mentioned, also after a time phrase (three months previously).

  1. She had previously worked for the city.
  2. As previously mentioned, the office is closed on Monday.
  3. He had left the company three months previously.

once#

Pronunciation
/wʌns/ · WUNS
Русский
один раз; когда-то
Type
time; also frequency (once a week)
Position
mid or end — mid for 'at one time in the past' (I once lived there); end for 'one time' (I've been there once)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; once a week, once more, at once
Contrast
Position changes the meaning: I once lived in Ohio (когда-то) against I've been to Ohio once (один раз). at once is 'immediately' or 'at the same time'; all at once is usually 'suddenly'. The conjunction once (Once you start, you can't stop = как только) belongs to another dictionary.

Once has 7 distinct senses.

1. One time; on one occasion. (один раз)

  1. I've only been to New York once.
  2. We meet once a week.
  3. Take one pill once a day with food.

2. At some time in the past, but no longer. (когда-то, однажды)

  1. This town was once a busy port.
  2. I once met him at an airport in Dallas.
  3. The house once belonged to a famous writer.

3. Ever; even one time (after a negative). (ни разу; хоть раз)

  1. He never once said thank you.
  2. Not once did she complain about the pain.

4. Again; one more time (once more, once again). (ещё раз, снова)

  1. Let's try it once more from the top.
  2. Once again, the team lost at home.

5. Immediately (at once). (сразу, немедленно)

  1. Come here at once!
  2. The doctor saw me at once.

6. At the same time (at once). (одновременно, сразу)

  1. Don't all talk at once.
  2. I can't do three things at once.

7. Suddenly (all at once). (вдруг, внезапно)

  1. All at once, the lights went out.
  2. All at once she started laughing.

someday#

Pronunciation
/ˈsʌmdeɪ/ · SUM-day
Русский
когда-нибудь
Type
time
Position
front or end
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses, about the future

At some unknown time in the future.

  1. I want to visit Japan someday.
  2. Someday you'll understand why I did it.
  3. Maybe someday we'll live by the ocean.

meanwhile is an adverb, not a conjunction: I cooked; meanwhile, he set the table, or I cooked while he set the table — not I cooked meanwhile he set the table. in the meantime can replace it for 'until then', but not for contrast.

Meanwhile has 3 distinct senses.

1. At the same time as something else is happening. (тем временем)

  1. I'll make the salad. Meanwhile, you can set the table.
  2. The police searched the house; meanwhile, the suspect was already in Mexico.

2. In the time before something expected happens. (пока, а пока что)

  1. The new office opens in May. Meanwhile, we're working from home.
  2. Your passport will take six weeks; meanwhile, don't book any flights.

3. Used to contrast two situations. (между тем, в то же время)

  1. Rents keep going up. Meanwhile, wages have barely moved.
  2. The city spends millions on stadiums; meanwhile, schools are falling apart.
