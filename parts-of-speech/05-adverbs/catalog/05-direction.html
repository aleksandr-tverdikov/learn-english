Adverbs of direction and adverbial particles#

Where to? — up, down, back, forward, sideways, clockwise, and the particles that complete phrasal verbs.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Direction adverbs answer where to? or which way?. A large part of this group doubles as the particles of phrasal verbs — turn off, give up, come back — which is where their meaning stops being about direction at all. Each entry lists the literal directional sense first and then the most important non-literal ones; the phrasal verbs themselves are in the verb dictionary.

up#

Pronunciation
/ʌp/ · UHP
Русский
вверх, наверх
Type
direction; also particle — the second word of many phrasal verbs (give up, eat up)
Position
end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but always after a pronoun (pick it up)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
Contrast
With an object after it, up is a preposition (walk up the hill); with none, it is the adverb (look up). As a particle it can come before or after a noun object (pick up the box, pick the box up) but must follow a pronoun: pick it up, never pick up it. Very often up does not mean вверх at all but ‘completely’, like a Russian perfective prefix: eat up = съесть, use up = израсходовать, drink up = допить. What's up? and Time's up are adjective uses.

Up has 11 distinct senses.

1. Toward a higher place or position. (вверх, наверх)

  1. She looked up at the sky.
  2. Pick your clothes up off the floor.
  3. Is this elevator going up?

2. Into an upright position, standing or sitting straight. (прямо; встать)

  1. Please stand up when the judge comes in.
  2. Sit up straight at the table.
  3. He helped the old man up after he slipped.

3. Out of bed; awake. (не спать, на ногах)

  1. Are the kids up yet?
  2. We stayed up until two watching the game.
  3. I got up at six this morning.

4. To a higher amount, level, volume, or intensity. (выше, больше, громче)

  1. Gas prices went up again this week.
  2. Can you turn the volume up?
  3. Speak up, please; I can't hear you.

5. Completely, so that nothing is left or the action is finished. (полностью, до конца)

  1. Eat up your vegetables.
  2. Somebody used up all the hot water.
  3. Clean up your room before dinner.

6. So as to be closed, fastened, or tied together. ((застегнуть, завязать, закрыть))

  1. Zip up your jacket; it's freezing.
  2. Don't forget to lock up when you leave.
  3. Tie up the garbage bag before you take it out.

7. Into pieces or small parts. (на куски, на части)

  1. She tore the letter up and threw it away.
  2. Cut the chicken up into small pieces.

8. Into existence, view, or attention. (возникнуть, появиться)

  1. Your name came up at the meeting.
  2. Sorry, something came up and I can't make it.
  3. He didn't show up until nine.

9. Close to a person or place, approaching. ((подойти) близко, вплотную)

  1. A stranger came up and asked for directions.
  2. A black car pulled up outside the house.
  3. Go ahead, I'll catch up.

10. To or in the north, or to a place thought of as more important or central. (на север; в город, в центр)

  1. We're driving up to Maine for the weekend.
  2. They moved up north after they retired.

11. Ahead of an opponent by a stated score. (впереди (по счету))

  1. We were ten points up at halftime.
  2. The Yankees were two runs up going into the ninth.

down#

Pronunciation
/daʊn/ · DOWN
Русский
вниз
Type
direction; also particle — the second word of many phrasal verbs (calm down, break down)
Position
end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but always after a pronoun (write it down)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
Contrast
down the street is the preposition and means ‘along, по улице’, not ‘downhill’. The pronoun goes before the particle: write it down, never write down it. I feel down (мне грустно) is an adjective. Down to Texas and up to Chicago for south and north are everyday American speech, even across flat land.

Down has 9 distinct senses.

1. Toward a lower place or position. (вниз)

  1. Sit down and make yourself at home.
  2. She looked down at her shoes.
  3. The sun goes down around eight in the summer.

2. To a lower amount, level, volume, speed, or intensity. (ниже, меньше, тише)

  1. Turn the music down; the baby's asleep.
  2. Prices have finally come down.
  3. Slow down, you're driving too fast.

3. So as to stop working or operating. ((сломаться, закрыться))

  1. My car broke down on the highway.
  2. The factory shut down last year.
  3. The website went down for an hour this morning.

4. On paper; in writing. (записать, на бумаге)

  1. Write the address down before you forget it.
  2. Did you get all of that down?

5. Firmly in place, or so as to be found or caught. (прочно; (выследить, поймать))

  1. Tie the tarp down so it doesn't blow away.
  2. Nail the boards down before the storm hits.
  3. The police finally tracked him down.

6. To or in the south, or away from the center of things. (на юг; на юге)

  1. We drove down to Florida for spring break.
  2. My grandparents live down south.

7. From an earlier generation or time to a later one. (из поколения в поколение)

  1. The ring was handed down from my grandmother.
  2. These stories have been passed down for centuries.

8. As a first payment, with the rest paid later. (первоначальным взносом)

  1. We put ten percent down on the house.
  2. Drive it home today with no money down.

9. Behind an opponent by a stated score. (позади (по счету))

  1. We were down by six at halftime.
  2. They came back from three goals down.

in#

Pronunciation
/ɪn/ · IN
Русский
внутрь, в
Type
direction; also place and particle (fill in, give in)
Position
end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (hand it in)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
Contrast
Russian в covers three English forms: come in (adverb, no object), come into the room (preposition into, movement) and be in the room (preposition in, location). Come in the house is common in American speech, but into is the standard for movement. in meaning ‘fashionable’ (Wide jeans are in) is an adjective.

In has 6 distinct senses.

1. Into or toward the inside of a place, container, or vehicle. (внутрь)

  1. Come in and sit down.
  2. Get in, I'll give you a ride.
  3. The roof caved in under the snow.

2. At home or at one's place of work. (дома; на месте, на работе)

  1. Is Dr. Patel in today?
  2. I'm not coming in tomorrow; I'm sick.
  3. We stayed in and watched a movie.

3. Arriving, or received at the place where it is expected. (прибывать, поступать)

  1. What time does your flight get in?
  2. The election results are starting to come in.

4. To a person or organization in authority, handed over. (сдать, подать)

  1. Please hand your essays in by Friday.
  2. Viewers sent in thousands of photos.

5. Taking part, included with others. (участвуя, вместе со всеми)

  1. If you're ordering pizza, count me in.
  2. Everyone joined in on the chorus.

6. Into a space, so as to fill or complete it. (вписать, заполнить)

  1. Fill in the blanks with the correct word.
  2. The kids colored in the pictures.

out#

Pronunciation
/aʊt/ · OWT
Русский
наружу, вон
Type
direction; also place and particle (find out, run out)
Position
end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (take it out)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, especially verbs of motion; phrasal verbs
Contrast
Before a place, standard English uses out of: get out of the car. American English drops of before door and window (look out the window, run out the door), where British keeps it. The pictures can run opposite ways: the lights went out = погасли, but the stars came out = появились. out in baseball (he's out) is an adjective.

Out has 11 distinct senses.

1. Away from the inside of a place or container. (наружу, вон)

  1. The kids ran out into the yard.
  2. Can you take the trash out?
  3. Get out!

2. Away from home or work, often for a social activity. (не дома, вне дома)

  1. Sorry, she's out for lunch.
  2. Let's eat out tonight.
  3. We went out on Friday night.

3. So as to be found, understood, or solved. ((выяснить, разобраться))

  1. Did you ever find out who sent the flowers?
  2. I can't figure out this remote.
  3. Don't worry, we'll work it out.

4. So as to stop burning or shining. ((погаснуть, потушить))

  1. The lights went out during the storm.
  2. Put your cigarette out, please.

5. Until finished, or until nothing is left. (до конца; кончиться)

  1. Our money ran out in the second week.
  2. Hear me out before you decide.
  3. The concert sold out in ten minutes.

6. Aloud, so as to be clearly heard. (вслух, громко)

  1. Read the list out loud.
  2. She cried out in pain.

7. Into view, or into public knowledge or sale. (появиться; выйти в свет)

  1. The sun came out after lunch.
  2. Her new album comes out next month.
  3. Sooner or later, the truth will come out.

8. So as to be removed or left out. (исключить, вычеркнуть)

  1. Cross out the wrong answer.
  2. Leave the onions out, please.

9. To many people, distributed. (раздать)

  1. The teacher handed out the tests.
  2. They're giving out free samples at the store.

10. Into unconsciousness. (без сознания)

  1. He passed out from the heat.
  2. The boxer was knocked out in the third round.

11. Far away from land, a town, or the center. (далеко (от берега, города))

  1. They live way out in the country.
  2. The boat was a mile out when the storm hit.

back#

Pronunciation
/bæk/ · BAK
Русский
назад, обратно
Type
direction; also time (back in 2010) and particle (pay back, hold back)
Position
end — after the verb; with a phrasal verb, before or after a noun object but after a pronoun (give it back)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; phrasal verbs; time phrases (back in the day)
Contrast
back is not ‘again’: come back = вернуться, come again = прийти ещё раз. With a pronoun: give it back, never give back it. a few years back is informal American for ago. Push a meeting back makes it later; move it forward usually means earlier — say the date if it matters.

Back has 6 distinct senses.

1. To the place, person, or condition where someone or something was before. (обратно, назад)

  1. I'll be back in five minutes.
  2. Put the milk back in the fridge.
  3. She went back to sleep.

2. In return or in reply. (в ответ)

  1. Can I call you back in an hour?
  2. He never paid me back.
  3. She smiled back at him.

3. Toward the rear, away from the front. (назад)

  1. Step back from the edge.
  2. Lean back and relax.
  3. Don't look back.

4. In or into the past; ago. ((тому) назад, в прошлом)

  1. Back in the 1990s, few people had a cell phone.
  2. I met her a few years back.

5. Under control, kept from moving forward or coming out. (сдерживать)

  1. The police held the crowd back.
  2. She fought back tears.

6. To a later time, or to an earlier setting on a clock. (на более позднее время; (часы) назад)

  1. The meeting was pushed back to Friday.
  2. Remember to set your clocks back on Sunday.

forward#

Pronunciation
/ˈfɔːrwərd/ · FOR-wurd
Русский
вперёд
Type
direction; also time and particle (come forward, put forward)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
not gradable — further forward does the comparing
Modifies
verbs, especially verbs of motion
Form
-ward from fore; British also forwards
Contrast
American English says forward; British also forwards. Move the meeting forward usually means earlier, but some Americans hear later — name the day. look forward to takes a noun or -ing: I look forward to seeing you, never to see you. forward is also a verb (forward an email).

Forward has 4 distinct senses.

1. Toward the front, or in the direction one is facing. (вперёд)

  1. Take one step forward.
  2. She leaned forward to hear better.
  3. The car jerked forward and stalled.

2. Toward the future or toward progress. (вперёд, дальше)

  1. It's time to move forward with the plan.
  2. From this day forward, things will be different.

3. Into view or public attention, so as to be offered or known. (выступить; выдвинуть)

  1. Two witnesses have come forward.
  2. She put forward a new proposal.

4. To an earlier date, or to a later setting on a clock. (на более ранний срок; (часы) вперёд)

  1. Set your clocks forward one hour tonight.
  2. We moved the deadline forward to Monday.

backward#

Pronunciation
/ˈbækwərd/ · BAK-wurd
Русский
назад
Type
direction; also manner (count backward)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
-ward from back; American English uses backward and backwards alike
Contrast
Both backward and backwards are American; the adjective is only backward (a backward glance; a backward country = отсталая). Three Russian phrases, three English ones: задом наперёд = backward (your shirt is on backward), наизнанку = inside out, вверх ногами = upside down.

Backward has 5 distinct senses.

1. Toward the back, or in the direction behind one. (назад; навзничь)

  1. He took a step backward.
  2. She fell backward into the pool.

2. In reverse order, from the end to the beginning. (в обратном порядке)

  1. Can you say the alphabet backward?
  2. Count backward from ten.

3. With the back part in front. (задом наперёд)

  1. You have your shirt on backward.
  2. He wears his baseball cap backward.

4. Toward the past, or to a worse state. (назад (в прошлое, к худшему))

  1. Critics say the new law takes the country backward.
  2. Critics called the decision a big step backward.

5. Thoroughly, in every detail (in backward and forward). (вдоль и поперёк)

  1. She knows the rules backward and forward.
  2. I've read that contract backward and forward.

upward#

Pronunciation
/ˈʌpwərd/ · UHP-wurd
Русский
вверх
Type
direction
Position
end; upward of goes before a number
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; numbers (upward of 500)
Form
-ward from up; British usually upwards
Contrast
American English prefers upward, British upwards, though Americans often say upwards of too. upward of 50 means ‘more than 50’ (свыше), not ‘up to 50’. The adjective goes before a noun: an upward trend.

Upward has 3 distinct senses.

1. Toward a higher place. (вверх)

  1. The balloon floated upward into the clouds.
  2. Point the camera upward.

2. Toward a higher amount, level, or rank. (в сторону повышения)

  1. Home prices are moving upward again.
  2. The forecast was revised upward.

3. More than a stated number (in upward of). (свыше, более)

  1. Upward of 500 people showed up.
  2. The repairs could cost upward of $10,000.

downward#

Pronunciation
/ˈdaʊnwərd/ · DOWN-wurd
Русский
вниз
Type
direction
Position
end — after the verb, or after from + a rank (from the CEO downward)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; ranks and levels after from
Form
-ward from down; British usually downwards
Contrast
American downward, British downwards. The adjective sits before a noun (a downward spiral). For лицом вниз the everyday phrase is face down, not face downward.

Downward has 3 distinct senses.

1. Toward a lower place. (вниз)

  1. The path slopes downward to the beach.
  2. She glanced downward at her phone.

2. Toward a lower amount or level. (в сторону понижения)

  1. Sales estimates were revised downward.
  2. Interest rates are heading downward.

3. From a higher rank or level to all the lower ones. (и ниже (по должности, уровню))

  1. Everyone from the CEO downward took a pay cut.
  2. The rule applies to all staff, from managers downward.

inward#

Pronunciation
/ˈɪnwərd/ · IN-wurd
Русский
внутрь
Type
direction
Position
end — after the verb
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
-ward from in; British also inwards
Contrast
Don't confuse with inwardly, which means ‘privately, in one's mind’: Inwardly, she was furious = в душе. The adjective goes before a noun: inward calm. American inward, British also inwards.

Inward has 2 distinct senses.

1. Toward the inside or the center. (внутрь)

  1. The door opens inward.
  2. The walls began to bend inward.

2. Toward one's own mind and feelings. (в себя, внутрь себя)

  1. After the loss, he turned inward and stopped calling friends.
  2. Meditation asks you to look inward.

outward#

Pronunciation
/ˈaʊtwərd/ · OWT-wurd
Русский
наружу
Type
direction
Position
end — after the verb
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
-ward from out; British also outwards
Contrast
For внешне use outwardly: He was outwardly calm, never He was outward calm. The adjective goes before a noun: outward appearance. American outward, British also outwards.

Outward has 2 distinct senses.

1. Toward the outside, or away from the center. (наружу; в стороны)

  1. The door opens outward.
  2. The city keeps spreading outward.

2. Toward other people or the world outside oneself or one's group. (вовне, на внешний мир)

  1. The company needs to look outward for new ideas.
  2. Try to focus outward instead of worrying about yourself.

northward#

Pronunciation
/ˈnɔːrθwərd/ · NORTH-wurd
Русский
на север
Type
direction
Position
end — after the verb
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs of motion and extent
Form
-ward from north; British also northwards
Contrast
In everyday American speech the plain compass word is the adverb: drive north, head north, up north. northward sounds written or technical. No to before the bare word: go north, but to the north.

Toward the north.

  1. The storm is moving northward along the coast.
  2. Many birds fly northward in the spring.
  3. The highway runs northward to the Canadian border.

The everyday adverb is home with no to: go home, head home. homeward is literary; homeward bound (на пути домой) is a fixed phrase.

sideways#

Pronunciation
/ˈsaɪdweɪz/ · SYD-wayz
Русский
вбок, боком
Type
direction; also manner (sideways = боком)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Form
side + -ways; sidewise is a rare American variant
Contrast
Always with -s; there is no adverb sideway. In informal American English go sideways means ‘go wrong’, not a literal direction. A sideways move at work is neither a promotion nor a demotion.

Sideways has 4 distinct senses.

1. To or toward one side. (вбок, в сторону)

  1. The crab scuttled sideways across the sand.
  2. She stepped sideways to let him pass.
  3. The car skidded sideways on the ice.

2. With one side facing forward or up. (боком)

  1. Turn the couch sideways to get it through the door.
  2. He sat sideways in the chair.

3. To a job or position of about the same rank. (на равноценную должность)

  1. She moved sideways into marketing.
  2. He didn't get promoted; he was shifted sideways.

4. Informally, into a bad or unexpected state; wrong. (пойти не так)

  1. The deal went sideways at the last minute.
  2. Things went sideways when the power went out.

clockwise#

Pronunciation
/ˈklɑːkwaɪz/ · KLAHK-wyz
Русский
по часовой стрелке
Type
direction
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs of turning and motion
Form
clock + -wise (‘in the manner of’)
Contrast
The opposite is American counterclockwise, British anticlockwise. The same form is an adjective before a noun: a clockwise direction.

In the direction the hands of a clock move.

  1. Turn the lid clockwise to close it.
  2. The numbers on the dial go clockwise from zero to ten.
  3. Stir the sauce clockwise for two minutes.

In the direction opposite to the way the hands of a clock move.

  1. Turn the knob counterclockwise to open the valve.
  2. The skaters moved counterclockwise around the rink.
  3. Loosen the bolt by turning it counterclockwise.

left#

Pronunciation
/lɛft/ · LEFT
Русский
налево, влево
Type
direction
Position
end — right after the verb (turn left)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs of motion and turning
Contrast
No preposition after turn: turn left, never turn to left; with the noun it is turn to the left or American take a left. Don't confuse it with left, the past tense of leave (She left early). right works the same way and is in the flat-adverb group.

Left has 2 distinct senses.

1. Toward or on the left side. (налево, влево)

  1. Turn left at the light.
  2. Look left, then right, before you cross.
  3. Scoot left a little so I can sit down.

2. Toward liberal or left-wing political views. (влево (в политике))

  1. The party has moved left on climate policy.
  2. Voters in the city lean left.

along#

Pronunciation
/əˈlɔːŋ/ · uh-LAWNG
Русский
вперёд; с собой
Type
direction; also particle (get along, go along with)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs; phrasal verbs
Contrast
With an object along is a preposition meaning вдоль (walk along the river); without one it is the adverb (walk along). along with means ‘together with’. get along = ладить; British also get on. all along means ‘the whole time’, not ‘all the way along something’.

Along has 5 distinct senses.

1. Forward, onward, on one's way. (вперёд, дальше)

  1. A police officer told the crowd to move along.
  2. We were driving along when a tire blew.

2. With oneself or with someone else. (с собой, вместе)

  1. Bring your sister along.
  2. Can I come along?
  3. Take an umbrella along just in case.

3. Making progress toward completion. (продвигаться)

  1. How's your essay coming along?
