More adverbs: A–C#

Adverbs A–C beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Mostly -ly adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (by and large, on purpose). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or -ly forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

a bit#

Pronunciation
/ə ˈbɪt/ · uh BIT
Русский
немного, чуть-чуть
Type
degree — a downtoner, informal
Position
before its word — or end, after a verb (rest a bit)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, comparatives, adverbs and verbs
Contrast
Before a plain adjective it prefers unwelcome qualities: a bit late, a bit tired, not a bit wonderful — use a little or pretty there. Before a noun it needs of: a bit of luck.

To a small degree; slightly, or for a short time.

  1. I'm a bit worried about the weather.
  2. Could you turn the music down a bit?
  3. Let's wait a bit before we call him.

a good deal#

Pronunciation
/ə ˌɡʊd ˈdiːl/ · uh guud DEEL
Русский
намного; много
Type
degree
Position
before its word — before a comparative (a good deal better); end after a verb
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
comparatives, too, and verbs
Contrast
Not the noun a good deal (выгодная сделка): I got a good deal on the car. Before a noun it needs of: a good deal of time.

A good deal has 2 distinct senses.

1. Much; considerably, with a comparative or too. (намного, гораздо)

  1. She looks a good deal better than she did last week.
  2. The new route is a good deal shorter.

2. A lot; much or often, with a verb. (много, часто)

  1. He travels a good deal for work.
  2. We've talked about it a good deal at home.

a little#

Pronunciation
/ə ˈlɪtəl/ · uh LIT-uhl
Русский
немного, слегка
Type
degree — a downtoner
Position
before its word — or end, after a verb (relax a little)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, comparatives, adverbs and verbs
Contrast
A little is positive (немного); little without a is negative (мало): He sleeps a little in the afternoon vs He sleeps little. Unlike a bit, it sits comfortably before pleasant adjectives: a little happier.

To a small degree; slightly.

  1. I'm a little tired after the flight.

a lot#

Pronunciation
/ə ˈlɑːt/ · uh LAHT
Русский
намного; много, часто
Type
degree; also frequency
Position
end — after the verb and object; before its word with a comparative (a lot better)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and comparatives
Contrast
Always two words: alot is a misspelling. It cannot go before a plain adjective: a lot better, but not a lot good — say very good. Informal; in formal writing prefer much or a great deal.

A lot has 2 distinct senses.

1. Much; far, with a comparative. (намного, гораздо)

  1. I feel a lot better today.
  2. This phone is a lot cheaper online.

2. Very much or very often, with a verb. (много, часто)

  1. We go to the beach a lot in the summer.
  2. Thanks a lot for your help.
  3. He talks a lot when he's nervous.

about#

Pronunciation
/əˈbaʊt/ · uh-BOWT
Русский
около, примерно; почти
Type
degree — an approximator
Position
before its word — before numbers and amounts, and before ready, done, finished
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
numbers, amounts and times; adjectives like ready and done
Contrast
Before a noun, about is usually a preposition meaning 'о, про' (a book about dogs), and be about to means 'вот-вот, собираться'. For 'here and there' Americans say around (walk around) where British English says walk about.

About has 2 distinct senses.

1. Approximately; a little more or less than. (около, примерно)

  1. The drive takes about three hours.
  2. There were about fifty people at the party.
  3. I'll be there at about six.

2. Almost; nearly, often in just about. (почти)

  1. I'm about ready to leave.
  2. We're just about done with the kitchen.
  3. He's about the only person who knows the password.
