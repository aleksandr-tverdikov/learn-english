# Degree adverbs that turn it down

> How much, but less? — *fairly*, *rather*, *somewhat*, *slightly*, *barely*, *almost*, *enough*, *kind of*.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Downtoners weaken or approximate. Several carry negative force (*barely*, *scarcely*) and so cannot be combined with another negative. *Almost* and *nearly* are close but not identical, *enough* follows an adjective instead of preceding it (*old enough*, not *enough old*), and *kind of* and *sort of* are the everyday American hedges that formal writing replaces with *somewhat*.

---
### rather

**Pronunciation:** /ˈræðər/ &middot; *RATH-ur*
**Русский:** довольно; лучше; вернее; скорее
**Type:** degree; also linking (*rather* 'instead') and preference in *would rather*
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective, adverb or comparative (*rather long*, *rather better*); mid in *would rather*; front in the 'instead' sense
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, comparatives, verbs of liking (*I rather like it*), and *a/an* + adjective + noun (*rather a long time*)
**Contrast:** In American speech *rather* 'quite' sounds formal or British; everyday American English says *pretty* or *kind of* (*pretty cold*). *Would rather* takes a bare infinitive — *I'd rather go*, not *I'd rather to go* — and a past-tense clause about someone else (*I'd rather you stayed*). *Rather than* (*walk rather than drive*) works as a conjunction or preposition and is covered there. Russian *скорее* also means 'sooner, faster' — that is *sooner* or *faster*, never *rather*.

Rather has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To a fairly large degree; quite, often more than expected.** (довольно, весьма)

1. The movie was *rather* long, but I enjoyed it.
2. The instructions were *rather* confusing.
3. She talked *rather* quickly, so I missed a few words.

**2. Used with *would* (*'d rather*) to say what someone prefers.** (лучше, охотнее)

4. I'd *rather* stay home tonight.
5. Would you *rather* have tea or coffee?
6. I'd *rather* you didn't tell anyone.

**3. More exactly; used after *or* to correct or refine what was just said.** (вернее, точнее)

7. She's my cousin — or *rather*, my second cousin.
8. We left at six, or *rather* a little after six.

**4. Instead; on the contrary — introducing the true situation after a negative statement.** (скорее, наоборот, напротив)

9. The problem isn't the price. *Rather*, it's the long wait.
10. He didn't calm down; *rather*, he got even angrier.
11. The goal is not to win the argument but *rather* to understand the other side.

### somewhat

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌmwʌt/ &middot; *SUM-wut*
**Русский:** несколько, отчасти, в некоторой степени
**Type:** degree — a downtoner, more formal than *kind of* or *sort of*
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, adverbs and comparatives; end or mid with verbs (*It helped somewhat*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, comparatives, and verbs
**Form:** compound of *some* + *what*
**Contrast:** Russian *несколько* means both 'somewhat' and 'several' — *несколько устал* is *somewhat tired*, and *several* never modifies an adjective. *Somewhat* does not stand before a noun: say *a somewhat odd idea*. *Somewhat of a* (*somewhat of a surprise*) is a pronoun use that careful writers replace with *something of a* or *a bit of a*.

To some degree but not very much; a little.

1. I was *somewhat* surprised by her answer.
2. Prices have fallen *somewhat* since last year.
3. The second edition is *somewhat* longer than the first.

### slightly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈslaɪtli/ &middot; *SLYT-lee*
**Русский:** слегка, немного, чуть
**Type:** degree — a downtoner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, adverbs and comparatives; mid or end with verbs
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, comparatives, adverbs, verbs, and the participle *built*
**Form:** -ly from *slight*
**Contrast:** *Slightly* modifies adjectives and verbs, never a noun: *slightly different*, *a slight difference*, not *slightly difference*. Russian *немного* before a noun is *a little* (*a little money*), not *slightly*. 'Not at all' is *not in the slightest*, not *not slightly*.

Slightly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. A little; to a small degree.** (слегка, немного, чуть)

1. The new version is *slightly* faster.
2. I'm *slightly* worried about the weather.
3. The price went up *slightly* in March.

**2. In a slim, small way — used of how a person is built.** (хрупко, худощаво (сложён))

4. He was a quiet, *slightly* built man in his fifties.
5. She is *slightly* built but surprisingly strong.

### moderately

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɑːdərətli/ &middot; *MAHD-ur-uht-lee*
**Русский:** умеренно, в меру, в средней степени
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and participles (*moderately priced*); end in the 'within limits' sense (*drink moderately*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more moderately priced*, *eat more moderately*
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, and verbs
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *moderate*, pronounced MAHD-ur-uht — not like the verb *moderate* (MAHD-uh-rayt)
**Contrast:** Before an adjective *moderately* is faint praise: *moderately successful* means 'so-so', not 'quite successful'. For eating and drinking the everyday phrase is *in moderation* (*drink in moderation*); *moderately* sounds more clinical.

Moderately has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To a medium degree; fairly but not very.** (умеренно, в средней степени)

1. The show was only *moderately* successful.
2. Most of the rooms are *moderately* priced.
3. Stir in the flour and cook over *moderately* high heat.

**2. Without going to extremes; within reasonable limits.** (умеренно, в меру)

4. He drinks only *moderately* — a glass of wine with dinner, at most.
5. Exercise *moderately* at first and build up slowly.

### partly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɑːrtli/ &middot; *PAHRT-lee*
**Русский:** частично, отчасти
**Type:** degree — says 'not completely'
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, noun phrases and *because* or *due to*; mid with verbs (*it partly explains*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, verbs, noun phrases (*partly my fault*), and reason phrases
**Form:** -ly from the noun *part*
**Contrast:** *Partly* and *partially* overlap in 'not completely', but for reasons and in the pair *partly... partly* American usage prefers *partly* (*partly because*, not *partially because*); for a physical, unfinished state *partially* is common (*partially cooked*). Both Russian *частично* and *отчасти* translate *partly*; for *отчасти* with a reason, use *partly*.

Partly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not completely; to some extent.** (частично)

1. It'll be *partly* cloudy tomorrow, with a chance of showers.
2. The door was *partly* open.
3. What she said is only *partly* true.

**2. Used to give one reason or cause among several.** (отчасти)

4. I moved to Denver *partly* for the job and *partly* to be near my sister.
5. The delay was *partly* my fault.
6. Sales fell, *partly* because of the bad weather.

### partially

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɑːrʃəli/ &middot; *PAHR-shuh-lee*
**Русский:** частично, не полностью
**Type:** degree — says 'not completely'
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and participles; mid with verbs (*has partially recovered*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *partial*
**Contrast:** The adjective *partial* also means 'biased' and 'fond of' (*partial to chocolate*), but *partially* today means only 'not completely' — an American reader takes *judged partially* as 'judged in part'. For 'with bias' say *unfairly* or *with bias*; the old sense lives on only in *impartially*. For reasons use *partly*, not *partially* (*partly because*).

Not completely; to some extent.

1. The road is *partially* closed for repairs.
2. He is *partially* deaf in one ear.
3. Add the *partially* cooked pasta to the sauce.

### barely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɛrli/ &middot; *BAIR-lee*
**Русский:** едва, еле, с трудом
**Type:** degree — a downtoner with negative force; also time
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; front with inversion in literary style
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, quantifiers and numbers (*barely enough*, *barely 20 people*)
**Form:** -ly from *bare*, but not its meaning (*bare* = naked, uncovered)
**Contrast:** *Barely* is already negative, so never add *not*: *I can barely hear*, not *I can't barely hear*; tag questions are positive (*She barely knows him, does she?*). The time pattern is *barely... when*, not *than* (*than* goes with *no sooner*). Fronted *Barely* needs inversion: *Barely had we arrived when it started to rain*. Russian *едва ли* means 'unlikely' — that is *hardly likely* or *probably not*, not *barely*.

Barely has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Only just; almost not.** (едва, еле, с трудом)

1. I could *barely* keep my eyes open.
2. She *barely* passed the exam.
3. There was *barely* enough food for everyone.

**2. Only a very short time before; just.** (только-только, едва (как))

4. We had *barely* sat down when the phone rang.
5. She had *barely* started the new job when the company shut down.
6. *Barely* had the game started when it began to rain.

### almost

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːlmoʊst/ &middot; *AWL-mohst*
**Русский:** почти; чуть не
**Type:** degree — an approximator
**Position:** mid — before the main verb (*I almost fell*); before its word with adjectives, numbers, *all*, *every*, *no* and *always*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers, and quantifiers (*almost all*, *almost nobody*)
**Contrast:** *Almost* is not a determiner: *almost all people* or *most people*, never *almost people*, and *almost all of us*, not *almost of us*. *Almost* and *nearly* are close, but only *almost* goes with negative words (*almost nothing*, *almost never*, not *nearly nothing*), and only *nearly* takes *very* or *not* (*very nearly*, *not nearly enough*). For 'я чуть не упал' say *I almost fell* — no negative, not *I almost didn't fall*.

Almost has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Not quite; very nearly.** (почти)

1. Dinner's *almost* ready.
2. *Almost* everyone came to the meeting.
3. She *almost* always takes the bus.

**2. Used with a past verb for something that very nearly happened but did not.** (чуть не, едва не)

4. I *almost* missed the flight.
5. He slipped on the ice and *almost* fell.

### practically

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpræktɪkli/ &middot; *PRAK-tik-lee*
**Русский:** практически, почти; на практике
**Type:** degree; also manner and viewpoint
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, quantifiers and verbs; end in the 'sensibly' sense (*think practically*); front as a viewpoint adverb (*Practically speaking*)
**Comparison:** more/most — only in the 'sensibly' sense (*think more practically*); not gradable as 'almost'
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, quantifiers (*practically everyone*), and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *practical*; the *-al-* is usually swallowed (PRAK-tik-lee)
**Contrast:** As 'almost' *practically* is informal — in formal writing use *almost* or *virtually*. It can be ambiguous: *practically impossible* usually means 'almost impossible' but may mean 'impossible in practice', just like Russian *практически*. 'Practical, sensible' about a person is the adjective *practical*, not *practically*.

Practically has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Almost; very nearly.** (практически, почти)

1. The stadium was *practically* empty by the fourth quarter.
2. She *practically* begged me to come.
3. It costs *practically* nothing.

**2. In a sensible, realistic way.** (практично, здраво)

4. We need to think *practically* about the budget.
5. Try to look at the problem *practically*, not emotionally.

**3. In practice; from a practical point of view.** (на практике, с практической точки зрения)

6. *Practically* speaking, we can't finish before Friday.
7. The idea is fine in theory, but *practically* it won't work.

### virtually

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɜːrtʃuəli/ &middot; *VUR-choo-uh-lee*
**Русский:** практически, почти; виртуально
**Type:** degree — an approximator; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, quantifiers and verbs; end in the 'online' sense (*meet virtually*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, quantifiers (*virtually everyone*), and phrases (*virtually overnight*)
**Form:** -ly from *virtual*
**Contrast:** In most texts *virtually* means 'almost', not *виртуально*: *virtually unknown* is 'почти неизвестный'. Only when it says how something is done — *meet virtually*, *held virtually*, *tour virtually* — does it mean 'online'. Russian *фактически* in the sense 'in fact' is *actually* or *in fact*, not *virtually*.

Virtually has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Almost completely; for all practical purposes.** (практически, почти)

1. It's *virtually* impossible to get a table there on a Saturday.
2. *Virtually* all of the tickets have been sold.
3. The two phones are *virtually* identical.

**2. Online or by computer, not in person.** (виртуально, онлайн)

4. The conference will be held *virtually* this year.
5. You can tour the museum *virtually* from home.

### enough

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈnʌf/ &middot; *ih-NUF*
**Русский:** достаточно
**Type:** degree — sets a sufficient level; also stance (*oddly enough*)
**Position:** after its word — after adjectives, adverbs and verbs (*old enough*, *slept enough*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and verbs; a following *to*-infinitive or *for* phrase gives the purpose
**Contrast:** The adverb follows its word: *old enough*, not *enough old* — Russian *достаточно взрослый* puts it first. Before a noun *enough* is a determiner and comes first: *enough money*, *enough time*. With adjective + noun it goes after the adjective: *a big enough room*. The purpose follows with *to* or *for*: *old enough to vote*, *warm enough for a swim*. *I've had enough* (pronoun) and *Enough!* (interjection) are other word classes.

Enough has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To the necessary degree; sufficiently.** (достаточно)

1. Are you old *enough* to drive?
2. She didn't run fast *enough* to catch the bus.
3. I didn't sleep *enough* last night.

**2. Fairly or reasonably, but not especially — often followed by *but*.** (вполне, довольно-таки)

4. He's nice *enough*, but I wouldn't lend him money.
5. She sings well *enough*, but she'll never be a star.

**3. Already quite — stressing that something is bad or hard even without anything more.** (и так, и без того)

6. Being a new parent is tiring *enough* without a two-hour commute.
7. Things are hard *enough* without you making jokes.
8. It's bad *enough* that we lost — now the bus has broken down.

**4. After *sure* and some -ly stance adverbs, showing how the speaker sees a fact.** (как ни странно; и действительно)

9. Strangely *enough*, I'd never been to the ocean before.
10. I said it would rain, and sure *enough*, it poured.

### relatively

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrɛlətɪvli/ &middot; *REL-uh-tiv-lee*
**Русский:** относительно, сравнительно
**Type:** degree — a downtoner that implies a comparison
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives, adverbs and *few*, *little*, *small*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and quantifiers (*relatively few*)
**Form:** -ly from *relative*
**Contrast:** Russian *относительно* also means 'concerning' (*относительно вашего письма*) — that is *regarding* or *about*, never *relatively*. 'In relation to' is *relative to* (*expensive relative to its size*), not *relatively to*. Don't pair it with a comparative: *relatively cheap*, not *relatively cheaper than*.

Fairly, when compared with other things or with what is usual.

1. The apartment is *relatively* cheap for this part of town.
2. *Relatively* few people showed up.
3. *Relatively* speaking, it was a mild winter.

### reasonably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːzənəbli/ &middot; *REE-zuhn-uh-blee*
**Русский:** вполне, достаточно; разумно
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and participles (*reasonably priced*); end in the 'sensibly' sense; mid after a modal (*can reasonably expect*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more reasonably priced*, *behave more reasonably*
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, adverbs, and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *reasonable*
**Contrast:** Before an adjective *reasonably* is a downtoner, not *разумно*: *reasonably good* means 'fairly good', and *reasonably priced* means 'not too expensive'. The sense 'justifiably' comes after a modal: *can reasonably expect*, *could reasonably argue*.

Reasonably has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Fairly; to a satisfactory or acceptable degree.** (вполне, довольно)

1. The food was *reasonably* good, and the service was fast.
2. Everything in the store is *reasonably* priced.
3. I'm *reasonably* sure the office closes at five.

**2. In a sensible or fair way.** (разумно, благоразумно)

4. Let's discuss this *reasonably*, without shouting.
5. She reacted very *reasonably* when I told her about the dent in her car.
6. If you'd acted more *reasonably*, we wouldn't be here.

**3. With good reason; justifiably.** (с полным основанием, обоснованно)

7. You can *reasonably* expect a reply within a week.
8. No one could *reasonably* blame her for leaving.

### comparatively

**Pronunciation:** /kəmˈpɛrətɪvli/ &middot; *kuhm-PAIR-uh-tiv-lee*
**Русский:** сравнительно, относительно
**Type:** degree — a downtoner that implies a comparison; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and adverbs; end in the 'by comparison' sense (*studied comparatively*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and verbs of study
**Form:** -ly from *comparative*
**Contrast:** The stress is on the second syllable: kuhm-PAIR-uh-tiv-lee, not KAHM-pur-. Like *relatively*, it already implies a comparison, so it takes the plain adjective: *comparatively cheap*, not *comparatively cheaper*; the standard follows with *compared with* or *compared to* (*cheap compared with New York*), not *than*.

Comparatively has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Fairly, when compared with others or with what is usual.** (сравнительно, относительно)

1. The job pays *comparatively* well for the area.
2. The disease is *comparatively* rare in children.
3. *Comparatively* speaking, we were lucky.

**2. By means of comparison — in study and research.** (сравнительным методом, в сравнении)

4. The two languages are studied *comparatively* in the second semester.
5. The data were analyzed *comparatively* across five countries.

### mildly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmaɪldli/ &middot; *MYLD-lee*
**Русский:** слегка; мягко
**Type:** degree — a downtoner; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and participles; end in the 'gently' sense (*she said mildly*)
**Comparison:** more/most — *more mildly*
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles, and verbs of speaking and reacting
**Form:** -ly from *mild*
**Contrast:** *To put it mildly* is fixed — not *to say it mildly* — and signals understatement: *upset, to put it mildly* means 'very upset'. Russian *мягко* about touch or sound is *softly* or *gently* (*close the door gently*, *speak softly*); *mildly* is about tone and degree.

Mildly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Slightly; to a small degree.** (слегка, немного)

1. I was *mildly* surprised that she agreed.
2. The movie was *mildly* entertaining at best.
3. The salsa is only *mildly* spicy.

**2. In a gentle, calm way; not harshly.** (мягко, спокойно)

4. “That's not quite right,” she said *mildly*.
5. He complained *mildly* about the noise and went back to bed.

**3. In *to put it mildly*: saying that the reality is stronger than your words.** (мягко говоря)

6. The meeting didn't go well, to put it *mildly*.
7. Her boss was upset, to put it *mildly*.

### marginally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɑːrdʒənəli/ &middot; *MAHR-juh-nuh-lee*
**Русский:** незначительно, чуть; едва
**Type:** degree — a downtoner
**Position:** before its word — before adjectives and comparatives; end with verbs (*rose marginally*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** comparatives, adjectives, and verbs of change
**Form:** -ly from *marginal* (from *margin*)
**Contrast:** Russian *маргинальный* suggests people on the edge of society; English *marginally* just means 'slightly' or 'only just' — *marginally better* is 'чуть лучше'. It is formal; in speech Americans say *a little* or *slightly*.

Marginally has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very slightly; by a small amount.** (незначительно, чуть, немного)

1. Sales were *marginally* higher than last year.
2. Interest rates rose *marginally* in June.
3. This model is *marginally* cheaper, but the battery is worse.

**2. Only just; to a limited, borderline degree.** (едва, на грани)

4. The business is only *marginally* profitable.
5. His comments were *marginally* relevant at best.
