Flat adverbs: the same form as the adjective#

Adverbs with no -ly — fast, hard, late, straight — that look like adjectives and are not mistakes.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

A flat adverb has the same form as its adjective. These are the older English adverbs that survived the spread of -ly, and they are fully standard: drive fast, work hard, arrive late. The trap is the reverse assumption — that adding -ly always gives the same meaning. For many of these words it gives a different word entirely, and that partner is in group 3. American speech also uses many informal flat adverbs (drive slow, come quick, hold tight) that formal writing avoids; each entry says which kind it is.

fast#

Pronunciation
/fæst/ · FAST
Русский
быстро; крепко
Type
manner; also degree (fast asleep)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
-er/-est — faster, fastest
Modifies
verbs; asleep in fast asleep
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective fast; there is no fastly
Contrast
There is no fastly: He drives fast, never He drives fastly. The old sense 'firmly' survives in set phrases and has nothing to do with speed: hold fast means hold on tight, stuck fast means it will not move, and fast asleep means deeply asleep, not that someone fell asleep quickly.

Fast has 4 distinct senses.

1. Quickly; at high speed. (быстро)

  1. Don't talk so fast — I can't follow you.
  2. She ran faster than anyone else on the team.
  3. Prices are rising fast this year.

2. Firmly, so that it cannot move or come loose. (крепко, прочно, намертво)

  1. The truck was stuck fast in the mud.
  2. Hold fast to the railing when the boat turns.
  3. The senator stood fast and refused to change her vote.

3. Deeply, in fast asleep. (крепко (спать))

  1. By nine o'clock the kids were fast asleep.
  2. He was so fast asleep that he didn't hear the alarm.

4. Ahead of the correct time, of a clock or watch. (вперёд (часы спешат))

  1. The clock in the kitchen runs five minutes fast.
  2. My watch has been running fast ever since I dropped it.

hard#

Pronunciation
/hɑːrd/ · HAHRD
Русский
усердно; сильно
Type
manner; also degree
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
-er/-est — harder, hardest
Modifies
verbs
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective hard; hardly is a different word
Contrast
Hardly is not the adverb of hard in meaning: it means 'almost not' (group 3). She works hard = она много работает; She hardly works = она почти не работает. Russian тяжело about bad news is also hard: He took it hard.

Hard has 5 distinct senses.

1. With a lot of effort, energy or attention. (усердно, упорно; как следует)

  1. She worked hard to pay off her student loans.
  2. Think hard before you sign that contract.
  3. If you try harder, you'll make the team next year.

2. With force; strongly or heavily. (сильно)

  1. It was raining hard when we left.
  2. He hit the ball hard toward left field.
  3. Press hard on the cut to stop the bleeding.

3. With pain or distress, about news or a loss. (тяжело (переживать))

  1. She took her father's death very hard.
  2. Don't take it so hard — everyone fails a test sometimes.

4. As far as possible in one direction; sharply. (круто, до упора)

  1. Turn the wheel hard to the right.
  2. The pilot banked hard left to avoid the storm.

5. Closely following; right after, in hard on the heels of. (сразу же, вслед за)

  1. The second storm came hard on the heels of the first.
  2. The announcement came hard on the heels of a bad quarter.

late#

Pronunciation
/leɪt/ · LAYT
Русский
поздно, с опозданием
Type
time
Position
end — after the verb or its object; before in, at, next in late at night
Comparison
-er/-est — later, latest
Modifies
verbs; time phrases (late in the day)
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective late
Contrast
Lately is a different word: it means 'recently, в последнее время' (group 3). I got home late = я поздно пришёл домой; I haven't slept well lately = в последнее время я плохо сплю. Later meaning 'afterward' (See you later) is a time adverb of its own (group 6).

Late has 3 distinct senses.

1. After the expected, usual or proper time. (поздно, с опозданием)

  1. The train got in forty minutes late.
  2. Sorry, I'm running late — start without me.
  3. She stayed later than everyone else to finish the report.

2. Near the end of a period of time. (в конце; поздно)

  1. The accident happened late at night.
  2. He started painting late in life.
  3. The package should arrive late next week.

3. Recently, in as late as and of late. (ещё (совсем недавно))

  1. As late as 2010, the town had no traffic lights.
  2. The house was lived in until as late as the 1980s.
  3. The senator has been unusually quiet of late.

early#

Pronunciation
/ˈɜːrli/ · UR-lee
Русский
рано, заранее
Type
time
Position
end — after the verb or its object; before in, on in early in the morning
Comparison
-er/-est — earlier, earliest
Modifies
verbs; time phrases (early in the year)
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective early; the -ly is part of the word, so there is no earlily
Contrast
Early is about the clock or calendar — before the usual time (рано, раньше срока); soon is about how long from now (скоро). Come early = приходите пораньше; Come soon = приходите поскорее. Russian раньше meaning 'in the past' is before or used to, not earlier: I used to smoke. Earlier meaning 'ранее, сегодня уже' (I called you earlier) has its own entry (group 6).

Early has 2 distinct senses.

1. Before the usual, expected or planned time. (рано, раньше срока, заранее)

  1. We got to the airport two hours early.
  2. The baby was born three weeks early.
  3. Can you come in earlier tomorrow?

2. Near the beginning of a period of time. (рано; в начале)

  1. I get up early in the morning.
  2. Early in her career, she worked as a waitress.
  3. We knew early on that the plan wouldn't work.

straight#

Pronunciation
/streɪt/ · STRAYT
Русский
прямо; сразу; подряд
Type
manner; also direction, time (straight home) and duration (three days straight)
Position
end — after the verb; before home, ahead, to; after a time phrase in three days straight
Comparison
-er/-est — straighter, straightest; only in the line and posture sense
Modifies
verbs; place words (straight ahead); time phrases (five days straight)
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective straight; straightly is almost never used
Contrast
Russian прямо as an intensifier is right, not straight: прямо сейчас = right now, прямо здесь = right here. Straight means in a line, without stopping, or honestly: go straight home. Straight away (сразу) is heard in the US, but right away is far more common.

Straight has 6 distinct senses.

1. In a straight line or upright position; not curving or bending. (прямо, ровно)

  1. Go straight for two blocks and turn left at the light.
  2. Sit up straight and look at the camera.
  3. Can you stand a little straighter?

2. Directly, without stopping or delay. (сразу, прямиком)

  1. I went straight home after work.
  2. He walked straight into a glass door.
  3. The kids went straight to bed.

3. Honestly; without hiding anything, or, in go straight, without breaking the law. (прямо, откровенно, начистоту; завязать (с криминалом))

  1. Just tell me straight — am I getting fired?
  2. I'll give it to you straight: the car isn't worth fixing.
  3. After prison he went straight and opened a bakery.

4. Without a break; in a row, after a number and a time word. (подряд)

  1. It rained for five days straight.
  2. The Yankees have won six games straight.

5. Clearly, in think straight and see straight. (ясно, нормально (соображать))

  1. I'm so tired I can't think straight.
  2. After three drinks he couldn't see straight.

6. Without water, ice or anything mixed in, of a drink. (неразбавленным, в чистом виде)

  1. He drinks his whiskey straight.
  2. Do you want your bourbon straight or on the rocks?

long#

Pronunciation
/lɔːŋ/ · LAWNG
Русский
долго; давно
Type
duration; also time (long ago)
Position
end — Did you wait long?; mid in have long suspected; before ago, before, after
Comparison
-er/-est — longer, longest
Modifies
verbs; ago, before, after, since
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective long
Contrast
In affirmative statements American English says a long time, not bare long: I waited a long time, not I waited long. Bare long is normal in questions and negatives (Did you wait long?, It won't take long) and after too, so, as (I stayed too long). Long is about time; for distance use far or a long way — sports talk is the exception (throw long, the putt went long). As long as (если только; пока) is a conjunction.

Long has 4 distinct senses.

1. For a long time. (долго)

  1. Have you been waiting long?
  2. It won't take long to fix.
  3. Can you stay a little longer?

2. A long time before or after a point, or for a long time up to now. (давно; задолго)

  1. The house was built long ago.
  2. We were friends long before we started dating.
  3. Scientists have long suspected a link between the two.

3. Throughout a whole period, in all day long, all year long. (весь (день) напролёт)

  1. The baby cried all night long.
  2. The pool is open all summer long.

4. Any more, in no longer and not any longer. (больше не, уже не)

  1. She no longer works here.
  2. I can't wait any longer.

high#

Pronunciation
/haɪ/ · HY

Position

Comparison

Contrast
Highly does not mean 'at a great height': it means 'very' or 'very well' (highly recommended, think highly of — group 3). The kite flew high, never flew highly; a highly successful company, never a high successful company.

High has 3 distinct senses.

1. At or to a great height. (высоко)

  1. The eagle circled high above the canyon.
  2. Throw the ball higher so I can catch it.
  3. Our office is high up on the fortieth floor.

2. At a high level, rank, amount or pitch. (высоко; на высоком уровне)

  1. Aim high and don't settle for less.
  2. The company ranks high on lists of the best places to work.
  3. She can sing much higher than her sister.

3. Everywhere, in high and low. (повсюду, везде)

  1. We searched high and low for the keys.
  2. They looked high and low but never found the dog.

low#

Pronunciation
/loʊ/ · LOH

низко
Type
place; also degree (buy low)
Position
end — after the verb or its object
Comparison
-er/-est — lower, lowest
Modifies
verbs

Contrast
Lowly is an adjective meaning 'humble, of low rank' (a lowly clerk), not the adverb of low. Lower is also a verb: Lower your voice = говорите тише.

Low has 4 distinct senses.

1. At or to a low position; near the ground. (низко)

  1. The plane flew low over the fields.
  2. Bend lower and keep your back straight.
  3. The sun hung low in the sky.

2. At a low level, amount, price or setting. (низко; дёшево; на малом огне)

  1. Buy low and sell high.
  2. Turn the heat down low and let it simmer.
  3. Cook the ribs low and slow for about six hours.

3. Quietly, or at a deep pitch. (тихо; низко (о голосе))

  1. They talked low so the kids wouldn't hear.
  2. He can sing lower than anyone in the choir.

4. Out of sight, so as not to attract attention, in lie low. (затаиться, залечь на дно)

  1. The suspect is probably lying low until things calm down.
  2. Stay home and lie low for a couple of days.

deep#

Pronunciation
/diːp/ · DEEP
Русский
глубоко
Type
place; also time (deep into the night) and degree
Position
end — after the verb; before into, under phrases
Comparison
-er/-est — deeper, deepest
Modifies
verbs
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective deep
Contrast
For feelings and degree use deeply: deeply sorry, deeply moved, I deeply regret it — never deep sorry (group 3). Flat deep is for physical depth or distance and a few set phrases: dig deep, deep into the night, run deep. With breathe both work: breathe deep, breathe deeply.

Deep has 3 distinct senses.

1. Far down, far in, or far from the front or surface. (глубоко; далеко)

  1. The roots go deep into the soil.
  2. They hiked deeper into the woods.
  3. The quarterback threw deep to the end zone.

2. Far into a period of time. (до поздней (ночи), до самого (конца))

  1. We talked deep into the night.
  2. The game went deep into overtime.

3. At a deep level of feeling or effort, in dig deep and run deep. (изо всех сил; глубоко (укоренившись))

  1. The team had to dig deep to win in the final minutes.
  2. Old grudges run deep in this town.

near#

Pronunciation
/nɪr/ · NEER
Русский
близко, рядом; почти
Type
place; also time and degree (near perfect)
Position
end — after the verb; before its word in near perfect
Comparison
-er/-est — nearer, nearest
Modifies
verbs; adjectives (near impossible)
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective near
Contrast
Near is usually a preposition (near the station); it is an adverb only when no noun follows (come near). For location, everyday American speech prefers nearby: They live nearby. For 'almost', formal writing uses nearly (group 3): nearly impossible, not near impossible. Nowhere near (далеко не) is informal but standard.

Near has 3 distinct senses.

1. At or to a short distance in space or time. (близко, рядом)

  1. Don't come any nearer — the dog bites.
  2. As the wedding drew near, she got more nervous.
  3. The deer came near enough to eat from my hand.

2. Almost; very nearly, informal, before an adjective. (почти)

  1. It was near impossible to find parking.
  2. The team played a near perfect game.

3. Not at all, in nowhere near and not anywhere near. (далеко не, и близко не)

  1. The house is nowhere near finished.
  2. We don't have anywhere near enough chairs.

far#

Pronunciation
/fɑːr/ · FAHR
Русский
далеко; намного
Type
place; also degree (far better) and time (so far)
Position
end — after the verb; before its word in far better, far too
Comparison
irregular — farther, farthest; also further, furthest
Modifies
verbs; comparatives and too (far better, far too)
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective far; compares irregularly
Contrast
In affirmative statements bare far sounds formal; Americans say a long way: We walked a long way. Far is normal in questions and negatives (Is it far?, It's not far) and with too, so, away, from. Before a comparative use far or much, never very: far better, not very better. Far from + adjective means 'not at all': far from perfect = далеко не идеальный. For distance American English prefers farther; further also means 'more, in addition' (group 2).

Far has 5 distinct senses.

1. At or to a great distance. (далеко)

  1. How far is the airport from here?
  2. They don't live far from us.
  3. We drove farther than we'd planned.

2. Much; by a large amount, before a comparative or too, and in by far. (намного, гораздо; слишком)

  1. This version is far better than the last one.
  2. The rent is far too high.
  3. She's by far the best player on the team.

3. To a particular point, degree or stage. (насколько; далеко (зайти))

  1. How far did you get with the reading?
  2. This time he's gone too far.
  3. As far as I know, the store is still open.

4. Up to now, in so far and thus far. (пока, до сих пор)

  1. So far, nobody has complained.
  2. Thus far the plan is working.

5. A long way in time, before into, back and ahead. (далеко (во времени); до поздней (ночи); ещё (в))

  1. The party went on far into the night.
  2. Her family's roots go as far back as the 1700s.
  3. It's too early to plan that far ahead.

Flat wrong goes only after the verb or object: You spelled it wrong. Before a verb or participle use wrongly: He was wrongly accused. Wrongly also carries the idea of 'unfairly, by mistake' (group 3). Don't get me wrong = не поймите меня неправильно.

Wrong has 2 distinct senses.

  3. Don't get me wrong — I like her a lot.

2. Badly, so that a problem happens, in go wrong. (не так (пойти); сломаться)

  1. Something went wrong with the engine.
  2. What could possibly go wrong?
  3. Where did we go wrong with this project?

Pronunciation
/raɪt/ · RYT
Русский
прямо, как раз; сразу; правильно
Type
focusing; also time (right away), manner (do it right), degree (right to the end) and direction (turn right)
Position

Comparison

Russian прямо before a place or time word is right, not straight: right now, right here, right in front of you. Right now means 'at this moment'; just now means 'a moment ago'. Rightly (group 3) means 'justifiably': She was rightly proud; You guessed right uses the flat form. Right? at the end of a sentence is a question tag, and Right. alone is an interjection.

Right has 5 distinct senses.

1. Exactly; directly, before a place or time word. (прямо, как раз, точно)

  1. The keys were right here on the table.
  2. She was sitting right in front of me.

2. Immediately; without delay, in right away, right now, right back. (сразу, сейчас же)

  1. I need those files right away.
  2. Hold on, I'll be right back.
  3. Stop that right now!

3. Correctly; as it should be. (правильно, как надо)

  1. Did I pronounce that right?
  2. Nothing is going right today.
  3. If I remember right, the party starts at eight.

4. All the way; completely. (до конца, насквозь, совсем)

  1. The nail went right through the board.
  2. Read the contract right to the end.
  3. He turned right around and walked out.

5. To or toward the right side. (направо)

  1. Turn right at the second light.
  2. Look left and right before you cross.

direct#

Pronunciation
/dəˈrɛkt/ · duh-REKT
Русский
прямо, напрямую
Type
manner; also direction
Position
end — after the verb (fly direct, buy direct)
Comparison

Contrast
Flat direct is used mainly after verbs of travel and dealing: fly direct, buy direct, call direct. Directly covers everything else and is the only choice before an adjective or verb: directly affected, directly opposite. Directly can also mean 'very soon' (I'll be there directly, older and Southern US). Both /dəˈrɛkt/ and /daɪˈrɛkt/ are standard American pronunciations.

Direct has 2 distinct senses.

1. Without stopping or changing along the way. (прямым рейсом, без пересадок)

  1. You can fly direct from Chicago to Tokyo.
  2. This bus goes direct to the airport.

2. Without anyone or anything in between. (напрямую, без посредников)

  1. We buy our coffee direct from the farmers.
  2. If there's a problem, contact me direct.
  3. The payment goes direct into your account.

short#

Pronunciation
/ʃɔːrt/ · SHORT
Русский
не дотянуть; резко
Type
manner; also degree (fall short)
Position
end — after the verb or its object (stop short, cut it short)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies

Form

Shortly means 'soon' (скоро): I'll be there shortly (group 3) — it has nothing to do with length. Short of works like a preposition meaning 'except, without going as far as': Short of a miracle, we'll lose. In short (короче говоря) is a linking phrase.

Short has 4 distinct senses.

  1. The ball landed short of the green.
  2. Sales fell short of expectations.
  3. Our team came up short in the final round.

2. Suddenly and abruptly, in stop short and pull up short. (резко, внезапно)

  1. She stopped short when she saw the snake.
  2. The driver pulled up short at the crosswalk.

3. Earlier than planned, in cut short. (досрочно (прервать))

  1. We had to cut our vacation short.
