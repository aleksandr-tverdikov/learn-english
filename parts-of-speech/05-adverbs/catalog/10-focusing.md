# Focusing adverbs

> Only this, also that — *only*, *also*, *even*, *merely*, *especially*, *mainly*, *either*, *as well*.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A focusing adverb points at one part of the sentence and either restricts attention to it (*only*, *merely*, *especially*) or adds it to something already said (*also*, *too*, *even*). Moving one changes the meaning — *Only Ana saw it* and *Ana only saw it* say different things — so every entry here gives its position rule with a pair of examples. *Either* replaces *too* in negative sentences: *I don’t like it either*.

---
### only

**Pronunciation:** /ˈoʊnli/ &middot; *OHN-lee*
**Русский:** только, лишь, всего лишь
**Type:** focusing; also time and degree
**Position:** before its word — directly before the word or phrase it focuses; in speech usually mid, before the main verb, with stress on the focused word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** nouns, pronouns, numbers, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, phrases and clauses
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: *Only* Ana saw the email (no one else saw it); Ana *only* saw the email (she saw it but did not read or answer it); Ana saw *only* the email (nothing else). Fronted *only* + a time phrase or clause brings inversion: *Only then did I understand*, not *Only then I understood*. *Not only … but also* inverts too when it opens the sentence: *Not only did she win, but she also set a record*. The adjective (*the only child* — единственный) and the informal conjunction (*I'd go, only I'm busy* — но) are other words. Russian *только что* is *just*, not *only*: *He has just left*. *If only* takes a past form for a wish about now and the past perfect for regret about the past: *If only I knew* (если бы я знал), *If only I had known* (если бы я тогда знал).

Only has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Nothing or no one else; exclusively.** (только (и никто, ничто больше))

1. *Only* Ana knew the password.
2. This parking lot is for employees *only*.
3. You can *only* pay by card here.

**2. No more than; just — used to make an amount, a person or a problem sound small or unimportant.** (всего, всего лишь)

4. The ride *only* takes ten minutes.
5. Don't worry, it's *only* a scratch.
6. She was *only* sixteen when she moved to New York.

**3. As recently as; not until — with a time word, showing that something happened very recently or later than expected.** (только, лишь (не раньше чем))

7. I *only* found out about it yesterday.
8. We met *only* last month, but it feels like years.
9. I *only* realized my mistake when I got home.

**4. With the result, often disappointing, that; in the end merely — in *only to* and before verbs like *make* and *cause*.** (лишь, только; а в итоге)

10. He ran to the gate, *only* to find that the flight had closed.
11. Yelling at him will *only* make things worse.
12. Cutting prices would *only* hurt the brand.

**5. Very, completely — in *only too*, often with *happy*, *glad*, *well* or *true*.** (только (рад), очень; слишком (хорошо))

13. I'd be *only* too happy to help.
14. She knew *only* too well what could go wrong.
15. The rumors turned out to be *only* too true.

**6. Used in *if only* to express a strong wish or regret about something that is not true.** (если бы (только))

16. *If only* I'd listened to you!
17. *If only* we had more time.
18. *If only* he would call back.

### merely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɪrli/ &middot; *MEER-lee*
**Русский:** всего лишь, просто, лишь
**Type:** focusing — restricts and plays down
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; or before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, nouns and noun phrases, adjectives, prepositional phrases
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *mere*
**Contrast:** *Merely* means 'no more than', never 'no one else': *Only Ana came* (nobody else came), not *Merely Ana came*. It is more formal than *just* and *only*. In *not merely … but (also)* it works like *not only … but also*: *He was not merely late but rude*.

Only; no more than — used to say that something is small, simple or less important than it might seem.

1. I was *merely* asking a question.
2. The delay is *merely* a few days.
3. This is not *merely* a technical problem; it's a legal one.

### simply

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪmpli/ &middot; *SIM-plee*
**Русский:** просто
**Type:** focusing; also degree and manner
**Position:** mid — before the main verb or before its word in the focusing and degree senses; end in the manner sense
**Comparison:** not gradable — except in the manner sense (*more simply*, *most simply*)
**Modifies:** verbs, adjectives, nouns and noun phrases, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *simple*: the -le becomes -ly
**Contrast:** Position changes the meaning: She explained it *simply* (in an easy, clear way) vs. She *simply* explained it (she just explained it and did nothing more). Russian *просто* as a sentence opener (*Просто я устал*) is not *Simply, I'm tired* but *It's just that I'm tired* or *I'm just tired*.

Simply has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Just; only; nothing more than.** (просто, всего лишь)

1. It's *simply* a matter of time.
2. To register, *simply* fill out this form.
3. He didn't mean any harm — he was *simply* trying to help.

**2. Absolutely; really — used to give strong emphasis, often with a negative.** (просто, совершенно)

4. The view from the top was *simply* amazing.
5. I *simply* can't believe she said that.
6. We *simply* don't have the money.

**3. In a simple, plain or easy-to-understand way.** (просто, скромно, понятно)

7. The teacher explained the rule *simply* and clearly.
8. They live *simply*, without a car or a TV.
9. Put *more simply*, we're out of cash.

### purely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpjʊrli/ &middot; *PYUUR-lee*
**Русский:** чисто, исключительно, сугубо
**Type:** focusing — restricts to one kind, cause or purpose
**Position:** before its word — usually before an adjective or a prepositional phrase
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, prepositional phrases, nouns
**Form:** -ly from *pure*: keeps the *e*
**Contrast:** Russian *чисто* has two sides: 'purely, only' is *purely* (*purely by chance*), but 'cleanly, without mistakes' is not — *She speaks English perfectly* or *without an accent*, not *She speaks English purely*. The fixed pair *purely and simply* means 'only and for no other reason'.

Only; entirely — for this reason, or of this kind, and no other.

1. We met *purely* by chance.
2. I do it *purely* for fun.
3. Their relationship is *purely* professional.

### solely

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsoʊlli/ &middot; *SOHL-lee*
**Русский:** исключительно, единственно, только
**Type:** focusing — restricts to one person, cause or purpose
**Position:** before its word — or mid, before the main verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, prepositional phrases, verbs, nouns
**Form:** -ly from *sole* 'single, only': keeps the *e* — *solely*, not *soly*
**Contrast:** *Solely* means 'with no other person or cause involved', not 'without company': *She lives alone* (одна), not *She lives solely*. It is formal; in conversation Americans say *only* or *just*. *Solely responsible* is a common fixed phrase — несёт полную (единоличную) ответственность.

Only; not involving anyone or anything else.

1. The driver was *solely* responsible for the accident.
2. The money will be used *solely* for research.
3. Don't judge a school *solely* on its test scores.

### exclusively

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈskluːsɪvli/ &middot; *ik-SKLOO-siv-lee*
**Русский:** исключительно; эксклюзивно
**Type:** focusing — restricts to one person, group or place
**Position:** before its word — or end, after the verb and its object
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, prepositional phrases, adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *exclusive*
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *исключительно* also means 'extremely' (*исключительно красивый*), but English *exclusively* never does — say *exceptionally beautiful*, not *exclusively beautiful*. *Exclusively* only ever means 'and nothing or no one else'.

Exclusively has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Only; with nothing or no one else included.** (исключительно, только)

1. The course is taught *exclusively* in Spanish.
2. For three months he ate *exclusively* plant-based food.
3. The group focuses *exclusively* on small businesses.

**2. Given, sold or shown to one person, company or outlet and no other.** (эксклюзивно)

4. The singer spoke *exclusively* to NBC about her divorce.
5. These sneakers are sold *exclusively* on our website.
6. The series streams *exclusively* on one platform.

### particularly

**Pronunciation:** /pərˈtɪkjələrli/ &middot; *pur-TIK-yuh-lur-lee*
**Русский:** особенно
**Type:** focusing; also degree
**Position:** before its word — before the noun, phrase or adjective it points at; or mid, before the main verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** nouns and noun phrases, adjectives, adverbs, verbs, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *particular*
**Contrast:** *Particularly* goes before the word it focuses; *in particular* usually goes after it: *particularly the South* = *the South in particular*. *Not particularly* is a softener — 'not very', не особо: *I'm not particularly hungry*. As with *especially*, do not open a sentence with it to mean 'most of all': *I particularly like jazz*, not *Particularly I like jazz*.

Particularly has 3 distinct senses.

**1. More than others; especially — pointing at one member of a group.** (особенно, в частности)

1. The storm hit coastal towns, *particularly* in the South.
2. I love old movies, *particularly* westerns.
3. Traffic is bad, *particularly* on Friday afternoons.

**2. Very; to an unusual degree — and with *not*, 'not very'.** (особенно, очень; не особо)

4. It was a *particularly* cold winter.
5. The food wasn't *particularly* good.
6. She seemed *particularly* happy that morning.

**3. Specifically; for a clear, deliberate reason.** (особо, специально)

7. He *particularly* asked to see you.
8. I *particularly* wanted you to hear this.

### especially

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈspɛʃəli/ &middot; *ih-SPESH-uh-lee*
**Русский:** особенно
**Type:** focusing; also degree
**Position:** before its word — before the noun, phrase, clause or adjective it points at; or mid, before the main verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** nouns and noun phrases, prepositional phrases, clauses, adjectives, verbs
**Form:** -ly from the rare adjective *especial*, related to *special*
**Contrast:** Do not open a sentence with *especially* the way Russian opens with *особенно*: *I especially like apples* or *I like fruit, especially apples*, not *Especially I like apples*. *Especially* and *specially* share the 'for a purpose' sense (*made especially for you*), but only *especially* means 'above all': *I love fruit, especially apples*, not *specially apples*.

Especially has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Above all; more than others.** (особенно)

1. Kids, *especially* toddlers, need a lot of sleep.
2. I *especially* liked the ending.
3. Drive carefully, *especially* when it's raining.

**2. Very; to a great degree — and with *not*, 'not very'.** (особенно, очень; не особо)

4. This has been an *especially* hard year.
5. I'm not *especially* interested in politics.
6. The soup was *especially* good today.

**3. For one particular purpose or person; specially.** (специально)

7. I baked this cake *especially* for you.
8. The app was designed *especially* for older users.

### mainly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪnli/ &middot; *MAYN-lee*
**Русский:** главным образом, в основном
**Type:** focusing — picks out the largest part or the chief reason
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; or before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, nouns and noun phrases, prepositional phrases, clauses
**Form:** -ly from *main*
**Contrast:** *Mainly* does not usually go at the end: *They mainly eat fish* or *They eat mainly fish*, not *They eat fish mainly*. *Mainly* and *mostly* are close, but only *mostly* means 'most of the time': *I mostly work from home* is more natural than *mainly* for habits. Russian *в основном* as 'on the whole' (*В основном всё хорошо*) is *Overall, everything is fine* or *Things are mostly fine*.

More than anything else; for the most part.

1. The audience was *mainly* college students.
2. We moved here *mainly* because of the schools.
3. Their customers are *mainly* in the Midwest.

### chiefly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃiːfli/ &middot; *CHEEF-lee*
**Русский:** главным образом, преимущественно
**Type:** focusing — picks out the largest part or the chief reason
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; or before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, nouns and noun phrases, prepositional phrases, clauses
**Form:** -ly from the adjective *chief*
**Contrast:** *Chiefly* means 'mainly', not 'as a chief' or 'like a boss'. It is formal and written; in conversation Americans say *mainly* or *mostly*.

Mainly; more than anything else.

1. The disease *chiefly* affects older adults.
2. The town is known *chiefly* for its wine.
3. His novels were written *chiefly* for young readers.

### primarily

**Pronunciation:** /praɪˈmɛrəli/ &middot; *pry-MAIR-uh-lee*
**Русский:** главным образом, в первую очередь
**Type:** focusing — picks out the main part, purpose or reason
**Position:** before its word — or mid, before the main verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** prepositional phrases, clauses, verbs, adjectives, nouns
**Form:** -ly from *primary*
**Contrast:** *Primarily* is 'mainly', not 'first in time' as Russian *первично* or *изначально* suggests: *The house was originally a school*, not *primarily a school* (which means 'mainly a school'). US/UK split: American stress is usually on the second syllable, *pry-MAIR-uh-lee*; British is *PRY-muh-ruh-lee*.

Mainly; more than anything else.

1. The app is designed *primarily* for teachers.
2. We are *primarily* a family business.
3. The problem is *primarily* a financial one.

### also

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːlsoʊ/ &middot; *AWL-soh*
**Русский:** также, тоже; кроме того
**Type:** focusing — additive; also linking
**Position:** mid — before the main verb, after *be* and the first auxiliary; front as a linker
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs, whole clauses, nouns and phrases
**Contrast:** *Too* and *also* at the end of a negative become *either*: *I don't like it either* ('тоже не'), not *I don't like it too* or *I don't like it also*. Mid *also* before a negative is fine when it adds a new point: *The room was small, and it also didn't have a window*. Russian speakers put *also* at the end (*I like it also*); American English puts it mid (*I also like it*) and uses *too* at the end. In short replies say *Me too*, not *Me also*. *Not only … but also* splits around the two things joined: *She not only sings but also dances*.

Also has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In addition; too.** (также, тоже)

1. She speaks Spanish, and she *also* speaks French.
2. The hotel is *also* close to the beach.
3. He's not only smart but *also* very kind.

**2. Besides; and another thing — at the start of a sentence, adding a new point.** (кроме того, и ещё)

4. The rent is too high. *Also*, it's far from work.
5. *Also*, don't forget to lock the back door.

### either

**Pronunciation:** /ˈiːðər/ &middot; *EE-thur*
**Русский:** тоже (не), также (не)
**Type:** focusing — additive, in negative clauses
**Position:** end — at the end of the negative clause
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole negative clause
**Contrast:** *Either* is the negative partner of *too*: *I like it too* but *I don't like it either*, not *I don't like it too*. In replies, *Me neither* is standard; *Me either* is very common in American speech but still marked as informal. The determiner and pronoun (*either side*, *either of them* — любой из двух) and the conjunction *either … or* (или … или) are different words. The pronunciation *EYE-thur* is also heard in the US.

Either has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Also; too — used after a negative statement.** (тоже (не))

1. I don't eat meat, and my husband doesn't *either*.
2. If you're not going, I won't go *either*.
3. "I've never been to Texas." "I haven't *either*."

**2. Moreover; for that matter — adding a negative point that makes what was said stronger.** (к тому же (и не), и притом (не))

4. There's a great taco place nearby, and it's not expensive *either*.
5. It's a great little car, and it doesn't use much gas *either*.

### neither

**Pronunciation:** /ˈniːðər/ &middot; *NEE-thur*
**Русский:** тоже не, также не
**Type:** focusing — additive, in negative clauses; also negative
**Position:** front — opens the clause, and the auxiliary comes before the subject; alone after a pronoun in *Me neither*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause, answering or adding to a negative one
**Contrast:** *Neither* brings inversion: *Neither do I*, *Neither can she*, not *Neither I do*. It already carries the negative, so no *not*: *Neither did he*, not *Neither didn't he*. *Neither do I* = *I don't either*; after a positive statement use *So do I*. The determiner and pronoun (*neither answer*, *neither of them* — ни один из двух) and the conjunction *neither … nor* (ни … ни) are different words. The pronunciation *NY-thur* is also heard in the US.

Also not — used after a negative statement to say the same is true of another person or thing.

1. "I can't swim." "*Neither* can I."
2. She didn't call, and *neither* did her brother.
3. "I don't like horror movies." "Me *neither*."

### notably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnoʊtəbli/ &middot; *NOH-tuh-blee*
**Русский:** в частности, особенно; примечательно
**Type:** focusing; also degree and stance
**Position:** before its word — before the example it introduces or the adjective it strengthens; front as a comment on the whole sentence
**Comparison:** more/most — mostly in *most notably*, 'above all'
**Modifies:** nouns and noun phrases, adjectives, whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *notable*
**Contrast:** *Notably* is 'remarkably, in a way worth noting', not 'visibly': something you can see is *noticeably* — *She's noticeably thinner*, not *notably thinner*. Introducing an example, it is close to *especially* and *in particular*, and the most common form is *most notably*.

Notably has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Especially; in particular — introducing an important example.** (в частности, особенно)

1. Several cities, *notably* Denver and Austin, have grown fast.
2. The band had many hits, *most notably* "Hotel California."
3. Some groups, *notably* farmers, opposed the plan.

**2. In a way that is remarkable or easy to notice.** (на редкость, примечательно)

4. It was a *notably* successful year for the company.
5. The senator was *notably* absent from the meeting.

**3. It is worth pointing out that — commenting on the whole sentence.** (примечательно, что)

6. *Notably*, no one on the board objected.
7. *Notably*, the report says nothing about costs.

### specifically

**Pronunciation:** /spəˈsɪfɪkli/ &middot; *spuh-SIF-ik-lee*
**Русский:** конкретно, именно, специально
**Type:** focusing; also manner and linking
**Position:** before its word — or mid, before the main verb; front as *more specifically* or *specifically*, 'to be exact'
**Comparison:** more/most — mainly in *more specifically*, 'to be more exact'
**Modifies:** verbs, prepositional phrases, nouns and noun phrases, whole sentences
**Form:** -ly from *specific*: -ic adds -ally, pronounced /ɪkli/
**Contrast:** False friend: Russian *специфически* / *специфично* means 'peculiarly, in a distinctive way', which English *specifically* never means — *It tastes unusual* or *It has a distinctive taste*, not *It tastes specifically*. *Specifically* is about exactness and a clear purpose.

Specifically has 3 distinct senses.

**1. For one particular purpose, person or group and no other.** (специально)

1. The program was created *specifically* for veterans.
2. These shoes are made *specifically* for running.
3. The law *specifically* targets online lenders.

**2. Clearly and directly; in a way that leaves no doubt.** (прямо, чётко, ясно)

4. I *specifically* told you not to touch my laptop.
5. The contract *specifically* says no pets.

**3. To be exact; namely — narrowing down what was just said.** (а именно, конкретно, точнее)

6. We need help with marketing — *specifically*, social media.
7. The problem is the software, or, *more specifically*, the billing module.
8. What *specifically* don't you like about it?

### exactly

**Pronunciation:** /ɪɡˈzæktli/ &middot; *ig-ZAKT-lee*
**Русский:** точно, ровно, именно
**Type:** focusing; also degree and manner
**Position:** before its word — before the word, number or phrase it makes precise; end in the manner sense; alone as a reply
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** nouns and noun phrases, numbers and times, wh-words and clauses, adjectives, verbs
**Form:** -ly from *exact*
**Contrast:** *Exactly!* agrees with what someone just said (вот именно). Russian *Точно!* also means 'Oh right, I forgot' — that is *Oh, right!* or *That's right!*, not *Exactly!*. Russian *точно* as 'definitely' (*Он точно придёт*) is *He'll definitely come*, not *He'll exactly come*. *Not exactly* is often a polite or ironic 'not at all'.

Exactly has 6 distinct senses.

**1. Just; in every way — stressing that something is the very thing meant.** (именно, точно, в точности)

1. That's *exactly* what I was going to say.
2. The twins look *exactly* alike.
3. The house is *exactly* the way we left it.

**2. No more and no less — with numbers, amounts and times.** (ровно, точно)

4. The bill came to *exactly* $50.
5. The meeting starts at *exactly* nine.
6. It's been *exactly* a year since we met.

**3. Accurately and carefully, with no mistakes.** (точно, в точности)

7. Follow the instructions *exactly*.
8. Copy the code *exactly* as it appears on the screen.

**4. In questions, asking for the precise detail, often with some impatience.** (конкретно, всё-таки, именно)

9. What *exactly* do you want from me?
10. Where *exactly* did you park the car?

**5. Not really; not at all — in *not exactly*, used to soften a negative or to be ironic.** (не то чтобы, не совсем)

11. He's not *exactly* a genius.
12. "Were you surprised?" "Not *exactly*."
13. Five hours in traffic isn't *exactly* my idea of fun.

**6. Yes, that's right — used alone as a reply to show strong agreement.** (вот именно, точно)

14. "So the real problem is the price?" "*Exactly*."
15. "We should have left earlier." "*Exactly*!"

### precisely

**Pronunciation:** /prɪˈsaɪsli/ &middot; *prih-SYS-lee*
**Русский:** точно, именно, ровно
**Type:** focusing; also degree and manner
**Position:** before its word — before the word, number or phrase it makes precise; end in the manner sense; alone as a reply
**Comparison:** more/most — in the manner sense and in *more precisely*, 'to be more exact'
**Modifies:** nouns and noun phrases, numbers and times, clauses, verbs
**Form:** -ly from *precise*: keeps the *e*
**Contrast:** *Precisely* is a more formal *exactly*. *Or, more precisely,* corrects what was just said — точнее, and *precisely because* stresses that the reason is surprising: *I trust him precisely because he disagrees with me*. Russian *точно* meaning 'definitely' is not *precisely*: *I'll definitely be there*.

Precisely has 5 distinct senses.

**1. Exactly; just — stressing that something is the very thing or reason meant.** (именно, как раз)

1. That's *precisely* the point I was trying to make.
2. People came *precisely* because the event was free.
3. That's *precisely* why I called you.

**2. No more and no less — with numbers, amounts and times.** (ровно, точно)

4. The train left at *precisely* 7:15.
5. The box measures *precisely* two feet across.

**3. Accurately and with great care over detail.** (точно, аккуратно)

6. The parts must be cut *precisely*.
7. She describes the city *more precisely* than any other writer.

**4. To be exact — in *more precisely*, correcting or narrowing down what was just said.** (точнее, а точнее)

8. The store opened in early 2020 — or, *more precisely*, in January.
9. The problem is the car — *more precisely*, the brakes.

**5. Yes, that's right — used alone as a reply to show agreement.** (вот именно, совершенно верно)

10. "So you want us to wait?" "*Precisely*."
11. "It's cheaper in the long run." "*Precisely*."

### likewise

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlaɪkwaɪz/ &middot; *LYK-wyz*
**Русский:** так же, тоже, аналогично; взаимно
**Type:** focusing — additive; also linking
**Position:** end — in *do likewise*; front as a linker; alone as a reply
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs (*do likewise*), whole clauses
**Form:** compound of *like* + *-wise* 'way', written as one word
**Contrast:** *Do likewise* means 'do the same thing', not 'do it like someone'. As a reply, *Likewise* returns a polite wish or feeling (*Nice to meet you. — Likewise.*), close to Russian *взаимно*; for a simple fact say *Me too*: *I'm hungry. — Me too*, not *Likewise*.

Likewise has 3 distinct senses.

**1. In the same way; the same thing.** (так же, то же самое)

1. Watch what I do, and then do *likewise*.
2. She stood up to leave, and the others did *likewise*.
3. He treats everyone with respect and expects to be treated *likewise*.

**2. Also; similarly — linking a new point to the one before.** (аналогично, также)

4. Parents should read to their kids every day. Teachers, *likewise*, should make time to read aloud.
5. Prices rose in March. *Likewise*, wages went up slightly.

**3. The same to you — used alone as a reply to a polite remark.** (взаимно, мне тоже)

6. "It was great to meet you." "*Likewise*."
7. "Have a good weekend!" "*Likewise*!"
