More adverbs: P–S#

Adverbs P–S beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Mostly -ly adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (by and large, on purpose). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or -ly forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

p.m.#

Pronunciation
/ˌpiːˈɛm/ · pee-EM
Русский
после полудня, вечера (о времени)
Type
time — after noon, with a clock time
Position
after its word — after the number
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
clock times (5 p.m., 6:30 p.m.)
Form
abbreviation of Latin post meridiem, after noon
Contrast
Americans use the 12-hour clock where Russian uses 19:00: say 7 p.m. Use it only after a number: not in the p.m., not 7 o'clock p.m., and not 7 p.m. in the evening (the evening is already in p.m.). 12 p.m. is noon, which confuses people, so say noon or midnight.

After noon — used after a time between noon and midnight.

  1. The store closes at 9 p.m.
  2. My flight lands at 6:45 p.m. Eastern time.
  3. Office hours are 1 to 4 p.m., Monday through Friday.

Part is usually a noun (a part of the plan). As an adverb it comes before a noun or adjective, most often in pairs: part man, part wolf. Before a single adjective, partly sounds more natural: partly true.

pat#

Pronunciation
/pæt/ · PAT
Русский
назубок; не отступать (stand pat)
Type
manner — only in down pat and stand pat
Position
end — after down or stand
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
down in have something down pat, and stand in stand pat
Form
flat — the same form as the adjective pat
Contrast
The adjective before a noun is critical: a pat answer is a quick, rehearsed reply (шаблонный, заученный ответ). The verb and noun pat mean похлопать, похлопывание: pat the dog. Stand pat comes from poker and is mainly American.

Pat has 2 distinct senses.

1. Perfectly, learned by heart — in have or get something down pat. (назубок, в совершенстве)

  1. She practiced her speech until she had it down pat.
  2. It took me a month to get the dance steps down pat.

2. Without changing anything — in stand pat, keep a decision or position. (не отступать, оставаться при своём)

  1. The Fed is expected to stand pat on interest rates this month.
  2. Despite the losing streak, the team stood pat at the trade deadline.
