Negative and function adverbs#

not, no, any, as, else, together — the small grammatical adverbs that everything else leans on.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Not is an adverb, and the most frequent one in the language. With it here are the function adverbs that do grammatical work rather than adding meaning: no and any before a comparative (no better, any faster), as in comparisons of equality (as tall as), else after a question word or indefinite (who else, something else), and together and all in their adverb uses (all alone).

not#

Pronunciation
/nɑːt/ · NAHT
Русский
не
Type
negative; also focusing in not only
Position
mid — after be and the first auxiliary; before to and -ing forms; before its word when it negates one word or phrase
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs and clauses, infinitives and -ing forms, adjectives, adverbs, noun phrases, quantifiers; stands for a whole clause in I hope not
Form
contracts to n't after be, have, do and the modals: isn't, doesn't, can't, won't (from will)
Contrast
Not negates verbs, adjectives and adverbs; before a noun use the determiner no or not any: I have no time or I don't have any time, not I have not time. A main verb needs do: I don't know, not I not know or the archaic I know not. One negative per clause: Russian Я никогда ничего не видел is I have never seen anything, not I haven't never seen nothing. English moves the negative up to think, believe, expect: Я думаю, он не прав is naturally I don't think he's right. Position changes the meaning with infinitives: I decided not to go (I stayed home) vs. I didn't decide to go (I made no decision). Not all the students passed (some failed) is clear; All the students didn't pass is ambiguous. A fronted not phrase inverts: Not once did he call; Not until Friday will we know. In short replies say I hope not, I guess not, probably not — but I don't think so; I think not is formal.

Not has 7 distinct senses.

1. Used to make a verb or a whole clause negative. (не)

  1. I do not agree with you.
  2. She's not coming tonight.
  3. Try not to wake the baby.

2. Used before a single word or phrase to make only that part negative. (не (перед словом или фразой))

  1. Not everyone likes jazz.
  2. Call me, not my assistant.
  3. She's a colleague, not a friend.

3. Used instead of a whole negative clause — after hope, guess, be afraid, and after if, or and probably. (нет; не)

  1. Is it going to rain? — I hope not.
  2. Take the job if you like it; if not, keep looking.
  3. Ready or not, here I come.

4. Not a single one; none at all — emphatic, in not a, not one, not a single. (ни (один), ни (слова))

  1. Not one person showed up.
  2. There wasn't a cloud in the sky — not a single one.
  3. Not a word to anyone about this.

5. Not just this, and that as well — in not only … but (also). (не только… но и)

  1. She not only wrote the song but also sang it.
  2. Not only is it cheaper, it's faster too.

6. Used before a negative word to say something positive in a modest, understated way. (не- (неплохо, нередко — смягчённое утверждение))

  1. Honestly, the pizza was not bad.
  2. It's a not unreasonable request.
  3. Delays like this are not uncommon in winter.

7. In not that, used to deny a reason or meaning someone might read into what you just said. (не то чтобы)

  1. He's moving to Denver — not that I care.
  2. Not that it matters now, but I was right.

no#

Pronunciation
/noʊ/ · NOH
Русский
больше не; ничуть не
Type
negative — before comparatives and a few adjectives; also degree
Position
before its word — before a comparative, longer, sooner, more, less, different or good
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
comparative adjectives and adverbs, more, less, longer, sooner; the adjectives different and good
Contrast
The adverb no goes only before a comparative and a few words like different and good: no better, no longer, no different. Before an ordinary adjective use not: He's not tall, not He's no tall. Before a noun no is a determiner (no money), and No. as an answer is an interjection — both are in those dictionaries. No more than means 'only' (It costs no more than $10 — всего лишь), while not more than means 'at most' (не больше); no less than stresses a large amount (целых). Russian больше не is no longer or not … anymore: I no longer work there or I don't work there anymore, not I don't work there more — and never both in one clause. No longer goes mid, anymore at the end. Fronted no sooner inverts and takes than, not when: No sooner had we sat down than the phone rang. He's no more a doctor than I am means neither of us is a doctor (он такой же врач, как я), not that he is a better one.

No has 6 distinct senses.

1. Not now, though it was before — in no longer and, in formal or literary use, no more. (больше не, уже не)

  1. He no longer works here.
  2. We can no longer afford to wait.
  3. The old theater is no more; it closed in 2019.

2. Not at all; in no way — before a comparative, different or good, to say there is no difference in degree. (ничуть не, нисколько не)

  1. The new model is no faster than the old one.
  2. Her second book is no different from her first.
  3. This old phone charger is no good anymore.

3. Only, or as much as — before more than or less than and an amount, to stress how small or how large it is. (не более, всего лишь; не менее, целых)

  1. The walk takes no more than ten minutes.
  2. He paid no less than $2,000 for the ticket.
  3. The storm dumped no less than two feet of snow on the city.

4. Used after a name or fact to stress that it is surprising or impressive — in no less at the end of a phrase. (не кто-нибудь, а; ни много ни мало)

  1. She got a thank-you note from the governor, no less.
  2. He showed up in a tuxedo, no less.

5. Immediately after; as soon as — in no sooner … than. (едва… как; не успел… как)

  1. No sooner had I left than it started to pour.
  2. I had no sooner paid than I saw a better deal online.

6. Used to take back a word you just said and replace it with a stronger or more exact one. (нет, даже; вернее)

  1. Hundreds, no, thousands of fans lined the street.
  2. It was a good year — no, a great year.

any#

Pronunciation
/ˈɛni/ · EN-ee
Русский
хоть немного; ничуть не
Type
degree — in questions, negatives and if clauses
Position
before its word — before a comparative, good or different; end in informal American didn't help any
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
comparative adjectives and adverbs, longer, more, and the adjectives good and different; a verb phrase in informal speech
Contrast
The adverb any works only in questions, negatives and if clauses, and only before a comparative, good or different: Is it any cheaper? is fine, Is it any cheap? is not — say Is it cheap at all? In an affirmative sentence use a little or a bit: It's a little better, not It's any better. Not any + comparative equals no + comparative: It's not any better = It's no better. Russian любой is the determiner any (any size, any time), which is in the determiner dictionary, not this adverb. American spelling splits anymore (time: I don't live there anymore — больше не) from any more (amount: I don't want any more — determiner). It didn't help any is informal American; in writing use at all.

Any has 3 distinct senses.

1. At all; to any degree — before a comparative, good or different. (хоть немного, хоть сколько-нибудь; (в отрицании) ничуть не)

  1. Do you feel any better today?
  2. Is the sequel any good?
  3. The traffic isn't any worse than usual.

2. For more time; still — in any longer, in negatives, questions and if clauses. (больше (не), дольше)

  1. I can't wait any longer.
  2. If this takes any longer, we'll miss the train.

3. At all — at the end of a negative sentence or a question; informal American. (нисколько, вообще, хоть сколько-то)

  1. The medicine didn't help any.
  2. Did you sleep any last night?
  3. That kid hasn't grown any since June.

none#

Pronunciation
/nʌn/ · NUHN
Русский
ничуть не, не слишком
Type
degree — negative, in none too and none the + comparative
Position
before its word — before too or the + a comparative
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
too + an adjective or adverb; the + a comparative
Form
fixed patterns only: none too + adjective or adverb, none the + comparative
Contrast
The pronoun none (none of us) is in the pronoun dictionary; the adverb lives only in none too and none the + comparative. None the needs the: none the wiser, not none wiser. A Russian reader can take She was none too pleased as 'никто не был доволен' — it means she was not pleased at all, said as understatement, usually about annoyance. And none too soon means 'and not a moment too early' (давно пора), not 'too early'. No wiser than compares with someone else; none the wiser compares with before — you learned nothing. Nonetheless (однако) is one word in American spelling and is in the linking group.

None has 2 distinct senses.

1. Not at all; not very — in none too, usually as understatement. (не слишком, не очень-то)

  1. The clerk looked none too happy to see us.
  2. The motel was none too clean.
  3. The ambulance finally arrived, and none too soon.

2. Not at all; in no way — in none the + comparative, often with for. (ничуть не, нисколько не)

  1. I read the manual twice and was none the wiser.
  2. The car was none the worse for the long trip.
  3. She was none the happier for all her money.

as#

Pronunciation
/æz/ · AZ
Русский
так же, столь же
Type
degree — equality, the first as in as … as
Position
before its word — before the adjective, adverb, much or many being compared
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, much, many, little, few; an adjective with a + noun in as good a cook
Form
the same spelling as the conjunction and preposition as (the second as in as … as); both are usually weak, əz, in connected speech and full æz only when stressed
Contrast
Only the first as in as … as is this adverb; the second as, and as meaning 'like', 'because', 'while' or 'in the role of', are in the conjunction and preposition dictionaries. Use the plain form, not the comparative: as tall as, not as taller as. For multiples say twice as expensive as, not twice expensive as. The everyday American negative is not as … as; not so … as is formal. Russian такой же высокий, как is as tall as, but такого же роста, как is the same height as — never the same tall as. After the second as, speech uses me and careful writing I am: She's as tall as me / as tall as I am. With a singular noun the article moves: as good a cook as, not as a good cook as. As well ('too' — тоже) is a separate phrase.

As has 4 distinct senses.

1. To the same degree; equally — the first as in as … as, or alone when the comparison is understood. (так же, такой же (… как); столь же)

  1. Nobody works as hard as Dana.
  2. It's not as expensive as I thought.
  3. I used to run every day, but I'm not as fit now.

2. After twice, three times, half and similar words: that many times the size, amount or degree. (в (два, три) раза; вдвое)

  1. The new apartment is twice as big.
  2. Tickets cost three times as much as last year.
  3. My commute is half as long now.

3. Before many, much, long, little or few and a number: to stress how large or how small an amount is. (целых, аж, до; всего)

  1. As many as 300 homes lost power.
  2. A good hearing aid can cost as much as $6,000.
  3. You can get there in as little as an hour.

4. In as much: the same thing, just that — when something confirms what you already suspected. (так (я и думал); то же самое)

  1. The store's closed? I thought as much.
  2. Everyone suspected as much, but nobody said it.

else#

Pronunciation
/ɛls/ · ELS
Русский
ещё; другой, иначе
Type
focusing — additive (what else) or excluding (someone else); also linking in or else
Position
after its word — after who, what, where, how, why, the some-, any-, no-, every- words, and little and much
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
question words; indefinite pronouns and adverbs (someone, anything, nowhere, everybody); little, much
Form
the possessive goes on else: someone else's, nobody else's
Contrast
Else never stands before a noun and never after which or an ordinary noun: another person or other people, not else person; which other book, not which else book. Russian ещё splits several ways: What else do you need? (after a question word), Do you want more coffee? (with a noun), one more time or another day; and ещё не is not yet. The possessive 's goes on else: someone else's phone, not someone's else phone. Somewhere else and elsewhere mean the same; elsewhere is in the place group. Or else as a threat can end the sentence: Pay up, or else!

Else has 3 distinct senses.

1. Other; different — a person, thing, place or way instead of the one mentioned. (другой; (кто-то) другой, (где-то) ещё, иначе)

  1. This isn't my jacket — it must be someone else's.
  2. Let's eat somewhere else tonight.
  3. How else could we have done it?

2. In addition; more — besides what was already mentioned. (ещё)

  1. What else do you need from the store?
  2. Is anyone else coming?
  3. There's not much else we can do.

3. Otherwise; if not — in or else, often as a warning or a threat. (иначе, а то)

  1. Hurry up, or else we'll miss the bus.
  2. You'd better return it by Friday — or else!

together#

Pronunciation
/təˈɡɛðər/ · tuh-GETH-ur
Русский
вместе
Type
manner — with each other or into one; also time
Position
end — after the verb or its object; after the particle in phrasal verbs (put it together)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs
Contrast
All together (everyone at once, in one group — все вместе) is not altogether (completely; in total — совсем, всего): Let's sing it all together / That's $40 altogether. Russian вместе for the total of a bill or a count is altogether or in all, not together; together adds things up only as put together or taken together (вместе взятые): He earns more than the two of us put together. Together with does not make a singular subject plural: The manager, together with two assistants, is on the way. Russian Мы с Анной пошли вместе is Anna and I went together or I went with Anna, not We with Anna went together. The informal adjective together (She's so together — собранная) is in the adjective dictionary.

Together has 6 distinct senses.

1. With each other; in a group or as a team. (вместе, сообща)

  1. We grew up together in Ohio.
  2. Let's work on this together.
  3. The whole family had dinner together.

2. Into one place, piece or whole; so that things are joined or combined. (вместе, воедино; (соединить) друг с другом)

  1. Mix the flour and sugar together.
  2. Tape the two pieces together.
  3. Add these numbers together.

3. In a romantic relationship; as a couple. (вместе (как пара))

  1. They've been together for twelve years.
  2. Are Mike and Jen still together?

4. Combined; counted or considered as a whole — often in put together and taken together. (вместе взятые, в сумме)

  1. He earns more than the two of us put together.
  2. Together, the three stores employ about 200 people.
  3. Taken together, these studies point to the same cause.

5. So that they suit or match each other — mainly with go and fit. ((сочетаться) друг с другом)

  1. Those colors don't really go together.
  2. Wine and chocolate go together surprisingly well.

6. At the same time; at once. (одновременно, разом)

  1. The two runners crossed the finish line together.
  2. The two alarms went off together.

all#

Pronunciation
/ɔːl/ · AWL
Русский
совсем, совершенно
Type
degree — completely; also in tied scores
Position
before its word — before an adjective, adverb, prepositional phrase, the + comparative, too or but; after the number in scores
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, prepositional phrases (all over, all through), the + comparative, too; numbers in scores
Contrast
The determiner and pronoun all (all the students, We all agree) are in those dictionaries; the adverb modifies adjectives and phrases. All but has two readings: before an adjective or verb it means 'almost' (all but impossible — почти невозможно); before a noun or number it means 'everyone except' (all but one — все, кроме одного). All the better needs the. Watch the one-word look-alikes: all ready (everyone ready) vs. already (уже); all together vs. altogether (совсем, всего); all right is standard in American writing, alright informal. Russian всё meaning 'still' is still, not all: Он всё ждёт is He's still waiting; всё равно is anyway. In informal American speech be all introduces a quote (And he's all, 'No way') — keep it out of writing.

All has 7 distinct senses.

1. Completely; entirely — and, informally, very. (совсем, совершенно, весь)

  1. She lives all alone in that big house.
  2. You're all wet — did you walk here?
  3. The kids got all excited about the snow.

2. Throughout; in every part of a place or a period of time — before over, around, through and along. (по всему, повсюду; всё (время))

  1. There were toys all over the floor.
  2. The baby cried all through the night.
  3. I knew it all along.

3. Very; so — in not all that + adjective or adverb, to say something is less than people might expect. (не так уж, не очень-то)

  1. The movie wasn't all that good.
  2. It's not all that far — maybe ten minutes on foot.
  3. Nobody was all that surprised when he quit.

4. Almost; very nearly — in all but before an adjective, participle or verb. (почти, практически)

  1. The project is all but finished.
  2. By noon the snow had all but melted.
  3. It was all but impossible to find parking.

5. Even more, for a reason just given — in all the + comparative. (тем более, ещё (лучше))

  1. If you can come early, all the better.
  2. The win was all the sweeter after last year's loss.
  3. That's all the more reason to call her.

6. Much more than is good or wanted — in all too. (слишком (уж))

  1. Accidents like this happen all too often.
  2. The ending was all too predictable.

7. Each; for each side — in a tied score, as in tennis or baseball. (по, поровну (о равном счёте: 3:3))

  1. It's three-all going into the ninth inning.
  2. At thirty-all, she served an ace.
