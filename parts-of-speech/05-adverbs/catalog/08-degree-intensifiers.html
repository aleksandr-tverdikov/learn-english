Degree adverbs that turn it up#

How much? — very, really, so, too, quite, extremely, totally, absolutely and the other intensifiers.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Intensifiers strengthen an adjective, an adverb or a verb. The key distinction is gradable versus extreme: very goes with gradable adjectives (very cold) and absolutely with extreme ones (absolutely freezing), and mixing them (very freezing, absolutely cold) sounds wrong to a native ear. Too means more than is wanted, not very — too expensive is a complaint. And quite means very in American English but often only fairly in British English.

very#

Pronunciation
/ˈvɛri/ · VAIR-ee
Русский
очень
Type
degree — the basic intensifier for gradable adjectives and adverbs
Position
before its word
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
gradable adjectives and adverbs, much and many, superlatives and own
Contrast
Very cannot modify a verb: say I like it very much or I really like it, never I very like it (Russian очень нравится). It does not go with extreme adjectives either — absolutely freezing, not very freezing — or before a comparative: much better, not very better. With verb-like past participles English uses much or greatly: much appreciated, not very appreciated (but very interested and very tired are fine). Not very is a polite way of saying the opposite: not very good means fairly bad. In at the very end and this very moment, very is an adjective meaning exactly this.

Very has 2 distinct senses.

1. To a high degree; extremely. (очень)

  1. It's very cold outside today.
  2. She speaks English very well.
  3. Thank you very much for coming.

2. Used to stress a superlative or the words own, same, next, first and last. (самый; же (на следующий же день))

  1. This is the very best pizza in town.
  2. She finally has her very own room.
  3. He called me back the very next day.

really#

Pronunciation
/ˈriːəli/ · REE-uh-lee
Русский
очень; действительно, на самом деле
Type
degree — the everyday spoken intensifier; also stance (in fact)
Position
mid — before the main verb (I really like it), and before its word with adjectives (really good)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, verbs, and whole statements
Form
-ly from real
Contrast
Where really goes changes the meaning: I really don't like him is strong dislike, while I don't really like him is a mild не особо. Unlike very, really can modify a verb: I really want to go. Really? said alone as a reply (Правда?) works as an interjection. In casual American speech real replaces it before adjectives (real good), but not in writing.

Really has 4 distinct senses.

1. Very; very much — adding force to an adjective, an adverb or a verb. (очень, правда)

  1. That was a really good movie.
  2. She drives really fast.
  3. I really like your new haircut.

2. In fact; in reality, as opposed to what seems true. (на самом деле, в действительности)

  1. What really happened that night?
  2. He looks forty, but he's really almost sixty.
  3. Nobody really believed her story.

3. With not: not much or not exactly — a soft way of saying no. (не особо, не совсем)

  1. “Are you hungry?” “Not really.”
  2. I don't really understand how it works.
  3. It's not really my kind of music.

4. Used to add force to a protest, reassurance or apology; honestly. (право, честное слово, ну правда)

  1. It's no trouble at all, really.
  2. Really, Tom, you could have called first.

/ɪkˈstriːmli/ · ik-STREEM-lee
Русский

degree — a strong intensifier for gradable adjectives and adverbs

not gradable

Contrast
Like very, extremely goes with gradable words (extremely cold, extremely difficult), not with extreme ones: absolutely freezing, absolutely perfect, not extremely freezing or extremely perfect. It does not modify a verb on its own: I liked it very much, not I extremely liked it. It is more formal than really or so.

To a very high degree; very.

  1. The test was extremely difficult.
  2. He was extremely lucky to survive the crash.
  3. She handled the situation extremely well.

incredibly#

Pronunciation
/ɪnˈkrɛdəbli/ · in-KRED-uh-blee
Русский
невероятно
Type
degree — a strong intensifier; also stance, as a sentence adverb
Position
before its word — front when it comments on the whole sentence
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, and whole sentences
Form
-ly from incredible
Contrast
Before an adjective incredibly just means very, very (incredibly tired) and says nothing about belief. At the front of a sentence, with a comma, it means it is hard to believe that: Incredibly, he won is как ни удивительно, он победил.

Incredibly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Extremely; to a very great degree. (невероятно, чрезвычайно)

  1. The view from the top was incredibly beautiful.
  2. I'm incredibly grateful for your help.
  3. She works incredibly hard.

2. In a way that is hard to believe — commenting on a whole fact. (как ни удивительно, невероятно, но)

  1. Incredibly, nobody was hurt in the fire.
  2. Incredibly, the old car still runs.

Pronunciation
/ˈtɛrəbli/ · TAIR-uh-blee
Русский
ужасно, очень
Type
degree — an intensifier; also manner (very badly)
Position
before its word — for very; end for very badly (played terribly)

Position shows the meaning. Before an adjective terribly only means very, even about good things: terribly kind, terribly important. After a verb it means very badly: He sang terribly. Terribly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Very; extremely. (ужасно, очень)

  1. I'm terribly sorry to hear about your father.
  2. The movie wasn't terribly exciting.
  3. The kids miss you terribly.

2. Very badly. (ужасно, отвратительно)

  1. Our team played terribly last night.
  2. I slept terribly last night.
  3. She treated him terribly.

Pronunciation
/ˈɔːfəli/ · AW-fuh-lee
Русский
ужасно, очень
Type
degree — an informal intensifier; also manner (very badly)
Position
before its word — for very; end for badly
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies

Before an adjective awfully means very and carries no bad sense: That's awfully nice of you is a compliment. The meaning badly comes only after a verb, and even there terribly or badly is more usual. In casual American speech awful itself is used this way (awful good), but that is nonstandard in writing.

Awfully has 2 distinct senses.

1. Very; extremely. (очень, ужасно)

  1. That's awfully kind of you.
  2. You're up awfully early.
  3. It's awfully quiet in here.

2. Very badly; in an unpleasant way. (ужасно, скверно)

  1. He behaved awfully at the wedding.
  2. The band played awfully that night.

so#

Pronunciation
/soʊ/ · SOH
Русский
так, настолько, очень
Type
degree — an intensifier; also manner, linking, and a stand-in for a clause (I think so)
Position
before its word — for degree; front in So do I, end in I think so
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, much and many, verbs; replaces a whole clause
Contrast
So goes before an adjective with no noun; before a + adjective + noun use such: so nice, but such a nice day, not a so nice day. So, not very, starts a result: so tired that I fell asleep. After think, hope, guess and be afraid, English needs so where Russian has так or nothing: I think so, I hope so, never I think it. So do I (with inversion) means и я тоже; for a negative use Neither do I. So it is (no inversion) means and that is true. At the start of a clause (It rained, so we stayed home) and in so that (purpose) it is a conjunction.

So has 10 distinct senses.

1. Very; to a great degree — often emotional or exclamatory. (так, очень)

  1. I'm so happy for you!
  2. Thank you so much.
  3. Why are you driving so fast?

2. Used instead of repeating a clause, after verbs like think, hope and say, and in if so. (так; да (думаю, да))

  1. “Will it rain?” “I don't think so.”
  2. I told you so!
  3. Are you coming? If so, bring a jacket.

3. To the degree stated or shown, especially with that for a result, or in only so much. (настолько, так)

  1. She was so tired that she fell asleep at her desk.
  2. The fish was about so big.
  3. There's only so much I can do.

4. Also; in the same way — with inversion: so + auxiliary + subject. (тоже, так же)

  1. “I'm starving.” “So am I.”
  2. Tom passed the exam, and so did his sister.

5. Approximately — in or so, after a number or amount. (примерно, или около того)

  1. I'll be back in an hour or so.
  2. There were twenty or so people at the meeting.

6. For that reason; therefore — after and. (поэтому, так что)

  1. The store was closed, and so we went home.
  2. He was sick and so missed the game.

7. In this way; like this — also in and so on. (так, таким образом)

  1. Hold the bat like so, with your hands together.
  2. It so happened that we were in town that week.
  3. Bring pens, paper, tape and so on.

8. Informal: definitely, completely — before a verb, not, or a noun phrase. (совершенно, точно)

  1. I am so not going to that party.
  2. That jacket is so last year.
  3. You are so getting grounded for this.

9. Used to agree with something you notice or have just been told. (и правда, действительно)

  1. “Look, it's snowing.” “So it is!”
  2. “The baby's asleep.” “So she is.”

10. Informal: used to contradict a negative statement. (ещё как, да)

  1. “You didn't clean your room.” “I did so!”
  2. “You can't swim.” “Can so!”

too#

Pronunciation
/tuː/ · TOO
Русский
слишком; тоже
Type
degree — more than is wanted; also focusing (also)
Position
before its word — for more than wanted; end for also
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, much and many; a whole clause in the sense also
Contrast
Too is not very: too expensive means more than I can or want to pay and is a complaint (слишком), while very expensive only describes. Before a noun the order is too big a car, never a too big car; and it is much too hot, not too much hot. In the sense also it ends an affirmative clause (in formal writing it can follow the subject between commas: I, too, was surprised); after a negative use either: I don't like it either, not I don't like it too. Do not confuse it with to and two, which sound the same.

Too has 5 distinct senses.

1. More than is needed, wanted or possible. (слишком)

  1. This coffee is too hot to drink.
  2. You're driving too fast.
  3. There were too many people at the beach.

2. Also; as well — at the end of a clause. (тоже, также)

  1. “I love pizza.” “Me too.”
  2. Bring a jacket, and an umbrella too.
  3. She speaks French, and German too.

3. Very — in negatives and in polite set phrases. (очень, не очень)

  1. I'm not too sure about that.
  2. You're too kind.
  3. We'd be only too happy to help.

4. And what is more — adding a surprising or annoying fact. (к тому же, да ещё)

  1. He lost his wallet, and on his birthday too.
  2. She got a raise, and in her first year too.

5. Informal: used to contradict a negative statement. (ещё как, да)

  1. “You didn't feed the dog.” “I did too!”
  2. “That's not true.” “It is too!”

quite#

Pronunciation
/kwaɪt/ · KWYT
Русский
довольно, весьма; совсем
Type
degree — an intensifier in American English; also a maximizer (quite right)
Position
before its word — and before a or an (quite a party)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives, adverbs, verbs, and a + noun
Contrast
US/UK split: in American English quite good usually means really good; in British English, said flatly, it often means only fairly good — remember this when reading British texts. With words that cannot be graded (right, sure, finished) it means completely in both. Not quite means not completely (не совсем), not not at all. The word order is quite a big house, and the British one-word reply Quite. (именно) is rare in the US. Do not confuse it with quiet (KWY-uht).

Quite has 3 distinct senses.

1. Very; to a considerable degree — also in quite a few and quite a lot. (довольно, весьма, очень; немало)

  1. The food there is quite good.
  2. We waited quite a long time.
  3. Quite a few people left early.

2. Completely; entirely — with words that cannot be graded, and in not quite. (совсем, вполне, совершенно)

  1. You're quite right.
  2. I'm not quite ready yet.
  3. Are you quite sure you want to leave?

3. Before a, an or the + noun: a remarkable or unusual example of. (настоящий, что надо, ну и)

  1. That was quite a party last night!
  2. She's quite the cook.
  3. It's been quite a day.

totally#

Pronunciation
/ˈtoʊtəli/ · TOH-tuh-lee
Русский
полностью, совершенно
Type
degree — a maximizer; also stance (definitely) in informal speech
Position
before its word — also mid, before a verb (I totally forgot)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
adjectives and verbs; alone as a reply

Contrast
False friend: totally never means в общем, итого. For a sum use in total: In total, we spent $50, not Totally we spent $50. In casual American speech totally is also a strong yes and a plain intensifier (totally awesome); in writing prefer completely.

Totally has 2 distinct senses.

1. Completely; entirely. (полностью, совершенно)

  1. The two plans are totally different.
  2. I totally forgot about the meeting.
  3. The town was totally destroyed by the storm.

2. Informal: definitely — used to agree or to add emphasis. (точно, конечно, ещё как)

  1. “That was a great show.” “Totally.”
  2. I'm totally going to win this time.
  3. You should totally come with us.

To the fullest degree; totally.

absolutely#

Pronunciation
/ˌæbsəˈluːtli/ · ab-suh-LOOT-lee
Русский
совершенно, абсолютно; безусловно
Type
degree — a maximizer for extreme and non-gradable adjectives; also stance (Absolutely! as a reply)
Position
before its word — mid before a verb (I absolutely love it); alone as a reply
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
extreme and non-gradable adjectives, verbs, no and nothing; alone as a reply
Form
-ly from absolute
Contrast
The gradable/extreme split: absolutely goes with extreme or yes-or-no words — absolutely freezing, absolutely exhausted, absolutely right — and sounds wrong with plain gradable ones: very cold, not absolutely cold. A few strong positive words take both (absolutely beautiful). As a one-word reply Absolutely is a warm yes, and Absolutely not is a firm no.

Absolutely has 3 distinct senses.

1. Completely; totally. (совершенно, абсолютно)

  1. I'm absolutely exhausted.
  2. You're absolutely right.
  3. There's absolutely nothing to eat in this house.

2. Without any doubt — used to add strong emphasis to a verb or statement. (обязательно, безусловно, определённо)

  1. You absolutely have to see this movie.
  2. I absolutely love your new apartment.
  3. It was absolutely the best meal I've ever had.

3. Used alone as a reply: yes, certainly — or, with not, certainly not. (конечно, безусловно; ни в коем случае)

  1. “Can I borrow your pen?” “Absolutely.”
  2. “Should we give up?” “Absolutely not.”

2. In a perfect way; without any mistakes or faults. (идеально, безупречно, прекрасно)

Contrast
Very cannot go before a comparative: say way better, much better or far better, never very better. Way is the informal choice; use much or far in writing. The order is way too expensive, not too way expensive. The replies No way! and Way! are interjections, and away is a different word: He lives far away, but way back in 1995.

Way has 3 distinct senses.

1. Much; a lot — before comparatives, too, more and less. (намного, гораздо)

  1. This phone is way better than my old one.
  2. The tickets were way too expensive.
  3. We brought way more food than we needed.

2. Far — before words of place or time. (далеко, задолго, аж)

  1. They live way out in the country.
  2. I met him way back in high school.
  3. We're way behind schedule.

3. Slang: very — before an adjective. (очень, реально)

  1. That movie was way cool.
  2. That test was way hard.

Real for really (real good, real quick) is everyday American speech but counts as nonstandard: in writing and formal speech use really or very. It replaces only the very sense, so not I real like it or What real happened? Everywhere else real is the adjective: a real diamond.

Very; really.

  1. That was real nice of you.
  2. I'll be back real quick.
  3. She did real well on the test.

Contrast
Russian глубоко is usually deeply in everyday English: deeply grateful, deeply sorry. Profoundly is more formal and goes most often with change and effect (profoundly changed, profoundly affected). It never means physical depth — that is deep (dig deep). Profoundly deaf means completely deaf.

To a very great and serious degree; deeply.

  1. The war profoundly changed the country.
  2. I was profoundly moved by her speech.
  3. The child was born profoundly deaf.

Type
degree — with verbs of opinion and feeling; also manner (with strength)

mid or end — I strongly agree, I feel strongly

Modifies
verbs of opinion (agree, believe, recommend, oppose), participles, some adjectives

Contrast
Russian сильно is wider than strongly. Strongly goes with opinion and advice — strongly agree, strongly recommend, strongly oppose, feel strongly about — not with liking, wanting or missing: I really want it, I miss you very much, not I strongly want it or I strongly miss you. Heavy rain is raining hard, not raining strongly.

Strongly has 2 distinct senses.

1. Very much and firmly — about an opinion, belief or feeling. (решительно, настоятельно, твёрдо)

  1. I strongly disagree with that decision.
  2. We strongly recommend booking in advance.
  3. She feels more strongly about it than I do.

2. With strength, force or intensity; powerfully. (сильно, крепко, мощно)

  1. The room smelled strongly of paint.
  2. He is tall and strongly built.
  3. The economy grew strongly last year.

greatly#

Pronunciation
/ˈɡreɪtli/ · GRAYT-lee
Русский

Type
degree — a formal intensifier for verbs and participles
Position
mid or end — greatly improved, has improved greatly
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies

Contrast
Greatly is not a general very: it goes with verbs and participles (greatly appreciated, differ greatly), not with plain adjectives — very happy, not greatly happy. It is also too formal for liking: I like it very much, not I greatly like it. In casual American speech great itself works as an adverb meaning very well (It works great), which is a different meaning.

Very much; to a large degree.

  1. Your help is greatly appreciated.
  2. Her English has improved greatly since last year.
  3. The two versions differ greatly.
