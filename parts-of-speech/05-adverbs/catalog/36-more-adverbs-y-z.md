# More adverbs: Y–Z

> Adverbs Y–Z beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### you bet

**Pronunciation:** /ˌjuː ˈbɛt/ &middot; *yoo BET*
**Русский:** ещё бы, конечно
**Type:** stance — strong, informal yes
**Position:** front — before a clause, or alone as a reply
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Contrast:** Informal and very American. As a reply to *Thanks*, it means *you're welcome*: *“Thanks for the ride.” “You bet.”* It has nothing to do with gambling.

Certainly; of course.

1. *You bet* I'll be there.
2. “Are you coming to the game?” “*You bet*!”
3. *You bet* we were scared.

### zealously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈzɛləsli/ &middot; *ZEL-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** рьяно, усердно, ревностно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of guarding, defending, enforcing and pursuing
**Form:** -ly from *zealous*

With great energy and devotion to a cause or duty.

1. She *zealously* guards her privacy.
2. The new manager enforced the rules *zealously*.
3. Lawyers must represent their clients *zealously*.
