# Adverbs of place

> Where? — *here*, *there*, *everywhere*, *upstairs*, *abroad*, *home*, and the words that locate without an object.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Place adverbs answer *where?* and normally go at the end of the clause. Many of them look like prepositions (*inside*, *above*, *behind*) and become adverbs simply by dropping the object: *wait inside the car* is a preposition, *wait inside* is an adverb. The one that catches Russian speakers is *home*, which takes no preposition after verbs of motion — *go home*, never *go to home*.

---
### here

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɪr/ &middot; *HEER*
**Русский:** здесь, сюда, вот
**Type:** place; also direction and time
**Position:** end — or front with inversion: *Here comes the bus*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun (*the people here*)
**Contrast:** Russian has two words, *здесь* (where?) and *сюда* (where to?); English uses *here* for both, with no preposition: *Come here*, never *come to here*. In the presenting use a noun subject comes after the verb (*Here comes the bus*, *Here are your keys*), but a pronoun does not (*Here it comes*, *Here they are*).

Here has 5 distinct senses.

**1. In or at this place.** (здесь, тут)

1. Sit *here* next to me.
2. Is Anna *here* today?
3. It's really cold in *here*.

**2. To this place.** (сюда)

4. Come *here* for a second.
5. How long did it take you to get *here*?
6. We moved *here* from Ohio in 2019.

**3. At this point in a process, discussion or piece of writing; in this matter or situation.** (в этом месте, на этом этапе, в данном случае)

7. Let's stop *here* for today.
8. *Here* the author changes her argument.
9. The real problem *here* is the cost.

**4. Used to present, offer or point out something, or to say that someone or something has arrived.** (вот)

10. *Here* are your keys.
11. *Here* comes the bus.
12. *Here* you go — one coffee, no sugar.

**5. Used when raising a glass to someone in a toast.** (за (кого-то, что-то))

13. *Here*'s to the happy couple!
14. *Here*'s to a great year.

### there

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðɛr/ &middot; *THAIR*
**Русский:** там, туда, вот
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or front with inversion: *There goes the last train*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun (*that man there*)
**Contrast:** The *there* of *there is / there are* is a dummy subject, not this adverb, and does not mean *там*: *There is a pharmacy there* uses both. Like *here*, it covers both *там* and *туда* with no preposition: *go there*, never *go to there*. With a noun subject the verb comes first (*There goes my bus*), with a pronoun it does not (*There it goes*). Do not confuse it with *their* and *they're*, which sound the same.

There has 8 distinct senses.

**1. In or at that place.** (там)

1. I left my bag over *there*.
2. Were you *there* when it happened?
3. Put the boxes down *there*, by the door.

**2. To that place.** (туда)

4. We drove *there* in two hours.
5. I've never been *there*, but I'd love to go.
6. Wait for me — I'll be *there* in five minutes.

**3. At that point in a process, discussion or story; in that respect.** (на этом, в этом (месте, отношении))

7. I'll stop *there* and take questions.
8. *There* I have to disagree with you.
9. The story could have ended *there*, but it didn't.

**4. Used to point out or draw attention to someone or something, or to show that you have found them.** (вот)

10. *There* goes the last train.
11. *There* you are — I've been looking everywhere for you.
12. *There* she is — right on time.

**5. Used after a greeting or to call someone's attention.** ((не переводится); эй)

13. Hi *there*! How's it going?
14. Hello *there* — can I help you find something?

**6. Available to help and support someone, especially emotionally.** (рядом (быть опорой))

15. My sister was always *there* for me.
16. Whatever happens, I'll be *there* for you.

**7. Existing or available, even if not yet used or noticed.** (есть, имеется)

17. The talent is *there* — he just needs to work harder.
18. The money is *there* if we need it.
19. The demand is clearly *there*, so why not open a second store?

**8. At the goal or the result that you want; succeeding.** (у цели; (добиться) своего)

20. The design isn't quite *there* yet.
21. It took years of practice, but she finally got *there*.

### everywhere

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛvriˌwɛr/ &middot; *EV-ree-wair*
**Русский:** везде, всюду
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or front for emphasis
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *every* + *where*, like *somewhere*, *anywhere*, *nowhere*
**Contrast:** *Not everywhere* means *не везде* (only some places). For *нигде* after a negative verb use *anywhere*: *I can't find it anywhere*, not *everywhere*. Before a clause it works as a conjunction meaning *wherever*: *Everywhere we went, people were friendly*.

In, at or to every place.

1. I've looked *everywhere* for my glasses.
2. There were people *everywhere* downtown.
3. My little brother follows me *everywhere*.

### nowhere

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnoʊˌwɛr/ &middot; *NOH-wair*
**Русский:** нигде, никуда
**Type:** place; also direction, negative and degree
**Position:** end — front only with inversion: *Nowhere is this clearer than…*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; *nowhere near* modifies adjectives, numbers and phrases
**Form:** *no* + *where*; the negative of *somewhere* and *anywhere*
**Contrast:** English allows one negative per clause. Russian *Я никуда не иду* is *I'm not going anywhere* or *I'm going nowhere*, never *I'm not going nowhere*. Fronted, it forces inversion: *Nowhere have I seen such beautiful beaches*. *The middle of nowhere* and *out of nowhere* use *nowhere* as a noun.

Nowhere has 4 distinct senses.

**1. Not in or at any place.** (нигде)

1. My keys were *nowhere* to be found.
2. *Nowhere* in the contract does it mention overtime.
3. The kids were *nowhere* in sight.

**2. Not to any place.** (никуда)

4. After the fire, the family had *nowhere* else to go.
5. I'm going *nowhere* until you tell me the truth.

**3. To no progress, result or success, especially with *get*, *go* or *be*.** (ни к чему (не привести), ни с места, ничего не добиться)

6. We talked for hours and got *nowhere*.
7. This relationship is going *nowhere*.
8. Without my first coach, I'd be *nowhere* today.

**4. In *nowhere near*: not at all; far from.** (далеко не, совсем не)

9. The report is *nowhere near* finished.
10. The sequel is *nowhere near* as good as the original.
11. We have *nowhere near* enough chairs.

### somewhere

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌmˌwɛr/ &middot; *SUM-wair*
**Русский:** где-то, куда-то
**Type:** place; also direction and degree
**Position:** end — before a number or time in the approximate sense: *somewhere around fifty*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; numbers after *around* or *between*
**Form:** *some* + *where*; American informal *someplace* means the same
**Contrast:** Use *somewhere* in statements, and in offers and requests that expect *yes* (*Would you like to go somewhere?*); use *anywhere* in negatives and most questions (*I didn't go anywhere*). An adjective follows it: *somewhere quiet*, not *quiet somewhere*.

Somewhere has 4 distinct senses.

**1. In or at a place that is not known or not named.** (где-то, где-нибудь)

1. I left my phone *somewhere* in the car.
2. Let's sit *somewhere* quiet.
3. He lives *somewhere* near Boston.

**2. To a place that is not known or not named.** (куда-то, куда-нибудь)

4. Are you going *somewhere*?
5. Let's go *somewhere* warm this winter.

**3. About; approximately, with a number, a time or a range.** (примерно, где-то)

6. She's *somewhere* around fifty.
7. We got home *somewhere* around midnight.
8. A good laptop costs *somewhere* between $800 and $1,200.

**4. In *get somewhere*: to make progress.** (сдвинуться с места, добиться чего-то)

9. Now we're getting *somewhere*!
10. After three meetings we finally got *somewhere* on the budget.

### anywhere

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛniˌwɛr/ &middot; *EN-ee-wair*
**Русский:** где-нибудь, куда-нибудь, где угодно
**Type:** place; also direction and degree
**Position:** end — before a number in the range sense: *anywhere from three to five hours*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; numbers after *from* or *between*
**Form:** *any* + *where*; American informal *anyplace* means the same
**Contrast:** In a statement *anywhere* means *где угодно* (any place at all), not *где-то*: *I left it somewhere*, not *I left it anywhere*. After a negative verb it gives *нигде / никуда*: *I can't find it anywhere*. Do not add a second negative (*not … nowhere*).

Anywhere has 5 distinct senses.

**1. In negatives and questions: in, at or to any place.** (где-нибудь, куда-нибудь; (с отрицанием) нигде, никуда)

1. I can't find my keys *anywhere*.
2. Did you go *anywhere* last weekend?
3. Is there a pharmacy *anywhere* near here?

**2. In or to any place at all; it does not matter where.** (где угодно, куда угодно)

4. Sit *anywhere* you like.
5. You can buy these batteries *anywhere*.
6. I'd go *anywhere* with you.

**3. Any amount within a range, with *from … to* or *between*.** (где-то (от … до))

7. The drive takes *anywhere* from three to five hours.
8. Tickets cost *anywhere* between $50 and $500.

**4. In *not anywhere near*: not at all; far from.** (далеко не, совсем не)

9. I'm not *anywhere near* ready.
10. The new stadium isn't *anywhere near* as big as the old one.

**5. In negatives and questions with *get*: to any progress or result.** (ни к чему (не привести))

11. Arguing with him won't get you *anywhere*.
12. Are we getting *anywhere* with this plan?

### elsewhere

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛlsˌwɛr/ &middot; *ELS-wair*
**Русский:** в другом месте, в другое место
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or front, especially in news: *Elsewhere, stocks fell*; also after a noun: *people elsewhere*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun
**Form:** *else* + *where*
**Contrast:** *Elsewhere* is a little more formal than *somewhere else*; in negatives and questions everyday speech uses *anywhere else*: *We couldn't find it anywhere else*.

In, at or to another place or other places.

1. If you don't like the prices, shop *elsewhere*.
2. *Elsewhere* in the state, rents are much lower.
3. As I mentioned *elsewhere* in the report, costs are rising.

### upstairs

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʌpˈstɛrz/ &middot; *up-STAIRZ*
**Русский:** наверху, наверх
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or after a noun: *the neighbors upstairs*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun
**Form:** *up* + *stairs*; the final -s belongs to the adverb, it is not a plural
**Contrast:** No preposition after a verb of motion: *go upstairs*, not *go to upstairs*. Before a noun it is an adjective, stressed on the first syllable (*the UPstairs bathroom*).

Upstairs has 2 distinct senses.

**1. On a higher floor of a building.** (наверху, этажом выше)

1. My office is *upstairs*.
2. The kids are playing *upstairs*.
3. We can hear the neighbors *upstairs* every night.

**2. Up the stairs to a higher floor.** (наверх)

4. She ran *upstairs* to get her coat.
5. Can you take these bags *upstairs*?

### downstairs

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdaʊnˈstɛrz/ &middot; *down-STAIRZ*
**Русский:** внизу, вниз
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or after a noun: *the apartment downstairs*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun
**Form:** *down* + *stairs*; the final -s belongs to the adverb, it is not a plural
**Contrast:** No preposition after a verb of motion: *come downstairs*, not *come to downstairs*. Before a noun it is an adjective, stressed on the first syllable (*a DOWNstairs bedroom*).

Downstairs has 2 distinct senses.

**1. On a lower floor of a building, especially the ground floor.** (внизу, этажом ниже)

1. Breakfast is served *downstairs*.
2. I'll wait for you *downstairs* in the lobby.
3. The people *downstairs* complained about the noise.

**2. Down the stairs to a lower floor.** (вниз)

4. He hurried *downstairs* to answer the door.
5. Carry the laundry *downstairs* for me, please.

### outside

**Pronunciation:** /ˌaʊtˈsaɪd/ &middot; *owt-SYD*
**Русский:** на улице, снаружи, наружу
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — or front: *Outside, it was snowing*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun (*the noise outside*)
**Contrast:** Russian *на улице* about weather or being out of the house is *outside*, not *on the street*: *It's cold outside*, *The kids are playing outside*. With an object it is a preposition (*outside the house*); American English also says *outside of the house*.

Outside has 3 distinct senses.

**1. Not in a building; in the open air.** (на улице, на дворе)

1. It's freezing *outside*.
2. The kids are playing *outside*.
3. Let's eat *outside* tonight.

**2. Out of a building or room, to the outside.** (на улицу, наружу)

4. Could you step *outside* for a minute?
5. I'll take the dog *outside*.

**3. On the outer side or surface of something.** (снаружи)

6. The house looks old *outside*, but it's modern inside.
7. We're having the house painted *outside* this summer.

### inside

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɪnˈsaɪd/ &middot; *in-SYD*
**Русский:** внутри, внутрь, в доме
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end — front for the feelings sense: *Inside, she was furious*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Contrast:** With an object it is a preposition (*inside the car*); American English also says *inside of the car*, and *inside of an hour* means *within an hour*. *Inside out* means *наизнанку*, not *изнутри наружу*.

Inside has 3 distinct senses.

**1. In a building, room or container.** (внутри, в доме)

1. Let's wait *inside* until the rain stops.
2. It's much warmer *inside*.
3. The box had a note *inside*.

**2. Into a building, room or container.** (внутрь, в дом)

4. Come *inside* — it's getting dark.
5. She opened the envelope and looked *inside*.

**3. In your mind or feelings, often hidden from others.** (в душе, внутри)

6. She smiled, but *inside* she was furious.
7. After the breakup, I felt empty *inside*.

### indoors

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɪnˈdɔːrz/ &middot; *in-DORZ*
**Русский:** в помещении, в дом
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *in* + *doors*; the adjective is *indoor*, with no -s
**Contrast:** The adverb ends in -s and the adjective does not: *We played indoors* but *an indoor pool*, never *an indoors pool* or *played indoor*.

Indoors has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a building rather than outside.** (в помещении, дома)

1. The kids stayed *indoors* all day because of the rain.
2. Smoking *indoors* is not allowed.

**2. Into a building.** (в дом, в помещение)

3. We went *indoors* when it started to rain.
4. Bring the plants *indoors* before the first frost.

### outdoors

**Pronunciation:** /ˌaʊtˈdɔːrz/ &middot; *owt-DORZ*
**Русский:** на улице, на открытом воздухе
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *out* + *doors*; the adjective is *outdoor*, with no -s
**Contrast:** The adverb ends in -s and the adjective does not: *We ate outdoors* but *an outdoor café*. *The great outdoors* is a noun (природа, дикая местность).

Outdoors has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Outside a building; in the open air.** (на улице, на свежем воздухе)

1. We ate *outdoors* on the patio.
2. He works *outdoors* all year round.

**2. Out of a building into the open air.** (на улицу)

3. Let's take the kids *outdoors* for some fresh air.
4. When the rain stopped, we moved the party *outdoors*.

### nearby

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnɪrˈbaɪ/ &middot; *neer-BY*
**Русский:** поблизости, рядом
**Type:** place
**Position:** end — or after a noun: *a café nearby*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun
**Form:** *near* + *by*, written as one word
**Contrast:** With an object use *near*: *near the school*; *nearby the school* also occurs but is much less common and sounds awkward to many speakers. Before a noun it is an adjective (*a nearby town*); after a noun it is the adverb (*a town nearby*).

Not far away; close by.

1. Do you live *nearby*?
2. There's a gas station *nearby* if you need one.
3. A police officer was standing *nearby* and saw everything.

### abroad

**Pronunciation:** /əˈbrɔːd/ &middot; *uh-BRAWD*
**Русский:** за границей, за границу
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun (*news from abroad*)
**Form:** *a-* + *broad*
**Contrast:** No preposition before it after a verb: *go abroad*, *live abroad*, not *go to abroad* or *in abroad* (but *from abroad* is fine). Do not confuse it with *aboard* (*на борту*). In everyday American speech *overseas* is often used instead, though *study abroad* is the fixed phrase.

Abroad has 3 distinct senses.

**1. In a foreign country.** (за границей)

1. She lived *abroad* for ten years.
2. Many students spend a semester studying *abroad*.

**2. To a foreign country.** (за границу)

3. We're going *abroad* this summer.
4. He moved *abroad* right after college.

**3. Formal: spreading widely among people; in circulation.** (ходить (о слухах), распространяться)

5. Rumors were *abroad* that the plant would close.
6. There is a new mood of anger *abroad* in the country.

### overseas

**Pronunciation:** /ˌoʊvərˈsiːz/ &middot; *oh-vur-SEEZ*
**Русский:** за границей, за границу, за океаном
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *over* + *seas*
**Contrast:** No preposition after a verb: *go overseas*, not *go to overseas*. Before a noun it is an adjective (*overseas markets*). Americans use it for almost any trip abroad except to Canada or Mexico.

Overseas has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a foreign country, especially one across an ocean.** (за границей, за океаном)

1. My brother is serving *overseas* with the Army.
2. The company makes most of its money *overseas*.

**2. To a foreign country across an ocean.** (за границу, за океан)

3. She moved *overseas* for a job in Tokyo.
4. We ship our products *overseas* every week.

### overhead

**Pronunciation:** /ˌoʊvərˈhɛd/ &middot; *oh-vur-HED*
**Русский:** над головой, наверху
**Type:** place
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *over* + *head*
**Contrast:** The adverb is stressed on the last syllable (*planes flew overHEAD*); the adjective and noun are stressed on the first (*OVERhead bin*, *OVERhead costs*). The noun *overhead* means *накладные расходы*.

Above your head; in the sky.

1. Planes flew *overhead* all night.
2. A hawk was circling *overhead*.
3. A single bulb swung *overhead* in the basement.

### underground

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʌndərˈɡraʊnd/ &middot; *un-dur-GROWND*
**Русский:** под землёй, в подполье
**Type:** place; also direction and manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *under* + *ground*
**Contrast:** The adverb is stressed on the last syllable; the adjective on the first (*an UNderground garage*). *The Underground* is the London subway — in the US say *subway*.

Underground has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Below the surface of the ground.** (под землёй)

1. The power lines run *underground*.
2. Moles spend most of their lives *underground*.
3. The miners work almost a mile *underground*.

**2. In or into secret, hiding from the authorities.** (в подполье, на нелегальном положении)

4. The group went *underground* after it was banned.
5. He lived *underground* for years under a false name.

### downtown

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdaʊnˈtaʊn/ &middot; *down-TOWN*
**Русский:** в центре города, в центр
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *down* + *town*
**Contrast:** An American word: *downtown* is the central business district, not the lower part of town (British English says *in town* or *the city centre*). No preposition after a verb: *go downtown*, not *go to downtown* — *to downtown Chicago* uses it as a noun. In Manhattan it also means the southern part.

Downtown has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In the center or main business district of a city.** (в центре города)

1. She works *downtown* at a bank.
2. Parking *downtown* costs a fortune.

**2. To or toward the center of a city.** (в центр города)

3. Let's take the train *downtown*.
4. We drove *downtown* for dinner and a show.

### uptown

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʌpˈtaʊn/ &middot; *up-TOWN*
**Русский:** в северной части города, в жилые кварталы
**Type:** place; also direction
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** *up* + *town*
**Contrast:** An American word with no British equivalent: the part of a city away from downtown, usually residential, and in Manhattan the northern part. The adjective *uptown* can also mean *upscale* (*an uptown restaurant*). No preposition after a verb: *go uptown*.

Uptown has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In the part of a city away from the center, usually residential, or in New York the northern part.** (в северной части города, в жилом районе)

1. My grandparents live *uptown*.
2. The museum is *uptown*, near the park.

**2. To or toward that part of a city.** (в северную часть города)

3. Take the express train *uptown* to 110th Street.
4. After the show, we headed *uptown*.

### ahead

**Pronunciation:** /əˈhɛd/ &middot; *uh-HED*
**Русский:** впереди, вперёд, заранее
**Type:** place; also direction and time
**Position:** end — or after a noun: *the road ahead*
**Comparison:** not gradable — use *further*: *further ahead*
**Modifies:** verbs; also follows a noun (*the road ahead*)
**Form:** *a-* + *head*
**Contrast:** With an object it becomes *ahead of* (*ahead of me*, *ahead of time*). *Go ahead* usually means *давай, пожалуйста* (permission), not *иди вперёд*. For the direction of a single step say *forward*: *step forward*, not *step ahead*.

Ahead has 6 distinct senses.

**1. In front; further forward in space.** (впереди)

1. The road *ahead* is closed.
2. There's a gas station a mile *ahead*.
3. Keep your eyes straight *ahead*.

**2. Forward, in the direction you are facing or going.** (вперёд)

4. She ran *ahead* to get us a table.
5. The kids raced *ahead* down the trail.

**3. In or into the future.** (впереди, на будущее)

6. We have a busy week *ahead*.
7. It's time to look *ahead* and make a plan.

**4. Before the time something happens; in advance.** (заранее)

8. Call *ahead* to reserve a table.
9. If you plan *ahead*, the move will be easy.

**5. Winning, leading, or in a better position than others.** (впереди (по очкам), в выигрыше)

10. Our team was *ahead* by ten points at halftime.
11. If you buy now, you'll come out *ahead* in the long run.

**6. In *go ahead*: to start or continue doing something; used to give permission.** (давай(те), приступать, состояться)

12. "Can I ask a question?" "Sure, go *ahead*."
13. Go *ahead* and start eating — don't wait for me.
14. The concert will go *ahead* despite the rain.

### behind

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈhaɪnd/ &middot; *bih-HYND*
**Русский:** сзади, позади
**Type:** place; also direction and time
**Position:** end
