Viewpoint and domain adverbs#

From this point of view — financially, technically, legally, personally, ideally, strictly speaking.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Viewpoint adverbs narrow the frame in which a statement is true: Financially, it makes sense claims nothing about whether it makes sense in any other way. They usually open the sentence with a comma. The pattern is productive — almost any -al adjective can do it — so this group lists the frequent ones and the ones whose viewpoint sense differs from their manner sense (technically, practically, personally).

financially#

Pronunciation
/fəˈnænʃəli/ · fuh-NAN-shuh-lee
Русский
финансово, в финансовом отношении
Type
viewpoint; also manner — in terms of money
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint (Financially, it works); before an adjective (financially stable); end after a verb (support them financially)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles, verbs
Form
-ly from financial; the first syllable is also said fy-
Contrast
Russian материально помогать is help financially, not materially — English materially means significantly (it did not materially change the result).

Financially has 2 distinct senses.

1. From the point of view of money; when money is considered. (с финансовой точки зрения, в финансовом плане)

  1. Financially, the move to Texas made a lot of sense.
  2. The plan looks good financially, but it will take years.
  3. Financially, we're in much better shape than last year.

2. In a way that concerns money — having it, earning it, or giving it. (материально, финансово)

  1. My parents still support me financially.
  2. She wants to be financially independent by thirty.
  3. A lot of families are struggling financially right now.

politically#

Pronunciation
/pəˈlɪtɪkli/ · puh-LIT-ik-lee
Русский
политически, с политической точки зрения
Type
viewpoint; also manner — in terms of politics
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective or participle (politically active)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles, verbs
Form
-ly from political; the -al- is usually not pronounced: puh-LIT-ik-lee
Contrast
Politically correct is a fixed phrase: avoiding words that could offend a group, often said with irony. It does not mean correct from a political point of view. Its opposite is politically incorrect.

Politically has 2 distinct senses.

1. From a political point of view; when politics or public opinion is considered. (с политической точки зрения)

  1. Politically, raising taxes right before an election would be suicide.
  2. The plan makes sense on paper, but politically it's a nonstarter.
  3. Politically, the governor can't afford to lose the suburbs.

2. In a way connected with politics, government, or political parties. (политически)

  1. My grandmother was politically active her whole life.
  2. Police say the attack was politically motivated.
  3. It's one of the most politically divided states in the country.

technically#

Pronunciation
/ˈtɛknɪkli/ · TEK-nik-lee
Русский
формально, строго говоря; технически
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma for 'strictly speaking' (Technically, …); mid (it's technically legal); before an adjective (technically difficult)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ly from technical; the -al- is usually not pronounced: TEK-nik-lee
Contrast
The most common American sense has nothing to do with technology: Technically, he's still my boss means формально — the rules say so, though real life may differ, and a but often follows. Russian технически covers only sense 2. Don't confuse it with practically or virtually, which mean almost.

Technically has 2 distinct senses.

1. According to the exact rules or the strict meaning of words, even if not in reality. (формально, строго говоря)

  1. Technically, you're still married until the judge signs the papers.
  2. It's technically legal, but it's a terrible idea.
  3. Technically, the store closes at nine, but they let people finish shopping.

2. In terms of technology or technique — the equipment and methods involved, or the skill shown in an art, sport, or craft. (технически, с технической точки зрения, в плане техники)

  1. Technically, the new phone is a big step forward.
  2. The bridge was technically difficult to build.
  3. She's a technically brilliant pianist, but her playing feels cold.

legally#

Pronunciation
/ˈliːɡəli/ · LEE-guh-lee
Русский
юридически; законно
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective (legally binding); end after a verb (buy it legally)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles, verbs
Form
-ly from legal
Contrast
Legally blind is an American legal category — sight so poor (20/200 or worse with glasses) that a person qualifies for benefits — not total blindness. Where Russian says юридически, English says legally: legally binding is юридически обязывающий.

Legally has 2 distinct senses.

1. From the point of view of the law; as far as the law is concerned. (юридически, с юридической точки зрения)

  1. Legally, the landlord has to return your deposit within 30 days.
  2. Once you sign it, the contract is legally binding.
  3. Parents are legally responsible for getting their kids to school.

2. In a way that is allowed by law. (законно, легально)

  1. You can't legally park here after 6 p.m.
  2. You have to be 21 to buy alcohol legally.
  3. He came into the country legally.

medically#

Pronunciation
/ˈmɛdɪkli/ · MED-ik-lee
Русский
с медицинской точки зрения; медикаментозно
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective or participle (medically necessary); end after a verb (treated medically)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles, verbs
Form
-ly from medical; the -al- is usually not pronounced: MED-ik-lee
Contrast
Medically is about medicine as a field; medicinally means as a medicine (herbs used medicinally). In American health insurance, medically necessary is a fixed term for the care a plan has to pay for.

Medically has 2 distinct senses.

1. From a medical point of view; in terms of health and medicine. (с медицинской точки зрения, медицински)

  1. Medically, there's nothing wrong with him — the pain may be stress.
  2. Insurance only covers the surgery if it's medically necessary.
  3. She was medically cleared to play on Saturday.

2. By means of medical treatment, especially drugs rather than surgery. (медикаментозно, с помощью медицины)

  1. Most cases can be managed medically without an operation.
  2. The patient was put in a medically induced coma.

personally#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɜːrsənəli/ · PUR-suh-nuh-lee
Русский
лично; по-моему
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma for an opinion (Personally, I…); mid before the verb for 'yourself' (she personally called); end after take it or know someone
Comparison
not gradable — except take it very personally
Modifies
whole clauses, verbs, adjectives (personally responsible)
Form
-ly from personal; double l
Contrast
Personally is not in person: I met him in person means face to face; I know him personally means we are acquainted, not just by name. Take it personally is the set phrase for принимать на свой счёт. The opinion sense almost always goes with I or we: Personally, I think….

Personally has 4 distinct senses.

1. Used to show that you are giving your own opinion. (лично я, по-моему)

  1. Personally, I think the first movie was better.
  2. I don't care for sushi personally, but the kids love it.
  3. Personally, I'd wait until the price drops.

2. By yourself, not through someone else. (лично, сам)

  1. The mayor personally called to apologize.
  2. I'll deliver the check personally.
  3. You'll be personally responsible for the equipment.

3. As a person — in terms of character or a private relationship, not a professional or public role. (лично, как человек)

  1. I don't know the senator personally, only from the news.
  2. The two candidates dislike each other personally, not just politically.

4. As an attack on or criticism of yourself, especially in take something personally. (на свой счёт)

  1. Don't take it personally — he's rude to everyone.
  2. She took the rejection very personally.

theoretically#

Pronunciation
/ˌθiːəˈrɛtɪkli/ · thee-uh-RET-ik-lee
Русский
теоретически, в теории
Type
viewpoint
Position
any position — front with a comma; mid (you could theoretically); before an adjective (it's theoretically possible)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives, verbs
Form
-ly from theoretical; the -al- is usually not pronounced
Contrast
Like technically, sense 1 hints that reality is different: Theoretically, it's possible usually means don't count on it. The natural partner is in practice: Theoretically…, but in practice….

Theoretically has 2 distinct senses.

1. In theory — according to what should happen, though it may not happen in practice. (теоретически, в теории)

  1. Theoretically, the drive takes three hours, but not with Friday traffic.
  2. It's theoretically possible to see the northern lights from Chicago.
  3. Theoretically, anyone can apply for the job.

2. In terms of theory; from the standpoint of an academic or scientific theory. (в теоретическом плане)

  1. The paper is theoretically interesting but has very little data.
  2. Her research is theoretically grounded in behavioral economics.

statistically#

Pronunciation
/stəˈtɪstɪkli/ · stuh-TIS-tik-lee
Русский
статистически, по статистике
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective (statistically significant)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles
Form
-ly from statistical; the -al- is usually not pronounced
Contrast
Statistically significant is a technical term — unlikely to be the result of chance. It is not a synonym of large or important: a tiny difference can be statistically significant.

Statistically has 2 distinct senses.

1. According to statistics; when you look at the numbers. (по статистике, статистически)

  1. Statistically, flying is much safer than driving.
  2. Statistically, most new restaurants close within a few years.
  3. Young men are statistically more likely to get into car accidents.

2. By the methods of statistics. (статистически)

  1. The difference between the two groups was not statistically significant.
  2. The survey results were analyzed statistically.

historically#

Pronunciation
/hɪˈstɔːrɪkli/ · hih-STOR-ik-lee
Русский
исторически, традиционно
Type
viewpoint; also time
Position
any position — front with a comma for 'in the past'; mid (have historically outperformed); before an adjective (historically accurate)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, verbs, adjectives
Form
-ly from both historic and historical; the -al- is usually not pronounced
Contrast
One adverb serves two adjectives: historic (important in history) and historical (about history), so both historically important and historically accurate. In sense 1 the tense shows whether the pattern goes on: present perfect if it still does (Historically, the state has voted Democratic), past simple if it has ended (Historically, women were paid less). In American English the article is a, not an: a historically important building.

Historically has 2 distinct senses.

1. In the past, over a long period; traditionally; also compared with the past (historically low). (исторически, традиционно, издавна)

  1. Historically, this neighborhood has been home to Italian immigrants.
  2. Mortgage rates hit historically low levels during the pandemic.
  3. Historically, women were paid less for the same work.

2. In terms of history; from the point of view of a historian. (исторически, с исторической точки зрения)

  1. The movie isn't very historically accurate.
  2. It's a historically important building — the treaty was signed here.
  3. Historically, the Battle of Gettysburg was the turning point of the Civil War.

culturally#

Pronunciation
/ˈkʌltʃərəli/ · KUHL-chur-uh-lee
Русский
в культурном отношении
Type
viewpoint
Position
any position — front with a comma; before an adjective (culturally diverse)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives
Form
-ly from cultural; double l
Contrast
False friend: Russian культурно (вести себя культурно) means politely, with good manners — English politely, decently. Culturally only means in terms of culture.

Culturally has 2 distinct senses.

1. In terms of a society's customs, traditions, and beliefs. (в культурном отношении, с точки зрения культуры)

  1. Culturally, the two countries have a lot in common.
  2. Our school is culturally diverse — the kids speak twenty languages at home.
  3. In some places it isn't culturally acceptable to refuse food from a host.

2. In terms of the arts — music, theater, museums, and literature. (в плане культурной жизни)

  1. Culturally, Chicago has as much to offer as New York.
  2. The town is small but culturally rich, with two theaters and a jazz club.

economically#

Pronunciation
/ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪkli/ · ek-uh-NAHM-ik-lee
Русский
экономически; экономно
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective (economically viable); end after a verb for 'thriftily' (live economically)
Comparison
more/most — only in the 'thriftily' sense (more economically); not gradable as a viewpoint
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives and participles, verbs
Form
-ly from both economic (about the economy) and economical (saving money); also said ee-kuh-NAHM-ik-lee
Contrast
The two senses belong to two adjectives: economic growth (экономический) but an economical car (экономичный). The adverb is the same word, so context decides: economically weak is экономически слабый; use it economically is экономно.

Economically has 2 distinct senses.

1. In terms of the economy, trade, or wealth. (экономически, в экономическом отношении)

  1. Economically, the town never recovered after the plant closed.
  2. The project isn't economically viable without federal money.
  3. Kids from economically disadvantaged families get free lunch.

2. In a way that uses little money, time, or material, without waste. (экономно, бережливо)

  1. We're trying to live more economically until I find a new job.
  2. Use the paint economically — it's all we have.
  3. She writes economically, never wasting a word.

structurally#

Pronunciation
/ˈstrʌktʃərəli/ · STRUHK-chur-uh-lee
Русский
структурно; по конструкции
Type
viewpoint
Position
any position — front with a comma; before an adjective (structurally sound); end after a verb (changed structurally)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
whole clauses, adjectives, verbs
Form
-ly from structural; double l
Contrast
In engineering Russian, конструктивно (по конструкции) is structurally or in design — not constructively, which in English only means in a helpful way (constructive criticism). Structurally sound is the set phrase for a building that is safe and not about to fall down.

Structurally has 2 distinct senses.

1. In terms of how a building, bridge, or object is built and holds together. (по конструкции, в плане прочности)

  1. The inspector said the house is structurally sound.
  2. The bridge was structurally damaged in the earthquake.
  3. Structurally, the old barn is in better shape than it looks.

2. In terms of the way the parts of a system, text, or organization are arranged. (структурно, по структуре)

  1. Structurally, the novel moves between three timelines.
  2. English and German are structurally similar.
  3. The economy has changed structurally since the 1980s.

Scientifically has 2 distinct senses.

2. Using the careful, systematic methods of science. (научно, по научной методике)

  1. The drug has never been scientifically tested on children.
  2. We need to approach the problem more scientifically, one variable at a time.

Position

more/most — in the 'rightly' sense (more morally); not gradable as a viewpoint

False friend: Russian морально often means emotionally or mentally — морально тяжело is emotionally draining, морально подготовиться is prepare mentally, морально устал is mentally exhausted, and морально устаревший is outdated or obsolete. Morally has 2 distinct senses.

1. From the point of view of right and wrong. (морально, с нравственной точки зрения)

  1. Morally, what he did was wrong, even if it was legal.
  2. I think it's morally unacceptable to lie to a patient.
  3. Is the company morally responsible for the spill?

2. In a way that is right and good; by high moral standards. (нравственно, порядочно)

  1. She tried to live morally in a business full of cheats.
  2. Most people believe they behave more morally than their neighbors.

socially#

Pronunciation
/ˈsoʊʃəli/ · SOH-shuh-lee
Русский
социально; в компании; в общении
Type
viewpoint; also manner
Position
any position — front with a comma as a viewpoint; before an adjective (socially acceptable); end after a verb (see someone socially)
Comparison

Modifies

Form

Contrast
I drink socially means only at parties and dinners with friends, not alone and not much — it has nothing to do with drinking in public. Don't confuse socially with sociably (in a friendly, outgoing way).

Socially has 3 distinct senses.

1. In terms of society, social class, or what people in general accept. (социально, в социальном отношении)

  1. Smoking indoors is no longer socially acceptable.
  2. Socially, smartphones have changed how teenagers date.
  3. The program helps socially disadvantaged kids get into college.

2. In situations where people meet for pleasure, outside work or duty. (в компании, в неформальной обстановке, вне работы)

  1. We work together, but we never see each other socially.
  2. I only drink socially, maybe a beer at a barbecue.
  3. It's hard to meet people socially after you move to a new city.

3. In terms of relationships with other people and the ability to get along with them. (в общении, в плане общения)

  1. He's brilliant but socially awkward.
  2. Our daughter did fine academically but struggled socially in middle school.

In everyday English physically is about the body or the material world; for the school subject say in terms of physics. In casual American speech I physically can't is an exaggeration for I really can't.

Physically has 3 distinct senses.

1. In terms of the body rather than the mind. (физически)

  1. You need to be physically fit to join the fire department.

2. In terms of material things or actual presence, as opposed to online, on paper, or in the mind. (физически, реально, фактически)

  1. The company is registered in Delaware but physically located in Denver.
  2. Students must be physically present to take the exam.

3. According to the laws of nature; in reality at all. (физически (невозможно), никак)

  1. It's physically impossible to be in two places at once.
  2. There's no way — I physically can't finish this by Friday.

officially#

Pronunciation
/əˈfɪʃəli/ · uh-FISH-uh-lee
Русский
официально
Type
viewpoint; also manner and stance
Position
any position — mid before the verb (officially opens); front with a comma for the public version (Officially, …)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies
verbs, whole clauses, adjectives and participles
Form
-ly from official; double l
Contrast
Russian официально also means formal in style — официально одет is formally dressed, официальный тон is a formal tone. English officially only means by authority or for the record. Sense 3 (It's officially summer) is informal but very common in American speech.

Officially has 3 distinct senses.

1. By people in authority or according to the rules; for the record. (официально)

  1. The new bridge officially opens on Monday.
  2. The White House has not officially confirmed the report.
  3. You aren't officially enrolled until you pay the fee.

2. According to what is said publicly, which may not be the whole truth. (официально, по официальной версии)

  1. Officially, he resigned; everyone knows he was fired.
  2. Officially, the trip was about trade, but the senator spent most of it on the beach.

3. Informal: now definitely, beyond any doubt. (окончательно, теперь уже точно)

  1. It's ninety degrees — summer is officially here.
  2. I'm officially addicted to this show.

/aɪˈdiːəli/ · eye-DEE-uh-lee
Русский
в идеале; идеально
Type
viewpoint; also degree
Position
any position — front with a comma for 'in an ideal world' (Ideally, …); mid (you should ideally); before a participle for 'perfectly' (ideally located)
Comparison
not gradable
Modifies

Contrast
Russian идеально is usually perfectly: идеально убрано is perfectly clean, всё прошло идеально is everything went perfectly. English ideally in that sense goes only with a few participles — suited, located, placed, positioned, situated. Otherwise ideally means in an ideal world.

Ideally has 2 distinct senses.

1. In the best possible situation; if things were exactly as you want them. (в идеале, в идеальном случае)

  1. Ideally, we'd leave by six to beat the traffic.
  2. Ideally, the new hire would speak Spanish.
  3. You should ideally get seven to eight hours of sleep.

2. Perfectly; in the best possible way, used with a few participles. (идеально, прекрасно)
