# More adverbs: J–O

> Adverbs J–O beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### jarringly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɑːrɪŋli/ &middot; *JAHR-ing-lee*
**Русский:** резко, режуще
**Type:** degree — before an adjective; also manner
**Position:** before its word — usually an adjective (*jarringly different*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *jarring*

In a sudden, unpleasant way that clashes with what is around it.

1. The ending felt *jarringly* different from the rest of the movie.
2. The alarm went off *jarringly* at 5 a.m.
3. A *jarringly* bright sign stood next to the old church.

### jauntily

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɔːntəli/ &middot; *JAWN-tuh-lee*
**Русский:** бодро, беспечно, лихо
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs, especially of walking and wearing
**Form:** -ly from *jaunty*

In a cheerful, lively and confident way.

1. He walked *jauntily* down the street, whistling.
2. She wore her hat tilted *jauntily* to one side.
3. The little dog trotted *jauntily* beside its owner.

### jealously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒɛləsli/ &middot; *JEL-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** ревниво; ревностно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also before a participle (*jealously guarded*)
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *jealous*

Jealously has 2 distinct senses.

**1. With jealousy or envy.** (ревниво, завистливо)

1. He watched *jealously* as she danced with someone else.
2. The other kids looked *jealously* at his new bike.

**2. Very carefully, so as not to lose or share something.** (ревностно, бдительно)

3. The recipe is a *jealously* guarded family secret.
4. The two agencies *jealously* protect their budgets.

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs of movement
**Form:** -ly from *jerky*

With sudden, uneven starts and stops.

1. The old elevator moved *jerkily* up to the tenth floor.

### jointly

**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also mid or before a participle (*jointly owned*)

Together, shared by two or more people or groups.

**Type:** manner

**Comparison:** more/most

**Form:** -ly from *jubilant*

With great joy, especially after a success or victory.

1. Fans cheered *jubilantly* as the final whistle blew.

### judicially

**Pronunciation:** /dʒuˈdɪʃəli/ &middot; *joo-DISH-uh-lee*
**Русский:** в судебном порядке, судом
**Type:** manner — by or through a court
**Position:** end — also before a participle (*judicially approved*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs and participles
**Form:** -ly from *judicial*
**Contrast:** Not *judiciously* 'with good judgment': *judicially* is about courts and judges.

By a court or judge, or in a way that concerns the courts.

1. The settlement must be *judicially* approved before it takes effect.
2. The right to privacy has been *judicially* recognized for decades.
3. When the two sides couldn't agree, the dispute was settled *judicially*.

### judiciously

**Pronunciation:** /dʒuˈdɪʃəsli/ &middot; *joo-DISH-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** разумно, благоразумно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — also mid
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs, especially *use*, *spend*, *choose*
**Form:** -ly from *judicious*
**Contrast:** Not *judicially* 'by a court': *judiciously* means 'with good judgment'. Russian *юридически* is *legally* or *judicially*, never *judiciously*.

With good judgment and care, especially in using something limited.

1. Use the hot sauce *judiciously* — it's very strong.
2. The school spent its small budget *judiciously*.
3. She chose her words *judiciously* during the interview.

### just about

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌst əˈbaʊt/ &middot; *JUHST uh-BOWT*
**Русский:** почти, практически
**Type:** degree — almost
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, verbs, and words like *everyone*, *everything*, *all*
**Contrast:** *Just about to* + verb is different: *about to* means вот-вот, and *just* strengthens it (*The movie is just about to start*).

Almost; very nearly.

1. I'm *just about* ready — give me two minutes.
2. *Just about* everyone was there.
3. We've tried *just about* everything.

### just in case

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌst ɪn ˈkeɪs/ &middot; *JUHST in KAYS*
**Русский:** на всякий случай
**Type:** manner — as a precaution
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause
**Contrast:** At the end of a clause it is an adverb (*Take an umbrella, just in case*). Followed by a clause it is a conjunction (*Take an umbrella just in case it rains* = на случай, если). It is not *if*: *in case it rains* means as a precaution against rain, not on condition that it rains.

As a precaution, because something might happen.

1. Take an umbrella, *just in case*.
2. I made extra food *just in case*.
3. Write down my number, *just in case*.

---

### just in time

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʌst ɪn ˈtaɪm/ &middot; *just in TYM*
**Русский:** как раз вовремя, в последний момент
**Type:** time
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Contrast:** *In time* means *early enough* (*in time for dinner*); *on time* means *at the scheduled time, not late* (*the train left on time*). *Just in time* is the close call.

Only a moment before it would have been too late.

1. We got to the gate *just in time*.
2. The ambulance arrived *just in time*.
3. She finished the slides *just in time* for the meeting.

### just now

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʌst ˈnaʊ/ &middot; *juhst NOW*
**Русский:** только что; сейчас
**Type:** time
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb
**Contrast:** In American English *just now* usually means *a moment ago* and takes the simple past (*I saw him just now*, not *I have seen him just now*). For the present Americans more often say *right now*.

Just now has 2 distinct senses.

**1. A moment ago.** (только что)

1. Who was that on the phone *just now*?
2. I saw her in the hallway *just now*.
3. What did you say *just now*?

**2. At this moment; right now.** (сейчас, в данный момент)

4. I'm busy *just now* — can I call you back?
5. She's not in the office *just now*.

### just then

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʌst ˈðɛn/ &middot; *juhst THEN*
**Русский:** в этот самый момент, как раз тогда
**Type:** time — at that exact moment in a past story
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause, usually in the past tense
**Contrast:** *Just then* points to a moment in a story (в этот момент); *just now* usually means a moment ago, measured from the present (только что).

At exactly that moment, often when something unexpected happened.

1. I was about to leave, and *just then* the phone rang.
2. *Just then*, the lights went out.
3. She walked in *just then* and heard everything.

### justifiably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌstɪfaɪəbli/ &middot; *JUS-tih-fy-uh-blee*
**Русский:** обоснованно, по праву
**Type:** stance — the speaker agrees the feeling or action is reasonable
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective (*justifiably proud*); front with a comma
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *justifiable*

For a good reason; in a way that is fair and reasonable.

1. Her parents are *justifiably* proud of her.
2. Customers were *justifiably* angry about the delays.
3. *Justifiably* or not, he felt he'd been treated unfairly.

### keenly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkiːnli/ &middot; *KEEN-lee*
**Русский:** остро, живо, сильно
**Type:** degree — with *aware*, *felt*, *interested*; also manner
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or participle (*keenly aware*); end after a verb
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives, participles and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *keen*

Strongly and sharply, in feeling, awareness or interest.

1. She was *keenly* aware that everyone was watching her.
2. His absence was *keenly* felt at the reunion.
3. Fans are *keenly* awaiting the band's new album.

### kind of

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkaɪnd əv/ &middot; *KYND uhv*
**Русский:** вроде, как бы, немного
**Type:** degree — a downtoner; informal
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs, and verbs
**Form:** the noun *kind* + *of*, fused; often spelled *kinda* in casual writing
**Contrast:** It is an adverb only before a verb, adjective or adverb (*I'm kind of tired*). Before a noun it is the noun *kind* + *of* (*What kind of car?* = какая машина). *Sort of* works the same way.

Somewhat; a little; in a way.

1. I'm *kind of* tired — can we go home?
2. The movie was *kind of* boring.
3. I *kind of* hoped you'd call.

---

### kinda

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkaɪndə/ &middot; *KYN-duh*
**Русский:** как бы, вроде, довольно
**Type:** degree — an informal downtoner
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective, adverb or verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, adverbs and verbs
**Form:** spoken spelling of *kind of*
**Contrast:** *Kinda* is how *kind of* sounds in fast speech. It is fine in texts and casual writing but not in essays, reports or business email — write *kind of*, *somewhat* or *a little* there.

Informal for *kind of*: somewhat, a little.

1. I'm *kinda* tired, so I'll stay home tonight.
2. That movie was *kinda* weird.
3. I *kinda* like him, to be honest.

### kindly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkaɪndli/ &middot; *KYND-lee*
**Русский:** любезно, по-доброму; будьте добры
**Type:** manner; also a formal request marker (*Kindly reply by Friday*)
**Position:** end — in the manner sense; mid before the verb in requests and *kindly offered*
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *kind*; also an adjective (*a kindly old man*)
**Contrast:** *Kindly* is also an adjective (*a kindly face*). *Not take kindly to* means 'dislike': *He doesn't take kindly to criticism*. *Kindly* in requests sounds formal or irritated in American English — *please* is the everyday word.

Kindly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. In a kind, generous or friendly way.** (любезно, по-доброму)

1. A neighbor *kindly* offered to water our plants.
2. She smiled *kindly* at the nervous boy.
3. He doesn't take *kindly* to being told what to do.

**2. Please; used in formal or annoyed requests.** (будьте добры, прошу)

4. *Kindly* return the form by Friday.
5. Would you *kindly* stop kicking my seat?

### knowingly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnoʊɪŋli/ &middot; *NOH-ing-lee*
**Русский:** сознательно; понимающе
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid — for 'deliberately'; end in the 'understanding' sense (*smiled knowingly*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *knowing*

Knowingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Deliberately, while knowing the facts.** (сознательно, заведомо)

1. He *knowingly* sold a car with bad brakes.
2. I would never *knowingly* lie to you.
3. It is a crime to *knowingly* make a false statement to the police.

**2. In a way that shows you understand something secret or not said.** (понимающе, многозначительно)

4. She smiled *knowingly* when I mentioned his name.
5. The two friends looked at each other *knowingly*.

### lamentably

**Pronunciation:** /ləˈmɛntəbli/ &middot; *luh-MEN-tuh-blee*
**Русский:** прискорбно, плачевно
**Type:** degree — formal, before an adjective; also stance
**Position:** before its word — before an adjective or *few* (*lamentably slow*); front with a comma as a stance adverb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives, quantifiers and whole clauses
**Form:** -ly from *lamentable*

To a disappointing or regrettable degree; also, used to say a fact is regrettable.

1. Public transportation in the city is *lamentably* slow.

### large

**Pronunciation:** /lɑːrdʒ/ &middot; *LAHRJ*
**Русский:** крупно, во весь рост; на широкую ногу
**Type:** manner — only in set phrases (*loom large*, *live large*)
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** -er/-est — *larger*, *largest*
**Modifies:** a few verbs: *loom*, *figure*, *live*, *write*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *large*
**Contrast:** *Large* is almost always an adjective (*a large house*). As an adverb it lives only in set phrases; *by and large* 'generally' and *at large* 'not caught' are fixed phrases with their own meanings.

In a big, important or noticeable way; in *loom large* and *live large*.

1. The deadline loomed *large* over the whole team.
2. As the election got closer, the economy loomed *larger* in voters' minds.
3. After the lottery win, they started living *large*.

### last but not least

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlæst bət ˌnɑːt ˈliːst/ &middot; *LAST buht naht LEEST*
**Русский:** последний по порядку, но не по значению
**Type:** linking — introducing the final item in a list
**Position:** front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a whole clause or the last item in a list

Mentioned last, but just as important as the others.

1. *Last but not least*, I want to thank my parents.
2. And *last but not least*, our drummer, Kevin!
3. Bring sunscreen, water and, *last but not least*, your ticket.

### later on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌleɪtər ˈɑːn/ &middot; *lay-tur AHN*
**Русский:** позже, потом
**Type:** time — at a later point
**Position:** end — or front
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** a verb or a whole clause

At a later time; afterward.

1. I'll explain it to you *later on*.
2. *Later on*, she moved to Chicago.
3. We'll deal with that problem *later on* in the course.

### laterally

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlætərəli/ &middot; *LAT-uh-ruh-lee*
**Русский:** в сторону, вбок; по горизонтали
**Type:** direction — sideways
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of movement
**Form:** -ly from *lateral*
**Contrast:** *Move laterally* in a company means to change jobs at the same level, not to be promoted. *Think laterally* is mostly British; Americans say *think outside the box*.

Sideways; or, in a job, to a position at the same level.

1. The crab moved *laterally* across the sand.
2. Instead of a promotion, she moved *laterally* to the marketing team.
3. The quarterback threw the ball *laterally* to the running back.

### laughingly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlæfɪŋli/ &middot; *LAF-ing-lee*
**Русский:** со смехом; в насмешку
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — for 'with a laugh'; mid before a participle in the ironic sense (*laughingly called*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of speaking and naming
**Form:** -ly from *laughing*

Laughingly has 2 distinct senses.

**1. While laughing, or in a joking way.** (со смехом, смеясь)

1. "You're hopeless," she said *laughingly*.
2. He *laughingly* admitted he had no idea how to cook.

**2. Ironically; used when the name given to something is far from the truth.** (в насмешку, иронически)

3. We stayed in what was *laughingly* called a luxury hotel.
4. The closet I work in is *laughingly* referred to as an office.

### least of all

**Pronunciation:** /ˈliːst əv ˈɔːl/ &middot; *LEEST uhv AWL*
**Русский:** меньше всего, и уж точно не
**Type:** focusing — after a negative, picking out the least likely case
**Position:** after its word — after the negative statement, before the person or thing picked out
**Comparison:** not gradable

### legibly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlɛdʒəbli/ &middot; *LEJ-uh-blee*
