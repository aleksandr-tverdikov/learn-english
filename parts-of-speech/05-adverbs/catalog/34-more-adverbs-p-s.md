# More adverbs: P–S

> Adverbs P–S beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

[← The grammar of adverbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Mostly *-ly* adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (*by and large*, *on purpose*). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or *-ly* forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

---
### p.m.

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpiːˈɛm/ &middot; *pee-EM*
**Русский:** после полудня, вечера (о времени)
**Type:** time — after noon, with a clock time
**Position:** after its word — after the number
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** clock times (*5 p.m.*, *6:30 p.m.*)
**Form:** abbreviation of Latin *post meridiem*, after noon
**Contrast:** Americans use the 12-hour clock where Russian uses 19:00: say *7 p.m.* Use it only after a number: not *in the p.m.*, not *7 o'clock p.m.*, and not *7 p.m. in the evening* (the evening is already in *p.m.*). *12 p.m.* is noon, which confuses people, so say *noon* or *midnight*.

After noon — used after a time between noon and midnight.

1. The store closes at 9 *p.m.*
2. My flight lands at 6:45 *p.m.* Eastern time.
3. Office hours are 1 to 4 *p.m.*, Monday through Friday.

### painfully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪnfəli/ &middot; *PAYN-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** мучительно, до боли; болезненно
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — or end when it means with pain
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives (*painfully shy*), adverbs and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *painful*

Painfully has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Very, to an unpleasant degree — often with *slow*, *shy*, *aware* or *obvious*.** (мучительно, до боли, крайне)

1. The line at the DMV moved *painfully* slowly.
2. She was *painfully* shy as a child.
3. It was *painfully* obvious that he hadn't read the book.

**2. In a way that causes physical or emotional pain.** (больно, болезненно)

4. He fell *painfully* on his elbow.
5. Her knee was still *painfully* swollen.

### paradoxically

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpɛrəˈdɑːksɪkli/ &middot; *pair-uh-DAHK-sik-lee*
**Русский:** парадоксально, как ни странно
**Type:** stance — the opposite of what you would expect
**Position:** front — or before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable

**Type:** manner — as an aside
**Position:** front or end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of saying and whole clauses

1. She mentioned, almost *parenthetically*, that she was moving to Chicago.

### part

**Pronunciation:** /pɑːrt/ &middot; *PAHRT*
**Русский:** частично, наполовину
**Type:** degree — to some extent
**Position:** before its word — usually in pairs (*part …, part …*)
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** nouns and adjectives
**Contrast:** *Part* is usually a noun (*a part of the plan*). As an adverb it comes before a noun or adjective, most often in pairs: *part man, part wolf*. Before a single adjective, *partly* sounds more natural: *partly true*.

Partly; to some extent — often in pairs.

1. The creature was *part* man, *part* wolf.
2. The trip was *part* business, *part* vacation.
3. Her dog is *part* husky.

### part-time

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpɑːrtˈtaɪm/ &middot; *pahrt-TYM*
**Русский:** неполный рабочий день, на полставки
**Type:** duration — for only part of the usual working hours
**Position:** end — after the verb
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs (*work*, *study*, *teach*)
**Contrast:** Adverb after a verb: *She works part-time*. Adjective before a noun: *a part-time job*. The opposite is *full-time*.

For only part of the normal working or study hours.

1. She works *part-time* at a bookstore.
2. He's studying *part-time* while he raises two kids.
3. Many students work *part-time* to pay for college.

### pat

**Pronunciation:** /pæt/ &middot; *PAT*
**Русский:** назубок; не отступать (*stand pat*)
**Type:** manner — only in *down pat* and *stand pat*
**Position:** end — after *down* or *stand*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** *down* in *have something down pat*, and *stand* in *stand pat*
**Form:** flat — the same form as the adjective *pat*
**Contrast:** The adjective before a noun is critical: *a pat answer* is a quick, rehearsed reply (шаблонный, заученный ответ). The verb and noun *pat* mean похлопать, похлопывание: *pat the dog*. *Stand pat* comes from poker and is mainly American.

Pat has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Perfectly, learned by heart — in *have* or *get something down pat*.** (назубок, в совершенстве)

1. She practiced her speech until she had it down *pat*.
2. It took me a month to get the dance steps down *pat*.

**2. Without changing anything — in *stand pat*, keep a decision or position.** (не отступать, оставаться при своём)

3. The Fed is expected to stand *pat* on interest rates this month.
4. Despite the losing streak, the team stood *pat* at the trade deadline.

### patently

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪtəntli/ &middot; *PAYT-uhnt-lee*
**Русский:** явно, очевидно
**Type:** degree — obviously, usually with a negative adjective
**Position:** before its word
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** adjectives (*patently false*, *patently absurd*)
**Form:** -ly from *patent* in the sense obvious

Very clearly; in a way nobody can doubt.

1. That claim is *patently* false.
2. It was *patently* obvious that she didn't want to go.
3. The rule is *patently* unfair to part-time workers.

### pathetically

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈθɛtɪkli/ &middot; *puh-THET-ik-lee*
**Русский:** жалко, до смешного; жалобно
**Type:** degree; also manner
**Position:** before its word — or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** adjectives (*pathetically small*) and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *pathetic*
**Contrast:** False friend: *pathetically* is not патетически (with pathos, grandly). It means жалко or жалобно, and often shows contempt: *pathetically low pay*. For патетически use *with great emotion* or *grandly*.

Pathetically has 2 distinct senses.

**1. So badly or so little that it deserves pity or scorn.** (жалко, до смешного, позорно)

1. The pay is *pathetically* low.
2. Our team played *pathetically* in the second half.
3. He was *pathetically* grateful for any attention.

**2. In a sad way that makes people feel sorry for you.** (жалобно, жалостно)

4. The puppy whimpered *pathetically* outside the door.
5. She looked up at me *pathetically* and asked for help.

### peaceably

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpiːsəbli/ &middot; *PEE-suh-blee*
**Русский:** мирно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs (*assemble*, *settle*, *live*)
**Form:** -ly from *peaceable*
**Contrast:** *Peaceably* stresses behaving without fighting and sounds formal or legal (the First Amendment's *peaceably to assemble*). *Peacefully* is the everyday word and also means calmly, quietly: *sleeping peacefully*, never *sleeping peaceably*.

Without fighting or causing trouble.

1. The protesters gathered *peaceably* outside city hall.
2. The two sides settled the dispute *peaceably*.
3. The First Amendment protects the right of the people *peaceably* to assemble.

### peacefully

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpiːsfəli/ &middot; *PEES-fuh-lee*
**Русский:** мирно; спокойно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *peaceful*

Peacefully has 2 distinct senses.

**1. Without violence or fighting.** (мирно)

1. The protest ended *peacefully*.
2. The two countries resolved the conflict *peacefully*.

**2. Calmly and quietly.** (спокойно, тихо, мирно)

3. The baby was sleeping *peacefully* in her crib.
4. He died *peacefully* at home, surrounded by family.

### peculiarly

**Pronunciation:** /pɪˈkjuːljərli/ &middot; *pih-KYOOL-yur-lee*

**Type:** manner; also degree
**Position:** end — or before its word
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *peculiar*

Peculiarly has 2 distinct senses.

### pedantically

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈdæntɪkli/ &middot; *puh-DAN-tik-lee*
**Русский:** дотошно, занудно, педантично
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — or mid
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs and adjectives
**Form:** -ly from *pedantic*
**Contrast:** Russian педантичный is often praise (аккуратный, пунктуальный). English *pedantic* and *pedantically* are criticism: too concerned with small rules and details, showing off knowledge. For praise use *meticulously* or *carefully*.

In a way that is too concerned with small details or rules.

1. "Technically, a tomato is a fruit," he said *pedantically*.
2. She *pedantically* corrected every comma in my email.
3. Don't be so *pedantically* literal about the rules.

### per capita

**Pronunciation:** /pər ˈkæpɪtə/ &middot; *pur KAP-ih-tuh*
**Русский:** на душу населения, на человека
**Type:** manner — for each person, in statistics
**Position:** end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** verbs of spending, earning, using; numbers
**Form:** Latin *by heads*
**Contrast:** Before a noun it works as an adjective: *per capita income*. As an adverb it follows the verb or figure: *spends more per capita*.

For each person in a population.

1. The US spends more on health care *per capita* than any other country.
2. The state collects about $3,000 in taxes *per capita*.
3. Coffee consumption *per capita* is highest in Finland.

**Pronunciation:** /sɪˈnɑːnəməsli/ &middot; *sih-NAHN-uh-muhs-lee*
**Русский:** синонимично, как синонимы
**Type:** manner
**Position:** end — mostly after *used*
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** the verb *use*
**Form:** -ly from *synonymous*

With the same meaning.

1. The two terms are often used *synonymously*.
2. In this article, 'weather' and 'climate' are not used *synonymously*.
3. Many people use 'Holland' and 'the Netherlands' *synonymously*.

### synthetically

**Pronunciation:** /sɪnˈθɛtɪkli/ &middot; *sin-THET-ik-lee*
**Русский:** синтетически, искусственно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** before its word — before participles (*synthetically produced*); also end
**Comparison:** not gradable
**Modifies:** participles and verbs
**Form:** -ly from *synthetic*

By a chemical process, not naturally.

1. The vitamin can be produced *synthetically*.
2. *Synthetically* made diamonds are much cheaper.
3. The fabric is *synthetically* dyed.

### systematically

**Pronunciation:** /ˌsɪstəˈmætɪkli/ &middot; *sis-tuh-MAT-ik-lee*
**Русский:** систематически, планомерно
**Type:** manner
**Position:** mid or end
**Comparison:** more/most
**Modifies:** verbs
**Form:** -ly from *systematic*

Using an organized method, step by step.

1. The police *systematically* searched every house on the street.
2. Women were *systematically* excluded from the club.
3. We need to go through the data *systematically*.
