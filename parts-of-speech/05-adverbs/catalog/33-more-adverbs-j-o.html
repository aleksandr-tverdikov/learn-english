More adverbs: J–O#

Adverbs J–O beyond the grammar groups — what a check against WordNet’s adverb lemmas showed a learner still needs.

← The grammar of adverbs · All groups

Mostly -ly adverbs of manner, plus the multi-word adverbs (by and large, on purpose). WordNet lists far more than this. The rest were archaic, technical, British-only, or -ly forms nobody uses, and a rejection pass left them out on purpose.

Contrast
At the end of a clause it is an adverb (Take an umbrella, just in case). Followed by a clause it is a conjunction (Take an umbrella just in case it rains = на случай, если). It is not if: in case it rains means as a precaution against rain, not on condition that it rains.

As a precaution, because something might happen.

  1. Take an umbrella, just in case.
  2. I made extra food just in case.
  3. Write down my number, just in case.

In American English just now usually means a moment ago and takes the simple past (I saw him just now, not I have seen him just now). For the present Americans more often say right now.

Just now has 2 distinct senses.

2. At this moment; right now. (сейчас, в данный момент)

  1. I'm busy just now — can I call you back?
  2. She's not in the office just now.

Kindly has 2 distinct senses.

  1. Kindly return the form by Friday.
  2. Would you kindly stop kicking my seat?
