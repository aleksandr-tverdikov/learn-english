# Proper nouns, brand names, and words that lost their capital

> *Aspirin*, *escalator*, and *zipper* were once trademarks. *Kleenex* and *Xerox* still are. The capital letter is the whole grammar here.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A **proper noun** names one specific thing — *Chicago*, *Tuesday*, *Shakespeare* — and in English it is **always capitalized**. This is worth stating plainly because most European languages capitalize far less: Russian, French, and Spanish all write days, months, and nationalities in lowercase, and their speakers routinely undercapitalize in English. In English, capitalize days, months, nationalities, languages, religions, holidays, and brand names. Do **not** capitalize seasons — *spring*, *summer* — which is the trap in the other direction.

Proper nouns also behave differently grammatically: most take no article and no plural. *I live in Chicago*, never ✗ *the Chicago*, never ✗ *two Chicagos* — unless you deliberately make it a common noun: *there are three Springfields in this state*.

The interesting part of this group is the **middle ground**: words that began as registered trademarks and became ordinary nouns. This is called **genericization**, and it is a company's nightmare — a brand that becomes the word for the product loses its trademark. *Aspirin*, *escalator*, *zipper*, *heroin*, *thermos*, *granola*, and *laundromat* all lost their protection and are now written lowercase. *Kleenex*, *Xerox*, *Band-Aid*, *Velcro*, and *Google* are still protected: legally they should be capitalized and used as adjectives — *a Kleenex tissue* — though almost nobody does this in speech.

For a learner the practical rule is simple: the words below marked **generic** are ordinary lowercase nouns you can use freely. The ones marked **still a trademark** are capitalized in careful writing, and have a neutral equivalent you can use instead.

### aspirin

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæspərɪn/ &middot; *AS-pur-in*
**Русский:** аспирин
**Plural:** *aspirins* — for tablets
**Countability:** uncountable as a drug; countable as tablets
**Agreement:** singular verb — *aspirin is a blood thinner*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** **generic** in the United States; still a Bayer trademark in some countries
**Contrast:** Bayer lost the American trademark after the First World War, so *aspirin* is lowercase here. In Canada and Germany it is still *Aspirin*, capitalized. The generic chemical name is *acetylsalicylic acid*.

The common pain-relieving drug.

**1. The drug.** Uncountable. (аспирин)

1. *Aspirin* is also used as a blood thinner.
2. She takes low-dose *aspirin* daily.
3. *Aspirin* is not recommended for children.

**2. Tablets.** Countable. (таблетки аспирина)

4. Take two *aspirins* and lie down.
5. Both *aspirins* had dissolved by then.

**See also:** [heroin](#heroin) &middot; [escalator](#escalator)

### escalator

4. She fell on an *escalator* and broke her wrist.
5. The word was a trademark until 1950.
