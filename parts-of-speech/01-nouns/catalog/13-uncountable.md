# Uncountable nouns

> ✗ *an information*, ✗ *two advices*, ✗ *many furnitures*. These nouns have no plural and take no *a* — you count them with *a piece of*, *an item of*, *a bit of*.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

An uncountable noun names something English treats as a mass rather than as separate units. Three consequences follow, and every one of them produces a predictable learner error:

1. **No plural.** ✗ *informations*, ✗ *advices*, ✗ *furnitures*, ✗ *equipments*.
2. **No *a* or *an*.** ✗ *an advice*, ✗ *a homework*. Use *some*, or nothing at all.
3. **A singular verb.** *The news **is** good.* *This furniture **is** expensive.*

To count them you need a **partitive** — a separate countable noun that packages the mass: *a piece of advice*, *an item of clothing*, *a bit of luck*, *two slices of bread*. Each entry below gives the partitive that native speakers actually use, because the wrong one is as noticeable as no partitive at all: *a piece of furniture* is normal, ✗ *a slice of furniture* is not.

Two warnings. First, countability is a property of a **sense**, not of a word — many nouns here have a countable sense too, and those are cross-referenced to [dual-category nouns](14-dual-category.md). Second, countability does not transfer between languages: *information*, *advice*, and *furniture* are all countable in Russian and uncountable in English, which is exactly why they are the errors you hear most.

### information

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɪnfərˈmeɪʃən/ &middot; *in-fur-MAY-shuhn*
**Русский:** информация, сведения
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural, no *a*
**Agreement:** singular verb — *the information is out of date*
**Counted with:** *a piece of information*, *a bit of information*, *an item of information*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *informations* and ✗ *an information* are among the most frequent errors made by speakers of Russian, French, German, and Spanish, in all of which the word is countable. English has no plural for it at all.

Facts or knowledge provided about something.

**1. Facts provided or learned.** (информация)

1. This *information* is confidential.
2. We need more *information* before deciding.
3. She gave me one useful piece of *information*.
4. All the *information* is on the website.

**2. Data held by a system.** (данные, сведения)

5. The form collects personal *information*.
6. Several pieces of *information* were missing from the file.

**See also:** [advice](#advice) &middot; [knowledge](#knowledge) &middot; [data](08-latin-um-a.md#datum)

### advice

**Pronunciation:** /ədˈvaɪs/ &middot; *uhd-VYSE* — noun, with /s/
**Русский:** совет, советы
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural, no *a*
**Agreement:** singular verb — *her advice was excellent*
**Counted with:** *a piece of advice*, *a word of advice*, *some advice*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *advices* and ✗ *an advice* are wrong. Note also the noun/verb pair: the noun *advice* /ədˈvaɪs/ ends in /s/, the verb *advise* /ədˈvaɪz/ in /z/. Different spelling, different sound, constantly confused.

Guidance or recommendations about what someone should do.

**1. Recommendations about what to do.** (совет)

1. Let me give you one piece of *advice*.
2. Her *advice* was to wait a week.
3. He never takes *advice* from anyone.
4. I need some *advice* about the contract.

**2. Professional or expert guidance.** (консультация)

5. Seek legal *advice* before signing.
6. The best *advice* I ever got came from my first boss.

### furniture

**Русский:** мебель
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural, no *a*
**Agreement:** singular verb — *the furniture is being delivered*
**Counted with:** *a piece of furniture*, *an item of furniture*

The movable objects that make a room usable — chairs, tables, beds.

2. They bought one piece of *furniture* in ten years.
3. The apartment came with *furniture*.

**2. Attributive.** (мебельный)

5. There's a *furniture* store on the corner.
6. Several pieces of *furniture* were damaged in the move.

### research

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːsɜːrtʃ/ &middot; *REE-surch* — also /rɪˈsɜːrtʃ/ *rih-SURCH*
**Русский:** исследование, исследования
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural in standard use

**1. Systematic investigation.** (исследование)

1. Her *research* focuses on sleep and memory.
2. More *research* is needed.
3. The *research* was funded by the university.
4. He published two pieces of *research* that year.

**2. Looking into something informally.** (изучение вопроса)

5. I did some *research* before buying the car.
6. A little *research* would have saved them a lot of money.

### proof

**Pronunciation:** /pruːf/ &middot; *proof*
**Русский:** доказательство
**Countability:** uncountable in the main sense; countable as a printed draft
**Agreement:** singular verb — *the proof is conclusive*
**Counted with:** *a piece of proof*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** In the sense 'evidence' the word is uncountable — ✗ *two proofs that he was there*. In mathematics and printing it is countable: *a proof*, *two proofs*. Related to [evidence](#evidence) but stronger: evidence supports, proof settles.

Facts establishing that something is true.

**1. Conclusive evidence.** Uncountable. (доказательство)

1. Do you have any *proof* of that?
2. The receipt is *proof* of purchase.
3. There is no *proof* either way.

**2. A demonstration in mathematics or logic.** Countable. (доказательство теоремы)

4. The *proof* runs to eighty pages.
5. Both *proofs* rely on the same lemma.

**3. A trial print of a text or photograph.** Countable. (корректура, пробный оттиск)

6. She marked up the *proofs* in red.

**See also:** [evidence](#evidence)

### knowledge

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɑːlɪdʒ/ &middot; *NAH-lij* — the *k* is silent
**Русский:** знание, знания
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural
**Agreement:** singular verb — *his knowledge is impressive*
**Counted with:** *a body of knowledge*, *an area of knowledge*
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** takes *a* only when modified — *a good knowledge of Spanish*
**Contrast:** ✗ *knowledges* is wrong. The one place *a* appears is before a modified *knowledge of* something: *a working knowledge of French*, *a deep knowledge of the subject*. That construction is standard and worth learning as a unit.

Facts, information, and skills acquired through experience or education.

**1. What someone knows.** (знания)

1. His *knowledge* of the period is remarkable.
2. She has a working *knowledge* of three languages.
3. The job requires *knowledge* of both systems.

**2. Awareness of a fact.** (осведомлённость)

4. To my *knowledge*, nobody has complained.
5. It happened without my *knowledge*.

**3. Human understanding in general.** (познание)

6. Scientific *knowledge* advances by argument.

**See also:** [information](#information) &middot; [advice](#advice)

### money

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmʌni/ &middot; *MUN-ee*
**Русский:** деньги
**Countability:** uncountable — no plural in ordinary use
**Agreement:** singular verb — *the money is gone*
**Counted with:** *a sum of money*, *an amount of money*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *monies* (also *moneys*) exists in legal and financial writing, meaning separate sums
**Contrast:** The trap for Russian speakers is agreement: Russian *деньги* is plural, English *money* is singular. Say *the money **is** on the table*, never ✗ *the money are*. The technical plural *monies* means distinct funds, not banknotes.

A medium of exchange; wealth.

**1. Currency and wealth.** (деньги)

1. The *money* is already in your account.
2. How much *money* do you need?
3. All the *money* was spent on repairs.
4. They raised a large sum of *money*.

**2. In legal and financial use — monies.** (денежные суммы)

5. All *monies* received must be recorded separately.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. That's the best value for *money* in the store.
7. *Money* doesn't grow on trees.
