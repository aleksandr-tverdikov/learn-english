# Core vocabulary: emotions

> The everyday nouns of emotions — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### joy

**Pronunciation:** /dʒɔɪ/ &middot; *JOY*
**Русский:** радость
**Countability:** uncountable

A feeling of great happiness.

1. Her face lit up with *joy* when she saw us.
2. The whole team jumped for *joy* after the goal.
3. There is real *joy* in doing work you care about.

**Pronunciation:** /dɪˈlaɪt/ &middot; *dih-LYT*
**Русский:** восторг, удовольствие
**Countability:** uncountable

1. To my *delight*, the flight was on time.

3. He takes *delight* in proving people wrong.

**Pronunciation:** /juːˈfɔːriə/ &middot; *yoo-FOR-ee-uh*
**Русский:** эйфория

### contentment

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɛntmənt/ &middot; *kuhn-TENT-muhnt*
**Русский:** удовлетворённость, довольство
**Countability:** uncountable
**Contrast:** The related noun *content* (=содержимое) is stressed KAHN-tent, but *contentment* keeps the second-syllable stress: kuhn-TENT-muhnt.

A quiet feeling of being satisfied with things as they are.

1. He sighed with *contentment* and put his feet up.
2. There is a deep *contentment* in a slow Sunday morning.
3. Money bought him comfort but not *contentment*.
