Nouns that are countable and uncountable — with different meanings#

A hair is one strand; hair is what grows on your head. A glass is a container; glass is a material. Same word, two grammars, two meanings.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Countability in English is a property of a sense, not of a word. A great many nouns sit in both categories, and the shift is never random — it follows one of three regular patterns:

  • Material → object made of it. Glass is the substance, a glass is a drinking vessel. Paper is the material, a paper is a newspaper or an essay. Iron is the metal, an iron presses shirts.
  • Mass → a unit or instance of it. Hair is the mass on your head, a hair is one strand. Coffee is the drink, a coffee is one cup. Time is duration, a time is an occasion.
  • Abstract → a specific example. Experience is accumulated knowledge, an experience is one event. Business is commerce, a business is one company.

The practical consequence: adding or dropping a changes what you have said. ✗ I found a hair in my soup and I found hair in my soup are both grammatical, and they describe two very different soups.

hair#

Pronunciation
/hɛr/ · hair
Русский
волосы; волос
Countability
both — uncountable as a mass, countable as single strands
Agreement
singular verb for the mass — her hair is long; plural for strands — two hairs were found
Counted with
a strand of hair, a lock of hair, a head of hair
Type
concrete
Contrast
This is the textbook case. She has beautiful hair — the mass on her head. There's a hair in my food — one strand. Saying ✗ she has beautiful hairs describes something disturbing.

The threadlike growth on the head and body.

1. The mass on someone's head. Uncountable. (волосы)

  1. Her hair is longer than it used to be.
  2. He's losing his hair.
  3. She washed her hair twice.

2. A single strand. Countable. (волос)

  1. There's a hair in my soup.
  2. Two hairs were recovered from the scene.
  3. He pulled a gray hair out and threw it away.

3. In fixed expressions. (в устойчивых выражениях)

  1. Let your hair down for once.
  2. That was a hair-raising drive.
  3. She won by a hair.

glass#

Pronunciation
/ɡlæs/ · glas
Русский
стекло; стакан; очки
Countability
both — uncountable as a material, countable as a vessel
Agreement
singular for the material — the glass is toughened; plural for vessels — the glasses are clean
Counted with
a pane of glass, a sheet of glass, a piece of glass
Type
concrete
Variants
glasses also means spectacles, and in that sense is plural-only — see glasses
Contrast
Three grammars in one word. The material is uncountable; a drinking vessel is countable; and glasses meaning spectacles is a plural-only noun that takes a plural verb and no singular.

The transparent material; a drinking vessel; spectacles.

1. The material. Uncountable. (стекло)

  1. The whole wall is glass.
  2. Broken glass covered the sidewalk.
  3. The window pane is toughened glass.

2. A drinking vessel. Countable. (стакан)

  1. Pour me a glass of water.
  2. All the glasses are in the dishwasher.
  3. He knocked a glass off the table.

3. Spectacles — glasses. Plural only. (очки)

  1. I can't read without my glasses.

The material; a newspaper; a written document.

  1. He reads the paper every morning.
  2. Both local papers covered the story.

3. An academic article or essay. Countable. (статья, работа)

  1. She published three papers last year.
  2. The paper is due on Monday.

4. Official documents — papers. (документы)

  1. The border guard asked for our papers.

2. A forest. Usually the woods. (лес)

  1. They walked in the woods all afternoon.
  2. The woods behind the house are full of deer.

3. In fixed expressions. (в устойчивых выражениях)

  1. We're not out of the woods yet.
  2. Knock on wood.

both — uncountable as a substance, countable as a serving
Agreement

Contrast
Ordering two coffees is completely normal English — the countable sense means servings. But ✗ I spilled a coffee on the carpet meaning the liquid is odd; say some coffee. The same double life belongs to tea, beer, and wine.

The drink and the beans it is made from.

1. The drink or the substance. Uncountable. (кофе)

  1. The coffee has gone cold.
  2. Do you want some coffee?
  3. We're out of coffee again.

2. A serving of coffee. Countable. (чашка кофе)

  1. Two coffees, please.
  2. She's already had three coffees today.

3. Attributive. (кофейный)

  1. There's a coffee shop on every corner.

chicken#

Pronunciation
/ˈtʃɪkɪn/ · CHIK-in
Русский
курица; курятина
Countability
both — countable as the animal, uncountable as the meat
Agreement
singular for the meat — the chicken is overcooked; plural for birds — the chickens are in the yard
Counted with
a piece of chicken, a breast of chicken
Type
concrete
Contrast
This animal/meat split is one of the most systematic patterns in English. Live birds are countable; the meat is uncountable. ✗ I ate a chicken means you ate an entire bird. Compare lamb, fish, turkey, and duck, which work the same way — and cow/beef, pig/pork, sheep/mutton, where English uses different words entirely.

The bird, and its meat.

1. The living bird. Countable. (курица)

  1. They keep six chickens in the backyard.
  2. A chicken got loose in the road.

2. The meat. Uncountable. (курятина)

  1. We're having chicken for dinner.
  2. The chicken is a little dry.
  3. Add two pieces of chicken to the pan.

3. A coward. Informal, countable. (трус)

  1. Don't be such a chicken.

time#

Pronunciation
/taɪm/ · tyme
Русский
время; раз
Countability
both — uncountable as duration, countable as occasions
Agreement
singular for duration — time is short; plural for occasions — three times were recorded
Counted with
a period of time, an amount of time
Type
abstract
Contrast
The two senses take different question words. How much time? asks about duration; how many times? asks about occasions. Mixing them — ✗ how much times — is a very common error.

Duration; an occasion; a point in the day.

1. Duration. Uncountable. (время)

  1. We don't have much time.
  2. Time passes slowly here.
  3. It takes a lot of time to learn.

2. An occasion. Countable. (раз)

  1. I've seen it three times.
  2. The first time was the worst.
  3. How many times do I have to ask?

3. A period in history. Countable, often plural. (времена)

  1. In Roman times this was a garrison town.

4. A point in the day. (время суток)

  1. What time is it?

Agreement

✗ There is a room for one more person means a bedroom is available. Without the article it means space is available. The difference matters at a hotel desk.

A part of a building; also available space.

  1. The house has four rooms upstairs.
  2. She booked a room for two nights.

2. Available space. Uncountable. (место)

  1. Is there room for one more?
  2. There isn't enough room in the trunk.
  3. Make room on the table.

3. Scope or opportunity. Uncountable, figurative. (возможность, простор)

  1. There's room for improvement.

  1. It was a terrifying experience.
  2. Those early experiences shaped everything he wrote.

Sound, especially loud or unwanted sound.

1. Unwanted sound generally. Uncountable. (шум)

  1. The noise from the street is constant.
  2. There was too much noise to hear anything.
  3. Try to make less noise coming in.

2. Individual sounds. Countable. (звуки)

  1. The engine is making strange noises.
  2. I heard a noise in the kitchen.

3. In fixed expressions. (в устойчивых выражениях)

  1. He's been making noises about quitting.

  2. Strange sounds came from the basement.
