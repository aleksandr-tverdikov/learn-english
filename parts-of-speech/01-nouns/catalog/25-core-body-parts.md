# Core vocabulary: the body

> The everyday nouns of the body — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### body

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɑːdi/ &middot; *BAH-dee*
**Русский:** тело
**Plural:** *bodies*
**Countability:** countable

The whole physical structure of a person or animal.

1. Yoga taught me how my *body* actually works.
2. Cold water is a shock to the *body*.
3. Their *bodies* were still sore from Saturday's game.

### arm

**Pronunciation:** /ɑːrm/ &middot; *AHRM*
**Русский:** рука (от плеча до кисти)
**Plural:** *arms*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Russian рука covers the whole limb; English splits it — the arm ends at the wrist, and the hand takes over from there.

The limb that runs from the shoulder down to the hand.

1. He broke his *arm* falling off a ladder.
2. She carried the groceries in both *arms*.
3. Put your *arm* around her for the photo.

### leg

**Pronunciation:** /lɛɡ/ &middot; *LEG*
**Русский:** нога
**Plural:** *legs*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Russian нога covers the whole limb; the English leg stops at the ankle, and below that it is the foot.

One of the two limbs a person stands and walks on.

1. My *legs* were killing me after the hike.
2. He crossed his *legs* and leaned back.
3. She hurt her left *leg* playing soccer.

### hand

**Pronunciation:** /hænd/ &middot; *HAND*
**Русский:** рука, кисть руки
**Plural:** *hands*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Never use hand for the whole рука — you hold a bag in your hand but carry a child in your arms.

The part of the body below the wrist, with fingers and a thumb.

1. Wash your *hands* before dinner.
2. He shook my *hand* and introduced himself.
3. Her *hands* were freezing without gloves.

### finger

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɪŋɡər/ &middot; *FING-gur*
**Русский:** палец (на руке)
**Plural:** *fingers*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** English counts the thumb separately, so a hand has four fingers plus a thumb; and a палец on the foot is a toe, never a finger.

One of the four long jointed parts of the hand, not counting the thumb.

1. I cut my *finger* chopping onions.
2. She ran a *finger* down the map.
3. His *fingers* were stained with ink.

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃiːkboʊn/ &middot; *CHEEK-bohn*
**Русский:** скула
**Plural:** *cheekbones*
**Countability:** countable

Either of the two bones just below the eyes.

1. She has high *cheekbones*.
2. The punch broke his *cheekbone*.
3. Shadows fell under his *cheekbones*.

### hamstring

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhæmstrɪŋ/ &middot; *HAM-string*
**Русский:** подколенное сухожилие
**Plural:** *hamstrings*
**Countability:** countable

One of the tendons or muscles at the back of the thigh.

1. He pulled a *hamstring* in the first quarter.
2. *Hamstring* injuries take weeks to heal.
3. Stretch your *hamstrings* before you run.
