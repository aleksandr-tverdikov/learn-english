# Core vocabulary: printing and publishing

> The everyday nouns of printing and publishing — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

### printing press

**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɪntɪŋ prɛs/ &middot; *PRIN-ting PRESS*
**Русский:** печатный станок
**Plural:** *printing presses*
**Countability:** countable

A machine that presses inked type or plates onto paper to make many copies.

1. The museum has a working *printing press* from 1850.
2. Gutenberg's *printing press* changed how books were made.
3. Two of the shop's *printing presses* run all night.

### pulp

**Pronunciation:** /pʌlp/ &middot; *PULP*
**Русский:** мякоть (плода); бумажная масса, целлюлоза; бульварное чтиво

Pulp has 3 distinct senses.

**1. The soft juicy part of a fruit or vegetable.** (мякоть (плода))

1. I like orange juice with plenty of *pulp*.
2. Scoop the *pulp* out of the melon.
3. Strain the juice to get rid of the *pulp*.

**2. The soft wet mass of wood fiber that paper is made from.** (бумажная масса, целлюлоза)

4. The mill grinds the logs into *pulp*.
5. Recycled paper is mixed back into the *pulp*.
6. Wet *pulp* is pressed and dried into sheets.

**3. Cheap sensational fiction, originally printed on rough paper.** (бульварное чтиво)

7. He wrote detective *pulp* for a penny a word.
8. The store keeps a shelf of old *pulp* by the window.
9. Critics dismissed the novel as *pulp*.
