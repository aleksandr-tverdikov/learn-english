# Plurals borrowed from other languages

> *Graffito → graffiti*, *cherub → cherubim*, *bureau → bureaux*. Italian, Hebrew, and French plurals that English kept.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Latin and Greek supply most of English's foreign plurals, but not all. A smaller set came from **Italian** (*graffiti*, *tempi*, *virtuosi*, *paparazzi*), **Hebrew** (*cherubim*, *seraphim*, *kibbutzim*), and **French** (*bureaux*, *châteaux*, *tableaux*).

They share a pattern of behavior. In each case the borrowed plural is used by specialists, a regular English plural is available to everyone else, and — with the Italian ones especially — **the foreign plural has often been reinterpreted as a singular mass noun**. Almost nobody says *a graffito*; people say *graffiti* and treat it as uncountable, exactly as happened to *data*. The French plurals are the opposite case: *bureaux* and *châteaux* are spelled differently but pronounced identically to their singulars, so the distinction exists only in writing.

### graffito

**Pronunciation:** /ɡrəˈfiːtoʊ/ &middot; *gruh-FEE-toh*
**Русский:** граффити, настенная надпись
**Plural:** *graffiti* /ɡrəˈfiːti/ &middot; *gruh-FEE-tee*
**Countability:** *graffiti* is now usually uncountable
**Agreement:** usually singular — *graffiti is a problem here*
**Pattern:** Italian *-o* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the singular *graffito* survives only in archaeology and art history
**Contrast:** Strictly *graffiti* is plural and *graffito* singular. In practice English has made *graffiti* a mass noun taking a singular verb, and *graffito* sounds like a technical term. Both *graffiti is everywhere* and *the graffiti are political* can be defended; the first is far commoner.

Words or drawings scratched or sprayed on a wall.

**1. Wall writing, as a mass phenomenon.** Uncountable in ordinary use. (граффити)

1. *Graffiti* covers the whole underpass.
2. The city spends millions removing *graffiti*.
3. Political *graffiti* appeared overnight.

**2. A single inscription.** Technical. (отдельная надпись)

4. A Roman *graffito* on the wall names a gladiator.
5. The *graffiti* in the excavated house are mostly obscene.

**See also:** [tempo](#tempo) &middot; [paparazzo](#paparazzo)

### tempo

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɛmpoʊ/ &middot; *TEM-poh*
**Русский:** темп
**Plural:** *tempos* — usual &middot; *tempi* /ˈtɛmpi/ in music
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the tempi vary between movements*
**Pattern:** Italian *-o* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *tempos* in general use; *tempi* among classical musicians
**Contrast:** Both are correct. *Tempi* signals a classical-music context; using it about a basketball game would sound strange.

The speed at which music is played, or at which anything proceeds.

**1. Musical speed.** (темп)

1. The conductor took the second movement at a brisk *tempo*.
2. His *tempi* are famously fast.
3. Both *tempos* are marked in the score.

**2. The pace of any activity.** (темп, ритм)

4. The *tempo* of the negotiations picked up.
5. She controls the *tempo* of the whole game.

**See also:** [virtuoso](#virtuoso) &middot; [solo](05-o-plurals.md#solo)

### virtuoso

**Pronunciation:** /ˌvɜːrtʃuˈoʊsoʊ/ &middot; *vur-choo-OH-soh*
**Русский:** виртуоз
**Plural:** *virtuosos* — usual &middot; *virtuosi* /ˌvɜːrtʃuˈoʊsi/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the virtuosi were all under twenty*
**Pattern:** Italian *-o* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *virtuosos* is commoner in American English; *virtuosi* is used in classical criticism

Someone with outstanding technical skill, especially in music.

**1. A highly skilled performer.** (виртуоз)

1. She was a *virtuoso* by the age of twelve.
2. Three *virtuosos* shared the stage.
3. The competition attracts *virtuosi* from every continent.

**2. Attributive — displaying great skill.** (виртуозный)

4. It was a *virtuoso* performance from start to finish.
5. His closing argument was a *virtuoso* piece of work.

**See also:** [tempo](#tempo)

### paparazzo

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpɑːpəˈrɑːtsoʊ/ &middot; *pah-puh-RAHT-soh*
**Русский:** папарацци
**Plural:** *paparazzi* /ˌpɑːpəˈrɑːtsi/ &middot; *pah-puh-RAHT-see*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the paparazzi were waiting outside*
**Pattern:** Italian *-o* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the singular *paparazzo* is rare; most speakers use *paparazzi* for any number
**Contrast:** Like *graffiti*, the plural has largely swallowed the singular. Strictly one photographer is *a paparazzo*, but ✗ *a paparazzi* is now very common in speech.

A freelance photographer who pursues celebrities.

**1. The photographer.** (папарацци)

1. The *paparazzi* followed her car from the airport.
2. A single *paparazzo* waited by the stage door.
3. She has sued three *paparazzi* this year.
4. *Paparazzi* photographs sell for enormous sums.
5. The restaurant has a back entrance to avoid the *paparazzi*.

**See also:** [graffito](#graffito)

### cherub

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃɛrəb/ &middot; *CHER-uhb*
**Русский:** херувим; ангелочек
**Plural:** *cherubim* /ˈtʃɛrəbɪm/ (biblical) &middot; *cherubs* (art, children)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the cherubim guard the ark*
**Pattern:** Hebrew *-im* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *cherubim* (angels of scripture) &middot; *cherubs* (chubby winged children in art, or a sweet-looking child)
**Contrast:** ✗ *Cherubims* is a double plural — the *-im* is already the plural ending — though it appears in older translations of the Bible. The two plurals divide by register: solemn *cherubim*, everyday *cherubs*.

An angelic being of the second order; in art, a chubby winged child.

**1. A biblical angel.** Plural *cherubim*. (херувим)

1. Two *cherubim* were carved above the ark.
2. The *cherubim* stand between heaven and earth.

**2. A winged child in painting or sculpture.** Plural *cherubs*. (путти, ангелочек)

3. The ceiling is covered in gold *cherubs*.
4. A stone *cherub* sits on each gatepost.

**3. A sweet-faced child.** Affectionate. (ангелочек)

5. She was a perfect *cherub* until she turned three.

**See also:** [seraph](#seraph) &middot; [kibbutz](#kibbutz)

### seraph

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɛrəf/ &middot; *SER-uhf*
**Русский:** серафим
**Plural:** *seraphim* /ˈsɛrəfɪm/ — also *seraphs*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the seraphim sang*
**Pattern:** Hebrew *-im* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *seraphim* in scripture and poetry; *seraphs* in general use
**Contrast:** ✗ *Seraphims* is a double plural. The adjective is *seraphic*, meaning blissfully serene.

An angelic being of the highest order.

**1. The angel.** (серафим)

1. The *seraphim* have six wings each.
2. A single *seraph* stood before the throne.
3. Painters place *seraphs* at the top of the composition.
4. The hymn describes *seraphim* covering their faces.
5. Her expression was almost *seraphic*.

**See also:** [cherub](#cherub)

### kibbutz

**Pronunciation:** /kɪˈbʊts/ &middot; *kih-BUUTS*
**Русский:** кибуц
**Plural:** *kibbutzim* /ˌkɪbʊtˈsiːm/ &middot; *kib-uut-SEEM* — also *kibbutzes*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the kibbutzim were founded in the 1920s*
**Pattern:** Hebrew *-im* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *kibbutzim* is standard; *kibbutzes* occurs in casual English
**Contrast:** Note the stress shift: *kih-BUUTS* in the singular, *kib-uut-SEEM* in the plural, with the stress on the ending.

A collective community in Israel, traditionally agricultural.

**1. The community.** (кибуц)

1. He grew up on a *kibbutz* in the north.
2. Most *kibbutzim* have privatized since the 1990s.
3. The *kibbutz* runs a dairy and a guest house.
4. Several *kibbutzim* were founded by the same group.
5. Volunteers came from Europe to work on the *kibbutz*.

**See also:** [cherub](#cherub)

### bureau

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbjʊroʊ/ &middot; *BYUUR-oh*
**Русский:** бюро; комод
**Plural:** *bureaus* — usual in American English &middot; *bureaux* /ˈbjʊroʊz/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both bureaus reported the same figures*
**Pattern:** French *-x* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *bureaus* is standard American; *bureaux* is chiefly British
**Contrast:** The two plurals are pronounced identically — /ˈbjʊroʊz/ — so the choice is purely a spelling one. American style guides prefer *bureaus*.

A government department or news office; also a chest of drawers.

**1. An office or agency.** (бюро, ведомство)

1. The Federal *Bureau* of Investigation opened a case.
2. Both news *bureaus* were closed to save money.
3. She runs the paper's Tokyo *bureau*.

**2. A chest of drawers.** American usage. (комод)

4. His keys were on top of the *bureau*.
5. They bought two matching *bureaus*.

**See also:** [plateau](#plateau) &middot; [tableau](#tableau)

### plateau

**Pronunciation:** /plæˈtoʊ/ &middot; *pla-TOH*
**Русский:** плато; плато (в развитии)
**Plural:** *plateaus* — usual &middot; *plateaux* /plæˈtoʊz/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the plateaus rise sharply*
**Pattern:** French *-x* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *plateaus* is standard American; *plateaux* is chiefly British

An area of level high ground; a period of no change after growth.

**1. A flat elevated landform.** (плато)

1. The road climbs onto a high *plateau*.
2. These *plateaus* were carved by ancient rivers.
3. The *plateau* stretches for two hundred miles.

**2. A period of no further progress.** Figurative and very common. (плато, застой)

4. Sales have hit a *plateau*.
5. Learners often reach a *plateau* after the first year.
6. Both *plateaus* lasted about six months.

**See also:** [bureau](#bureau)

### tableau

**Pronunciation:** /tæˈbloʊ/ &middot; *ta-BLOH*
**Русский:** живая картина; сцена
**Plural:** *tableaux* /tæˈbloʊz/ — also *tableaus*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the tableaux were staged in silence*
**Pattern:** French *-x* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *tableaux* is commoner than *tableaus*, unlike *bureaus* and *plateaus*
**Contrast:** This is the one French plural in the group where the *-x* spelling still dominates in American English.

A striking arrangement of people or objects, as if in a picture.

**1. A staged scene of motionless figures.** (живая картина)

1. The pageant ended with a *tableau* of the founding.
2. Three *tableaux* were performed without a word.

**2. Any vivid scene.** Figurative. (сцена, картина)

3. The kitchen was a *tableau* of total disaster.
4. He described a *tableau* of families waiting in the rain.
5. Both *tableaux* stayed with me for years.

**See also:** [bureau](#bureau) &middot; [plateau](#plateau)

### schema

**Pronunciation:** /ˈskiːmə/ &middot; *SKEE-muh*
**Русский:** схема
**Plural:** *schemas* — usual &middot; *schemata* /ˈskiːmətə/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the schemas were merged*
**Pattern:** Greek *-ma* → *-mata* &middot; **Group:** 12
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *schemas* in computing and psychology; *schemata* in philosophy and formal scholarship
**Contrast:** *Schemas* is now overwhelmingly the commoner plural, especially in databases, where *database schemas* is the fixed phrase.

An outline, plan, or underlying structure.

**1. A structural plan.** Computing. (схема)

1. The database *schema* changed in version three.
2. Both *schemas* need to be migrated.
3. He validates the file against a JSON *schema*.

**2. A mental framework.** Psychology. (когнитивная схема)

4. Children build *schemas* to organize experience.
5. The *schemata* proposed by Kant are not empirical.

**See also:** [matrix](11-ex-ix-ices.md#matrix)
