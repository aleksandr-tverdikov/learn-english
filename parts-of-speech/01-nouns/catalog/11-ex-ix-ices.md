# *-ex* and *-ix* → *-ices*

> *Index → indices*, *matrix → matrices*. The technical plural and the everyday plural often coexist — and mean slightly different things.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Latin nouns ending in *-ex* or *-ix* formed their plural in *-ices*, pronounced /-ɪsiːz/ — *indices* is *IN-duh-seez*, *matrices* is *MAY-truh-seez*. Note that the stress moves back to the first syllable and the vowel of the ending changes.

Most of these words also have a perfectly good regular plural, and English has put the two to work on **different meanings**. *Indexes* are the lists at the back of books; *indices* are subscripts and economic measures. *Appendixes* are the organ; *appendices* are the sections at the end of a report. Where such a split exists, this file notes it — using the wrong one is not ungrammatical, but it reads as slightly off to anyone who knows the distinction.

---

### index

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪndɛks/ &middot; *IN-deks*
**Русский:** указатель; индекс
**Plural:** *indexes* (books) &middot; *indices* /ˈɪndəsiːz/ (mathematics, economics)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the indices have risen*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ex* → *-ices*, split by sense &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *indexes* (alphabetical lists) &middot; *indices* (numerical measures, subscripts)
**Contrast:** The split is real and worth keeping. The list at the back of a book has *indexes*; stock markets and mathematics have *indices*. Both are correct in either sense, but the convention is well established.

An alphabetical list of topics; also a number measuring something over time.

**1. An alphabetical list in a book.** Plural *indexes*. (указатель)

1. Look it up in the *index*.
2. Both *indexes* run to forty pages.
3. The book has no *index*, which is unforgivable.

**2. A measure or ratio.** Plural *indices*. (индекс)

4. The consumer price *index* rose again.
5. All the major *indices* closed lower.
6. Several economic *indices* point to a slowdown.

**3. In mathematics — a subscript or exponent.** Plural *indices*. (показатель, индекс)

7. The *indices* run from one to n.

**4. index finger.** (указательный палец)

8. She tapped the map with her *index* finger.

**See also:** [appendix](#appendix) &middot; [matrix](#matrix)

### appendix

**Pronunciation:** /əˈpɛndɪks/ &middot; *uh-PEN-diks*
**Русский:** приложение; аппендикс
**Plural:** *appendices* /əˈpɛndɪsiːz/ (documents) &middot; *appendixes* (the organ)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the appendices follow the bibliography*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ix* → *-ices*, split by sense &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *appendices* (sections of a document) &middot; *appendixes* (body parts)
**Contrast:** Documents get *appendices*; surgeons remove *appendixes*. The distinction is conventional rather than absolute, but it is consistently observed in both publishing and medicine.

A section added at the end of a document; also the small organ attached to the intestine.

**1. A supplementary section of a document.** Plural *appendices*. (приложение)

1. The data is in *Appendix* C.
2. Three *appendices* follow the main report.
3. Full transcripts appear in the *appendices*.

**2. The organ.** Plural *appendixes*. (аппендикс)

4. His *appendix* burst on a Sunday night.
5. The surgeon has removed hundreds of *appendixes*.

**See also:** [index](#index) &middot; [addendum](08-latin-um-a.md#addendum)

### matrix

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪtrɪks/ &middot; *MAY-triks*
**Русский:** матрица
**Plural:** *matrices* /ˈmeɪtrɪsiːz/ &middot; *MAY-truh-seez* — also *matrixes*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the matrices are invertible*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ix* → *-ices* &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *matrices* in mathematics and science; *matrixes* occasionally in general use
**Contrast:** In mathematics *matrices* is effectively obligatory — ✗ *matrixes* will look wrong in any technical context.

A rectangular array of numbers; a surrounding medium or structure in which something develops.

**1. In mathematics.** (матрица)

1. Multiply the two *matrices* in that order.
2. The *matrix* has three rows and four columns.
3. Not all *matrices* can be inverted.

**2. A grid used for organizing information.** Business use. (матрица, таблица)

4. We built a decision *matrix* to compare vendors.
5. Both risk *matrices* used the same scale.

**3. A surrounding structure in which something forms.** Geology, biology. (среда, основа)

6. The crystals grew in a *matrix* of soft clay.

**See also:** [index](#index) &middot; [vertex](#vertex)

### vertex

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɜːrtɛks/ &middot; *VUR-teks*
**Русский:** вершина (угла, фигуры)
**Plural:** *vertices* /ˈvɜːrtɪsiːz/ &middot; *VUR-tuh-seez* — also *vertexes*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the vertices are connected by edges*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ex* → *-ices* &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *vertices* in mathematics and computer graphics
**Contrast:** Do not confuse with *vortex*, a whirling mass — the two differ by one letter and mean entirely different things.

A point where lines or edges meet; the highest point of a shape.

**1. A corner point in geometry.** (вершина)

1. A triangle has three *vertices*.
2. Label each *vertex* with a letter.
3. The cube's eight *vertices* are equally spaced.

**2. A node in a graph or 3-D model.** (узел, вершина графа)

4. The model contains two million *vertices*.
5. Each *vertex* stores a position and a color.

**See also:** [apex](#apex) &middot; [vortex](#vortex)

### apex

**Pronunciation:** /ˈeɪpɛks/ &middot; *AY-peks*
**Русский:** вершина, верхушка
**Plural:** *apexes* — usual &middot; *apices* /ˈeɪpɪsiːz/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the apexes align*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ex* → *-ices*, now mostly regular &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *apexes* is standard in American English; *apices* is technical and rare

The highest point of something; the peak of a career or process.

**1. The highest point.** (вершина)

1. The *apex* of the roof is forty feet up.
2. Both *apexes* meet at the ridge line.
3. The trail follows the *apex* of the ridge.

**2. The peak of a career or development.** Figurative. (пик, расцвет)

4. She was at the *apex* of her career.
5. The empire reached its *apex* in the third century.

**3. In driving and racing.** (апекс поворота)

6. Clip the *apex* of the corner and accelerate out.

**See also:** [vertex](#vertex)

### vortex

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɔːrtɛks/ &middot; *VOR-teks*
**Русский:** вихрь, водоворот
**Plural:** *vortices* /ˈvɔːrtɪsiːz/ &middot; *VOR-tuh-seez* — also *vortexes*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the vortices form behind the wing*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ex* → *-ices* &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *vortices* in physics and meteorology; *vortexes* in general use
**Contrast:** Not to be confused with *vertex*, a corner point. The weather term *polar vortex* has made this word familiar to every American who watches a winter forecast.

A whirling mass of fluid or air; figuratively, something that draws things in.

**1. A whirling mass.** (вихрь, водоворот)

1. A *vortex* formed as the water drained.
2. *Vortices* trail from the wingtips.
3. The polar *vortex* pushed cold air south.

**2. Something that pulls people in.** Figurative. (водоворот)

4. He was caught in a *vortex* of debt and litigation.
5. The scandal became a *vortex* that swallowed three careers.

**See also:** [vertex](#vertex)

### codex

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkoʊdɛks/ &middot; *KOH-deks*
**Русский:** кодекс, рукописная книга
**Plural:** *codices* /ˈkoʊdɪsiːz/ &middot; *KOH-duh-seez* — also *codexes*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the codices are kept in the vault*
**Pattern:** Latin *-ex* → *-ices* &middot; **Group:** 11
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *codices* is standard in scholarship
**Contrast:** A *codex* is a book with pages, as opposed to a scroll — the format that replaced the scroll in late antiquity.

An ancient manuscript in book form.

**1. The manuscript.** (кодекс, древняя книга)

1. The library holds a fourth-century *codex*.
2. Several Maya *codices* survived the burnings.
3. Each *codex* was copied by hand.
4. The *codex* replaced the scroll by the fourth century.
5. Two *codices* were reunited after five hundred years.

**See also:** [matrix](#matrix)
