Latin -um → -a#

Datum → data, medium → media. The plural often ends up feeling like a singular — which is why data is and media is are now normal American English.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Latin neuter nouns in -um took -a in the plural, and English borrowed many of them for scholarly and technical use. What makes this group interesting is not the ending but what happened afterward: because the -a form does not look plural to an English speaker, several of these plurals have quietly turned into singular mass nouns.

Data is the clearest case. In scientific writing it still takes a plural verb — the data are inconsistent — but in ordinary American English the data is inconsistent is now standard and unremarkable. Media has gone further: the media is biased is normal, and mediums has split off as a separate plural for fortune-tellers. Agenda has completed the journey — it is now a singular noun with the regular plural agendas, and its Latin singular agendum has vanished.

Meanwhile many -um words never took the Latin plural at all: album, stadium, museum, forum, and asylum are all perfectly regular.

Variants

Contrast
The singular datum is now rare outside surveying and statistics; almost nobody says one datum. For the plural, both verbs are defensible: journals still require the data are, while everyday and business English overwhelmingly says the data is. Neither is an error, but know your audience.

Facts and figures collected for reference or analysis.

1. Information collected for analysis. Usually treated as a mass noun. (данные)

  1. The data is stored on three separate servers.
  2. All the data was lost in the crash.
  3. We need more data before deciding.

2. In scientific writing — a plural. (данные, мн. ч.)

  1. The data are consistent with the hypothesis.
  2. These data were collected over six years.

3. A single fact — datum. Rare, technical. (единица данных; репер)

  1. Each datum is timestamped at collection.
  2. Elevations are measured from a fixed datum.

Contrast
The plural depends entirely on the sense. Channels of communication and artistic materials take media; people who claim to contact the dead, and shirt sizes, take mediums. When media means the press, American English treats it as singular: the media is covering it closely.

A means of communication or expression; a substance something exists in; also a person who claims to contact spirits.

1. A channel of communication. Plural media, often treated as a singular mass noun. (средства массовой информации)

  1. Television is still a powerful medium.
  2. The media has moved on to the next story.
  3. Social media changed how the campaign was run.

2. A material an artist works in. (материал, техника)

  1. Oil is her preferred medium.
  2. He works across several media.

3. A substance in which something grows or travels. Science. (среда)

  1. The bacteria were grown in a nutrient medium.
  2. Sound needs a medium to travel through.

4. A person claiming to contact the dead. Plural mediums. (медиум)

  1. Two mediums were hired for the show.

5. A middle size or degree. (средний размер)

  1. I'll take the medium in blue.

  1. The imperial fora lie north of the Colosseum.
