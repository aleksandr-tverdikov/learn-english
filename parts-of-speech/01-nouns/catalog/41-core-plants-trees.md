# Core vocabulary: plants and trees

> The everyday nouns of plants and trees — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### plant

**Pronunciation:** /plænt/ &middot; *PLANT*
**Русский:** растение
**Plural:** *plants*
**Countability:** countable

A living thing that grows in soil and usually has leaves and roots.

1. I killed every *plant* I owned until somebody gave me a pothos.
2. She waters the *plants* on Sunday mornings.
3. That *plant* needs a lot more light than it's getting.

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlaɪlæk/ &middot; *LY-lak*
**Русский:** сирень
**Plural:** *lilacs*
**Countability:** countable

A bush with clusters of small purple or white flowers and a strong scent.

1. The *lilac* by the back door blooms for about two weeks.
2. You can smell the *lilacs* from the street.
3. She cut an armful of *lilac* for the table.

### lavender

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlævəndər/ &middot; *LAV-uhn-dur*
**Русский:** лаванда
**Countability:** uncountable

A low bush with narrow gray-green leaves and fragrant purple flowers.

1. There's *lavender* planted all along the walk.
2. She dries *lavender* and puts it in the drawers.
3. Bees are all over the *lavender* in July.

### dandelion

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdændəlaɪrn/ &middot; *DAN-duh-ly-uhn*
**Русский:** одуванчик
**Plural:** *dandelions*
**Countability:** countable

A common weed with a yellow flower that turns into a ball of white seeds.

1. The whole yard is *dandelions* by the middle of May.
2. He blew the seeds off a *dandelion*.
3. You can eat *dandelion* greens in a salad.

### clover

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkloʊvər/ &middot; *KLOH-vur*
**Русский:** клевер
**Countability:** uncountable
**Contrast:** Don't confuse *clover* (the plant) with *clove* (the spice, or a piece of garlic).

A low plant with three round leaves on each stem, common in lawns.

1. There's more *clover* in the lawn than grass.
2. She spent an hour looking for a four-leaf *clover*.
3. Bees work the *clover* all afternoon.

### wildflower

**Русский:** полевой цветок
**Plural:** *wildflowers*
**Countability:** countable

### chrysanthemum

**Pronunciation:** /krɪˈsænθəməm/ &middot; *krih-SAN-thuh-muhm*
**Русский:** хризантема
**Plural:** *chrysanthemums*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** The ch is pronounced like k, and the stress falls on the second syllable: krih-SAN-thuh-muhm.

A round full flower that blooms in the fall.

1. Every porch on the block has *chrysanthemums* in October.
2. She bought one *chrysanthemum* in a pot.
3. The *chrysanthemums* held up through the first frost.

### bouquet

**Pronunciation:** /boʊˈkeɪ/ &middot; *boh-KAY*
**Русский:** букет
**Plural:** *bouquets*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** The t is silent and the stress is on the second syllable: boh-KAY.

A bunch of flowers arranged and tied together.

1. She caught the *bouquet* at the wedding.
2. He showed up with a *bouquet* and an apology.
3. They deliver *bouquets* the same day.

### wreath

**Pronunciation:** /riːθ/ &middot; *REETH*
**Русский:** венок
**Plural:** *wreaths*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** The w is silent: REETH. The plural is voiced, REETHZ.

A ring of leaves or flowers used as a decoration.

1. There's a *wreath* on every door on the street.
2. She made the *wreath* out of pine and holly.
3. The *wreaths* go up right after Thanksgiving.
