# Nouns that come in gender pairs

> *Actor/actress*, *waiter/waitress*, *widow/widower*. Some pairs are alive, some are dying, and using a dying one marks you as out of date.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

English does not have grammatical gender the way Russian, German, or Spanish do — an English noun is not masculine or feminine, and no article or adjective agrees with it. What English has instead is a set of **lexical pairs**: separate words for a male and a female person in the same role.

The interesting fact about this group is that **it is shrinking, and fast.** Three things are happening at once:

**Pairs that survive** name relationships and titles where sex is the point: *husband/wife*, *son/daughter*, *king/queen*, *uncle/aunt*, *widow/widower*. Nobody objects to these and nobody is replacing them.

**Pairs that are collapsing** name jobs. The *-ess* form is dropping out and the unmarked form is taking over for everyone: *actor* now covers all actors, *server* has largely replaced *waiter/waitress*, *flight attendant* has completely replaced *steward/stewardess*, and *poetess* and *authoress* are dead. A woman in these jobs is normally called by the plain form.

**Compounds in *-man* that are being replaced** by neutral coinages: *chairman → chair* or *chairperson*, *spokesman → spokesperson*, *policeman → police officer*, *fireman → firefighter*, *salesman → salesperson*, *mailman → mail carrier*. In American professional writing the neutral form is now the default, and the *-man* form reads as dated or as deliberately excluding.

The practical rule for a learner: **when in doubt, use the unmarked form for anyone.** *Actor*, *server*, *chair*, *police officer* are safe for any person. The *-ess* forms below are given so you can recognize them, with a note on whether they are still usable.

### actor

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæktər/ &middot; *AK-tur*
**Русский:** актёр, актриса
**Plural:** *actors* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the actors were rehearsing*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *actress* /ˈæktrɪs/ is still used, especially in award categories
**Contrast:** *Actor* now covers performers of any sex, and most performers prefer it. *Actress* survives mainly because award shows keep separate categories. Neither is wrong, but *actor* is the safe default.

Someone who performs in plays or films.

**1. A performer of any sex.** (актёр, актриса)

1. She is one of the finest *actors* of her generation.
2. Both *actors* trained at the same school.
3. The *actors* were still rehearsing at midnight.

**2. The marked feminine form.** (актриса)

4. She won Best *Actress* that year.
5. Many *actresses* now ask to be called actors.

**See also:** [waiter](#waiter) &middot; [host](#host)

### waiter

**Pronunciation:** /ˈweɪtər/ &middot; *WAY-tur*
**Русский:** официант, официантка
**Plural:** *waiters* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the waiters were overwhelmed*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *waitress* /ˈweɪtrɪs/ is still common; *server* is the neutral American term
**Contrast:** In American restaurants *server* has largely replaced both. *Waitress* is not offensive and is still widely used, but *server* is what most restaurants say now.

Someone who serves food in a restaurant.

**1. The neutral or male form.** (официант)

1. Ask the *waiter* for the check.
2. Both *waiters* were run off their feet.
3. The *servers* here work for tips. *(neutral)*

**2. The marked feminine form.** (официантка)

4. Our *waitress* recommended the special.
5. Two *waitresses* covered the whole patio.

**See also:** [actor](#actor) &middot; [host](#host)

### host

**Pronunciation:** /hoʊst/ &middot; *hohst*
**Русский:** хозяин, хозяйка; ведущий
**Plural:** *hosts* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the hosts greeted everyone*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *hostess* /ˈhoʊstɪs/ survives in restaurants and on airlines
**Contrast:** *Host* is neutral and covers everyone. *Hostess* is still used for the person who seats guests in a restaurant, and in *air hostess*, which is itself being replaced by *flight attendant*.

Someone who receives guests, or presents a program.

**1. The neutral form.** (хозяин; ведущий)

1. Our *host* met us at the door.
2. Both *hosts* of the show were replaced.
3. The *hosts* had prepared far too much food.

**2. The marked feminine form.** (хозяйка; хостес)

4. The *hostess* showed us to a table.
5. Two *hostesses* worked the front of house.

**See also:** [waiter](#waiter) &middot; [steward](#steward)

### steward

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstuːərd/ &middot; *STOO-urd*
**Русский:** стюард, стюардесса
**Plural:** *stewards* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the stewards were trained in first aid*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *stewardess* /ˈstuːərdɪs/ is now dated; *flight attendant* has replaced both
**Contrast:** This pair has been almost completely replaced in aviation by **flight attendant**, which is the term airlines and staff use. *Stewardess* now sounds distinctly mid-century.

Someone who looks after passengers; also a manager of property.

**1. The aviation sense.** Now replaced. (стюард)

1. The *flight attendants* prepared the cabin. *(current term)*
2. Airlines stopped saying *stewardess* decades ago.
3. My grandmother was a *stewardess* in the 1960s.

**2. A manager or caretaker.** Still current. (управляющий, распорядитель)

4. He is a careful *steward* of the endowment.
5. Union *stewards* handle member complaints.

**See also:** [host](#host) &middot; [waiter](#waiter)

### widow

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwɪdoʊ/ &middot; *WID-oh*
**Русский:** вдова
**Plural:** *widows* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the widows received pensions*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the male counterpart is *widower* /ˈwɪdoʊər/
**Contrast:** This pair is fully alive and unmarked in either direction. Unusually, the **male** form is the derived one: *widower* is built from *widow*, not the other way around.

A woman whose spouse has died.

**1. The bereaved woman.** (вдова)

1. She was left a *widow* at thirty-four.
2. Both *widows* received the same pension.
3. War *widows* were granted special status.

**2. The male counterpart.** (вдовец)

4. He has been a *widower* for six years.
5. Two *widowers* in the building married each other's neighbors.

**See also:** [bride](#bride)

### bride

**Pronunciation:** /braɪd/ &middot; *bryd*
**Русский:** невеста
**Plural:** *brides* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the brides posed together*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the male counterpart is *groom* or *bridegroom*
**Contrast:** Fully alive. Note that *groom* is a shortening of *bridegroom*, which is itself built on *bride* — the same asymmetry as *widow/widower*. *Groom* also separately means someone who cares for horses.

A woman on her wedding day.

**1. The woman marrying.** (невеста)

1. The *bride* arrived twenty minutes late.
2. Both *brides* wore their mothers' dresses.
3. The *brides* posed together for a photograph.

**2. The male counterpart.** (жених)

4. The *groom* forgot the rings.
5. Both *bridegrooms* gave short speeches.

**See also:** [widow](#widow) &middot; [husband](#husband)

### husband

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhʌzbənd/ &middot; *HUZ-buhnd*
**Русский:** муж
**Plural:** *husbands* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *their husbands stayed home*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *wife*, plural *wives* — see [wife](04-f-to-ves.md#wife)
**Contrast:** Fully alive, and the neutral cover term is *spouse* or *partner*, which many couples now prefer. Note the irregular plural on the other half of the pair: *wife → wives*.

A married man, considered in relation to his spouse.

**1. The married man.** (муж)

1. Her *husband* teaches at the college.
2. Both *husbands* stayed home with the children.
3. He has been a good *husband* to her.

**2. The neutral cover term.** (супруг, партнёр)

4. Each employee may add one *spouse* to the plan.
5. Both *partners* signed the lease.

**See also:** [wife](04-f-to-ves.md#wife) &middot; [bride](#bride)

### king

**Pronunciation:** /kɪŋ/ &middot; *king*
**Русский:** король
**Plural:** *kings* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the kings met at the border*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *queen*
**Contrast:** Fully alive. Note the asymmetry in meaning: a *queen* may be a ruler in her own right **or** a king's wife, while a *king* is always a ruler. The wife of a reigning queen is a *prince consort*, not a king.

A male monarch.

**1. The monarch.** (король)

1. The *king* abdicated in 1936.
2. Both *kings* claimed the same territory.
3. Three *kings* ruled in a single decade.

**2. The female counterpart.** (королева)

4. The *queen* reigned for seventy years.
5. Two *queens* ruled in their own right.

**See also:** [prince](#prince) &middot; [emperor](#emperor)

### prince

**Pronunciation:** /prɪns/ &middot; *prins*
**Русский:** принц
**Plural:** *princes* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the princes were educated abroad*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *princess* /ˈprɪnsɛs/
**Contrast:** A live pair, since it names a hereditary title where sex is part of the definition. Unlike *actress*, *princess* is not going anywhere.

The son of a monarch, or a ruler of a small state.

**1. The male title.** (принц)

1. The *prince* was educated abroad.
2. Both *princes* renounced their claims.
3. Three *princes* attended the funeral.

**2. The female counterpart.** (принцесса)

4. The *princess* opened the new hospital.
5. Both *princesses* married commoners.

### duke

**Pronunciation:** /duːk/ &middot; *dook*
**Русский:** герцог
**Plural:** *dukes* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the dukes held vast estates*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *duchess* /ˈdʌtʃɪs/
**Contrast:** Note that the feminine form changes the stem, not just the ending: *duke → duchess*, not ✗ *dukess*. A *duchess* may hold the title herself or be a duke's wife.

A nobleman of the highest rank below a monarch.

**1. The male title.** (герцог)

1. The *duke* held estates in three counties.
2. Both *dukes* sat in the House of Lords.
3. Several *dukes* were created that year.

**2. The female counterpart.** (герцогиня)

4. The *duchess* opened the gardens to visitors.
5. Two *duchesses* attended the ceremony.

### emperor

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛmpərər/ &middot; *EM-pur-ur*
**Русский:** император
**Plural:** *emperors* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the emperors were deified*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *empress* /ˈɛmprɪs/
**Contrast:** Like *duke/duchess*, the feminine form contracts the stem: *emperor → empress*, not ✗ *emperoress*.

The ruler of an empire.

1. The *emperor* ruled for forty-one years.

3. Several *emperors* reigned within a single year.

### hero

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɪroʊ/ &middot; *HEER-oh*
**Русский:** герой
**Plural:** *heroes* — always *-es*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the heroes were honored*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the female counterpart is *heroine* /ˈhɛroʊɪn/
**Contrast:** *Hero* now covers everyone, and a brave woman is normally called *a hero*. *Heroine* survives mainly in literary criticism, meaning the main female character. Note the pronunciation trap: *heroine* /ˈhɛroʊɪn/ is spelled almost identically to *heroin*, the drug, and they sound the same.

Someone admired for courage; the main character of a story.

**1. The neutral form.** (герой)

1. The firefighters were treated as *heroes*.
2. She's a *hero* to everyone in that department.
3. Both *heroes* refused any recognition.

**2. The literary feminine form.** (героиня)

4. The novel's *heroine* narrates the whole story.
5. Both *heroines* end up disillusioned.

**See also:** [actor](#actor) &middot; [hero](05-o-plurals.md#hero)

### chairman

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃɛrmən/ &middot; *CHAIR-muhn*
**Русский:** председатель
**Plural:** *chairmen* /ˈtʃɛrmɛn/ — the mutation plural
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the chairmen met privately*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** neutral forms are *chair*, *chairperson*, and for a woman *chairwoman*
**Contrast:** In American professional usage **chair** is now the default: *she chairs the committee*, *the chair recognized the senator*. *Chairman* is still correct but reads as traditional or male-specific. Note the plural takes the [mutation plural](01-vowel-change.md#man) of *man*: *chairmen*, not ✗ *chairmans*.

The person who leads a meeting or organization.

**1. The traditional male form.** (председатель)

1. The *chairman* called the meeting to order.
2. Both *chairmen* resigned the same week.

**2. The neutral forms.** (председатель, нейтрально)

3. She has been *chair* of the department since 2020.
4. Both *chairpersons* signed the report.
5. The *chairwoman* set a three-minute limit on questions.

**See also:** [spokesman](#spokesman) &middot; [man](01-vowel-change.md#man)

### spokesman

**Pronunciation:** /ˈspoʊksmən/ &middot; *SPOHKS-muhn*
**Русский:** представитель, пресс-секретарь
**Plural:** *spokesmen* /ˈspoʊksmɛn/ — the mutation plural
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the spokesmen gave identical statements*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** neutral form *spokesperson*; feminine *spokeswoman*
**Contrast:** *Spokesperson* is now standard in American journalism, and news organizations use it by default unless the person's sex is relevant and known.

Someone who speaks on behalf of an organization.

**1. The traditional male form.** (представитель)

1. A company *spokesman* denied the report.
2. Both *spokesmen* gave identical statements.

**2. The neutral and feminine forms.** (пресс-секретарь)

3. A *spokesperson* confirmed the layoffs.
4. The *spokeswoman* refused to take questions.
5. Both *spokespersons* were quoted anonymously.

**See also:** [chairman](#chairman) &middot; [salesman](#salesman)

### salesman

**Pronunciation:** /ˈseɪlzmən/ &middot; *SAYLZ-muhn*
**Русский:** продавец, торговый представитель
**Plural:** *salesmen* /ˈseɪlzmɛn/ — the mutation plural
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the salesmen worked on commission*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** neutral forms *salesperson*, *sales representative*, *sales rep*; feminine *saleswoman*
**Contrast:** *Salesperson* and *sales rep* are the normal terms in American business now. *Salesman* survives in the title of the play *Death of a Salesman* and in casual speech.

Someone whose job is selling.

**1. The traditional male form.** (продавец)

1. A car *salesman* called three times.
2. Both *salesmen* worked entirely on commission.

**2. The neutral and feminine forms.** (продавец, нейтрально)

3. Every *salesperson* met quota this quarter.
4. The *saleswoman* knew the product cold.
5. Two *sales reps* cover the whole territory.

**See also:** [spokesman](#spokesman) &middot; [policeman](#policeman)

### policeman

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈliːsmən/ &middot; *puh-LEES-muhn*
**Русский:** полицейский
**Plural:** *policemen* /pəˈliːsmɛn/ — the mutation plural
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the policemen were first on the scene*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** neutral form *police officer*; feminine *policewoman*
**Contrast:** **Police officer** is the standard American term and what departments themselves use. Note also that [police](17-collective.md#police) as a collective is always plural: *the police **are** investigating*.

A member of a police force.

**1. The traditional male form.** (полицейский)

1. A *policeman* took the statement.
2. Both *policemen* were first on the scene.

**2. The neutral and feminine forms.** (сотрудник полиции)

3. Two *police officers* responded to the call.
4. The *policewoman* who arrested him testified.
5. Every *police officer* wears a body camera now.

### firefighter

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfaɪərfaɪtər/ &middot; *FY-ur-fy-tur*
**Русский:** пожарный
**Plural:** *firefighters* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the firefighters worked through the night*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the older term is *fireman*, plural *firemen*
**Contrast:** This replacement is essentially complete in American English: departments, news reports, and firefighters themselves say **firefighter**. *Fireman* now sounds like a children's book. Note that the replacement is a regular noun, so it escapes the *-man → -men* mutation plural.

Someone whose job is putting out fires.

**1. The neutral form.** (пожарный)

1. *Firefighters* worked through the night.
2. Both *firefighters* were treated for smoke inhalation.
3. She has been a *firefighter* for eleven years.

**2. The older form.** (пожарный, устар.)

4. Two *firemen* carried the ladder between them.
5. Most departments stopped saying *fireman* long ago.

**See also:** [policeman](#policeman) &middot; [salesman](#salesman)

### fiancé

**Pronunciation:** /ˌfiːɑːnˈseɪ/ &middot; *fee-ahn-SAY*
**Русский:** жених
**Plural:** *fiancés* — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *their fiancés met at the party*

A man engaged to be married.

2. Both *fiancés* were introduced that evening.
3. She brought her *fiancé* to the reunion.

**2. The female counterpart.** (невеста)

4. His *fiancée* is finishing law school.
5. Both *fiancées* chose the same venue.
