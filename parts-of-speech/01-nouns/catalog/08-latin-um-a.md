# Latin *-um* → *-a*

> *Datum → data*, *medium → media*. The plural often ends up feeling like a singular — which is why *data is* and *media is* are now normal American English.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Latin neuter nouns in *-um* took *-a* in the plural, and English borrowed many of them for scholarly and technical use. What makes this group interesting is not the ending but what happened afterward: because the *-a* form does not look plural to an English speaker, several of these plurals have **quietly turned into singular mass nouns**.

*Data* is the clearest case. In scientific writing it still takes a plural verb — *the data are inconsistent* — but in ordinary American English *the data is inconsistent* is now standard and unremarkable. *Media* has gone further: *the media is biased* is normal, and *mediums* has split off as a separate plural for fortune-tellers. *Agenda* has completed the journey — it is now a singular noun with the regular plural *agendas*, and its Latin singular *agendum* has vanished.

Meanwhile many *-um* words never took the Latin plural at all: *album*, *stadium*, *museum*, *forum*, and *asylum* are all perfectly regular.

### datum

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdeɪtəm/ &middot; *DAY-tuhm*
**Русский:** данные; исходная точка
**Plural:** *data* /ˈdeɪtə/ &middot; *DAY-tuh* — also /ˈdætə/ *DAT-uh*
**Countability:** *data* is countable in science, uncountable in general use
**Agreement:** either — *the data are* (science) / *the data is* (general)
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** both /ˈdeɪtə/ and /ˈdætə/ are standard in American English
**Contrast:** The singular *datum* is now rare outside surveying and statistics; almost nobody says *one datum*. For the plural, both verbs are defensible: journals still require *the data are*, while everyday and business English overwhelmingly says *the data is*. Neither is an error, but know your audience.

Facts and figures collected for reference or analysis.

**1. Information collected for analysis.** Usually treated as a mass noun. (данные)

1. The *data* is stored on three separate servers.
2. All the *data* was lost in the crash.
3. We need more *data* before deciding.

**2. In scientific writing — a plural.** (данные, мн. ч.)

4. The *data* are consistent with the hypothesis.
5. These *data* were collected over six years.

**3. A single fact — datum.** Rare, technical. (единица данных; репер)

6. Each *datum* is timestamped at collection.
7. Elevations are measured from a fixed *datum*.

**See also:** [medium](#medium) &middot; [criterion](10-greek-on-a.md#criterion)

### medium

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmiːdiəm/ &middot; *MEE-dee-uhm*
**Русский:** средство, среда; медиум
**Plural:** *media* /ˈmiːdiə/ (channels, materials) &middot; *mediums* (psychics, sizes)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** either — *the media is* (the press as one body) / *are* (the outlets)
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a*, split by sense &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *media* (communication, art materials, biology) &middot; *mediums* (spiritualists, clothing sizes)
**Contrast:** The plural depends entirely on the sense. Channels of communication and artistic materials take *media*; people who claim to contact the dead, and shirt sizes, take *mediums*. When *media* means the press, American English treats it as singular: *the media is covering it closely*.

A means of communication or expression; a substance something exists in; also a person who claims to contact spirits.

**1. A channel of communication.** Plural *media*, often treated as a singular mass noun. (средства массовой информации)

1. Television is still a powerful *medium*.
2. The *media* has moved on to the next story.
3. Social *media* changed how the campaign was run.

**2. A material an artist works in.** (материал, техника)

4. Oil is her preferred *medium*.
5. He works across several *media*.

**3. A substance in which something grows or travels.** Science. (среда)

6. The bacteria were grown in a nutrient *medium*.
7. Sound needs a *medium* to travel through.

**4. A person claiming to contact the dead.** Plural *mediums*. (медиум)

8. Two *mediums* were hired for the show.

**5. A middle size or degree.** (средний размер)

9. I'll take the *medium* in blue.

**See also:** [datum](#datum)

### bacterium

**Pronunciation:** /bækˈtɪriəm/ &middot; *bak-TEER-ee-uhm*
**Русский:** бактерия
**Plural:** *bacteria* /bækˈtɪriə/ &middot; *bak-TEER-ee-uh*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb in careful use — *bacteria are present*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *A bacteria* for one organism is extremely common in speech and still considered an error in writing — the singular is *bacterium*. Note that *bacteria* keeps its plural verb far more reliably than *data* does, because the singular is still in active use.

A single-celled microorganism.

**1. The organism.** (бактерия)

1. This *bacterium* survives in boiling water.
2. *Bacteria* are responsible for the smell.
3. Most *bacteria* in the gut are harmless.
4. A single *bacterium* can divide every twenty minutes.
5. The sample contained three species of *bacteria*.

**See also:** [fungus](06-latin-us-i.md#fungus) &middot; [amoeba](07-latin-a-ae.md#amoeba)

### curriculum

**Pronunciation:** /kəˈrɪkjələm/ &middot; *kuh-RIK-yuh-luhm*
**Русский:** учебная программа
**Plural:** *curricula* /kəˈrɪkjələ/ — also *curriculums*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the curricula were revised*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *curricula* is commoner in education writing; *curriculums* is accepted
**Contrast:** Note the Latin phrase *curriculum vitae* — a résumé — whose plural is *curricula vitae*. Americans usually just say *CV* or *résumé*.

The subjects taught by a school or in a program.

**1. A course of study.** (учебная программа)

1. The *curriculum* changes every four years.
2. Both *curricula* emphasize writing.
3. Music was cut from the *curriculum*.
4. State *curriculums* vary widely.
5. She helped design the new science *curriculum*.

**See also:** [syllabus](06-latin-us-i.md#syllabus)

### memorandum

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmɛməˈrændəm/ &middot; *mem-uh-RAN-duhm*
**Русский:** меморандум, служебная записка
**Plural:** *memoranda* /ˌmɛməˈrændə/ — also *memorandums*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the memoranda were filed*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** both plurals are correct; the shortened *memo* / *memos* is far commoner in speech
**Contrast:** In everyday American offices nobody says *memorandum* — they say *memo*. The full form survives in law and diplomacy, as in *memorandum of understanding*.

A written record or communication, especially within an organization.

**1. An official note.** (меморандум)

1. The *memorandum* was circulated to all partners.
2. Internal *memoranda* were subpoenaed.
3. Both sides signed a *memorandum* of understanding.
4. She filed the *memorandums* by date.
5. A confidential *memorandum* leaked to the press.

**See also:** [memo](05-o-plurals.md#memo)

### referendum

**Pronunciation:** /ˌrɛfəˈrɛndəm/ &middot; *ref-uh-REN-duhm*
**Русский:** референдум
**Plural:** *referendums* — usual &middot; *referenda*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both referendums failed*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *referendums* is preferred in American English and in most style guides
**Contrast:** Grammarians argue that *referenda* is technically wrong because the Latin word is a gerund, not a neuter noun. In practice both appear; *referendums* is safer.

A direct popular vote on a single question.

**1. A public vote.** (референдум)

1. The *referendum* passed by four points.
2. Two *referendums* are on the November ballot.
3. Voters rejected the *referendum*.
4. Several *referenda* were held that decade.

**2. Figuratively — a test of public opinion.** (проверка общественного мнения)

5. The election became a *referendum* on the mayor.

**See also:** [millennium](#millennium)

### millennium

**Pronunciation:** /mɪˈlɛniəm/ &middot; *mih-LEN-ee-uhm*
**Русский:** тысячелетие
**Plural:** *millennia* /mɪˈlɛniə/ — also *millenniums*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *three millennia have passed*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *millennia* is much commoner
**Contrast:** Two *l*'s and two *n*'s — the commonest misspelling in the word is ✗ *millenium*. It comes from *mille* 'thousand' and *annus* 'year'.

A period of a thousand years.

**1. A thousand-year period.** (тысячелетие)

1. The site has been occupied for two *millennia*.
2. A new *millennium* began in 2001.
3. These traditions go back *millennia*.
4. The stone has stood here for three *millennia*.
5. Historians divide the *millennium* into four periods.

**See also:** [referendum](#referendum)

### stratum

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstreɪtəm/ &middot; *STRAY-tuhm* — also /ˈstrætəm/
**Русский:** слой, пласт
**Plural:** *strata* /ˈstreɪtə/ &middot; *STRAY-tuh*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the strata are clearly visible*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** ✗ *stratums* is rare and best avoided
**Contrast:** ✗ *A strata* for one layer is a common error — the singular is *stratum*. The related *stratosphere* comes from the same root.

A layer, especially of rock or of society.

**1. A layer of rock or soil.** Geology. (пласт, слой)

1. Each *stratum* represents thousands of years.
2. The *strata* are tilted almost vertically.
3. Fossils appear in the lower *strata*.

**2. A level of society.** Figurative. (слой общества)

4. The policy affects every *stratum* of society.
5. All social *strata* were represented in the survey.

**See also:** [erratum](#erratum)

### erratum

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈrɑːtəm/ &middot; *ih-RAH-tuhm* — also /ɪˈreɪtəm/
**Русский:** опечатка, список опечаток
**Plural:** *errata* /ɪˈrɑːtə/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the errata are listed at the back*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** ✗ *erratums* is not used
**Contrast:** In practice the word appears almost only in the plural, and often as the fixed phrase *errata sheet* — the slip of corrections tucked into a printed book.

An error in printing or writing, listed as a correction.

**1. A published correction.** (опечатка)

1. A single *erratum* was noted in the second printing.
2. The *errata* are listed on the last page.
3. The publisher issued an *errata* sheet.
4. Twelve *errata* were found after publication.
5. Each *erratum* gives the page and the corrected text.

**See also:** [stratum](#stratum) &middot; [addendum](#addendum)

**Русский:** симпозиум
**Plural:** *symposia* /sɪmˈpoʊziə/ — also *symposiums*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both symposia were canceled*
**Pattern:** Latin *-um* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 8
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** both plurals are standard in American academic use
**Contrast:** From Greek by way of Latin, originally a drinking party — literally 'drinking together'.

An academic conference or meeting on a particular subject.

**1. A scholarly meeting.** (симпозиум)

1. She presented at a *symposium* in Chicago.
2. Two *symposia* are scheduled for the fall.
3. The *symposium* runs for three days.
4. Papers from both *symposiums* will be published.
5. A *symposium* on climate policy opens Monday.

### forum

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːrəm/ &middot; *FOR-uhm*
**Русский:** форум
**Plural:** *forums* — usual &middot; *fora* in classical contexts
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the forums are moderated*
**Pattern:** regular *-s* despite the Latin origin &middot; **Group:** 8 (trap)
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *forums* everywhere in modern English; *fora* only of Roman marketplaces
**Contrast:** Although Latin, this word takes a regular English plural in every ordinary use. *Fora* sounds precious outside archaeology. The same is true of *album → albums*, *stadium → stadiums*, *museum → museums*, and *asylum → asylums*.

A place or occasion for public discussion; also an online discussion board.

**1. A place for public discussion.** (форум)

1. The town hall is a *forum* for local complaints.
2. Several public *forums* were held before the vote.
3. This is not the right *forum* for that argument.

**2. An online discussion board.** (интернет-форум)

4. He posts on three different *forums*.
5. The *forums* are moderated by volunteers.

**3. The public square of a Roman town.** Plural *fora*. (форум, площадь)

6. The imperial *fora* lie north of the Colosseum.

**See also:** [symposium](#symposium) &middot; [stadium](#stadium)

A large enclosed sports ground with seating.

A building where objects of interest are displayed.

1. The *museum* opens at ten.
