Core vocabulary: war and the military#

The everyday nouns of war and the military — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

warfare#

Pronunciation
/ˈwɔːrfɛr/ · WAWR-fair
Русский
военные действия, ведение войны
Countability
uncountable

The activity of fighting a war, or a particular way of fighting one.

  1. Trench warfare defined the First World War.
  2. The rebels turned to guerrilla warfare.
  3. Chemical warfare is banned by treaty.

battle#

Pronunciation
/ˈbætəl/ · BAT-uhl
Русский
битва, сражение; борьба, противостояние
Plural
battles
Countability
countable

Battle has 2 distinct senses.

1. A fight between two armies in one place at one time. (битва, сражение)

  1. The battle lasted three days.
  2. He died in battle.
  3. Several battles were fought along the river.

2. A long, hard struggle against a problem or an opponent. (борьба, противостояние)

  1. She lost her battle with cancer last spring.
  2. The company faces a legal battle over the patent.
  3. Getting my kids to eat vegetables is a daily battle.

Русский

Countability
uncountable
Contrast

Actual fighting between armed forces.

  1. She saw combat on two deployments.
  2. The unit trained for close combat.
  3. He was wounded in combat.

conflict#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɑːnflɪkt/ · KAHN-flikt
Русский
конфликт; накладка в расписании
Plural

Countability
countable
Contrast
The noun is stressed KAHN-flikt; the verb to conflict is kuhn-FLIKT.

Conflict has 2 distinct senses.

1. A serious disagreement or period of fighting between groups. (конфликт)

  1. The conflict has displaced millions.
  2. Diplomats worked for months to end the conflict.
  3. Armed conflicts broke out in three provinces.

2. A clash between two things arranged for the same time. (накладка в расписании)

  1. I have a conflict on Tuesday at three.
  2. We moved the meeting because of a scheduling conflict.
  3. Check the calendar for conflicts before you book the room.

invasion#

Pronunciation
/ɪnˈveɪʒən/ · in-VAY-zhuhn
Русский
вторжение; нашествие, наплыв; посягательство (на частную жизнь)
Plural
invasions
Countability
countable

Invasion has 3 distinct senses.

1. The act of entering another country by force with an army. (вторжение)

  1. The invasion began before dawn.
  2. They resisted the invasion for months.
  3. Both invasions failed within weeks.

2. The arrival of a large number of unwanted people or things in a place. (нашествие, наплыв)

  1. Every June the town braces for an invasion of tourists.
  2. We had an invasion of ants in the kitchen after the rain.
  3. Shop owners complained about the summer invasion of college kids.

3. An act of interfering with someone's privacy or personal rights. (посягательство (на частную жизнь))

  1. She sued the magazine for invasion of privacy.
  2. Reading someone's texts is a serious invasion of privacy.
  3. Employees saw the tracking software as an invasion.

Pronunciation
/əˈtæk/ · uh-TAK
Русский

Plural
attacks
Countability
countable

Attack has 3 distinct senses.

1. An act of violence aimed at hurting an enemy. (атака, нападение)

  1. The attack came from the north.
  2. Two attacks were repelled that night.
  3. They launched a surprise attack on the airfield.

2. A sudden short period of an illness or strong feeling. (приступ (болезни, паники))

  1. He had a heart attack while shoveling snow.
  2. Cold air can trigger an asthma attack.
  3. She gets panic attacks in crowded stores.

3. Strong public criticism of a person or an idea. (нападки, резкая критика)

  1. The senator launched a bitter attack on the press.
  2. The column was a direct attack on the mayor's record.
  3. He's used to attacks from both parties.

Pronunciation
/ˈkæʒuəlti/ · KAZH-oo-uhl-tee

Contrast

Casualty has 2 distinct senses.

1. A person killed or wounded in a war or an accident. (потери; убитый или раненый)

  1. The battle produced heavy casualties.
  2. Civilian casualties rose sharply that spring.
  3. Every casualty was flown out by helicopter.

2. A person or thing that is harmed or lost because of some event or decision. (жертва, пострадавший (от чего-либо))

  1. The music program was the first casualty of the budget cuts.
  2. Privacy is often a casualty of new technology.
  3. Small bookstores were the casualties of that merger.

Massacre has 2 distinct senses.

  1. The second half was a massacre.
  2. Nobody expected such a massacre in the season opener.
  3. The last two games were massacres, 42-3 and 38-7.

division#

Pronunciation
/dɪˈvɪʒən/ · dih-VIZH-uhn
Русский
деление (арифметическое действие); раздел, разделение; раскол, разногласия; подразделение, отдел (компании); дивизия
Plural
divisions
Countability
countable

Division has 5 distinct senses.

1. The arithmetic operation of finding how many times one number goes into another. (деление (арифметическое действие))

  1. Third graders start long division this year.
  2. She's fine at multiplication but still struggles with division.
  3. Use division to find the average.

2. The act of splitting something into parts or sharing it out. (раздел, разделение)

  1. The will spells out the division of the property.
  2. They argued for months over the division of assets.
  3. The division of labor in that kitchen was never fair.

3. A serious disagreement that splits a group into opposing sides. (раскол, разногласия)

  1. The vote exposed deep divisions within the party.
  2. There's a growing division between the older and younger members.
  3. The issue created divisions that never healed.

4. A large section of a company or organization. (подразделение, отдел (компании))

  1. She runs the company's Latin American division.
  2. The consumer products division had a strong quarter.
  3. Two divisions were merged to cut costs.

5. A large army unit made up of several brigades. (дивизия)

  1. An armored division crossed the border.
  2. The division suffered heavy losses.
  3. Four divisions were committed to the offensive.
