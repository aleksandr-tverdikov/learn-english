# Partitive nouns — how to count the uncountable

> *A piece of advice*, *a slice of bread*, *a bar of soap*, *a pinch of salt*. The uncountable noun cannot take a number, so a countable one carries it.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

An [uncountable noun](13-uncountable.md) cannot follow a number: ✗ *two advices*, ✗ *three breads*. To count it you insert a **partitive** — a countable noun naming a portion, joined by *of*. The number then attaches to the partitive: *two **pieces** of advice*, *three **slices** of bread*.

Agreement follows the partitive, not the mass noun: *a piece of information **is** missing*, *two pieces of information **are** missing*.

The hard part is not the structure but the **choice**. English is fussy about which partitive goes with which noun, and the wrong one is immediately noticeable even though it is perfectly logical. *A piece of furniture* is right; ✗ *a slice of furniture* is not. *A bar of soap*, but ✗ *a bar of shampoo*. There is no rule — these are collocations, learned as pairs.

*Piece* is the general-purpose fallback and works with a great many abstract nouns: *a piece of advice, information, news, evidence, luck, work, music, research*. When you are unsure, *piece* is the safest choice.

---

### piece

**Pronunciation:** /piːs/ &middot; *pees*
**Русский:** кусок; штука
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a piece is* / *two pieces are*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** The all-purpose partitive, and the right default for abstract nouns: *a piece of advice, information, news, evidence, luck, music, research, work, furniture, equipment, cake, paper*. When no more specific word comes to mind, *piece* is almost never wrong.

A portion or unit of something.

**1. With abstract uncountables.** (единица чего-либо)

1. Let me give you a *piece* of advice.
2. That's a useful *piece* of information.
3. Two *pieces* of evidence were missing.

**2. With physical materials.** (кусок)

4. Hand me a *piece* of paper.
5. He cut three *pieces* of wood to length.
6. There's one *piece* of cake left.

**3. A created work.** (произведение)

7. She wrote a *piece* for the local paper.
8. It's a beautiful *piece* of music.

**See also:** [slice](#slice) &middot; [bit](#bit)

### bit

**Pronunciation:** /bɪt/ &middot; *bit*
**Русский:** немного, кусочек
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a bit is* / *two bits are*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** *A bit of* is the informal, smaller-sounding alternative to *a piece of*: *a bit of advice* is friendlier than *a piece of advice*. It also works as an adverb meaning 'slightly' — *a bit tired*.

A small amount or portion.

**1. A small amount.** (немного)

1. Give me a *bit* of time.
2. There's a *bit* of cheese left.
3. He needs a *bit* of luck.
4. She told me a few *bits* of news.

**2. As an intensifier — a bit.** (немного, слегка)

5. I'm a *bit* tired.
6. That's a *bit* much.

**See also:** [piece](#piece)

### slice

**Pronunciation:** /slaɪs/ &middot; *slyse*
**Русский:** ломтик, кусок
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a slice is* / *two slices are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *Slice* means a thin flat piece cut from something larger, so it collocates with bread, cheese, pizza, cake, meat, and lemon — but not with soap or furniture.

A thin flat piece cut from something.

**1. A cut piece of food.** (ломтик)

1. She toasted two *slices* of bread.
2. Add a *slice* of lemon.
3. There are three *slices* of pizza left.
4. He cut a thin *slice* of cheese.

**2. A share or portion.** Figurative. (доля)

5. Everyone wants a *slice* of the profits.

**See also:** [piece](#piece) &middot; [loaf](04-f-to-ves.md#loaf)

### bar

**Pronunciation:** /bɑːr/ &middot; *bar*
**Русский:** плитка, брусок
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a bar is* / *two bars are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *Bar* collocates tightly: *a bar of soap*, *a bar of chocolate*, *a bar of gold*, *a bar of music*. It does not extend to liquids or powders.

A solid rectangular block of something.

**1. A block of a solid substance.** (плитка, брусок)

1. We need another *bar* of soap.
2. He ate a whole *bar* of chocolate.
3. Two *bars* of gold were recovered.

**2. A unit of music.** (такт)

4. The melody enters after four *bars*.

**3. A long rigid piece of metal.** (перекладина, прут)

5. Iron *bars* covered the window.

**See also:** [piece](#piece) &middot; [lump](#lump)

### sheet

**Pronunciation:** /ʃiːt/ &middot; *sheet*
**Русский:** лист
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a sheet is* / *two sheets are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *A sheet of* is used for thin flat materials: paper, glass, metal, ice, plastic. For paper, both *a sheet of paper* and *a piece of paper* are normal.

A thin flat piece of material.

**1. A flat piece of material.** (лист)

1. Bring me a *sheet* of paper.
2. Two *sheets* of glass were delivered.
3. A *sheet* of ice covered the road.
4. He cut a *sheet* of plywood in half.

**2. Bedding.** (простыня)

5. The *sheets* need changing.

**See also:** [piece](#piece)

### pair

**Pronunciation:** /pɛr/ &middot; *pair*
**Русский:** пара
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular when *pair* is the head — *a pair of scissors is missing*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** The obligatory partitive for [plural-only two-part nouns](15-plural-only.md): *a pair of scissors, pants, jeans, glasses, binoculars, pliers*. Note the agreement: *a pair of scissors **is** on the desk* — the verb follows *pair*, not *scissors*.

A set of two matching things, or one object with two joined parts.

**1. With plural-only objects.** (пара — о парных предметах)

1. Hand me a *pair* of scissors.
2. She bought two *pairs* of jeans.
3. A *pair* of glasses was left on the table.

**2. Two matching items.** (пара)

4. He owns six *pairs* of shoes.
5. One *pair* of socks was missing.

**3. Two people or animals together.** (пара)

6. A *pair* of hawks nests on the roof.

**See also:** [scissors](15-plural-only.md#scissors)

### bunch

**Pronunciation:** /bʌntʃ/ &middot; *bunch*
**Русский:** пучок, гроздь, связка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular as a unit — *a bunch of grapes is*; plural with people — *a bunch of people are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *Bunch* collocates with things naturally growing or tied together: *grapes*, *bananas*, *flowers*, *keys*, *herbs*. Informally in American English *a bunch of* also just means 'a lot of', and then takes a plural verb: *a bunch of people **are** waiting*.

A group of things growing or fastened together.

**1. Things grouped together.** (гроздь, пучок)

1. Buy a *bunch* of bananas.
2. She brought a *bunch* of flowers.
3. Two *bunches* of grapes were left.
4. He pulled out a *bunch* of keys.

**2. Informally — a lot of.** American. (куча, много)

5. A *bunch* of people are waiting outside.
6. I have a whole *bunch* of things to do.

**See also:** [pair](#pair)

### pinch

**Pronunciation:** /pɪntʃ/ &middot; *pinch*
**Русский:** щепотка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a pinch is* / *two pinches are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** Used almost exclusively of dry seasonings: *a pinch of salt*, *a pinch of pepper*, *a pinch of cinnamon*. Literally the amount held between finger and thumb.

A very small amount, held between finger and thumb.

**1. A small quantity of a powder.** (щепотка)

1. Add a *pinch* of salt.
2. Two *pinches* of saffron is plenty.
3. Just a *pinch* of cinnamon on top.

**2. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

4. Take what he says with a *pinch* of salt.
5. In a *pinch*, you could use butter instead.

**See also:** [grain](#grain) &middot; [drop](#drop)

### drop

**Pronunciation:** /drɑːp/ &middot; *drahp*
**Русский:** капля
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a drop is* / *two drops are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** The standard partitive for liquids in small quantity: *a drop of water*, *a drop of blood*, *a drop of oil*. For larger amounts use *a glass of*, *a cup of*, *a bottle of*.

A small round quantity of liquid.

**1. A small quantity of liquid.** (капля)

1. Add two *drops* of vanilla.
2. There's not a *drop* of milk left.
3. A *drop* of blood fell on the paper.
4. Put three *drops* in each eye.

**2. A small amount generally.** (капелька)

5. He hasn't had a *drop* to drink all evening.

**See also:** [pinch](#pinch) &middot; [glass](14-dual-category.md#glass)

### grain

**Pronunciation:** /ɡreɪn/ &middot; *grayn*
**Русский:** зерно, крупинка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a grain is* / *two grains are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** The partitive for very small hard particles: *a grain of rice*, *a grain of sand*, *a grain of salt*, *a grain of truth*.

A single small hard particle.

**1. A single particle.** (крупинка, зерно)

1. Not a single *grain* of rice was left.
2. *Grains* of sand worked into everything.
3. A few *grains* of salt fell on the counter.

**2. A tiny amount, figuratively.** (крупица)

4. There isn't a *grain* of truth in it.

**3. The direction of fibers in wood.** (волокно, текстура)

5. Cut along the *grain*, not across it.

### lump

**Pronunciation:** /lʌmp/ &middot; *lump*
**Русский:** кусок, комок
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a lump is* / *two lumps are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** Used of shapeless solid masses: *a lump of sugar*, *a lump of coal*, *a lump of clay*, *a lump of dough*. Compare *bar*, which implies a regular rectangular shape.

A compact shapeless mass.

**1. A shapeless mass.** (кусок, комок)

1. Two *lumps* of sugar, please.
2. He threw a *lump* of coal on the fire.
3. She rolled the *lump* of dough flat.

**2. A swelling on the body.** (шишка, уплотнение)

4. The doctor found a small *lump*.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

5. I got a *lump* in my throat just watching it.

### bottle

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɑːtəl/ &middot; *BAH-tuhl*
**Русский:** бутылка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a bottle is* / *two bottles are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** Container partitives are the easiest group, because the container is visible: *a bottle of wine*, *a can of soup*, *a jar of honey*, *a carton of milk*, *a bag of flour*, *a box of cereal*, *a tube of toothpaste*.

The container, and the amount it holds.

**1. The amount a bottle holds.** (бутылка чего-либо)

1. We opened a *bottle* of wine.
2. Buy two *bottles* of olive oil.
3. He drank a whole *bottle* of water.

**2. The container itself.** (бутылка)

4. Put the empty *bottles* in the recycling.
5. The *bottle* is made of thick green glass.

### can

**Pronunciation:** /kæn/ &middot; *kan*
**Русский:** банка, консервная банка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a can is* / *two cans are*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the British word for the food container is *tin*
**Contrast:** American *can*, British *tin*, for the same sealed metal container. Both languages say *a can of soda*.

The metal container, and the amount it holds.

**1. The amount a can holds.** (банка чего-либо)

1. Add a *can* of crushed tomatoes.
2. We bought three *cans* of soup.
3. He drank a *can* of soda.

**2. The container itself.** (жестяная банка)

4. The empty *cans* go in the blue bin.
5. Open the *can* carefully — the edge is sharp.

### jar

**Pronunciation:** /dʒɑːr/ &middot; *jar*
**Русский:** банка (стеклянная)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a jar is* / *two jars are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** A *jar* is wide-mouthed, usually glass, and holds thick or solid food: *a jar of honey*, *a jar of jam*, *a jar of pickles*. A *bottle* is narrow-necked and holds liquid.

The wide-mouthed container, and the amount it holds.

**1. The amount a jar holds.** (банка чего-либо)

1. There's half a *jar* of jam left.
2. Two *jars* of honey came from one hive.
3. She opened a *jar* of pickles.

**2. The container itself.** (банка)

4. The *jars* are sterilized before filling.
5. He keeps screws in an old *jar*.

**See also:** [can](#can) &middot; [bottle](#bottle)

### carton

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːrtən/ &middot; *KAR-tuhn*
**Русский:** пакет, коробка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a carton is* / *two cartons are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** In American English *a carton of milk* and *a carton of eggs* are both standard, though the containers are quite different. For juice, *a carton* is the paper container and *a bottle* the plastic one.

The cardboard container, and the amount it holds.

**1. The amount a carton holds.** (пакет, упаковка)

1. Pick up a *carton* of milk.
2. Two *cartons* of eggs should be enough.
3. He drank half a *carton* of juice.

**2. The container itself.** (картонная коробка)

4. Flatten the *cartons* before recycling.
5. The *carton* was leaking at the bottom.

### loaf

**Pronunciation:** /loʊf/ &middot; *lohf*
**Русский:** буханка
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a loaf is* / *two loaves are*
**Type:** concrete

A shaped mass of bread.

**1. A whole bread.** (буханка)

### cup

**Pronunciation:** /kʌp/ &middot; *kup*
**Русский:** чашка; стакан (мера)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** follows the partitive — *a cup is* / *two cups are*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** In American recipes *a cup* is a fixed measure — eight fluid ounces — not just a drinking vessel. This is why American recipes measure flour and sugar by volume where European ones use weight.

The drinking vessel, and the amount it holds; in cooking, a fixed measure.

**1. A drink's worth.** (чашка)

1. She made a *cup* of tea.
2. Two *cups* of coffee is my limit.
3. He drank three *cups* of water.

**2. A cooking measure.** American. (стакан как мера)

4. Add two *cups* of flour.
5. The recipe calls for a *cup* of sugar.

**3. A trophy.** (кубок)

6. They lifted the *cup* for the third year running.
