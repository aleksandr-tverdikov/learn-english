Core vocabulary: insects and reptiles#

The everyday nouns of insects and reptiles — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

insect#

Pronunciation
/ˈɪnsɛkt/ · IN-sekt
Русский
насекомое
Plural
insects
Countability
countable

A small animal with six legs, a body in three parts, and usually wings.

  1. The porch light pulls in every insect in the neighborhood.
  2. She studies insects that live only in caves.
  3. Not every crawling thing is an insect — spiders have eight legs.

bug#

Pronunciation
/bʌɡ/ · BUG
Русский
жучок, насекомое (разг.); баг, ошибка в программе; инфекция, вирус (лёгкое недомогание); жучок (подслушивающее устройство)
Plural
bugs
Countability
countable
Contrast
In casual American English bug covers almost any small creepy-crawly, including spiders — insect is the precise word.

Bug has 4 distinct senses.

1. Any small crawling or flying insect, in everyday speech. (жучок, насекомое (разг.))

  1. There's a bug crawling up the wall behind you.
  2. The screen door keeps most of the bugs out.
  3. Some kind of bug landed in my coffee.

2. A mistake in a computer program that makes it behave wrongly. (баг, ошибка в программе)

  1. The update fixed a bug that erased saved files.
  2. Testers filed a dozen bugs on the first day.
  3. There's a bug in the checkout page.

3. A minor infection passed from one person to another. (инфекция, вирус (лёгкое недомогание))

  1. Half the office caught a stomach bug.
  2. She's home with some kind of bug.
  3. A nasty bug went through the daycare in January.

4. A tiny hidden microphone used to listen in secretly. (жучок (подслушивающее устройство))

  1. Agents planted a bug in the hotel room.
  2. They swept the office for bugs every month.
  3. A bug under the table recorded the whole meeting.

pest#

Pronunciation
/pɛst/ · PEST
Русский
вредитель; надоеда, приставала
Plural
pests
Countability
countable

Pest has 2 distinct senses.

1. An insect or animal that damages crops, food, or property. (вредитель)

  1. Deer are the worst pest in this garden.
  2. The farm loses about a tenth of its corn to pests.
  3. We hired someone to deal with the pests in the attic.

2. An annoying person who keeps bothering you. (надоеда, приставала)

  1. Stop being such a pest and let me finish this.
  2. The kids next door are complete pests.
  3. He made a pest of himself calling every hour.

Contrast

creature#

Pronunciation
/ˈkriːtʃər/ · KREE-chur
Русский
существо, тварь; человек определённого склада; порождение (среды)
Plural
creatures
Countability
countable

Creature has 2 distinct senses.

1. Any living animal, especially one that seems strange or unfamiliar. (существо, тварь)

  1. Some creature was scratching around under the deck all night.
  2. Tide pools are full of tiny creatures.
  3. It looked like a creature out of a science fiction movie.

2. A person whose behavior is completely shaped by some habit or force. (человек определённого склада; порождение (среды))

  1. I'm a creature of habit — the same breakfast every day.
  2. He is a creature of the party machine and always was.
  3. She's a creature of the theater, at home nowhere else.

fly#

Pronunciation
/flaɪ/ · FLY
Русский
муха; ширинка; мушка (рыболовная приманка)
Plural
flies
Countability
countable

Fly has 3 distinct senses.

1. A small flying insect with two wings that is often found around food and garbage. (муха)

  1. There's a fly buzzing against the kitchen window.
  2. Close the lid or you'll have flies all over the trash.
  3. He swatted the fly with a rolled-up magazine.

2. The opening at the front of a pair of pants, closed with a zipper or buttons. (ширинка)

  1. Your fly is open.
  2. The zipper on his fly broke on the way to work.
  3. These jeans have a button fly.

3. An artificial bait made to look like an insect, used in fishing. (мушка (рыболовная приманка))

  1. He ties his own flies all winter.
  2. She cast a small dry fly upstream.
  3. That fly has caught more trout than anything else in my box.

bee#

Pronunciation
/biː/ · BEE
Русский

Plural

Countability
countable
Contrast
Bee sounds exactly like the verb be — only context tells them apart in speech.

Bee has 2 distinct senses.

1. A flying insect that gathers nectar from flowers and can sting. (пчела)

  1. A bee landed on the rim of my soda can.
  2. The lavender out front is always covered in bees.
  3. He's allergic, so a bee sting is a real emergency.

2. A gathering where people compete at something or work together. (конкурс, коллективные посиделки (напр. spelling bee))

  1. She won the school spelling bee two years running.
  2. The neighbors held a quilting bee in the church hall.
  3. Our town still has a sewing bee every winter.

sting#

Pronunciation
/stɪŋ/ · STING
Русский

Plural
stings
Countability
countable
Contrast
Bees and wasps sting; mosquitoes and spiders bite. Russian укус covers both, but English never says a bee bite.

Sting has 2 distinct senses.

1. A painful wound made by a bee, wasp, or scorpion. (укус (жалящего насекомого))

  1. The sting swelled up by the next morning.
  2. He's had three wasp stings this summer.
  3. A scorpion sting is rarely fatal in this country.

2. A secret police operation set up to catch someone breaking the law. (операция под прикрытием, подстава)

  1. Detectives arrested six people in the sting.
  2. The sting was run out of a rented storefront.
  3. Federal agents set up a sting to catch the dealers.

countable

Bite has 2 distinct senses.

  1. I woke up with three bites on my ankle.
  2. Don't scratch the bite or it'll get infected.

2. A piece of food taken into the mouth at one time, or a quick small meal. (кусок (пищи); перекус)

  1. He took a bite of the sandwich and made a face.
  2. Let's grab a bite before the movie.
  3. There wasn't a bite of food left in the house.

  2. She had butterflies the whole ride to the airport.
  3. A few butterflies before a big game are perfectly normal.

Venom has 2 distinct senses.

1. The poison that a snake, spider, or scorpion injects with a bite or sting. (яд (змеиный, паучий))

  1. The venom attacks the blood, not the nerves.
  2. They milk the snakes for venom to make antivenom.
  3. Only a small amount of venom got into the wound.

2. Extreme bitterness or hatred in what someone says. (злоба, яд (в словах))

  1. He talked about his old boss with real venom.
  2. There was venom in her voice.
  3. The letter was three pages of pure venom.
