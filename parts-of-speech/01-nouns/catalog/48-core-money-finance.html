Core vocabulary: money and finance#

The everyday nouns of money and finance — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

quarter#

Pronunciation
/ˈkwɔːrtər/ · KWOR-tur
Русский
монета в 25 центов; квартал (года); четверть; квартал, район (города); жильё, помещение для проживания; казарма; четверть (период матча)
Plural
quarters
Countability
countable
Contrast
Two everyday senses: the 25-cent coin and a three-month period of a business year.

Quarter has 5 distinct senses.

1. An American coin worth twenty-five cents, or a three-month business period. (монета в 25 центов; квартал (года))

  1. The laundry machines need quarters.
  2. Sales were up in the third quarter.
  3. Do you have a quarter for the parking meter?

2. One of four equal parts of something. (четверть)

  1. Cut the sandwich into quarters.
  2. She poured a quarter of the batter into each pan.
  3. Almost a quarter of the class was absent that day.

3. A district of a city with a character of its own. (квартал, район (города))

  1. The shops in the historic quarter stay open late.
  2. They rented a walk-up in the artists' quarter.
  3. Tourists crowd the old quarter every summer.

4. Rooms provided for someone to live in. (жильё, помещение для проживания; казарма)

  1. The crew sleeps in cramped quarters below deck.
  2. Officers' quarters are on the north side of the base.
  3. Five of us spent the winter in close quarters.

5. One of the four periods a football or basketball game is divided into. (четверть (период матча))

  1. They scored twice in the fourth quarter.
  2. He sat out the first quarter with a sore ankle.
  3. The game was tied at the end of the third quarter.

check#

Pronunciation
/tʃɛk/ · CHEK
Русский
чек (банковский); счёт (в ресторане); проверка; галочка; сдерживающий фактор, ограничение
Plural
checks
Countability
countable
Contrast
American spelling is check; British English writes cheque for the bank sense.

Check has 5 distinct senses.

1. A printed form ordering your bank to pay someone from your account. (чек (банковский))

  1. He wrote me a check for the deposit.
  2. The check bounced.
  3. They still mail paper checks.

2. The bill for a meal at a restaurant. (счёт (в ресторане))

  1. Could we get the check, please?
  2. He grabbed the check before anyone else could reach it.
  3. The check came to forty dollars before tip.

3. An act of looking at something to make sure it is right or safe. (проверка)

  1. The job requires a background check.
  2. Give the tires a quick check before you drive.
  3. A spelling check caught the mistake.

4. A small mark put beside an item to show it is correct or done. (галочка)

  1. Put a check next to every name on the list.
  2. The teacher left a red check beside each right answer.
  3. Each finished task got a check in the margin.

5. Something that limits or controls a power. (сдерживающий фактор, ограничение)

  1. The courts act as a check on the president.
  2. The whole system rests on checks and balances.
  3. Rent rules are one check on rising housing costs.

credit#

Pronunciation
/ˈkrɛdət/ · KRED-ut

Credit has 4 distinct senses.

  1. Her credit is excellent.
  2. We bought the couch on credit.

  1. He needs six more credits to graduate.
  2. The course is worth three credits.
  3. Some of her credits didn't transfer to the new school.

4. The list of names shown at the start or end of a film or show. (титры)

  1. We stayed through the credits for the extra scene.
  2. Her name is in the credits as an assistant editor.
  3. The credits rolled before anyone moved.
