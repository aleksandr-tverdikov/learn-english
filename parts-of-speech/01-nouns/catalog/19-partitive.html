Partitive nouns — how to count the uncountable#

A piece of advice, a slice of bread, a bar of soap, a pinch of salt. The uncountable noun cannot take a number, so a countable one carries it.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

An uncountable noun cannot follow a number: ✗ two advices, ✗ three breads. To count it you insert a partitive — a countable noun naming a portion, joined by of. The number then attaches to the partitive: two pieces of advice, three slices of bread.

Agreement follows the partitive, not the mass noun: a piece of information is missing, two pieces of information are missing.

The hard part is not the structure but the choice. English is fussy about which partitive goes with which noun, and the wrong one is immediately noticeable even though it is perfectly logical. A piece of furniture is right; ✗ a slice of furniture is not. A bar of soap, but ✗ a bar of shampoo. There is no rule — these are collocations, learned as pairs.

Piece is the general-purpose fallback and works with a great many abstract nouns: a piece of advice, information, news, evidence, luck, work, music, research. When you are unsure, piece is the safest choice.

piece#

Pronunciation
/piːs/ · pees
Русский
кусок; штука
Countability
countable
Agreement
follows the partitive — a piece is / two pieces are
Type
concrete and abstract
Contrast
The all-purpose partitive, and the right default for abstract nouns: a piece of advice, information, news, evidence, luck, music, research, work, furniture, equipment, cake, paper. When no more specific word comes to mind, piece is almost never wrong.

A portion or unit of something.

1. With abstract uncountables. (единица чего-либо)

  1. Let me give you a piece of advice.
  2. That's a useful piece of information.
  3. Two pieces of evidence were missing.

2. With physical materials. (кусок)

  1. Hand me a piece of paper.
  2. He cut three pieces of wood to length.
  3. There's one piece of cake left.

3. A created work. (произведение)

  1. She wrote a piece for the local paper.
  2. It's a beautiful piece of music.

A small amount or portion.

  3. He needs a bit of luck.
  4. She told me a few bits of news.

  1. I'm a bit tired.
  2. That's a bit much.

The obligatory partitive for plural-only two-part nouns: a pair of scissors, pants, jeans, glasses, binoculars, pliers. Note the agreement: a pair of scissors is on the desk — the verb follows pair, not scissors.

A set of two matching things, or one object with two joined parts.

1. With plural-only objects. (пара — о парных предметах)

  1. Hand me a pair of scissors.
  2. She bought two pairs of jeans.
  3. A pair of glasses was left on the table.

2. Two matching items. (пара)

  1. He owns six pairs of shoes.
  2. One pair of socks was missing.

3. Two people or animals together. (пара)

  1. A pair of hawks nests on the roof.
