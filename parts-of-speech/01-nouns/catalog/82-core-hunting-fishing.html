Core vocabulary: hunting and fishing#

The everyday nouns of hunting and fishing — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

hunting#

Pronunciation
/ˈhʌntɪŋ/ · HUN-ting
Русский
охота
Countability
uncountable

The activity of chasing and killing wild animals for food or sport.

  1. Hunting is a big part of life in that part of the state.
  2. He gave up hunting after his knees went bad.
  3. Deer hunting brings a lot of money into small towns every fall.

  3. She's a better hunter than anyone else in the family.

hunt#

Pronunciation
/hʌnt/ · HUNT
Русский
охота (выезд, вылазка); поиски, розыск
Plural
hunts
Countability
countable

Hunt has 2 distinct senses.

1. One trip or occasion of hunting animals. (охота (выезд, вылазка))

  1. They're planning an elk hunt in Montana this fall.
  2. The morning hunt ended with nothing but wet boots.
  3. He's been on a dozen hunts and never fired a shot.

2. A search for something or someone that is hard to find. (поиски, розыск)

  1. The hunt for a new principal took all spring.
  2. Police called off the hunt at midnight.
  3. We're on the hunt for a bigger apartment.

decoy#

Pronunciation
/ˈdiːkɔɪ/ · DEE-koy
Русский
чучело (подсадная птица), приманка; приманка, отвлекающий манёвр
Plural
decoys
Countability
countable
Contrast
The noun is usually stressed on the first syllable (DEE-koy); as a verb the stress often moves to the second (di-KOY).

Decoy has 2 distinct senses.

1. A model of a bird set out on the water to draw real birds within range. (чучело (подсадная птица), приманка)

  1. We set out two dozen decoys before sunrise.
  2. One decoy had come loose and drifted off.
  3. He carves wooden decoys in the winter.

2. A person or thing used to lure someone into a trap or draw attention away. (приманка, отвлекающий манёвр)

  1. The parked van was a decoy while the real truck slipped out the back.
  2. Police used an unmarked car as a decoy.
  3. The fake email was a decoy to get her password.

  2. Her dog got a paw caught in a trap.

  1. Put a bucket under the trap before you loosen it.
  2. My wedding ring was sitting in the trap.
  3. The traps dry out in a house nobody lives in.

Pointer has 3 distinct senses.

1. A hunting dog that freezes and points its nose at hidden birds. (пойнтер)

  1. The pointer locked up at the edge of the corn.
  2. He runs two pointers on the prairie every October.
  3. A good pointer holds until the hunter walks in.

  1. Can you give me a few pointers on my resume?

  3. Her pointers about interviewing turned out to be useful.

3. A stick, needle, or arrow used to show where something is. (указка; стрелка, указатель)

  1. He tapped the map with a wooden pointer.
  2. Move the pointer over the icon and click.
  3. The dial's pointer stopped just below the red zone.
