# Core vocabulary: the city

> The everyday nouns of the city — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### city

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪti/ &middot; *SIH-tee*
**Русский:** город (крупный)
**Plural:** *cities*
**Countability:** countable

A large and important town where many people live and work.

1. She moved to the *city* right after college.
2. Both *cities* have great public transportation.
3. The whole *city* lost power for six hours.

---

### town

**Pronunciation:** /taʊn/ &middot; *TOWN*
**Русский:** город (небольшой), городок
**Plural:** *towns*
**Countability:** countable

A place with houses, stores and streets that is smaller than a city.

1. I grew up in a small *town* in Ohio.
2. There are two *towns* between here and the lake.
3. The whole *town* shows up for the parade.

### village

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɪlɪdʒ/ &middot; *VIH-lij*
**Русский:** деревня, село
**Plural:** *villages*
**Countability:** countable

A very small community in the countryside, smaller than a town.

1. Her grandparents still live in the *village*.
2. We drove through three tiny *villages* on the way.
3. The *village* has one store and one gas pump.

### downtown

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdaʊnˈtaʊn/ &middot; *down-TOWN*
**Русский:** центр города, деловой центр
**Plural:** *downtowns*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** American. British speakers say 'the city centre' or 'town centre'.

The central business area of an American city.

1. Parking *downtown* costs a fortune.
2. Portland has one of the most walkable *downtowns* in the country.
3. The new stadium brought life back to *downtown*.

**Русский:** пригород
**Plural:** *suburbs*
**Countability:** countable

A residential area outside the center of a city.

### neighborhood

**Pronunciation:** /ˈneɪbərhʊd/ &middot; *NAY-bur-huud*
**Русский:** район (жилой), микрорайон
**Plural:** *neighborhoods*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** American spelling. British English writes 'neighbourhood'.

### block

**Pronunciation:** /blɑːk/ &middot; *BLAHK*
**Русский:** квартал (между двумя улицами)
**Plural:** *blocks*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Americans measure walking distance in blocks; British English has no everyday equivalent.

The stretch of a street between two cross streets, or the buildings on it.

1. The pharmacy is two *blocks* from here.
2. They live on the same *block* as my sister.
3. We walked around the *block* to cool off.

### borough

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɜːroʊ/ &middot; *BUR-oh*
**Русский:** округ города, боро; район (административный)
**Plural:** *boroughs*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** The 'gh' is silent: it rhymes with 'furrow', not with 'bore'.

Borough has 2 distinct senses.

**1. One of the administrative divisions that a large city is split into.** (округ города, боро)

1. Queens is the largest *borough* by area.
2. All five *boroughs* were under a flood warning.
3. He's lived in the same *borough* his whole life.

**2. A self-governing district of a large city or state.** (район (административный))

4. Brooklyn is the most populous *borough*.
5. All five *boroughs* voted the same way.
6. The *borough* runs its own sanitation department.
