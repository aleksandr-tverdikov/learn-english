# When *-f* becomes *-ves*

> *Knife → knives*, *leaf → leaves*. The final /f/ voices to /v/ — but only for about two dozen words, and *roof*, *chief*, and *belief* are not among them.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A noun ending in *-f* or *-fe* sometimes changes it to *-ves* in the plural. The sound change is the real event: voiceless /f/ becomes voiced /v/ between two voiced sounds. English spelling then follows the sound.

The catch is that the change stopped being productive centuries ago, so **there is no rule you can apply to a new word** — there is only a list. Roughly two dozen nouns take *-ves*; every other *-f* noun takes a plain *-s*. The most common learner error is over-applying the pattern: ✗ *rooves*, ✗ *chieves*, ✗ *believes*. This file gives the *-ves* list first, then the traps that look like they belong to it and do not.

### knife

**Pronunciation:** /naɪf/ &middot; *nyfe* — the *k* is silent
**Русский:** нож
**Plural:** *knives* /naɪvz/ &middot; *nyvez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the knives are in the drawer*
**Pattern:** *-fe* → *-ves* &middot; **Group:** 4
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *knifes* is wrong as a noun plural — but it is right as a **verb**: *he knifes through the water*. The silent *k* survives from Old English, where it was pronounced.

The cutting tool or weapon.

**1. The cutting implement.** (нож)

1. Hand me the bread *knife*.
2. All the *knives* need sharpening.
3. He cut the rope with a pocket *knife*.

**2. A weapon.** (нож как оружие)

4. The man was carrying a *knife*.
5. Police recovered two *knives* at the scene.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. You could cut the tension with a *knife*.
7. She went under the *knife* last Tuesday.
8. That remark was a *knife* in the back.

### life

**Pronunciation:** /laɪf/ &middot; *lyfe*
**Русский:** жизнь
**Plural:** *lives* /laɪvz/ &middot; *lyvez*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in the abstract
**Agreement:** plural verb — *their lives were changed*
**Pattern:** *-fe* → *-ves* &middot; **Group:** 4
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** The plural noun *lives* /laɪvz/ and the verb *lives* /lɪvz/ (*she lives here*) are spelled identically and pronounced differently. Long /aɪ/ for the noun, short /ɪ/ for the verb.

The condition of being alive; the period of someone's existence; a way of living.

**1. The state of being alive.** Often uncountable. (жизнь)

1. Doctors fought to save his *life*.
2. Three *lives* were lost in the fire.
3. There is no *life* on that moon.

**2. The period of someone's existence.** (жизнь как срок)

4. She spent her whole *life* in the same town.
5. Their *lives* changed completely after the move.

**3. A way of living.** (образ жизни)

6. He wanted a quieter *life*.
7. City *life* never suited them.

**4. Energy, liveliness.** Uncountable. (оживление, энергия)

8. There was no *life* in the room at all.

**5. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

9. Not on your *life*.
10. She's the *life* of the party.
