Core vocabulary: food and meals#

The everyday nouns of food and meals — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

food#

Pronunciation
/fuːd/ · FOOD
Русский
еда, пища
Countability
uncountable

Anything people or animals eat to stay alive.

  1. We spent way too much on food this month.
  2. The food at that place is nothing special.
  3. There's plenty of food in the fridge.

meal#

Pronunciation
/miːl/ · MEEL
Русский
приём пищи, еда; мука грубого помола, крупа
Plural
meals
Countability
countable

Meal has 2 distinct senses.

1. An occasion when food is eaten, such as breakfast or dinner. (приём пищи, еда)

  1. Dinner is the only meal we eat together.
  2. He hasn't had a hot meal in days.
  3. Skipping meals isn't going to help.

2. Grain that has been coarsely ground. (мука грубого помола, крупа)

  1. Stir the meal slowly into boiling salted water.
  2. He works bone meal into the soil before planting.
  3. The mill grinds oats into a coarse meal.

Countability

course#

Pronunciation
/kɔːrs/ · KORS
Русский
блюдо (в составе обеда), подача; блюдо (в составе обеда: первое, второе)
Plural
courses
Countability
countable
Contrast
Sounds exactly like coarse (грубый) — only the spelling differs.

Course has 2 distinct senses.

1. One of the separate parts of a meal, served one after another. (блюдо (в составе обеда), подача)

  1. The main course was salmon.
  2. Dinner was three courses and took two hours.
  3. Soup came first, then the fish course.

2. One part of a meal served separately from the others. (блюдо (в составе обеда: первое, второе))

  1. The main course was salmon.
  2. Dinner was five courses and took three hours.
  3. We skipped the dessert course.

Pronunciation

Русский

Plural

Countability
countable

Dish has 2 distinct senses.

1. A particular kind of prepared food. (блюдо (кушанье))

  1. Lasagna is her signature dish.
  2. Each of us brought a dish to the potluck.
  3. The menu has a few vegetarian dishes.

2. A shallow container for serving or eating food. (тарелка, блюдо)

  1. Whose turn is it to do the dishes?
  2. He served the pasta in a shallow dish.
  3. Just leave the dish to soak.

recipe#

Pronunciation
/ˈrɛsəpi/ · RES-uh-pee
Русский
рецепт (кулинарный); верный путь к чему-л. (рецепт успеха, катастрофы)
Plural
recipes
Countability
countable
Contrast
Three syllables, RES-uh-pee — the final e is pronounced, so it does not rhyme with ripe.

Recipe has 2 distinct senses.

1. A set of instructions for cooking a particular dish. (рецепт (кулинарный))

  1. Can you send me the recipe for that soup?
  2. I followed the recipe exactly and it still came out flat.
  3. She has a whole box of her mother's recipes.

2. A set of conditions likely to bring about a particular result. (верный путь к чему-л. (рецепт успеха, катастрофы))

  1. Cutting the budget now is a recipe for disaster.
  2. Long hours and no sleep are a recipe for mistakes.
  3. There's no single recipe for success in this business.

Portion has 2 distinct senses.

  1. A large portion of the budget goes to salaries.
  2. Only a small portion of the land is farmed.
  3. She donates a portion of every paycheck.

picnic#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɪknɪk/ · PIK-nik
Русский
пикник; лёгкое дело, приятное занятие (обычно с отрицанием)
Plural
picnics
Countability
countable

Picnic has 2 distinct senses.

1. A meal eaten outdoors, away from home. (пикник)

  1. We had a picnic by the lake.
  2. Rain ruined the picnic.
  3. They pack the same picnic every year.

2. An easy or pleasant experience, almost always used in the negative. (лёгкое дело, приятное занятие (обычно с отрицанием))

  1. Recovering from the surgery was no picnic.
  2. Two weeks with three toddlers is no picnic.
  3. Moving in August was no picnic, believe me.

Staple has 2 distinct senses.

  2. He drove a staple right through the cable.

Wheat has 2 distinct senses.

1. A grain used to make most bread and pasta. (пшеница)

  1. This bread is made from whole wheat.

corn#

Pronunciation
/kɔːrn/ · KORN
Русский
кукуруза; мозоль (на пальце ноги)
Countability
uncountable
Contrast
In the US corn means кукуруза; the British call that maize, and their corn means grain in general.

Corn has 2 distinct senses.

1. A tall grain plant with large yellow kernels, eaten as a vegetable. (кукуруза)

  1. We grilled corn on the cob.
  2. The fields around here are all corn.
  3. Add a can of corn to the soup.

2. A small hard painful patch of thickened skin on a toe. (мозоль (на пальце ноги))

  1. New shoes gave him a corn on his little toe.
  2. She soaked her foot to soften the corn.
  3. The pharmacy sells pads for corns.

  1. He ordered a rye on the rocks.
  2. The bar keeps three kinds of rye.
  3. This cocktail works better with rye than bourbon.

dough#

Pronunciation
/doʊ/ · DOH
Русский

Countability
uncountable
Contrast
The ough is a plain OH — DOH, exactly like doe.

Dough has 2 distinct senses.

1. A thick mixture of flour and liquid that is baked into bread. (тесто)

  1. Let the dough rise for an hour.
  2. The dough was too sticky to work with.
  3. She rolled the dough into a ball.

2. A thick mixture of flour and liquid, kneaded before baking. (тесто (густое))

  1. Let the dough rise for an hour.
  2. The dough is too sticky — add a little more flour.
  3. She rolled the dough out thin.

Batter has 2 distinct senses.

  1. Pour the batter into the hot pan.

  1. The batter fouled off six pitches in a row.
  2. He struck out the first three batters.
  3. The batter stepped out of the box.

  1. Her brother gave the toast at the wedding.

  3. There were three toasts before the food came out.
