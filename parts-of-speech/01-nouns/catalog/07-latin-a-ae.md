# Latin *-a* → *-ae*

> *Larva → larvae*, *vertebra → vertebrae*. A small group, and for several of them the plain English *-s* has already won.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Latin first-declension nouns ending in *-a* took *-ae* in the plural, pronounced /iː/ in American English — *larvae* is *LAR-vee*, *vertebrae* is *VUR-tuh-bree*. English kept the ending mostly in **biology and anatomy**, where the Latin plural is still the professional norm.

Outside those fields the pattern is retreating. *Formulas* has almost completely displaced *formulae* in American English, *antennas* is standard for anything electronic, and only *larvae*, *vertebrae*, and *algae* still resist. A useful test: if the word belongs to a laboratory, expect *-ae*; if it belongs to daily life, expect *-s*.

---

### larva

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlɑːrvə/ &middot; *LAR-vuh*
**Русский:** личинка
**Plural:** *larvae* /ˈlɑːrviː/ &middot; *LAR-vee*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the larvae hatch in spring*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** ✗ *larvas* is not used in scientific or general writing
**Contrast:** This is one of the *-ae* plurals with no regular competitor at all. The adjective is *larval*.

The immature form of an insect or other animal.

**1. The immature stage.** (личинка)

1. The *larva* burrows into the wood.
2. Mosquito *larvae* live in standing water.
3. Thousands of *larvae* hatched overnight.
4. A single *larva* can destroy an entire leaf.
5. The *larvae* were collected for the study.

**See also:** [vertebra](#vertebra) &middot; [alga](#alga)

### vertebra

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɜːrtəbrə/ &middot; *VUR-tuh-bruh*
**Русский:** позвонок
**Plural:** *vertebrae* /ˈvɜːrtəbreɪ/ &middot; *VUR-tuh-bray* — also /-briː/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the vertebrae are fused*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** both /-breɪ/ and /-briː/ are accepted in American English
**Contrast:** ✗ *A vertebrae* for one bone is a very common error — the singular is *vertebra*, without the *e*. The adjective is *vertebral*, and *vertebrate* is a separate noun meaning an animal with a backbone.

One of the bones of the spine.

**1. A spinal bone.** (позвонок)

1. He cracked a *vertebra* in the accident.
2. The human spine has thirty-three *vertebrae*.
3. Two *vertebrae* had fused together.
4. The lowest *vertebra* takes the most load.
5. X-rays showed damage to several *vertebrae*.

**See also:** [larva](#larva)

### alga

**Pronunciation:** /ˈælɡə/ &middot; *AL-guh*
**Русский:** водоросль
**Plural:** *algae* /ˈældʒiː/ &middot; *AL-jee*
**Countability:** countable, and usually used as a plural mass noun
**Agreement:** plural verb — *algae are blooming in the lake*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** The singular *alga* is almost never used — in practice the word is *algae*, and the *g* changes sound between them: /ɡ/ in *AL-guh*, /dʒ/ in *AL-jee*. Most speakers treat *algae* as a mass noun taking a singular verb in casual speech: *algae is covering the pond*.

Simple photosynthetic organisms living mainly in water.

**1. The organisms.** (водоросли)

1. Green *algae* covered the surface of the pond.
2. A single *alga* is invisible without a microscope.
3. *Algae* bloom when the water warms.
4. The tank needs cleaning — the *algae* are back.
5. Some *algae* produce toxins dangerous to dogs.

**See also:** [larva](#larva) &middot; [amoeba](#amoeba)

### formula

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːrmjələ/ &middot; *FOR-myuh-luh*
**Русский:** формула; смесь для детского питания
**Plural:** *formulas* — usual &middot; *formulae* /ˈfɔːrmjəliː/ in mathematics
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in the baby-food sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the formulas are equivalent*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae*, now mostly replaced by *-s* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *formulas* dominates American English; *formulae* survives in mathematical writing
**Contrast:** Both are correct, but *formulas* is now the default even in most technical prose. In American English *formula* also means infant milk, and in that sense it is uncountable: *we're out of formula*.

A rule expressed in symbols; a fixed method; also infant milk.

**1. A mathematical or chemical rule.** (формула)

1. Apply the quadratic *formula*.
2. Both *formulas* give the same answer.
3. The book lists all the relevant *formulae*.

**2. A fixed method or recipe for success.** (рецепт, схема)

4. There is no *formula* for a good marriage.
5. The studio keeps reusing the same *formula*.

**3. Infant milk.** Uncountable, American English. (детская смесь)

6. The baby takes *formula* at night.

**See also:** [antenna](#antenna)

### antenna

**Pronunciation:** /ænˈtɛnə/ &middot; *an-TEN-uh*
**Русский:** антенна; усик (насекомого)
**Plural:** *antennas* (electronics) &middot; *antennae* /ænˈtɛniː/ (biology)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the antennas were replaced*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae*, split by sense &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *antennas* (radio, television) &middot; *antennae* (insects, and figuratively)
**Contrast:** The plural depends on the meaning, and this split is consistent. Metal on a roof: *antennas*. Feelers on a bug: *antennae*. The figurative sense — sensitivity to something — takes *antennae*.

A metal device for sending or receiving signals; also a sensory feeler on an insect.

**1. A radio or television aerial.** (антенна)

1. The *antenna* on the roof came down in the storm.
2. Both *antennas* need to point the same way.
3. Cell *antennas* are hidden inside the steeple.

**2. An insect's feeler.** (усик)

4. The beetle waved its *antennae*.
5. A moth's *antennae* detect scent from miles away.

**3. Figuratively — sensitivity to something.** Always *antennae*. (чутьё)

6. Her political *antennae* are extremely good.

**See also:** [formula](#formula)

### nebula

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɛbjələ/ &middot; *NEB-yuh-luh*
**Русский:** туманность
**Plural:** *nebulae* /ˈnɛbjəliː/ &middot; *NEB-yuh-lee* — also *nebulas*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the nebulae are visible in infrared*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *nebulae* is preferred in astronomy; *nebulas* is accepted
**Contrast:** The related adjective *nebulous* means vague — a figurative extension of 'cloudy'.

A cloud of gas and dust in space.

**1. The astronomical object.** (туманность)

1. The Orion *Nebula* is visible with binoculars.
2. Several *nebulae* appear in the same field of view.
3. A *nebula* is where new stars form.
4. These *nebulae* glow in ultraviolet light.
5. The telescope photographed a planetary *nebula*.

**See also:** [alga](#alga)

### amoeba

**Pronunciation:** /əˈmiːbə/ &middot; *uh-MEE-buh*
**Русский:** амёба
**Plural:** *amoebas* — usual &middot; *amoebae* /əˈmiːbiː/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the amoebas divide*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae*, now mostly *-s* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *amoebas* is commoner in American English; the British spelling is *amoeba* or *amœba*
**Contrast:** Both plurals are correct. The adjective *amoebic* appears in *amoebic dysentery*.

The single-celled organism that moves by changing shape.

**1. The organism.** (амёба)

1. An *amoeba* has no fixed shape.
2. The slide showed dozens of *amoebas*.
3. *Amoebae* reproduce by simple division.
4. A rare *amoeba* caused the infection.

**2. Figuratively — something shapeless.** (нечто аморфное)

5. The committee is an *amoeba* — it changes shape every month.

**See also:** [alga](#alga) &middot; [bacterium](08-latin-um-a.md#bacterium)

### alumna

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlʌmnə/ &middot; *uh-LUM-nuh*
**Русский:** выпускница
**Plural:** *alumnae* /əˈlʌmniː/ &middot; *uh-LUM-nee*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the alumnae established the fund*
**Pattern:** Latin *-a* → *-ae* &middot; **Group:** 7
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** the informal sex-neutral *alum* / *alums* is now very common in American usage
**Contrast:** Four forms to keep straight, and the two plurals differ by a single sound: *alumni* /-naɪ/ for men or a mixed group, *alumnae* /-niː/ for women only. Mixed groups always take *alumni*.

A woman who is a former student of a school or college.

**1. A female graduate.** (выпускница)

1. She is an *alumna* of the same college.
2. The *alumnae* organized the reunion.
3. Three *alumnae* sit on the board.
4. As an *alumna*, she gets the newsletter.
5. Both *alumnae* spoke at the ceremony.

**See also:** [alumnus](06-latin-us-i.md#alumnus)
