# Core vocabulary: money and finance

> The everyday nouns of money and finance — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

### dollar

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdɑːlər/ &middot; *DAH-lur*
**Русский:** доллар
**Plural:** *dollars*
**Countability:** countable

The basic unit of money in the United States and several other countries.

1. I found a *dollar* in the parking lot.
2. He makes about ninety thousand *dollars* a year.
3. The *dollar* gained against the euro this week.

**Plural:** *cents*

### account

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkaʊnt/ &middot; *uh-KOWNT*
**Русский:** счёт (банковский); счёт; клиент (в работе)
**Plural:** *accounts*
**Countability:** countable

Account has 2 distinct senses.

**1. An arrangement that lets you keep money at a bank.** (счёт (банковский))

1. I opened a checking *account* last week.
2. There's nothing left in my *account*.
3. They froze both *accounts* during the investigation.

**2. An arrangement with a bank or company, or a client a company works for.** (счёт; клиент (в работе))

4. She handles our biggest *account*.
5. The money went into the wrong *account*.
6. He lost two *accounts* in one quarter.

**2. A slip of paper proving that something was paid for.** (чек, квитанция)

4. Keep the *receipt* in case you need to return it.
5. I can't get reimbursed without a *receipt*.
6. She had a shoebox full of *receipts*.
