# Plural-only nouns (*pluralia tantum*)

> *Scissors*, *jeans*, *clothes*. Always plural, always a plural verb, and no singular exists — to count them you need *a pair of*.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These nouns exist only in the plural. There is no ✗ *a scissor*, no ✗ *a jean*, no ✗ *a clothe*. They take a plural verb — *the scissors **are** on the desk* — and they cannot follow *a* or a number directly.

Two families account for nearly all of them:

**Two-part objects.** Tools and garments made of two joined halves: *scissors*, *pliers*, *tongs*, *tweezers*, *binoculars*, *glasses*, *pants*, *jeans*, *shorts*, *pajamas*. These are counted with **a pair of** — *a pair of scissors*, *two pairs of jeans* — and note that *pair* is what the verb then agrees with: *a pair of scissors **is** missing*.

**Collections and remainders.** Nouns naming an aggregate: *clothes*, *goods*, *belongings*, *savings*, *surroundings*, *remains*, *outskirts*, *premises*, *stairs*, *thanks*, *congratulations*. These take no *pair of*; you simply use them plural, or find a different word — *a garment* for one item of *clothes*, *a step* for one of the *stairs*.

### scissors

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪzərz/ &middot; *SIZ-urz*
**Русский:** ножницы
**Countability:** plural only — no singular
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the scissors are blunt*
**Counted with:** *a pair of scissors*, *two pairs of scissors*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *a scissor* and ✗ *two scissors* are both wrong. Say *a pair of scissors*. When *pair* is the head, the verb goes singular: *a pair of scissors **is** missing*.

The two-bladed cutting tool.

**1. The tool.** (ножницы)

1. The *scissors* are in the top drawer.
2. Hand me a pair of *scissors*.
3. These *scissors* don't cut anything anymore.
4. She keeps three pairs of *scissors* in the studio.
5. Kitchen *scissors* work better for that.

**See also:** [pliers](#pliers) &middot; [pants](#pants)

### pants

**Pronunciation:** /pænts/ &middot; *pants*
**Русский:** брюки, штаны
**Countability:** plural only — no singular
**Agreement:** plural verb — *these pants are too long*
**Counted with:** *a pair of pants*, *two pairs of pants*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** American *pants* = British *trousers*; British *pants* means underwear
**Contrast:** ✗ *a pant* is wrong except in the clothing trade, where *pant* appears as a singular in catalogs. Note the transatlantic trap: asking a British person about your *pants* is asking about your underwear.

The garment covering the legs.

**1. The garment.** (брюки)

1. These *pants* are too long.
2. He bought two pairs of *pants*.
3. Your *pants* are in the dryer.
4. She wore black *pants* and a gray sweater.

**2. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

5. He was caught with his *pants* down.
6. She wears the *pants* in that family.

### clothes

**Pronunciation:** /kloʊz/ &middot; *klohz* — usually one syllable, rhyming with *close*
**Русский:** одежда
**Countability:** plural only — no singular
**Agreement:** plural verb — *my clothes are still wet*
**Counted with:** *an item of clothing*, *a garment* — never ✗ *a pair of clothes*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *a clothe* does not exist, and ✗ *clothes is* is wrong. For one item, switch to the uncountable [clothing](13-uncountable.md#clothing): *an item of clothing*. Most Americans pronounce this as one syllable, identical to the verb *close*.

Garments worn on the body.

**1. Garments.** (одежда)

1. My *clothes* are still in the dryer.
2. She packed enough *clothes* for a week.
3. All his *clothes* smell like smoke.
4. Warm *clothes* are essential up there.
5. He spends too much money on *clothes*.

### goods

**Pronunciation:** /ɡʊdz/ &middot; *guudz*
**Русский:** товары
**Countability:** plural only in this sense — no singular
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the goods were delivered*
**Counted with:** *an item of goods* is unnatural — use *a product*, *an item*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *a good* meaning one product is wrong in ordinary English, though economists do write *a public good*. The everyday singular is *a product* or *an item*.

Articles for sale or transport.

**1. Merchandise.** (товары)

1. The *goods* were delivered on Friday.
2. Stolen *goods* were found in the garage.
3. Electronic *goods* are taxed differently.
4. All *goods* must clear customs.

**2. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

5. He finally delivered the *goods*.
6. Police caught her with the *goods*.

### savings

**Pronunciation:** /ˈseɪvɪŋz/ &middot; *SAY-vingz*
**Русский:** сбережения
**Countability:** plural only in this sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *my savings are gone*
**Counted with:** *an amount of savings*
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *a saving* exists meaning a reduction in cost — *a saving of ten percent*
**Contrast:** Money set aside is always *savings*, plural. But *a saving* meaning an economy or reduction is countable and singular: *that's a saving of forty dollars*. Americans often say *a savings* in that sense, which sounds odd to British ears.

Money set aside; also reductions in cost.

**1. Money set aside.** Plural only. (сбережения)

1. Their *savings* are in a separate account.
2. He spent his entire *savings* on the trip.
3. My *savings* aren't earning much interest.

**2. A reduction in cost.** Countable. (экономия)

4. That's a *savings* of nearly two hundred dollars.
5. The changes produced real *savings*.

### premises

**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɛmɪsɪz/ &middot; *PREM-ih-siz*
**Русский:** помещение, здание
**Countability:** plural only in this sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the premises are locked*
**Counted with:** — use *a building*, *a property*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *a premise* exists, but means a proposition in an argument — a completely different word
**Contrast:** The two senses are unrelated. A building is *the premises*, always plural, even for one building. An assumption in logic is *a premise*, singular, plural *premises* — spelled the same, so context does all the work.

A building and its grounds; separately, a proposition in an argument.

**1. A building and its land.** Plural only. (помещение)

1. No smoking on the *premises*.
2. The *premises* were searched at dawn.
3. They were escorted off the *premises*.
4. The company moved to new *premises* last year.

**2. A proposition in an argument.** Countable, singular *premise*. (посылка, предпосылка)

5. The argument rests on a false *premise*.
6. Both *premises* are questionable.

**See also:** [outskirts](#outskirts)

### stairs

**Pronunciation:** /stɛrz/ &middot; *stairz*
**Русский:** лестница, ступени
**Countability:** plural in normal use
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the stairs are steep*
**Counted with:** *a flight of stairs*; one step is *a stair* or *a step*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *a stair* for one step exists but *step* is far commoner
**Contrast:** A staircase is *the stairs*, plural, and takes a plural verb. Use *a flight of stairs* to count staircases: *three flights of stairs*.

A set of steps between levels.

**1. The set of steps.** (лестница)

1. The *stairs* are steep and narrow.
2. She fell down the *stairs*.
3. Take the *stairs* — the elevator is broken.
4. There are two flights of *stairs* to the attic.
5. He was waiting at the top of the *stairs*.

### thanks

**Pronunciation:** /θæŋks/ &middot; *thangks*
**Русский:** благодарность, спасибо
**Countability:** plural only — no singular in this sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *thanks are due to everyone involved*
**Counted with:** — use *a word of thanks*
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** the uncountable *gratitude* covers the same ground formally
**Contrast:** ✗ *a thank* is wrong except in the compound *a thank-you*. As an interjection *thanks* is fixed and never singular.

An expression of gratitude.

**1. Gratitude expressed.** (благодарность)

1. Our *thanks* go to everyone who helped.
2. She got no *thanks* for any of it.
3. *Thanks* are due to the whole crew.

**2. As an interjection.** (спасибо)

4. *Thanks* for the ride.
5. No *thanks*, I've already eaten.

**3. thanks to — because of.** Fixed phrase. (благодаря)

6. *Thanks* to her, we finished on time.
