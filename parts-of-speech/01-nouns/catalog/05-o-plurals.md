# Nouns ending in *-o*

> *Potato → potatoes*, but *photo → photos*. The ending decides, and so does where the word came from.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A noun ending in *-o* takes either *-s* or *-es*, and there is a workable rule with a short list of exceptions on top of it.

**Vowel + o → *-os*.** *Radio → radios*, *studio → studios*, *video → videos*, *zoo → zoos*. No exceptions worth worrying about.

**Consonant + o → usually *-oes***, but only for a small core of well-established English words: *potato, tomato, hero, echo, veto, torpedo, embargo*. Those seven are the ones to memorize, because they take *-es* and nothing else.

**Everything else that ends in a consonant + o takes *-os*** if it is recent, foreign, musical, or a shortening: *piano, solo, taco, avocado, photo, memo, kilo*. A middle group genuinely allows both — *volcano, tornado, mosquito, cargo, motto, halo, zero* — and American English tends to prefer *-oes* for these in careful writing and *-os* in casual writing. Neither is an error.

### potato

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈteɪtoʊ/ &middot; *puh-TAY-toh*
**Русский:** картофель, картошка
**Plural:** *potatoes* /pəˈteɪtoʊz/ — always *-es*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as a foodstuff
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the potatoes are boiling*
**Pattern:** consonant + o → *-oes* &middot; **Group:** 5
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *potatos* is wrong — this is the most notorious *-oes* word in American English. There is no *e* in the singular, so the plural adds one.

The starchy tuber.

**1. The vegetable.** (картофель)

1. Peel four *potatoes* for the stew.
2. A baked *potato* comes with the steak.
3. We planted *potatoes* along the back fence.

**2. As a dish or foodstuff.** Often uncountable in this use. (картошка как еда)

4. Do you want rice or *potato* with that?
5. Mashed *potatoes* are the only thing he'll eat.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. Immigration is a political hot *potato*.
7. He spent the whole weekend as a couch *potato*.
8. That's small *potatoes* compared to the real problem.

**See also:** [tomato](#tomato) &middot; [hero](#hero)

### hero

A room or company where creative work is done; also a small apartment.

**1. A workroom for an artist or recording.** (студия)

1. The band spent six months in the *studio*.
2. Both *studios* are fully booked.
3. She rents a painting *studio* downtown.

**2. A film or television company.** (киностудия)

4. The *studio* pulled funding two weeks in.
5. All the major *studios* passed on the script.

**3. A one-room apartment.** American usage. (студия, однокомнатная квартира)

6. He lives in a *studio* near the university.

### video

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvɪdioʊ/ &middot; *VID-ee-oh*
**Русский:** видео, видеозапись
**Plural:** *videos* /ˈvɪdioʊz/ — always *-s*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as a medium
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the videos are uploading*
**Pattern:** vowel + o → *-s* &middot; **Group:** 5
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *videoes* is wrong.

A recording of moving images.

**1. A recorded clip or film.** (видеозапись)

1. She posted a *video* of the whole thing.
2. All the *videos* are on the shared drive.
3. Watch this *video* before the meeting.

**2. The medium generally.** Uncountable. (видео)

4. The course is delivered entirely by *video*.
5. We shot it on *video* rather than film.

**3. Attributive.** (видео-)

6. They joined by *video* call from three countries.
