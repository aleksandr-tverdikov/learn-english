Core vocabulary: business and commerce#

The everyday nouns of business and commerce — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

trade#

Pronunciation
/treɪd/ · TRAYD
Русский
торговля; ремесло
Plural
trades
Countability
both — countable and uncountable with different meanings

The buying and selling of goods, or a skilled job learned by training.

  1. Trade between the two countries has doubled since 2019.
  2. He learned a trade instead of going to college.
  3. The new tariffs hurt small shops that live on cross-border trade.

industry#

Pronunciation
/ˈɪndəstri/ · IN-duh-stree
Русский
отрасль; промышленность
Plural
industries
Countability
both — countable and uncountable with different meanings

A branch of business that makes one kind of product, or manufacturing as a whole.

  1. She spent twenty years in the music industry.
  2. Heavy industry still employs a lot of people up north.
  3. Several industries were hit hard by the shutdown.

corporation#

Pronunciation
/ˌkɔːrpəˈreɪʃən/ · kor-puh-RAY-shun
Русский
корпорация, акционерное общество
Plural
corporations
Countability
countable

A large business that the law treats as a single person.

  1. The corporation moved its head office to Texas.
  2. Big corporations pay far less tax than most people think.
  3. She left a huge corporation to run a bakery.

firm#

Pronunciation
/fɜːrm/ · FURM
Русский
фирма, компания
Plural
firms
Countability
countable

A company, especially one that sells professional services.

  1. He works for a law firm downtown.
  2. The firm has offices in six cities.
  3. Two accounting firms bid on the audit.

factory#

Pronunciation
/ˈfæktəri/ · FAK-tuh-ree
Русский
фабрика, завод
Plural
factories
Countability
countable

A building where goods are made by machine in large numbers.

  1. Her father worked at the factory for thirty years.
  2. The factory runs three shifts a day.
  3. Half the factories along the river closed in the nineties.

warehouse#

Pronunciation
/ˈwɛrhaʊs/ · WAIR-howss
Русский
склад
Plural
warehouses
Countability
countable

A large building where goods are stored before they are sold or shipped.

  1. Your order is still sitting in the warehouse in Memphis.
  2. They rented a warehouse out by the airport.
  3. Both warehouses are full through the holidays.

franchise#

Pronunciation
/ˈfræntʃaɪz/ · FRAN-chyze
Русский
франшиза; франчайзинговая точка
Plural
franchises
Countability
countable

The right to run a business under a big company's name, or a store run that way.

  1. He bought a pizza franchise in Tulsa.
  2. The franchise costs about two hundred thousand up front.
  3. There are over forty franchises in the state.

partnership#

Pronunciation
/ˈpɑːrtnərʃɪp/ · PART-nur-ship
Русский
партнёрство; товарищество
Plural
partnerships
Countability
countable

A business owned by two or more people, or a working tie between companies.

  1. The two of them formed a partnership right out of school.
  2. The partnership fell apart over money.
  3. We have partnerships with three local farms.

competition#

Pronunciation
/ˌkɑːmpəˈtɪʃən/ · kahm-puh-TISH-un
Русский
конкуренция; конкурс
Plural
competitions
Countability
both — countable and uncountable with different meanings

Rivalry between sellers, or an organized contest.

  1. There's a lot of competition in the coffee business.
  2. We keep an eye on what the competition is charging.
  3. She won a baking competition at the state fair.

executive#

Pronunciation
/ɪɡˈzɛkjətɪv/ · ig-ZEK-yuh-tiv
Русский
руководитель, топ-менеджер
Plural
executives
Countability
countable

A senior person who helps run a company and makes the big decisions.

  1. The executive who hired me is retiring.
  2. Three executives resigned in the same week.
  3. She's an executive at a software company.

supplier#

Pronunciation
/səˈplaɪrr/ · suh-PLY-ur
Русский
поставщик
Plural
suppliers
Countability
countable

A company that provides goods or materials to another company.

  1. Our main supplier raised prices again.
  2. We switched suppliers after the delays.
  3. The supplier ships out of Ohio.

vendor#

Pronunciation
/ˈvɛndər/ · VEN-dur
Русский
поставщик; торговец, продавец
Plural
vendors
Countability
countable

A person or company that sells something, especially to a business or on the street.

  1. A street vendor was selling hot dogs on the corner.
  2. We pay our vendors within thirty days.
  3. The software vendor handles all the updates.

Pronunciation
/ˈriːteɪlər/ · REE-tay-lur
Русский
розничный продавец, ритейлер
Plural
retailers
Countability
countable

A business that sells goods directly to the public.

  1. The retailer is closing forty stores.
  2. Online retailers took most of the holiday traffic.
  3. We sell through one big retailer and our own site.

manufacturer#

Pronunciation
/ˌmænjəˈfæktʃərər/ · man-yuh-FAK-chur-ur
Русский
производитель, изготовитель
Plural
manufacturers
Countability
countable

A company that makes goods in large quantities.

  1. The manufacturer covers the parts for two years.
  2. Most of these manufacturers are overseas.
  3. Call the manufacturer before you take it apart.

buyer#

Pronunciation
/ˈbaɪrr/ · BY-ur
Русский
покупатель; закупщик
Plural
buyers
Countability
countable

Someone who buys something, or whose job is buying stock for a store.

  1. The house had three buyers interested in one weekend.
  2. She's a buyer for a department store.
  3. We finally found a buyer for the truck.

career#

Pronunciation
/kəˈrɪr/ · kuh-REER
Русский
карьера, профессиональный путь
Plural
careers
Countability
countable
Contrast
Don't mix up career (kuh-REER) with carrier (KA-ree-ur), a company that hauls freight.

The series of jobs a person holds over a working life.

  1. She spent her whole career in banking.
  2. Teaching is a good career if you can live with the pay.
  3. Both careers took off after they moved west.
