Plural-only nouns (pluralia tantum)#

Scissors, jeans, clothes. Always plural, always a plural verb, and no singular exists — to count them you need a pair of.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

These nouns exist only in the plural. There is no ✗ a scissor, no ✗ a jean, no ✗ a clothe. They take a plural verb — the scissors are on the desk — and they cannot follow a or a number directly.

Two families account for nearly all of them:

Two-part objects. Tools and garments made of two joined halves: scissors, pliers, tongs, tweezers, binoculars, glasses, pants, jeans, shorts, pajamas. These are counted with a pair of — a pair of scissors, two pairs of jeans — and note that pair is what the verb then agrees with: a pair of scissors is missing.

Collections and remainders. Nouns naming an aggregate: clothes, goods, belongings, savings, surroundings, remains, outskirts, premises, stairs, thanks, congratulations. These take no pair of; you simply use them plural, or find a different word — a garment for one item of clothes, a step for one of the stairs.

2. A reduction in cost. Countable. (экономия)

  1. That's a savings of nearly two hundred dollars.
  2. The changes produced real savings.

premises#

Pronunciation
/ˈprɛmɪsɪz/ · PREM-ih-siz
Русский
помещение, здание
Countability
plural only in this sense
Agreement
plural verb — the premises are locked
Counted with
— use a building, a property
Type
concrete
Variants
a premise exists, but means a proposition in an argument — a completely different word
Contrast
The two senses are unrelated. A building is the premises, always plural, even for one building. An assumption in logic is a premise, singular, plural premises — spelled the same, so context does all the work.

A building and its grounds; separately, a proposition in an argument.

1. A building and its land. Plural only. (помещение)

  1. No smoking on the premises.
  2. The premises were searched at dawn.
  3. They were escorted off the premises.
  4. The company moved to new premises last year.

2. A proposition in an argument. Countable, singular premise. (посылка, предпосылка)

  1. The argument rests on a false premise.
  2. Both premises are questionable.
