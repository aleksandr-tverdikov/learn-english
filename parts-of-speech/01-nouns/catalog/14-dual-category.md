# Nouns that are countable and uncountable — with different meanings

> *A hair* is one strand; *hair* is what grows on your head. *A glass* is a container; *glass* is a material. Same word, two grammars, two meanings.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Countability in English is a property of a **sense**, not of a word. A great many nouns sit in both categories, and the shift is never random — it follows one of three regular patterns:

- **Material → object made of it.** *Glass* is the substance, *a glass* is a drinking vessel. *Paper* is the material, *a paper* is a newspaper or an essay. *Iron* is the metal, *an iron* presses shirts.
- **Mass → a unit or instance of it.** *Hair* is the mass on your head, *a hair* is one strand. *Coffee* is the drink, *a coffee* is one cup. *Time* is duration, *a time* is an occasion.
- **Abstract → a specific example.** *Experience* is accumulated knowledge, *an experience* is one event. *Business* is commerce, *a business* is one company.

The practical consequence: adding or dropping *a* changes what you have said. ✗ *I found a hair in my soup* and *I found hair in my soup* are both grammatical, and they describe two very different soups.

### hair

**Pronunciation:** /hɛr/ &middot; *hair*
**Русский:** волосы; волос
**Countability:** both — uncountable as a mass, countable as single strands
**Agreement:** singular verb for the mass — *her hair is long*; plural for strands — *two hairs were found*
**Counted with:** *a strand of hair*, *a lock of hair*, *a head of hair*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** This is the textbook case. *She has beautiful hair* — the mass on her head. *There's a hair in my food* — one strand. Saying ✗ *she has beautiful hairs* describes something disturbing.

The threadlike growth on the head and body.

**1. The mass on someone's head.** Uncountable. (волосы)

1. Her *hair* is longer than it used to be.
2. He's losing his *hair*.
3. She washed her *hair* twice.

**2. A single strand.** Countable. (волос)

4. There's a *hair* in my soup.
5. Two *hairs* were recovered from the scene.
6. He pulled a gray *hair* out and threw it away.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

7. Let your *hair* down for once.
8. That was a *hair*-raising drive.
9. She won by a *hair*.

**See also:** [glass](#glass) &middot; [paper](#paper)

### glass

**Pronunciation:** /ɡlæs/ &middot; *glas*
**Русский:** стекло; стакан; очки
**Countability:** both — uncountable as a material, countable as a vessel
**Agreement:** singular for the material — *the glass is toughened*; plural for vessels — *the glasses are clean*
**Counted with:** *a pane of glass*, *a sheet of glass*, *a piece of glass*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *glasses* also means spectacles, and in that sense is plural-only — see [glasses](15-plural-only.md#glasses)
**Contrast:** Three grammars in one word. The material is uncountable; a drinking vessel is countable; and *glasses* meaning spectacles is a plural-only noun that takes a plural verb and no singular.

The transparent material; a drinking vessel; spectacles.

**1. The material.** Uncountable. (стекло)

1. The whole wall is *glass*.
2. Broken *glass* covered the sidewalk.
3. The window pane is toughened *glass*.

**2. A drinking vessel.** Countable. (стакан)

4. Pour me a *glass* of water.
5. All the *glasses* are in the dishwasher.
6. He knocked a *glass* off the table.

**3. Spectacles — glasses.** Plural only. (очки)

7. I can't read without my *glasses*.

**See also:** [paper](#paper) &middot; [glasses](15-plural-only.md#glasses)

### paper

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪpər/ &middot; *PAY-pur*
**Русский:** бумага; газета; статья
**Countability:** both — uncountable as a material, countable as a document
**Agreement:** singular for the material — *the paper is thin*; plural for documents — *the papers are due*
**Counted with:** *a sheet of paper*, *a piece of paper*, *a ream of paper*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *Give me a paper* meaning a blank sheet is wrong — say *a sheet of paper* or *a piece of paper*. *A paper* means a newspaper, an academic article, or an exam.

The material; a newspaper; a written document.

1. We're out of *paper*.
2. Bring me a sheet of *paper*.
3. The report was printed on heavy *paper*.

**2. A newspaper.** Countable. (газета)

4. He reads the *paper* every morning.
5. Both local *papers* covered the story.

**3. An academic article or essay.** Countable. (статья, работа)

6. She published three *papers* last year.
7. The *paper* is due on Monday.

**4. Official documents — papers.** (документы)

8. The border guard asked for our *papers*.

### wood

**Pronunciation:** /wʊd/ &middot; *wuud*
**Русский:** дерево (материал); лес
**Countability:** both — uncountable as a material, countable as a forest
**Agreement:** singular for the material — *the wood is dry*; plural for forests — *the woods are dark*
**Counted with:** *a piece of wood*, *a plank of wood*, *a log*
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *woods* usually means a small forest, and is used as a plural even for one
**Contrast:** ✗ *a wood* for a piece of timber is wrong — say *a piece of wood*. In American English *the woods* means the forest, and takes a plural verb even when one forest is meant: *the woods are quiet*.

The hard material from trees; a small forest.

**1. The material.** Uncountable. (древесина)

1. The table is solid *wood*.
2. We need more *wood* for the fire.
3. He carved it from a single piece of *wood*.

**2. A forest.** Usually *the woods*. (лес)

4. They walked in the *woods* all afternoon.
5. The *woods* behind the house are full of deer.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. We're not out of the *woods* yet.
7. Knock on *wood*.

### coffee

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɔːfi/ &middot; *KAW-fee*
**Русский:** кофе; чашка кофе
**Countability:** both — uncountable as a substance, countable as a serving
**Agreement:** singular for the drink — *the coffee is cold*; plural for servings — *two coffees, please*
**Counted with:** *a cup of coffee*, *a pot of coffee*, *a bag of coffee*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** Ordering *two coffees* is completely normal English — the countable sense means servings. But ✗ *I spilled a coffee on the carpet* meaning the liquid is odd; say *some coffee*. The same double life belongs to *tea*, *beer*, and *wine*.

The drink and the beans it is made from.

**1. The drink or the substance.** Uncountable. (кофе)

1. The *coffee* has gone cold.
2. Do you want some *coffee*?
3. We're out of *coffee* again.

**2. A serving of coffee.** Countable. (чашка кофе)

4. Two *coffees*, please.
5. She's already had three *coffees* today.

**3. Attributive.** (кофейный)

6. There's a *coffee* shop on every corner.

### chicken

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃɪkɪn/ &middot; *CHIK-in*
**Русский:** курица; курятина
**Countability:** both — countable as the animal, uncountable as the meat
**Agreement:** singular for the meat — *the chicken is overcooked*; plural for birds — *the chickens are in the yard*
**Counted with:** *a piece of chicken*, *a breast of chicken*
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** This animal/meat split is one of the most systematic patterns in English. Live birds are countable; the meat is uncountable. ✗ *I ate a chicken* means you ate an entire bird. Compare *lamb*, *fish*, *turkey*, and *duck*, which work the same way — and *cow/beef*, *pig/pork*, *sheep/mutton*, where English uses different words entirely.

The bird, and its meat.

**1. The living bird.** Countable. (курица)

1. They keep six *chickens* in the backyard.
2. A *chicken* got loose in the road.

**2. The meat.** Uncountable. (курятина)

3. We're having *chicken* for dinner.
4. The *chicken* is a little dry.
5. Add two pieces of *chicken* to the pan.

**3. A coward.** Informal, countable. (трус)

6. Don't be such a *chicken*.

**See also:** [fish](03-unchanged.md#fish) &middot; [coffee](#coffee)

### time

**Pronunciation:** /taɪm/ &middot; *tyme*
**Русский:** время; раз
**Countability:** both — uncountable as duration, countable as occasions
**Agreement:** singular for duration — *time is short*; plural for occasions — *three times were recorded*
**Counted with:** *a period of time*, *an amount of time*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** The two senses take different question words. *How much time?* asks about duration; *how many times?* asks about occasions. Mixing them — ✗ *how much times* — is a very common error.

Duration; an occasion; a point in the day.

**1. Duration.** Uncountable. (время)

1. We don't have much *time*.
2. *Time* passes slowly here.
3. It takes a lot of *time* to learn.

**2. An occasion.** Countable. (раз)

4. I've seen it three *times*.
5. The first *time* was the worst.
6. How many *times* do I have to ask?

**3. A period in history.** Countable, often plural. (времена)

7. In Roman *times* this was a garrison town.

**4. A point in the day.** (время суток)

8. What *time* is it?

**See also:** [experience](#experience) &middot; [room](#room)

### room

**Pronunciation:** /ruːm/ &middot; *room*
**Русский:** комната; место, пространство
**Countability:** both — countable as a space in a building, uncountable as available space
**Agreement:** singular for space — *there is room for one more*; plural for rooms — *the rooms are booked*
**Counted with:** *a lot of room*, *plenty of room*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *There is a room for one more person* means a bedroom is available. Without the article it means space is available. The difference matters at a hotel desk.

A part of a building; also available space.

**1. A part of a building.** Countable. (комната)

1. The house has four *rooms* upstairs.
2. She booked a *room* for two nights.
3. Which *room* is the meeting in?

**2. Available space.** Uncountable. (место)

4. Is there *room* for one more?
5. There isn't enough *room* in the trunk.
6. Make *room* on the table.

**3. Scope or opportunity.** Uncountable, figurative. (возможность, простор)

7. There's *room* for improvement.

**See also:** [space](#space) &middot; [time](#time)

### space

**Pronunciation:** /speɪs/ &middot; *spays*
**Русский:** пространство; место; космос
**Countability:** both — uncountable as extent, countable as a defined area
**Agreement:** singular for extent — *space is limited*; plural for areas — *the spaces are numbered*
**Counted with:** *an amount of space*, *a lot of space*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *a space* meaning the universe is wrong — that sense is always uncountable and takes no article: *travel in space*, never ✗ *in the space*.

Extent or area; the universe beyond Earth; a defined area.

**1. Available extent.** Uncountable. (место, пространство)

1. There's not enough *space* on the shelf.
2. *Space* in the city is expensive.

**2. A defined area.** Countable. (место, участок)

3. I found a parking *space* two blocks away.
4. All the *spaces* are reserved.
5. They rent office *space* downtown.

**3. The universe beyond Earth.** Uncountable, no article. (космос)

6. The telescope was launched into *space*.

### experience

**Pronunciation:** /ɪkˈspɪriəns/ &middot; *ik-SPEER-ee-uhns*
**Русский:** опыт; случай, впечатление
**Countability:** both — uncountable as accumulated knowledge, countable as a single event
**Agreement:** singular for the mass — *experience is essential*; plural for events — *those experiences shaped her*
**Counted with:** *a piece of experience* is unnatural — use *years of experience*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *He has many experiences in marketing* means he lived through many marketing events, not that he is experienced. For qualifications say *a lot of experience* — uncountable.

Accumulated knowledge from doing something; also a single event lived through.

**1. Accumulated knowledge or skill.** Uncountable. (опыт)

1. The job requires five years' *experience*.
2. She has a lot of *experience* with this software.
3. *Experience* counts more than qualifications here.

**2. A single event lived through.** Countable. (впечатление, случай)

4. It was a terrifying *experience*.
5. Those early *experiences* shaped everything he wrote.
6. Living abroad was a valuable *experience*.

### sound

**Pronunciation:** /saʊnd/ &middot; *sownd*
**Русский:** звук

Vibrations that can be heard.

### failure

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfeɪljər/ &middot; *FAYL-yur*
**Русский:** неудача; отказ
**Countability:** both — uncountable as the concept, countable as an instance
**Agreement:** singular for the concept — *failure is instructive*; plural for instances — *the failures were catalogued*
**Counted with:** *a case of failure*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** Mirrors [success](#success) exactly. Note the technical sense — *engine failure*, *kidney failure* — which is uncountable and very common.
