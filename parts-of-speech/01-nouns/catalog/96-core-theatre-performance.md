# Core vocabulary: theatre and performance

> The everyday nouns of theatre and performance — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### play

**Pronunciation:** /pleɪ/ &middot; *PLAY*
**Русский:** пьеса, спектакль
**Plural:** *plays*
**Countability:** countable

A story written to be acted out on a stage.

1. We saw a *play* about two brothers running a diner.
2. She has written six *plays*, and three of them were produced.
3. The *play* runs two hours with one intermission.

### actress

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæktrəs/ &middot; *AK-truhs*
**Русский:** актриса
**Plural:** *actresses*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Many Americans now say *actor* for women too, and reserve *actress* for award categories.

A woman who performs a part in a play, film, or television show.

1. The *actress* playing the mother is only thirty.
2. Four *actresses* read for the same part.
3. She trained as an *actress* before she started directing.

### scene

**Pronunciation:** /siːn/ &middot; *SEEN*
**Русский:** сцена, эпизод (часть акта)
**Plural:** *scenes*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Sounds exactly like *seen*, the past participle of see.

A section of a play or film that happens in one place and time.

1. The best *scene* is the argument in the kitchen.
2. We rehearsed the last two *scenes* all afternoon.
3. He forgot his lines in the opening *scene*.

### act

**Pronunciation:** /ækt/ &middot; *AKT*
**Русский:** акт, действие (пьесы); номер (эстрадный)
**Plural:** *acts*
**Countability:** countable

A main division of a play, or a short self-contained performance.

1. The first *act* runs almost an hour.
2. Most modern plays have two *acts* instead of five.
3. Her comedy *act* is about twenty minutes long.

### comedy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːmədi/ &middot; *KAH-muh-dee*
**Русский:** комедия
**Plural:** *comedies*
**Countability:** countable

A play or film meant to be funny and to end happily.

1. The theater is doing a *comedy* this spring.
2. He writes dark *comedies* about office life.
3. It started as a *comedy* and turned very sad in the last act.

**Русский:** трагедия
**Plural:** *tragedies*
**Countability:** countable

A serious play that ends unhappily for the main character.

1. Hamlet is the best known *tragedy* in English.

3. The director staged the *tragedy* in modern dress.

**Plural:** *sets*

**Pronunciation:** /kjuː/ &middot; *KYOO*
**Русский:** реплика-сигнал, сигнал к выходу
**Plural:** *cues*
**Countability:** countable

A word or signal that tells a performer or technician to do something.

1. That line is your *cue* to come on stage.

**Русский:** монолог
**Plural:** *monologues*
**Countability:** countable

A long speech by one character or performer.

### soliloquy

**Pronunciation:** /səˈlɪləkwi/ &middot; *suh-LIL-uh-kwee*
**Русский:** монолог наедине с собой
**Plural:** *soliloquies*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Stress the second syllable: suh-LIL-uh-kwee.

A speech in which a character alone on stage says his thoughts aloud.

### curtain call

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɜːrtən kɔːl/ &middot; *KUR-tuhn KAWL*
**Русский:** выход на поклон
**Plural:** *curtain calls*
**Countability:** countable

The moment at the end of a show when the cast comes back on stage to be applauded.

1. The audience was still clapping through the third *curtain call*.
2. There were five *curtain calls* on closing night.
3. She was too sick to take her *curtain call*.

**Русский:** помощник режиссёра (помреж)

The person who runs rehearsals and calls the cues during a performance.

### stage fright

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsteɪdʒ fraɪt/ &middot; *STAYJ FRYT*
**Русский:** страх сцены, боязнь сцены
**Countability:** uncountable
**Contrast:** Uncountable: *a lot of stage fright*, never *a stage fright*.

Sudden fear felt before or while performing in front of people.

1. He gets terrible *stage fright* before every show.
2. A little *stage fright* is normal, she told the kids.
3. She overcame her *stage fright* after about a year.

**Plural:** *villains*

### protagonist

### antagonist

3. The *leading man* has almost no lines in the last act.
