# Core vocabulary: war and the military

> The everyday nouns of war and the military — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

### warfare

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwɔːrfɛr/ &middot; *WAWR-fair*
**Русский:** военные действия, ведение войны
**Countability:** uncountable

The activity of fighting a war, or a particular way of fighting one.

1. Trench *warfare* defined the First World War.
2. The rebels turned to guerrilla *warfare*.
3. Chemical *warfare* is banned by treaty.

### battle

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbætəl/ &middot; *BAT-uhl*
**Русский:** битва, сражение; борьба, противостояние
**Plural:** *battles*
**Countability:** countable

Battle has 2 distinct senses.

**1. A fight between two armies in one place at one time.** (битва, сражение)

1. The *battle* lasted three days.
2. He died in *battle*.
3. Several *battles* were fought along the river.

**2. A long, hard struggle against a problem or an opponent.** (борьба, противостояние)

4. She lost her *battle* with cancer last spring.
5. The company faces a legal *battle* over the patent.
6. Getting my kids to eat vegetables is a daily *battle*.

**Русский:** конфликт; накладка в расписании
**Plural:** *conflicts*

Conflict has 2 distinct senses.

### casualty

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkæʒuəlti/ &middot; *KAZH-oo-uhl-tee*
**Русский:** потери; убитый или раненый; жертва, пострадавший (от чего-либо)
**Plural:** *casualties*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Casualties covers both the dead and the wounded, not only the dead.

Casualty has 2 distinct senses.

**1. A person killed or wounded in a war or an accident.** (потери; убитый или раненый)

1. The battle produced heavy *casualties*.
2. Civilian *casualties* rose sharply that spring.
3. Every *casualty* was flown out by helicopter.

**2. A person or thing that is harmed or lost because of some event or decision.** (жертва, пострадавший (от чего-либо))

4. The music program was the first *casualty* of the budget cuts.
5. Privacy is often a *casualty* of new technology.
6. Small bookstores were the *casualties* of that merger.
