# Plurals that look exactly like the singular

> *One sheep, two sheep.* No ending, no vowel change — only the surrounding words tell you which you mean.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

For these nouns the plural is spelled and pronounced exactly like the singular. Nothing on the word itself marks number, so the **determiner and the verb carry the whole load**: *this sheep is* versus *these sheep are*. Get the verb wrong and the sentence becomes ambiguous rather than merely incorrect.

Three groups meet here. **Animals** that people hunt, herd, or fish for — *deer*, *sheep*, *fish*, *moose*, *trout* — a pattern old enough that new game animals still join it. **Nouns already ending in *-s*** whose *-s* is part of the stem, not a plural ending: *series*, *species*, *corps*, *headquarters*. And **numerals after a number**: *three dozen eggs*, *two hundred people* — but *dozens of eggs* once the number is vague.

### sheep

**Pronunciation:** /ʃiːp/ &middot; *sheep*
**Русский:** овца, овцы
**Plural:** *sheep* — unchanged
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** depends on meaning — *this sheep is* / *these sheep are*
**Pattern:** zero plural &middot; **Group:** 3
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *sheeps* is always wrong. The meat is *mutton* and the young animal is a *lamb*, both separate words. Compare *goat*, which is perfectly regular: *goats*.

The woolly farm animal; figuratively, a person who follows without thinking.

**1. The animal.** (овца)

1. A *sheep* wandered onto the road.
2. Forty *sheep* grazed on the far slope.
3. These *sheep* are ready for shearing.
4. The farm keeps *sheep* for wool rather than meat.

**2. A follower, someone easily led.** Mildly contemptuous. (стадо, безвольные люди)

5. They follow whatever the ads tell them, like *sheep*.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. He was the black *sheep* of the family.
7. That's a wolf in *sheep*'s clothing.

### fish

**Pronunciation:** /fɪʃ/ &middot; *fish*
**Русский:** рыба
**Plural:** *fish* — unchanged &middot; *fishes* /ˈfɪʃɪz/ for species
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as food
**Agreement:** depends on meaning — *the fish is* / *the fish are*
**Pattern:** zero plural, with a marked regular plural for kinds &middot; **Group:** 3
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *fish* (individuals) &middot; *fishes* (distinct species)
**Contrast:** The two plurals mean different things. *Six fish* is six animals; *the fishes of the Caribbean* is a set of species. As food the word is uncountable: *we ate fish*, never ✗ *we ate a fish* unless you mean the whole animal.

The aquatic animal, and its flesh as food.

**1. Individual animals.** Unchanged plural. (рыба, рыбы)

1. I caught four *fish* before lunch.
2. The *fish* in this tank are all the same age.
3. A single *fish* jumped clear of the water.

**2. Species or kinds — fishes.** Biology, and formal or literary writing. (виды рыб)

4. The book catalogs the *fishes* of the Great Lakes.

**3. As food — uncountable.** (рыба как еда)

5. We're having *fish* for dinner.
6. She doesn't eat *fish* or shellfish.

**4. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

7. There are plenty more *fish* in the sea.
8. He was a big *fish* in a small pond.
9. I felt like a *fish* out of water at that party.

### series

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪriːz/ &middot; *SEER-eez*
**Русский:** серия, ряд

4. The *series* was canceled after two seasons.
5. Both *series* are streaming now.

**3. In sports — a set of games between the same teams.** (серия матчей)

### dozen

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʌzən/ &middot; *DUZ-uhn*
**Русский:** дюжина
**Plural:** *dozen* after a number &middot; *dozens* when vague
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *two dozen eggs are enough*
**Pattern:** zero plural after a numeral &middot; **Group:** 3
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** The rule is exact and catches everyone. After a specific number, no *-s* and no *of*: *three dozen eggs*. Without a number, add both: *dozens of eggs*. The same rule governs *hundred*, *thousand*, *million*, and *score*.

A group of twelve.

1. We need three *dozen* eggs for the recipe.
2. She bought two *dozen* roses.
3. A half *dozen* people were waiting.

**2. Without a number — plural, with *of*.** (десятки)

4. *Dozens* of complaints arrived that week.
5. He has *dozens* of excuses and none of them work.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. Cheap sunglasses are a dime a *dozen* around here.
7. We talked nineteen to the *dozen* the whole drive.
