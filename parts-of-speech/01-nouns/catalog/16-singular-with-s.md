# Nouns that end in *-s* but are singular

> *The news **is** good.* *Physics **is** hard.* *Measles **is** contagious.* They look plural. They are not.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These nouns end in *-s* and take a **singular verb**. The *-s* is not a plural ending — it is part of the word — so ✗ *the news are good* and ✗ *physics are difficult* are wrong.

Three groups:

**Academic subjects in *-ics*** — *mathematics*, *physics*, *economics*, *politics*, *statistics*, *ethics*, *linguistics*. As the name of a field they are singular: *economics **is** a social science*. But several of them have a second, genuinely plural sense meaning someone's opinions or the facts themselves: *her politics **are** hard to pin down*, *the statistics **were** disputed*. That second sense takes a plural verb, and the split is worth learning.

**Diseases** — *measles*, *mumps*, *rabies*, *diabetes*, *herpes*, *shingles*. Always singular.

**Games** — *billiards*, *darts*, *checkers*, *dominoes*, *cards*. Singular as the name of the game: *darts **is** popular here*. The equipment is plural: *the darts **are** in the case*.

And [news](#news), which belongs to none of them and is the commonest of all.

---

### news

**Pronunciation:** /nuːz/ &middot; *nooz* — American; British says *nyooz*
**Русский:** новости
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *the news is good*
**Counted with:** *a piece of news*, *an item of news*, *a news story*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *The news are good* is wrong, and ✗ *a news* is wrong. This trips up speakers of most European languages, where the equivalent word is plural. For one item say *a piece of news* or *a news story*.

New information about recent events.

**1. Information about events.** (новости)

1. The *news* is better than we expected.
2. Have you heard the *news*?
3. That's the best piece of *news* all week.
4. No *news* is good *news*.

**2. Broadcast news.** (новостная программа)

5. He watches the *news* every night at six.
6. It was on the *news* this morning.

**3. Attributive.** (новостной)

7. Three *news* organizations reported the same figure.

**See also:** [information](13-uncountable.md#information) &middot; [mathematics](#mathematics)

### mathematics

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmæθəˈmætɪks/ &middot; *math-uh-MAT-iks*
**Русский:** математика
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *mathematics is required*
**Counted with:** *a branch of mathematics*, *a field of mathematics*
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** shortened to *math* in American English, *maths* in British
**Contrast:** ✗ *Mathematics are difficult* is wrong. Note the American short form is *math* — singular, no *-s* — while British English says *maths*. Both take a singular verb.

The study of numbers, quantity, and space.

**1. The academic field.** (математика)

1. *Mathematics* is required for the degree.
2. She teaches *mathematics* at the high school.
3. Pure *mathematics* interests him more than applied.
4. *Math* was never my strong subject.
5. The *mathematics* behind the model is straightforward.

**See also:** [physics](#physics) &middot; [economics](#economics)

### physics

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɪzɪks/ &middot; *FIZ-iks*
**Русский:** физика
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *physics is fascinating*
**Counted with:** *a branch of physics*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Physics are hard* is wrong. Unlike *politics* and *statistics*, this word has no plural sense at all — it is always singular.

The science of matter, energy, and their interactions.

**1. The academic field.** (физика)

1. *Physics* is his favorite subject.
2. She studied *physics* at Berkeley.
3. Particle *physics* requires enormous machines.
4. The *physics* of flight is well understood.
5. *Physics* explains why the bridge failed.

**See also:** [mathematics](#mathematics)

### economics

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɛkəˈnɑːmɪks/ &middot; *ek-uh-NAH-miks* — also /ˌiːkə-/
**Русский:** экономика (наука); экономические аспекты
**Countability:** singular as a field; plural in the 'financial aspects' sense
**Agreement:** singular as a field — *economics is a social science*; plural for aspects — *the economics of the deal are poor*
**Counted with:** *a branch of economics*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** The split is real and useful. *Economics **is** a difficult subject* — the field. *The economics of solar power **are** improving* — the financial facts of a particular case. Both are correct in their own sense.

The study of production and consumption; also the financial aspects of something.

**1. The academic field.** Singular. (экономика как наука)

1. *Economics* is required for the business degree.
2. He teaches *economics* at a small college.
3. Behavioral *economics* has changed the field.

**2. The financial realities of something.** Plural. (экономические показатели)

4. The *economics* of the project are terrible.
5. The *economics* of rural hospitals have never worked.

**See also:** [politics](#politics) &middot; [statistics](#statistics)

### politics

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɑːlətɪks/ &middot; *PAH-luh-tiks*
**Русский:** политика; политические взгляды
**Countability:** singular as a field; plural as someone's opinions
**Agreement:** singular as a field — *politics is a dirty business*; plural for views — *her politics are complicated*
**Counted with:** *a branch of politics*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** The commonest error is applying one verb to both senses. *Politics **is** exhausting* refers to the activity; *his politics **are** well to the left* refers to his opinions. Both are standard.

Political activity; also a person's political opinions.

**1. Political activity or the field.** Singular. (политика)

1. *Politics* is not for the thin-skinned.
2. She went into *politics* after law school.
3. Office *politics* makes the job harder than the work does.

**2. Someone's political views.** Plural. (политические взгляды)

4. His *politics* are hard to pin down.
5. Their *politics* have shifted over the years.

**See also:** [economics](#economics) &middot; [ethics](#ethics)

### statistics

**Pronunciation:** /stəˈtɪstɪks/ &middot; *stuh-TIS-tiks*
**Русский:** статистика (наука); статистические данные
**Countability:** singular as a field; plural as data
**Agreement:** singular as a field — *statistics is a branch of mathematics*; plural for figures — *the statistics are disputed*
**Counted with:** *a statistic* for one figure
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** This one has a true countable singular: *a statistic* means one number. So *statistics **is** hard* is the subject, *these statistics **are** wrong* are the numbers, and *that's a depressing statistic* is one of them. All three are correct.

The science of collecting and analyzing numerical data; also the data itself.

**1. The academic field.** Singular. (статистика как наука)

1. *Statistics* is required for the psychology major.
2. She teaches *statistics* to first-year students.

**2. Numerical data.** Plural. (статистические данные)

3. The *statistics* are disputed by both sides.
4. These *statistics* come from the census.
5. Crime *statistics* have fallen for six years.

**3. One figure.** Countable singular. (показатель)

6. That's a startling *statistic*.

**See also:** [economics](#economics) &middot; [politics](#politics)

### ethics

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛθɪks/ &middot; *ETH-iks*
**Русский:** этика; моральные принципы
**Countability:** singular as a field; plural as principles
**Agreement:** singular as a field — *ethics is a branch of philosophy*; plural for principles — *his ethics are questionable*
**Counted with:** *a branch of ethics*, *a code of ethics*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** Same split as *politics*. The discipline is singular; a person's moral standards are plural.

The study of moral principles; also someone's moral standards.

**1. The philosophical field.** Singular. (этика)

1. *Ethics* is required for all medical students.
2. Medical *ethics* has changed enormously.

**2. Moral principles held by someone.** Plural. (моральные принципы)

3. His *ethics* are questionable at best.
4. Their business *ethics* were never in doubt.
5. Journalistic *ethics* require naming the source.

**See also:** [politics](#politics)

### gymnastics

**Pronunciation:** /dʒɪmˈnæstɪks/ &middot; *jim-NAS-tiks*
**Русский:** гимнастика
**Countability:** singular as a sport; plural as feats
**Agreement:** singular for the sport — *gymnastics is demanding*; plural for feats — *his verbal gymnastics were impressive*
**Counted with:** *a gymnastics routine*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** The literal sport is singular. The figurative sense — elaborate contortions of logic or language — is plural: *mental gymnastics **are** required to believe that*.

The sport of physical exercises; figuratively, elaborate contortions.

**1. The sport.** Singular. (гимнастика)

1. *Gymnastics* is one of the hardest sports on the body.
2. She did *gymnastics* until she was sixteen.
3. Olympic *gymnastics* draws huge audiences.

**2. Elaborate contortions.** Plural, figurative. (изощрения)

4. The mental *gymnastics* required were exhausting.
5. His verbal *gymnastics* fooled nobody.

**See also:** [athletics](#athletics)

### athletics

**Pronunciation:** /æθˈlɛtɪks/ &middot; *ath-LET-iks*
**Русский:** спорт; лёгкая атлетика
**Countability:** singular, and plural in the 'sports programs' sense
**Agreement:** usually singular — *athletics is well funded here*
**Counted with:** *an athletics program*
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** in American English *athletics* means organized sports generally; in British English it means track and field
**Contrast:** The transatlantic difference matters. An American university's *athletics department* runs all its sports; a British *athletics* meeting is running, jumping, and throwing.

Organized sports; in British usage, track and field.

**1. Organized sports.** American usage. (спорт)

1. *Athletics* is heavily funded at that school.
2. He works in the *athletics* department.
3. College *athletics* generates enormous revenue.

**2. Track and field.** British usage. (лёгкая атлетика)

4. She competed in *athletics* at the national level.
5. The *athletics* events begin on Tuesday.

**See also:** [gymnastics](#gymnastics)

### measles

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmiːzəlz/ &middot; *MEE-zulz*
**Русский:** корь
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *measles is highly contagious*
**Counted with:** *a case of measles*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Measles are contagious* is wrong. All the *-s* disease names behave this way: *mumps*, *rabies*, *diabetes*, *shingles*, *herpes*. To count outbreaks, say *cases of measles*.

The contagious viral disease.

**1. The disease.** (корь)

1. *Measles* is highly contagious.
2. He had *measles* as a child.
3. *Measles* was nearly eliminated in the 1990s.
4. Twelve cases of *measles* were reported.
5. The *measles* vaccine is given in two doses.

**See also:** [mumps](#mumps) &middot; [rabies](#rabies)

### mumps

**Русский:** свинка, эпидемический паротит
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *mumps is rare now*
**Counted with:** *a case of mumps*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Mumps are* is wrong. Often used with *the*: *he has the mumps*.

The contagious viral disease causing swollen glands.

**1. The disease.** (свинка)

1. *Mumps* is rare now because of vaccination.
2. She caught *mumps* from her brother.
3. *Mumps* causes painful swelling of the jaw.
4. Two cases of *mumps* appeared on campus.
5. He was out of school for a month with the *mumps*.

### rabies

**Pronunciation:** /ˈreɪbiːz/ &middot; *RAY-beez*
**Русский:** бешенство
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *rabies is almost always fatal*
**Counted with:** *a case of rabies*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Rabies are* is wrong. The word is from Latin *rabies*, 'madness', where the *-es* was never a plural.

The viral disease transmitted by animal bite.

**1. The disease.** (бешенство)

1. *Rabies* is almost always fatal once symptoms appear.
2. The dog was vaccinated against *rabies*.
3. *Rabies* is carried by bats and raccoons here.
4. Three cases of *rabies* were confirmed in the county.
5. He needed the *rabies* shots after the bite.

**See also:** [measles](#measles) &middot; [diabetes](#diabetes)

### diabetes

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdaɪəˈbiːtiːz/ &middot; *dy-uh-BEE-teez* — also /-tɪs/
**Русский:** диабет
**Countability:** uncountable, singular only
**Agreement:** singular verb — *diabetes is manageable*
**Counted with:** *a case of diabetes*, *a type of diabetes*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Diabetes are* is wrong. Both final-syllable pronunciations — *-teez* and *-tis* — are accepted in American English.

The metabolic disease affecting blood sugar.

**1. The disease.** (диабет)

1. *Diabetes* is manageable with the right treatment.
2. Type 2 *diabetes* is far more common.
3. His *diabetes* was diagnosed at forty.
4. *Diabetes* runs in her family.
5. Untreated *diabetes* damages the kidneys.

### billiards

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɪljərdz/ &middot; *BIL-yurdz*
**Русский:** бильярд
**Countability:** singular as a game
**Agreement:** singular verb — *billiards is played on a smaller table*
**Counted with:** *a game of billiards*
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Billiards are* is wrong when naming the game. All game names in *-s* work this way: *darts is*, *checkers is*, *dominoes is*, *cards is*. The equipment, however, is plural: *the darts are missing*.

The cue-and-ball table game.

**1. The game.** (бильярд)

1. *Billiards* is played on a table without pockets.
2. He learned *billiards* from his grandfather.
3. *Billiards* was once the more fashionable game.
4. They played *billiards* until midnight.
5. A game of *billiards* takes real patience.

**See also:** [darts](#darts) &middot; [checkers](#checkers)

### darts

**Pronunciation:** /dɑːrts/ &middot; *darts*
**Русский:** дартс
**Countability:** singular as a game; plural as the objects
**Agreement:** singular for the game — *darts is popular in bars*; plural for the objects — *the darts are in the case*
**Counted with:** *a game of darts*; one object is *a dart*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** The clearest example of the game/equipment split. *Darts **is** harder than it looks* — the game. *His darts **are** custom-made* — the objects.

The game of throwing small pointed missiles at a board; also the missiles.

**1. The game.** Singular. (дартс)

1. *Darts* is more popular in Britain than here.
2. They play *darts* every Thursday.
3. *Darts* requires far more skill than people assume.

**2. The objects.** Plural. (дротики)

4. His *darts* are custom-weighted.
5. Three *darts* were stuck in the wall.

**See also:** [billiards](#billiards) &middot; [checkers](#checkers)

### checkers

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtʃɛkərz/ &middot; *CHEK-urz*
**Русский:** шашки
**Countability:** singular as a game; plural as the pieces
**Agreement:** singular for the game — *checkers is simple to learn*; plural for pieces — *the checkers are missing*
**Counted with:** *a game of checkers*
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** the British name for the same game is *draughts*, pronounced *drafts*
**Contrast:** Same split as *darts*: the game is singular, the pieces are plural.

The board game played with flat round pieces.

**1. The game.** Singular. (шашки)

1. *Checkers* is easy to learn and hard to master.
2. They played *checkers* on the porch all summer.
3. *Checkers* was solved by computer in 2007.

**2. The pieces.** Plural. (шашки как фигуры)

4. Half the *checkers* are missing from the box.
5. The red *checkers* were made of plastic.

**See also:** [darts](#darts) &middot; [billiards](#billiards)
