Core vocabulary: government and politics#

The everyday nouns of government and politics — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

senate#

Pronunciation
/ˈsɛnət/ · SEN-uht
Русский
сенат
Plural
senates
Countability
countable

The smaller of the two houses of a national or state legislature.

  1. The bill passed the senate by a single vote.
  2. She served four years in the state senate.
  3. Several state senates have taken up the same question.

Pronunciation
/ˈsɛnətər/ · SEN-uh-tur
Русский
сенатор
Plural
senators
Countability
countable

A member of a senate.

  1. Our senator voted against the bill.
  2. He was first elected senator twelve years ago.
  3. Both senators from our state showed up to the hearing.

congress#

Pronunciation
/ˈkɑːŋɡrəs/ · KAHNG-gruhs
Русский
конгресс (парламент США)
Plural
congresses
Countability
countable

The national lawmaking body of a country, especially the House and Senate in the United States.

  1. Congress has to approve the money before anything gets built.
  2. The president asked Congress for emergency funding.
  3. She has worked in Congress since she was twenty-five.

A person elected to speak and vote for a group of citizens.

The body of elected people that makes the laws of a state or country.

Pronunciation
/ˈkaʊnsəl/ · KOWN-suhl
Русский
совет (орган управления)
Plural
councils
Countability
countable
Contrast
Sounds exactly like counsel (advice, or a lawyer); the group is spelled council.

A group elected or chosen to run a town, organization, or program.

  1. The city council meets on the first Tuesday of the month.
  2. Our council voted to close the street to cars.
  3. Neighborhood councils have almost no real power here.

  1. A federal agency is looking into the spill.

  3. Three agencies are fighting over the same budget.

Plural

Countability
countable

The people who run a government during one leader's time in office.

  1. The current administration has cut taxes twice.
  2. That rule was written under the previous administration.
  3. Both administrations handled the border the same way.

authority#

Pronunciation
/əˈθɔːrəti/ · uh-THOR-uh-tee
Русский
власть, полномочия; орган власти
Plural
authorities
Countability
both — countable and uncountable with different meanings
Contrast
Uncountable when it means power; countable when it means an official body.

The legal right to give orders, or a public body that has that right.

  1. The mayor has no authority over the school district.
  2. The port authority owns all three bridges.
  3. Local authorities closed the road for two days.

Pronunciation

Русский
министерство
Plural
ministries
Countability
countable
Contrast
The US has no ministries; the same bodies are called departments.

A government department in charge of one area, such as defense or health.

  1. The foreign ministry issued a statement.
  2. She spent ten years at the ministry of education.
  3. Two ministries were merged to save money.

ministers
Countability

Contrast

A senior member of a government who is in charge of a department.

  2. He met with the defense minister in Berlin.
  3. Several ministers were replaced last month.
