# Collective nouns for animals

> *A pride of lions*, *a murder of crows*, *a parliament of owls*. English has a specific collective noun for almost every animal, and using the wrong one is instantly noticeable.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

English has an unusually rich set of collective nouns for groups of animals, called **terms of venery** — *venery* being an old word for hunting. Most were compiled in fifteenth-century hunting manuals, and a good number were probably invented as wordplay even then. They survive because English speakers enjoy them.

Three tiers, and it is worth knowing which is which:

**In genuine everyday use.** *A herd of cattle*, *a flock of birds*, *a school of fish*, *a pack of wolves*, *a swarm of bees*, *a pride of lions*, *a litter of puppies*, *a colony of ants*. Getting these wrong sounds plainly incorrect.

**Widely known but slightly literary.** *A gaggle of geese*, *a pod of whales*, *a troop of monkeys*, *a murder of crows*, *a covey of quail*. Recognized by most native speakers; used mainly in writing and nature documentaries.

**Ornamental and mostly for fun.** *A parliament of owls*, *an exaltation of larks*, *a shrewdness of apes*, *a business of ferrets*. These are real and dictionary-attested, but a native speaker is as likely to say *a group of owls*. Deploy them for delight, not for correctness.

Grammatically these behave like any [collective noun](17-collective.md): American English takes a singular verb — *the pride **is** hunting* — and the verb follows the collective, not the animal.

### pride

**Pronunciation:** /praɪd/ &middot; *pryd*
**Русский:** прайд (львов)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the pride is hunting*
**Counted with:** *a pride of lions*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Used **only** of lions. The unrelated abstract noun *pride*, meaning self-respect, is uncountable.

A group of lions.

**1. The group of lions.** (прайд)

1. A *pride* of lions was resting under the tree.
2. The *pride* is led by two related males.
3. Both *prides* hunt the same territory.
4. A young male leaves the *pride* at three.
5. Researchers have tracked this *pride* for a decade.

**See also:** [pack](#pack) &middot; [herd](17-collective.md#herd)

### murder

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɜːrdər/ &middot; *MUR-dur*
**Русский:** стая (ворон)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the murder is roosting*
**Counted with:** *a murder of crows*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** The most famous of the ornamental terms, and used only of crows. Homonymous with the ordinary noun *murder*, a killing.

A group of crows.

**1. The group of crows.** (стая ворон)

1. A *murder* of crows settled on the wires.
2. The *murder* is roosting in the same trees each night.
3. Both *murders* took off at once.
4. A *murder* of crows can strip a field fast.
5. The collective noun for crows is a *murder*.

### gaggle

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæɡəl/ &middot; *GAG-uhl*
**Русский:** стая (гусей на земле)
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the gaggle is blocking the path*
**Counted with:** *a gaggle of geese*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Strictly, *gaggle* is for geese **on the ground**; in flight they are *a skein* or *a wedge*. Also used jokingly of any noisy disorganized group of people.

A group of geese on the ground.

**1. The group of geese.** (стая гусей)

1. A *gaggle* of geese blocked the whole path.
2. The *gaggle* is grazing on the fairway.
3. In flight they are a skein, not a *gaggle*.

**2. A noisy group of people.** Humorous. (толпа, галдящая компания)

4. A *gaggle* of reporters waited outside.
5. Both *gaggles* of tourists arrived at once.
