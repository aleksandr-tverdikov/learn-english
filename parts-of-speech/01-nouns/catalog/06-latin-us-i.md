# Latin *-us* → *-i*

> *Cactus → cacti*, *fungus → fungi*. A real pattern with a short membership list — and *virus*, *campus*, and *bonus* are not in it.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Latin second-declension nouns ending in *-us* formed their plural in *-i*, and English kept that plural for words it borrowed as technical vocabulary. The pattern is real but **narrow**: it applies only to a specific list of learned borrowings, and even inside that list English usually also permits a regular *-es* plural, sometimes preferring it.

Three cautions. First, most *-us* words in English are **not** Latin second declension at all — *virus*, *campus*, *bonus*, *status*, *census*, *chorus*, *circus*, *bus* — and take a plain *-es*. Second, a few come from other declensions and pluralize differently: *corpus → corpora*, *genus → genera*, *opus → opera*. Third, *octopus* is Greek, not Latin, so ✗ *octopi* has no historical basis at all, however often it is said.

Pronunciation is worth its own note. The *-i* plural is /aɪ/ in American English — *cacti* is *KAK-ty*, *fungi* is *FUN-jy* or *FUN-gy*, *alumni* is *uh-LUM-ny*.

### cactus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkæktəs/ &middot; *KAK-tuhs*
**Русский:** кактус
**Plural:** *cacti* /ˈkæktaɪ/ &middot; *KAK-ty* — also *cactuses*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the cacti are blooming*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *cacti* (commoner in writing) &middot; *cactuses* (commoner in speech)
**Contrast:** Both plurals are fully correct. *Cacti* sounds more botanical; *cactuses* sounds more casual, and neither will be marked wrong.

The spiny desert plant.

**1. The plant.** (кактус)

1. A single *cactus* stood beside the road.
2. The greenhouse holds two hundred *cacti*.
3. She waters her *cactuses* about once a month.
4. Some *cacti* live for centuries.
5. He knocked the *cactus* off the windowsill.

**See also:** [fungus](#fungus) &middot; [nucleus](#nucleus)

### fungus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfʌŋɡəs/ &middot; *FUNG-guhs*
**Русский:** гриб, грибок
**Plural:** *fungi* /ˈfʌndʒaɪ/ &middot; *FUN-jy* — also /ˈfʌŋɡaɪ/, and *funguses*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in the mass sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *these fungi are edible*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *fungi* is standard in science and general use; *funguses* is rare
**Contrast:** Two pronunciations of the plural coexist in American English — *FUN-jy* and *FUN-gy* — and both are accepted. The adjective is *fungal*.

An organism of the kingdom that includes mushrooms, molds, and yeasts.

**1. The organism.** (гриб, грибок)

1. This *fungus* grows only on dead wood.
2. Several *fungi* were identified in the sample.
3. *Fungi* are neither plants nor animals.
4. A *fungus* had spread across the ceiling.

**2. An infection.** Medical. (грибковая инфекция)

5. He's treating a *fungus* on his foot.

**See also:** [cactus](#cactus) &middot; [bacterium](08-latin-um-a.md#bacterium)

### nucleus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnuːkliəs/ &middot; *NOO-klee-uhs*
**Русский:** ядро
**Plural:** *nuclei* /ˈnuːkliaɪ/ &middot; *NOO-klee-y* — also *nucleuses*, rarely
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the nuclei divide*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *nuclei* is standard in every technical field
**Contrast:** Americans say /ˈnuːkliəs/, without the /j/ of British *NYOO-*. The adjective is *nuclear* — famously mispronounced ✗ *NOO-kyuh-lur* instead of *NOO-klee-ur*.

The central part of an atom or a cell; figuratively, the core of a group.

**1. In physics and biology.** (ядро)

1. The *nucleus* contains protons and neutrons.
2. All the cell *nuclei* were stained blue.
3. Atomic *nuclei* fuse under enormous pressure.

**2. The central core of a group or plan.** Figurative. (ядро, основа)

4. Those four players formed the *nucleus* of the team.
5. A small *nucleus* of volunteers ran the whole event.

### radius

**Pronunciation:** /ˈreɪdiəs/ &middot; *RAY-dee-uhs*
**Русский:** радиус
**Plural:** *radii* /ˈreɪdiaɪ/ &middot; *RAY-dee-y* — also *radiuses*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the radii are equal*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *radii* in mathematics; *radiuses* occasionally elsewhere
**Contrast:** Note the double *i* in *radii* — the stem ends in *i* and the plural adds another. Same in *genii*. The word is also a bone in the forearm, with the same plural.

The distance from the center of a circle to its edge; also a bone in the forearm.

**1. In geometry.** (радиус)

1. Measure the *radius* of the circle.
2. The two *radii* meet at the center.
3. Draw *radii* every thirty degrees.

**2. A surrounding area.** Very common in everyday use. (радиус, округа)

4. There's no gas station within a ten-mile *radius*.
5. Police searched a two-block *radius*.

**3. The forearm bone.** (лучевая кость)

6. She fractured her *radius* in the fall.

**See also:** [nucleus](#nucleus)

### alumnus

**Pronunciation:** /əˈlʌmnəs/ &middot; *uh-LUM-nuhs*
**Русский:** выпускник
**Plural:** *alumni* /əˈlʌmnaɪ/ &middot; *uh-LUM-ny*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the alumni were invited*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *alumnus* (one man) &middot; *alumna* (one woman) &middot; *alumni* (men, or a mixed group) &middot; *alumnae* (women only) &middot; *alum* / *alums* (informal, sex-neutral)
**Contrast:** This word has a full Latin gender system that English kept, and it trips everybody. Note especially that ✗ *an alumni* is wrong for one person — say *an alumnus*, *an alumna*, or informally *an alum*. Mixed groups take *alumni*.

A former student of a school or college.

**1. A former student.** (выпускник)

1. He is an *alumnus* of the same program.
2. The *alumni* meet every October.
3. She is an *alumna* of the class of 1998.
4. Both *alumnae* endowed scholarships.

**2. Informally — alum.** Sex-neutral and increasingly standard in American usage. (выпускник, выпускница)

5. Two *alums* now run the company.

**3. Extended to any former member.** Of a company, team, or show. (бывший участник)

6. The show's *alumni* include three future stars.

**See also:** [stimulus](#stimulus)

### stimulus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstɪmjələs/ &middot; *STIM-yuh-luhs*
**Русский:** стимул, раздражитель
**Plural:** *stimuli* /ˈstɪmjəlaɪ/ &middot; *STIM-yuh-ly*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in the economic sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the stimuli were randomized*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** ✗ *stimuluses* is not used
**Contrast:** In economics the word is usually uncountable and singular — *the stimulus package*, *federal stimulus* — while in psychology it is countable and the plural *stimuli* is everywhere.

Something that provokes a reaction.

**1. In psychology and biology.** (раздражитель)

1. The subject responds to a visual *stimulus*.
2. All *stimuli* were presented in random order.
3. Painful *stimuli* produce the fastest reflex.

**2. Economic support intended to spur growth.** Usually uncountable. (стимул, стимулирующие меры)

4. Congress passed a second round of *stimulus*.
5. The checks were meant as *stimulus* for local business.

**3. Anything that encourages activity.** General use. (толчок, стимул)

6. The prize was a real *stimulus* to finish the work.

**See also:** [alumnus](#alumnus) &middot; [syllabus](#syllabus)

### syllabus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪləbəs/ &middot; *SIL-uh-buhs*
**Русский:** программа курса, силлабус
**Plural:** *syllabi* /ˈsɪləbaɪ/ &middot; *syllabuses* — both correct
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the syllabi are posted online*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *syllabi* and *syllabuses* are both fully standard in American academic use
**Contrast:** The Latin form is actually the result of a copying error in a Renaissance text, so the *-i* plural has no ancient authority — which has not stopped it from becoming standard.

The outline of topics and requirements for a course.

**1. A course outline.** (программа курса)

1. The *syllabus* lists every assignment and due date.
2. All *syllabi* must be submitted before term starts.
3. She reads the *syllabus* on the first day.
4. Both *syllabuses* cover the same material.
5. It isn't on the *syllabus*, so it isn't on the exam.

### focus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfoʊkəs/ &middot; *FOH-kuhs*
**Русский:** фокус; средоточие
**Plural:** *foci* /ˈfoʊsaɪ/ &middot; *FOH-sy* — also *focuses*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in the 'attention' sense
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the two foci of an ellipse are fixed*
**Pattern:** Latin *-us* → *-i* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Variants:** *foci* in mathematics and medicine; *focuses* everywhere else
**Contrast:** Usage has split cleanly by field. Geometry and pathology say *foci*; ordinary writing says *focuses*. Note the *c* is pronounced /s/ in *foci*, not /k/.

The center of interest or activity; the point at which rays converge.

**1. Attention or emphasis.** Usually uncountable. (внимание, средоточие)

1. Her *focus* was entirely on the deadline.
2. The report shifts *focus* halfway through.
3. Safety became the main *focus* of the meeting.

**2. In optics and photography.** (фокус)

4. The picture is out of *focus*.
5. Bring the far edge into *focus*.

**3. In mathematics and medicine — foci.** (фокусы)

6. An ellipse has two *foci*.
7. Several *foci* of infection were identified.

**See also:** [syllabus](#syllabus) &middot; [radius](#radius)

### octopus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɑːktəpəs/ &middot; *AHK-tuh-puhs*
**Русский:** осьминог
**Plural:** *octopuses* &middot; *octopodes* /ɑːkˈtɑːpədiːz/ in strict Greek
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as food
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the octopuses were moved to a larger tank*
**Pattern:** Greek, not Latin — regular *-es* &middot; **Group:** 6 (trap)
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *octopuses* is standard; *octopi* is common but has no basis; *octopodes* is technically correct and sounds absurd
**Contrast:** ✗ *Octopi* is the most confidently mispronounced plural in English. The word is **Greek**, so the Latin *-us → -i* rule never applied to it. If you want the historical plural it is *octopodes*; if you want to be understood, say *octopuses*.

The eight-armed marine mollusk.

**1. The animal.** (осьминог)

1. An *octopus* escaped through a two-inch gap.
2. The aquarium keeps three *octopuses*.
3. *Octopuses* can solve simple puzzles.
4. A giant Pacific *octopus* can weigh a hundred pounds.

**2. As food.** Uncountable. (осьминог как еда)

5. Grilled *octopus* was the best thing on the menu.

**See also:** [virus](#virus) &middot; [squid](03-unchanged.md#squid)

### virus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈvaɪrəs/ &middot; *VY-ruhs*
**Русский:** вирус
**Plural:** *viruses* /ˈvaɪrəsɪz/ — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *these viruses mutate quickly*
**Pattern:** regular *-es* despite the Latin look &middot; **Group:** 6 (trap)
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *Viri* and ✗ *virii* are both wrong and always have been. In Latin *virus* was a mass noun meaning 'poison' and had no plural at all, so English simply made a regular one. The same goes for *campus → campuses*, *bonus → bonuses*, *status → statuses*, *census → censuses*.

An infectious agent; also a malicious computer program.

**1. The infectious agent.** (вирус)

1. The *virus* spreads through the air.
2. Several *viruses* circulate every winter.
3. A new *virus* was identified in the region.

**2. A malicious program.** (компьютерный вирус)

4. His laptop picked up a *virus*.
5. The company scans for *viruses* nightly.

**3. Figuratively — something that spreads.** (зараза)

6. The rumor spread through the office like a *virus*.

**See also:** [octopus](#octopus) &middot; [campus](#campus)

### campus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkæmpəs/ &middot; *KAM-puhs*
**Русский:** кампус, территория учебного заведения
**Plural:** *campuses* /ˈkæmpəsɪz/ — regular
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both campuses are closed*
**Pattern:** regular *-es* despite the Latin look &middot; **Group:** 6 (trap)
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *campi* is a joke, not a plural. Latin *campus* meant 'field', and English borrowed it whole in the eighteenth century without its Latin inflection.

The grounds of a university, college, or large company.

**1. University grounds.** (кампус)

1. She lives on *campus*.
2. The university has three *campuses*.
3. Both *campuses* were built in the 1960s.
4. There's a bookstore in the middle of *campus*.

**2. A corporate site.** (территория компании)

5. The company's *campus* holds eight thousand employees.

### corpus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɔːrpəs/ &middot; *KOR-puhs*
**Русский:** корпус (текстов), свод
**Plural:** *corpora* /ˈkɔːrpərə/ &middot; *KOR-pur-uh* — also *corpuses*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the corpora are freely available*
**Pattern:** Latin third declension → *-ora* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *corpora* in linguistics and scholarship; *corpuses* occasionally
**Contrast:** Not ✗ *corpi*. This is a different Latin declension from *cactus*, and it gives *-ora*. Compare *genus → genera* and *opus → opera*, which behave the same way.

A body of writings or data collected for study.

**1. A collection of texts or data.** (корпус текстов)

1. The *corpus* contains a billion words of American English.
2. Both *corpora* are annotated for part of speech.
3. Linguists build *corpora* from newspapers and transcripts.
4. She searched the *corpus* for every use of the word.

**2. The complete works of an author.** (полное собрание)

5. His entire *corpus* fits in one volume.

### genus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒiːnəs/ &middot; *JEE-nuhs*
**Русский:** род (биологический)
**Plural:** *genera* /ˈdʒɛnərə/ &middot; *JEN-ur-uh*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *these genera are closely related*
**Pattern:** Latin third declension → *-era* &middot; **Group:** 6
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** Note the vowel shift: singular *JEE-nuhs*, plural *JEN-ur-uh*. Not ✗ *genuses* in scientific writing, and never ✗ *geni*. In classification a genus sits above a species.

A rank in biological classification, above species and below family.

**1. The taxonomic rank.** (род)

1. All these plants belong to the same *genus*.
2. Several *genera* were reclassified last year.
3. The *genus* includes about forty species.
4. Two *genera* of beetle live only on this mountain.
5. He named a new *genus* after his teacher.
