Latin -a → -ae#

Larva → larvae, vertebra → vertebrae. A small group, and for several of them the plain English -s has already won.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Latin first-declension nouns ending in -a took -ae in the plural, pronounced /iː/ in American English — larvae is LAR-vee, vertebrae is VUR-tuh-bree. English kept the ending mostly in biology and anatomy, where the Latin plural is still the professional norm.

Outside those fields the pattern is retreating. Formulas has almost completely displaced formulae in American English, antennas is standard for anything electronic, and only larvae, vertebrae, and algae still resist. A useful test: if the word belongs to a laboratory, expect -ae; if it belongs to daily life, expect -s.
