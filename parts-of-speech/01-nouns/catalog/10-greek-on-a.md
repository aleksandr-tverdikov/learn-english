# Greek *-on* → *-a*

> *Criterion → criteria*, *phenomenon → phenomena*. A group of four, and two of them are among the most misused words in English.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Greek neuter nouns in *-on* took *-a* in the plural. English borrowed only a handful, but two of them — *criterion* and *phenomenon* — appear constantly in academic and journalistic writing, and both are routinely used wrong.

The error is always the same: **the plural gets used as a singular.** ✗ *This criteria is important* and ✗ *An interesting phenomena* are both wrong, because *criteria* and *phenomena* are plural. The singulars are *criterion* and *phenomenon*, and a correctly used one sounds surprising precisely because it is so rarely heard.

Compare the identical drift in [datum → data](08-latin-um-a.md#datum). The difference is that *data* has genuinely completed the change and is now accepted as a mass noun, while *criteria* and *phenomena* have not — using them as singulars is still marked as an error in edited prose.

---

### criterion

**Pronunciation:** /kraɪˈtɪriən/ &middot; *kry-TEER-ee-uhn*
**Русский:** критерий
**Plural:** *criteria* /kraɪˈtɪriə/ &middot; *kry-TEER-ee-uh*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the criteria are strict*
**Pattern:** Greek *-on* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 10
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** ✗ *criterias* is doubly wrong — a plural made from a plural
**Contrast:** ✗ *This criteria* is wrong; say *this criterion* for one, *these criteria* for several. The plural takes a plural verb: *the criteria **are** listed below*, not ✗ *is listed*.

A standard by which something is judged.

**1. A standard of judgment.** (критерий)

1. Cost was the only *criterion* that mattered.
2. The application meets all five *criteria*.
3. What *criteria* are you using to decide?
4. Each *criterion* is weighted differently.
5. These *criteria* have not changed since 1998.

**See also:** [phenomenon](#phenomenon) &middot; [datum](08-latin-um-a.md#datum)

### phenomenon

**Pronunciation:** /fəˈnɑːmənɑːn/ &middot; *fuh-NAH-muh-nahn*
**Русский:** явление, феномен
**Plural:** *phenomena* /fəˈnɑːmənə/ &middot; *fuh-NAH-muh-nuh*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *these phenomena are unexplained*
**Pattern:** Greek *-on* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 10
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *phenomenons* is used only of remarkable people — *he is one of the great phenomenons of the sport*
**Contrast:** ✗ *A phenomena* is wrong. One is *a phenomenon*; several are *phenomena*. The exception is the 'extraordinary person' sense, which takes the English plural *phenomenons*.

An observable fact or event, especially a remarkable one.

**1. Something observed.** Science and general use. (явление)

1. The northern lights are a well-studied *phenomenon*.
2. Several *phenomena* remain unexplained.
3. Migration is a *phenomenon* found across species.

**2. A trend or occurrence in society.** (феномен)

4. Remote work became a global *phenomenon*.
5. These cultural *phenomena* deserve serious study.

**3. A remarkably talented person.** Plural *phenomenons*. (феномен, самородок)

6. At nineteen she was already a *phenomenon*.

**See also:** [criterion](#criterion)

### automaton

**Pronunciation:** /ɔːˈtɑːmətɑːn/ &middot; *aw-TAH-muh-tahn*
**Русский:** автомат, робот
**Plural:** *automata* /ɔːˈtɑːmətə/ — also *automatons*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the automata were built in the 1700s*
**Pattern:** Greek *-on* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 10
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *automata* in computer science and horology; *automatons* in general use

A self-operating machine; figuratively, a person acting mechanically.

**1. A self-operating machine.** (автомат)

1. The museum displays an eighteenth-century *automaton* that writes.
2. Clockwork *automata* were the wonder of their age.
3. Finite *automata* are a foundation of computer science.

**2. A person acting without thought.** Figurative. (робот, автомат)

4. After twelve hours he was working like an *automaton*.
5. The staff had been reduced to *automatons*.

**See also:** [phenomenon](#phenomenon)

### ganglion

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæŋɡliən/ &middot; *GANG-glee-uhn*
**Русский:** ганглий, нервный узел
**Plural:** *ganglia* /ˈɡæŋɡliə/ — also *ganglions*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the ganglia are clustered near the spine*
**Pattern:** Greek *-on* → *-a* &middot; **Group:** 10
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *ganglia* in anatomy; *ganglions* for the cyst on a wrist

A cluster of nerve cell bodies; also a harmless cyst near a joint.

**1. A nerve cluster.** Anatomy. (нервный узел)

1. The basal *ganglia* control movement.
2. Each *ganglion* relays signals to the spinal cord.
3. Sensory *ganglia* lie just outside the cord.

**2. A cyst on a tendon.** Usually *ganglions*. (ганглий, киста)

4. She had a *ganglion* removed from her wrist.
5. Both *ganglions* disappeared on their own.

**See also:** [automaton](#automaton)
