# Plurals of compound nouns

> *Mothers-in-law*, not ✗ *mother-in-laws*. Pluralize the noun that carries the meaning — which is usually not the last word.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A compound noun made of several words pluralizes on its **head** — the noun that names what the thing actually is. In *mother-in-law* the thing is a mother, so the plural is *mothers-in-law*. In *passerby* the thing is someone who passes, so it is *passersby*.

The rule in three cases:

**Noun + preposition phrase → pluralize the first noun.** *mothers-in-law*, *editors-in-chief*, *chiefs of staff*, *maids of honor*, *passersby*, *hangers-on*.

**Noun + postposed adjective → pluralize the noun.** *attorneys general*, *courts-martial*, *notaries public*, *poets laureate*. These come from French and Latin word order, where the adjective follows. In American English the regular *attorney generals* is also widely accepted and increasingly common.

**Solid compounds and *-ful* words → pluralize the end.** When the compound is written as one word, it takes a normal plural at the end: *cupfuls*, *spoonfuls*, *handfuls*, *bookcases*, *toothbrushes*. Note that *two spoonfuls* means two spoon-loads, while *two spoons full* means two separate spoons, each full.

### mother-in-law

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmʌðər ɪn lɔː/ &middot; *MUTH-ur-in-law*
**Русский:** тёща, свекровь
**Plural:** *mothers-in-law* — pluralize *mother*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both mothers-in-law were invited*
**Pattern:** noun + preposition phrase &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *Mother-in-laws* is the commonest error. The possessive, however, does go at the end: *my mother-in-law's house*. All the in-law terms follow: *fathers-in-law*, *brothers-in-law*, *sisters-in-law*, *sons-in-law*, *daughters-in-law*.

The mother of one's spouse.

**1. The relative.** (тёща, свекровь)

1. My *mother-in-law* is staying for two weeks.
2. Both *mothers-in-law* sat at the same table.
3. She gets along well with her *mother-in-law*.
4. The two *mothers-in-law* had never met.
5. His *mother-in-law*'s house is around the corner.

**See also:** [brother-in-law](#brother-in-law) &middot; [passerby](#passerby)

### brother-in-law

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbrʌðər ɪn lɔː/ &middot; *BRUTH-ur-in-law*
**Русский:** шурин, деверь, зять
**Plural:** *brothers-in-law* — pluralize *brother*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *his brothers-in-law both work there*
**Pattern:** noun + preposition phrase &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *Brother-in-laws* is wrong. English uses one term where Russian distinguishes several relationships — the brother of your spouse and the husband of your sister are both *brother-in-law*.

The brother of one's spouse, or the husband of one's sibling.

**1. The relative.** (шурин, деверь, зять)

1. My *brother-in-law* fixed the car.
2. Both *brothers-in-law* work for the same company.
3. He introduced me to his *brother-in-law*.
4. Her three *brothers-in-law* were all at the wedding.
5. I borrowed my *brother-in-law*'s truck.

**See also:** [mother-in-law](#mother-in-law)

### passerby

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpæsərbaɪ/ &middot; *PAS-ur-by*
**Русский:** прохожий
**Plural:** *passersby* /ˈpæsərzbaɪ/ — pluralize *passer*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *passersby stopped to look*
**Pattern:** noun + adverb &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** also written *passer-by* / *passers-by*
**Contrast:** ✗ *Passerbys* is wrong. The *-s* goes on *passer*, the noun, not on *by*, which is an adverb.

Someone who happens to be walking past.

**1. A person walking past.** (прохожий)

1. A *passerby* called an ambulance.
2. Several *passersby* stopped to help.
3. The *passersby* barely glanced at him.
4. One *passerby* filmed the whole thing.
5. Police are asking *passersby* to come forward.

**See also:** [hanger-on](#hanger-on) &middot; [mother-in-law](#mother-in-law)

Someone who finishes second in a competition.

### attorney general

**Pronunciation:** /əˌtɜːrni ˈdʒɛnərəl/ &middot; *uh-TUR-nee JEN-ur-uhl*
**Русский:** генеральный прокурор
**Plural:** *attorneys general* — traditional &middot; *attorney generals* — also accepted
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the attorneys general filed jointly*
**Pattern:** noun + postposed adjective &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *attorneys general* in legal and formal writing; *attorney generals* is common in American journalism
**Contrast:** *General* here is an adjective, not the military rank — the phrase means 'general attorney' in French word order. The traditional plural puts the *-s* on *attorney*. Both forms now appear in edited American prose.

The chief legal officer of a state or country.

**1. The office holder.** (генеральный прокурор)

1. The *attorney general* declined to comment.
2. Twelve *attorneys general* signed the brief.
3. Both *attorney generals* were appointed the same year.
4. She served as *attorney general* for six years.
5. State *attorneys general* sued jointly.

**See also:** [court-martial](#court-martial) &middot; [notary public](#notary-public)

### court-martial

**Pronunciation:** /ˌkɔːrt ˈmɑːrʃəl/ &middot; *kort-MAR-shuhl*
**Русский:** военный трибунал
**Plural:** *courts-martial* — traditional &middot; *court-martials* — also used
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both courts-martial were held in secret*
**Pattern:** noun + postposed adjective &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** *courts-martial* is standard in military and legal writing
**Contrast:** *Martial* is the adjective, so the noun *court* takes the plural. As a verb the compound is regular: *he was court-martialed*.

A military court.

**1. The military tribunal.** (военный трибунал)

1. He faced a *court-martial* for disobeying orders.
2. Two *courts-martial* were convened that month.
3. The *court-martial* found him not guilty.
4. Both *courts-martial* were closed to the press.

**2. As a verb.** Regular. (отдать под трибунал)

5. Three officers were *court-martialed*.

**See also:** [attorney general](#attorney-general)

### spoonful

**Pronunciation:** /ˈspuːnfʊl/ &middot; *SPOON-fuul*
**Русский:** ложка (как мера)
**Plural:** *spoonfuls* — pluralize the end
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *two spoonfuls are enough*
**Pattern:** solid compound → regular *-s* &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** abstract
**Variants:** *spoonsful* is an older form, now rare
**Contrast:** *Two spoonfuls* means two spoon-loads from one spoon. *Two spoons full* means two separate spoons, each full. The same distinction runs through *cupfuls*, *handfuls*, *mouthfuls*, and *armfuls*.

The amount a spoon will hold.

**1. A measure.** (ложка чего-либо)

1. Add two *spoonfuls* of sugar.
2. One *spoonful* is plenty.
3. She took three *spoonfuls* and stopped.
4. A *spoonful* of medicine helps it go down.
5. Both *spoonfuls* went into the same cup.

**See also:** [handful](#handful)

### handful

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhændfʊl/ &middot; *HAND-fuul*
**Русский:** горсть; горстка
**Plural:** *handfuls* — pluralize the end
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *a few handfuls are enough*
**Pattern:** solid compound → regular *-s* &middot; **Group:** 18
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *Handsful* is archaic. Note the very common figurative sense: *a handful* meaning a small number, or a difficult person.

The amount a hand will hold; a small number; a difficult person.

**1. What a hand holds.** (горсть)

1. He threw a *handful* of seed across the row.
2. Two *handfuls* of flour should do it.

**2. A small number.** Figurative. (горстка)

3. Only a *handful* of people showed up.
4. A *handful* of companies control the market.

**3. Someone difficult to manage.** Informal. (сущее наказание)

5. That child is a real *handful*.

**See also:** [spoonful](#spoonful)
