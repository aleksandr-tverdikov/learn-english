# Collective nouns

> *The team is winning* in America; *the team are winning* in Britain. One noun, one group, two grammars — and *police* is plural everywhere.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

A collective noun names a group as a unit: *team*, *family*, *committee*, *jury*, *audience*. The grammatical question is whether the group counts as one thing or as many people, and **this is the clearest grammatical difference between American and British English.**

**American English treats them as singular.** *The team **is** playing well. The committee **has** decided. My family **is** coming for Thanksgiving.* This is the default and the safe choice in American writing.

**British English commonly treats them as plural** when the members are acting individually: *the team **are** playing well*, *the government **have** announced*. To American ears this sounds wrong; to British ears the American version can sound wrong in the opposite direction.

Americans do use a plural verb when the sentence forces attention onto the individuals — *the jury **were** unable to agree among themselves* — but the ordinary choice is singular. There is one absolute exception: **police** is always plural, in every variety of English. ✗ *The police is investigating* is wrong everywhere.

Two constructions deserve their own note, because they behave in exactly opposite ways: *the number of X **is***, singular, but *a number of X **are***, plural.

### team

**Pronunciation:** /tiːm/ &middot; *teem*
**Русский:** команда
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the team is winning*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** *The team **is** winning* is American; *the team **are** winning* is British. Both are correct in their own variety. Use the singular in American writing.

A group of people working or playing together.

**1. A sports side.** (спортивная команда)

1. The *team* is playing better than last season.
2. Both *teams* were exhausted by the fourth quarter.
3. She made the *team* on her first try.

**2. A group working together.** (рабочая группа)

4. Our *team* is handling the migration.
5. Three *teams* are competing for the contract.
6. He built a *team* of twelve engineers.

**See also:** [family](#family) &middot; [staff](04-f-to-ves.md#staff)

### family

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfæməli/ &middot; *FAM-uh-lee*
**Русский:** семья
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *my family is coming*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Americans say *my family **is** coming for the holidays*; the British often say *are*. Note that *family* is also a rank in biological classification, above genus.

A group of related people; also a taxonomic rank.

**1. Related people.** (семья)

1. My *family* is coming for Thanksgiving.
2. Three *families* share the building.
3. The whole *family* was at the wedding.

**2. A group of related things.** (семейство)

4. Oaks belong to the beech *family*.
5. Both language *families* are unrelated to each other.

**3. Attributive.** (семейный)

6. It's a *family* business, now in its third generation.

**See also:** [team](#team) &middot; [couple](#couple)

### government

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡʌvərnmənt/ &middot; *GUV-urn-muhnt* — the first *n* is often dropped in speech
**Русский:** правительство
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the government is considering it*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** ✗ *The government are* is British, not American. Note that the first *n* is commonly unpronounced — *GUV-ur-muhnt* — but retained in careful speech.

The body governing a state or organization.

**1. The governing body.** (правительство)

1. The *government* is considering new rules.
2. Both *governments* signed the treaty.
3. The federal *government* employs two million people.

**2. The activity of governing.** Uncountable. (управление, власть)

4. *Government* is harder than campaigning.
5. She studied *government* and economics.

**See also:** [committee](#committee) &middot; [public](#public)

### committee

**Pronunciation:** /kəˈmɪti/ &middot; *kuh-MIT-ee*
**Русский:** комитет, комиссия
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the committee has decided*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Note the spelling: two *m*'s, two *t*'s, two *e*'s. Stress falls on the middle syllable.

A group appointed to decide or advise on something.

**1. The group.** (комитет)

1. The *committee* has decided to postpone the vote.
2. Both *committees* met the same afternoon.
3. She chairs the finance *committee*.
4. The *committee* meets on the first Monday of the month.

**2. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

5. A camel is a horse designed by *committee*.

**See also:** [government](#government) &middot; [jury](#jury)

### jury

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʊri/ &middot; *JUUR-ee*
**Русский:** присяжные, жюри
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** usually singular in American English — *the jury has reached a verdict*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Americans use the plural when the members are acting separately: *the jury **were** unable to agree among themselves*. When the group acts as one, singular: *the jury **has** reached a verdict*.

A body of people sworn to give a verdict; also a panel of judges in a competition.

**1. In a courtroom.** (присяжные)

1. The *jury* has reached a verdict.
2. Both *juries* deadlocked.
3. The *jury* were divided among themselves.
4. She was called for *jury* duty.

**2. A panel judging a competition.** (жюри)

5. The *jury* awarded first prize unanimously.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. The *jury* is still out on whether it works.

**See also:** [committee](#committee) &middot; [audience](#audience)

### audience

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɔːdiəns/ &middot; *AW-dee-uhns*
**Русский:** аудитория, зрители
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the audience was silent*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Note the separate sense 'a formal meeting with an important person', which is countable and unrelated to crowds: *an audience with the pope*.

The people watching or listening; also a formal interview.

**1. Spectators or listeners.** (зрители, слушатели)

1. The *audience* was completely silent.
2. Both *audiences* reacted the same way.
3. She writes for a general *audience*.
4. The *audience* applauded for five minutes.

**2. A formal meeting with someone important.** (аудиенция)

5. He was granted an *audience* with the ambassador.

**See also:** [crowd](#crowd) &middot; [public](#public)

### crowd

**Pronunciation:** /kraʊd/ &middot; *krowd*
**Русский:** толпа
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the crowd was getting restless*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Unlike *team* or *committee*, *crowd* names an unorganized group, so the plural verb is rarer even in British English.

A large group of people gathered together.

**1. A large gathering.** (толпа)

1. The *crowd* was getting restless.
2. Both *crowds* were peaceful.
3. A *crowd* had formed outside the courthouse.
4. She hates *crowds*.

**2. A particular social set.** Informal. (компания, круг)

5. He started running with a different *crowd*.

**See also:** [audience](#audience) &middot; [public](#public)

### public

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpʌblɪk/ &middot; *PUB-lik*
**Русский:** общественность, публика
**Countability:** uncountable, used with *the*
**Agreement:** either — *the public is entitled to know* / *the public are being asked to stay indoors*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Almost always *the public*, never ✗ *a public* in this sense. American English tends to the singular verb, but the plural is common when individual people are meant.

Ordinary people considered as a whole.

**1. People in general.** (общественность)

1. The *public* is entitled to know.
2. The *public* are being asked to avoid the area.
3. This part of the building is closed to the *public*.
4. She has never spoken about it in *public*.

**2. A particular set of people.** With a modifier. (публика, аудитория)

5. The museum has built a loyal *public*.

**See also:** [crowd](#crowd) &middot; [police](#police)

### police

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈliːs/ &middot; *puh-LEES*
**Русский:** полиция
**Countability:** plural only — no singular, no plural *-s*
**Agreement:** **always plural** — *the police are investigating*
**Counted with:** *a police officer*, *two police officers*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** ✗ *The police is investigating* is wrong in every variety of English, and ✗ *a police* for one officer is wrong too — say *a police officer*, *a cop*, or *an officer*. This is the one collective noun with no singular option at all.

The civil force responsible for law enforcement.

**1. The force, or its officers.** Always plural. (полиция)

1. The *police* are investigating the incident.
2. *Police* have released a description.
3. Call the *police*.
4. Two *police* officers were at the door.

**2. Attributive.** (полицейский)

5. A *police* car blocked the intersection.
6. The *police* report has not been released.

**See also:** [public](#public) &middot; [staff](04-f-to-ves.md#staff)

### couple

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkʌpəl/ &middot; *KUP-uhl*
**Русский:** пара
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** either — *the couple is expecting* / *the couple were arguing*
**Type:** collective
**Variants:** *a couple of* is standard; *a couple* without *of* is informal American — *a couple things*
**Contrast:** Two people romantically together take either verb; American English leans singular for the unit, plural when they act separately. Note that *a couple of* meaning 'a few' takes a plural verb: *a couple of people **are** waiting*.

Two people together; also an approximate small number.

**1. Two people in a relationship.** (пара)

1. The *couple* is expecting their first child.
2. The *couple* were arguing in the parking lot.
3. Three *couples* came to dinner.

**2. An approximate small number.** With *of*, plural verb. (пара, несколько)

4. A *couple* of people are still waiting.
5. It'll take a *couple* of days.
6. Give me a *couple* more minutes. *(informal, without* of*)*

**See also:** [family](#family) &middot; [number](#number)

### number

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnʌmbər/ &middot; *NUM-bur*
**Русский:** число, количество
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** **depends on the article** — *the number of X is* / *a number of X are*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** This is the most useful agreement rule in the language, and the two halves are exact opposites. ***The** number of applicants **is** rising* — one number, singular. ***A** number of applicants **are** waiting* — *a number of* just means 'several', plural. Getting this backward is one of the commonest errors in edited prose.

A quantity or figure; a quantity of things.

**1. the number of — a singular quantity.** (число, количество)

1. The *number* of applicants is rising every year.
2. The *number* of errors was small.

**2. a number of — meaning 'several'.** Plural. (ряд, несколько)

3. A *number* of people are still waiting outside.
4. A *number* of problems have come up.

**3. A figure or numeral.** (число, номер)

5. Pick a *number* between one and ten.
6. What's your phone *number*?

**See also:** [couple](#couple) &middot; [majority](#majority)

### majority

**Pronunciation:** /məˈdʒɔːrəti/ &middot; *muh-JOR-uh-tee*
**Русский:** большинство
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** depends on what follows — *the majority is* (as a unit) / *the majority of voters are* (individuals)
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** The verb follows the noun after *of*. *The majority of the members **are** opposed* — plural, because *members* is plural. *A two-thirds majority **is** required* — singular, because it is one threshold. Same rule for *minority*, *percentage*, and *half*.

More than half of a group.

**1. More than half of a group of people.** Plural verb when *of* + plural noun follows. (большинство)

1. The *majority* of voters are undecided.
2. A *majority* of the committee were against it.

**2. A margin of victory, or a required threshold.** Singular. (большинство голосов)

3. A two-thirds *majority* is required to amend the rules.
4. Her *majority* was just four hundred votes.
5. The party lost its *majority* in the last election.

**See also:** [number](#number)

### herd

**Pronunciation:** /hɜːrd/ &middot; *hurd*
**Русский:** стадо
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the herd is moving north*
**Counted with:** *a herd of cattle*, *a herd of elephants*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** The collective noun must match the animal: *a herd of* cattle, horses, elephants, deer; *a flock of* birds or sheep; *a school of* fish; *a pack of* wolves or dogs; *a swarm of* bees or insects; *a pride of* lions.

A large group of hoofed animals.

**1. A group of animals.** (стадо)

1. The *herd* is moving toward the river.
2. Two *herds* graze on the same land.
3. A *herd* of elephants crossed the road.
4. He keeps a *herd* of forty dairy cows.

**2. A crowd of people.** Disapproving. (стадо, толпа)

5. She has never followed the *herd*.

**See also:** [flock](#flock) &middot; [school](#school)

### flock

**Pronunciation:** /flɑːk/ &middot; *flahk*
**Русский:** стая, отара
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the flock is heading south*
**Counted with:** *a flock of birds*, *a flock of sheep*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Used of birds and of sheep, and — by extension from the shepherd metaphor — of a congregation.

A group of birds or sheep.

**1. A group of birds or sheep.** (стая, отара)

1. A *flock* of geese passed overhead.
2. The *flock* is heading south early this year.
3. Two *flocks* were grazing on the hillside.
4. He tends a *flock* of two hundred sheep.

**2. A congregation.** Figurative. (паства)

5. The pastor knows every member of his *flock*.

**See also:** [herd](#herd) &middot; [school](#school)

### school

**Pronunciation:** /skuːl/ &middot; *skool*
**Русский:** косяк (рыб); школа
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the school is moving away from the boat*
**Counted with:** *a school of fish*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Unrelated to the educational sense despite the identical spelling — this *school* comes from a Dutch word meaning 'crowd'. The related term *shoal* means the same thing.

A large group of fish swimming together.

**1. A group of fish.** (косяк)

1. A *school* of tuna passed beneath the boat.
2. The *school* is moving into deeper water.
3. Two *schools* merged into one enormous mass.
4. Divers filmed a *school* of hammerheads.
5. Small fish form *schools* for protection.

**See also:** [herd](#herd) &middot; [flock](#flock)

### swarm

**Pronunciation:** /swɔːrm/ &middot; *sworm*
**Русский:** рой
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** singular in American English — *the swarm is moving toward the orchard*
**Counted with:** *a swarm of bees*, *a swarm of insects*
**Type:** collective
**Contrast:** Used of insects, and figuratively of any fast-moving mass of people.

A large group of insects moving together.

**1. A mass of insects.** (рой)

1. A *swarm* of bees settled in the chimney.
2. The *swarm* is heading for the orchard.
3. Two *swarms* left the hive that week.
4. *Swarms* of locusts destroyed the crop.

**2. A crowd of people.** Figurative. (толпа)

5. A *swarm* of reporters met him at the airport.
