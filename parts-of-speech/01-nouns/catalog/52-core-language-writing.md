# Core vocabulary: language and writing

> The everyday nouns of language and writing — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Part of the **core-vocabulary tier**. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A **Contrast** line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field *does* misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

---
### marker

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɑːrkər/ &middot; *MAR-kur*
**Русский:** маркер, фломастер
**Plural:** *markers*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** American; the British usually say felt-tip or marker pen.

A pen with a thick felt tip that writes in bold color.

1. Somebody used a permanent *marker* on the whiteboard.
2. Label the box with a black *marker*.
3. Half these *markers* are dried out.

### highlighter

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhaɪlaɪtər/ &middot; *HY-ly-tur*
**Русский:** маркер-выделитель, текстовыделитель
**Plural:** *highlighters*
**Countability:** countable

A pen with bright see-through ink for marking text you want to find again.

1. She went through the contract with a yellow *highlighter*.
2. My *highlighter* bled through the page.
3. I keep three *highlighters* in different colors.

### crayon

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkreɪɑːn/ &middot; *KRAY-ahn*
**Русский:** восковой мелок
**Plural:** *crayons*
**Countability:** countable

A stick of colored wax that children draw with.

1. He drew the whole family in *crayon*.
2. There's a melted *crayon* in the back seat.
3. The restaurant hands out *crayons* with the kids' menu.

### ink

**Pronunciation:** /ɪŋk/ &middot; *INGK*
**Русский:** чернила
**Countability:** uncountable

The colored liquid that pens and printers write with.

1. The printer is out of *ink* again.
2. Sign it in blue or black *ink*.
3. The rain smeared the *ink* on the envelope.

### stamp

**Pronunciation:** /stæmp/ &middot; *STAMP*
**Русский:** почтовая марка
**Plural:** *stamps*
**Countability:** countable

A small printed square you stick on mail to pay for delivery.

1. This letter needs another *stamp*.
2. He's collected *stamps* since he was a kid.
3. A book of *stamps* went up in price again.

### punctuation

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpʌŋktʃuˈeɪʃən/ &middot; *puhngk-choo-AY-shuhn*
**Русский:** пунктуация, знаки препинания
**Countability:** uncountable

The marks such as commas and periods that break writing into readable pieces.

1. Your *punctuation* is all over the place in this paragraph.
2. The text came through with no *punctuation* at all.
3. We spent a whole week on *punctuation* rules.

### period

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɪriəd/ &middot; *PEER-ee-uhd*
**Русский:** точка (знак препинания)
**Plural:** *periods*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** American; the British call this mark a full stop.

The dot that ends a sentence.

1. You forgot the *period* at the end of that line.
2. Put a *period* there, not a comma.
3. He writes in short sentences with a lot of *periods*.

---

### comma

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɑːmə/ &middot; *KAH-muh*
**Русский:** запятая
**Plural:** *commas*
**Countability:** countable

The mark that separates parts of a sentence.

1. You need a *comma* before the word and.
2. She took out half the *commas* in my draft.
3. One *comma* changes the whole meaning of that sentence.

### semicolon

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsemiˌkoʊlən/ &middot; *SEM-ee-koh-luhn*
**Русский:** точка с запятой
**Plural:** *semicolons*
**Countability:** countable

The mark that joins two closely related sentences.

1. A *semicolon* would work better than a period there.
2. Nobody in the office knows how to use a *semicolon*.
3. Her writing is full of *semicolons*.

---

### apostrophe

**Pronunciation:** /əˈpɑːstrəfi/ &middot; *uh-PAHS-truh-fee*
**Русский:** апостроф
**Plural:** *apostrophes*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Four syllables with the stress in the middle: uh-PAHS-truh-fee.

The raised mark used in contractions and to show possession.

1. It's spelled with an *apostrophe* between the t and the s.
2. You left the *apostrophe* out of don't.
3. Ordinary plurals don't take *apostrophes*.

### hyphen

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhaɪfən/ &middot; *HY-fuhn*
**Русский:** дефис
**Plural:** *hyphens*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** A hyphen (-) joins words; the longer dash (—) sets a phrase off from the rest.

The short line that joins two words into one.

1. Is well-known written with a *hyphen*?
2. Her last name has a *hyphen* in it.
3. American style drops the *hyphens* in a lot of these words.

---

### asterisk

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæstərɪsk/ &middot; *AS-tuh-risk*
**Русский:** звёздочка (знак *)
**Plural:** *asterisks*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Ends in -risk: AS-tuh-risk. AS-tuh-riks is a very common slip.

The star-shaped mark used to flag a note or an exception.

1. The cheap fares all have an *asterisk* next to them.
2. Put an *asterisk* by the ones you're not sure about.
3. The fine print is marked with *asterisks*.

### slash

**Pronunciation:** /slæʃ/ &middot; *SLASH*
**Русский:** косая черта, слэш
**Plural:** *slashes*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** The line leaning forward is a slash; the one leaning the other way is a backslash.

The forward-leaning line used between alternatives or in web addresses.

1. Write and/or with a *slash* between them.
2. There's an extra *slash* in that link.
3. The date is written with *slashes*.

### underscore

**Pronunciation:** /ˈʌndərskɔːr/ &middot; *UHN-dur-skor*
**Русский:** нижнее подчёркивание (знак _)
**Plural:** *underscores*
**Countability:** countable

The low line used in file names and usernames in place of a space.

1. My username has an *underscore* in it.
2. Use an *underscore* instead of a space in the file name.
3. The address has two *underscores*.

### ampersand

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæmpərsænd/ &middot; *AM-pur-sand*
**Русский:** амперсанд (знак &)
**Plural:** *ampersands*
**Countability:** countable

The symbol that stands for the word and.

1. The company name is written with an *ampersand*.
2. Use an *ampersand* only if the logo does.
3. Old shop signs are full of *ampersands*.

### exclamation point

**Pronunciation:** /ˌekskləˈmeɪʃən pɔɪnt/ &middot; *eks-kluh-MAY-shuhn point*
**Русский:** восклицательный знак
**Plural:** *exclamation points*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** American; the British say exclamation mark.

The mark that shows a shout, surprise, or strong feeling.

1. One *exclamation point* is plenty.
2. She ends every text with an *exclamation point*.
3. The email had three *exclamation points* in the subject line.

### question mark

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkwestʃən mɑːrk/ &middot; *KWES-chuhn mark*
**Русский:** вопросительный знак
**Plural:** *question marks*
**Countability:** countable

The mark that ends a question.

1. You left off the *question mark*.
2. That sentence needs a *question mark*, not a period.
3. His notes are covered in *question marks*.

### quotation mark

**Pronunciation:** /kwoʊˈteɪʃən mɑːrk/ &middot; *kwoh-TAY-shuhn mark*
**Русский:** кавычка
**Plural:** *quotation marks*
**Countability:** countable
**Contrast:** Americans use double marks where the British often use single ones.

One of the pair of marks that enclose someone's exact words.

1. Put his exact words in *quotation marks*.
2. She made *quotation marks* in the air with her fingers.
3. The closing *quotation mark* is missing.
