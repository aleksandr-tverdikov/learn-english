# Plurals made by changing the vowel

> No ending at all — the vowel inside the word changes: *foot → feet*, *mouse → mice*. Seven nouns, and that is the entire list.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These are the oldest plurals in English, older than the *-s* that took over everything else. A prehistoric ending containing an *i* pulled the vowel of the preceding syllable forward in the mouth, then the ending itself wore away, leaving only the changed vowel behind to carry the meaning. Grammarians call it **i-mutation**. What matters for a learner is that the list is **closed and complete: seven nouns**. Nothing new ever joins it — *mongoose* was borrowed from Marathi long after the process died, which is why its plural is *mongooses*, and a computer *mouse* only became *mice* because the animal word was already irregular.

The seven divide into three sound patterns: /ʊ/ → /iː/ (*foot*, *tooth*, *goose* — the last with /uː/), /æ/ → /ɛ/ (*man*), and /aʊ/ → /aɪ/ (*mouse*, *louse*). *Woman* is the one that catches everybody, because the change you hear is in the **first** syllable — /ˈwʊmən/ → /ˈwɪmɪn/ — while the spelling changes in the second.

### man

**Pronunciation:** /mæn/ &middot; *man*
**Русский:** мужчина; человек
**Plural:** *men* /mɛn/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the men are waiting*
**Pattern:** vowel change /æ/ → /ɛ/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *mans* is never a noun plural. *Man's* with an apostrophe is the possessive (*one man's opinion*), and *mans* exists only as a verb (*he mans the desk on Fridays*).

An adult male human — and, in older or more formal usage, a human being of any sex. Every compound ending in *-man* pluralizes the same way, and in all of them the syllable is reduced to /-mən/ in the singular and /-mɛn/ or, in fast speech, also /-mən/ in the plural.

**1. An adult male human.** The core, everyday sense. (мужчина)

1. A *man* in a gray coat asked for directions.
2. Three *men* were waiting by the gate.
3. She trusts that *man* completely.

**2. A human being; people in general.** Older and now marked. *Man* without an article, meaning humanity, survives in fixed phrases and formal writing, but modern American usage prefers *humans*, *people*, or *humankind* — *early humans*, not *early man*, in current science writing. (человек; человечество)

4. *Man* has always looked at the stars and wondered.
5. It is the oldest tool *man* ever made.

**3. In compounds.** *-man* compounds inherit the irregular plural whole: *policeman → policemen*, *fireman → firemen*, *salesman → salesmen*. Note that many are now routinely replaced by neutral forms in American English — *police officer*, *firefighter*, *salesperson* — and those take a regular *-s*. (в составе сложных слов)

6. Two *firemen* carried the ladder between them.
7. The *chairmen* of both committees signed the letter.
8. Both *salespeople* met their quota. *(neutral form, regular plural)*

**4. As a form of address, and as an interjection.** Informal and very common in American speech, addressed to anyone regardless of sex. (чувак; ну ты даёшь)

9. Hey, *man*, can you move your car?
10. Oh *man*, I completely forgot.

**See also:** [woman](#woman) &middot; [person](02-en-and-suppletive.md#person)

### woman

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwʊmən/ &middot; *WUUM-uhn*
**Русский:** женщина
**Plural:** *women* /ˈwɪmɪn/ &middot; *WIM-in*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the women are waiting*
**Pattern:** vowel change /ʊ/ → /ɪ/ in the **first** syllable &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** The trap is the sound, not the spelling. The written change is *-a-* → *-e-* in the **second** syllable, but the change you actually hear is /ʊ/ → /ɪ/ in the **first**: *WUUM-uhn* → *WIM-in*. Say the plural wrong and it sounds like the singular.

An adult female human. The most mispronounced plural in the language for learners, because the spelling and the sound change in different places.

**1. An adult female human.** The core, everyday sense. (женщина)

1. A *woman* at the next table recognized him.
2. Four *women* founded the company.
3. The *women* in my family all live past ninety.

**2. Women collectively, as a group in society.** Usually plural and without an article. (женщины)

4. *Women* were admitted to the college in 1972.
5. The magazine is written for *women* over forty.

**3. In compounds.** Same behavior as *-man*: *businesswoman → businesswomen*, *spokeswoman → spokeswomen*. (в составе сложных слов)

6. Both *congresswomen* voted against the bill.
7. The *saleswomen* worked on commission.

**See also:** [man](#man)

### foot

**Pronunciation:** /fʊt/ &middot; *fut*
**Русский:** нога, ступня; фут (мера длины)
**Plural:** *feet* /fiːt/ &middot; *feet*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *my feet hurt*
**Pattern:** vowel change /ʊ/ → /iː/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *Foots* is not a plural. But note the fixed measurement phrase: before a noun, Americans say *a six-**foot** fence*, singular — the hyphenated modifier never takes the plural.

The body part below the ankle; also a unit of length equal to twelve inches, and by extension the bottom of anything upright.

**1. The body part.** (ступня, нога)

1. He broke his *foot* playing basketball.
2. My *feet* are killing me after that walk.
3. She stood barefoot, both *feet* in the cold sand.

**2. A unit of length — twelve inches.** In this sense American speech often keeps *foot* even where a plural is expected, especially with a following inches figure: *five foot ten*. As a modifier before a noun it is always singular. (фут)

4. The ceiling is nine *feet* high.
5. He's six *foot* two. *(conversational; also* six feet two*)*
6. They built a ten-*foot* wall around the property.

**3. The bottom or base of something.** Of a hill, a bed, a page, a staircase. (подножие, низ)

7. The cabin sits at the *foot* of the mountain.
8. A note at the *foot* of the page explains the symbol.
9. She left her slippers at the *foot* of the bed.

**4. In fixed expressions.** These do not vary — learn them whole. (в устойчивых выражениях)

10. I think I got off on the wrong *foot* with her.
11. He put his *foot* in his mouth again at dinner.
12. We'll have to go on *foot* from here.

**See also:** [tooth](#tooth) &middot; [goose](#goose)

### tooth

**Pronunciation:** /tuːθ/ &middot; *tooth*
**Русский:** зуб
**Plural:** *teeth* /tiːθ/ &middot; *teeth*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *her teeth are straight*
**Pattern:** vowel change /uː/ → /iː/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *tooths* does not exist. The adjective is *toothed*, and the verb *teethe* /tiːð/ — with a voiced *th* — means to grow one's first teeth.

One of the hard structures in the mouth; also, by extension, any of the projecting points on a comb, a saw, or a gear.

**1. The body part.** (зуб)

1. The dentist pulled one *tooth* and filled two others.
2. Brush your *teeth* before bed.
3. She knocked out a front *tooth* in the fall.

**2. A projecting point on a tool or machine.** (зубец, зуб)

4. Two *teeth* were missing from the comb.
5. The gear's *teeth* had worn smooth after years of use.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. He was fighting *tooth* and nail to keep the house.
7. The new law has no *teeth* — nobody enforces it.
8. I'm lying through my *teeth* and she knows it.

**See also:** [foot](#foot)

### goose

**Pronunciation:** /ɡuːs/ &middot; *goos*
**Русский:** гусь
**Plural:** *geese* /ɡiːs/ &middot; *gees*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the geese are flying south*
**Pattern:** vowel change /uː/ → /iː/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** *Mongoose* is **not** related to this word — it came from Marathi centuries later — so its plural is *mongooses*, never ✗ *mongeese*. Likewise *moose* is unchanged in the plural: *one moose, two moose*.

A large water bird; also, informally, a foolish person.

**1. The bird.** (гусь)

1. A *goose* nested by the pond every spring.
2. Canada *geese* covered the entire soccer field.
3. We roasted a *goose* for the holiday.

**2. A silly person.** Dated but still heard, affectionate rather than harsh. (глупыш, дурачок)

4. Don't be such a *goose* — of course they invited you.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

5. Her skin broke out in *goose* bumps. *(also* goosebumps*; the modifier stays singular)*
6. That was a wild *goose* chase — the address didn't even exist.
7. What's good for the *goose* is good for the gander.

**See also:** [foot](#foot) &middot; [tooth](#tooth)

### mouse

**Pronunciation:** /maʊs/ &middot; *mows* (rhymes with *house*)
**Русский:** мышь
**Plural:** *mice* /maɪs/ &middot; *myse*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the mice are in the attic*
**Pattern:** vowel change /aʊ/ → /aɪ/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** computer sense: *mice* is standard, *mouses* is occasionally used by manufacturers
**Contrast:** The noun ends in a voiceless /s/; the verb *mouse* and the plural of the unrelated verb sense can voice it. Compare *house* /haʊs/ (noun) with *houses* /ˈhaʊzɪz/ — the same voicing shift, though *house* is a regular plural.

The small rodent; also the hand-held pointing device, which inherited the animal's irregular plural.

**1. The rodent.** (мышь)

1. A *mouse* ran across the kitchen floor.
2. We caught three *mice* in the garage last week.
3. The cat brought a dead *mouse* to the door.

**2. The computer device.** Named for its resemblance to the animal, and it took the animal's plural with it. (компьютерная мышь)

4. My *mouse* stopped scrolling.
5. The lab replaced all the keyboards and *mice*.

**3. A timid person.** Usually in the fixed adjective *mousy*, or as a direct comparison. (тихоня, робкий человек)

6. She was quiet as a *mouse* all through dinner.

**See also:** [louse](#louse)

### louse

**Pronunciation:** /laʊs/ &middot; *lows* (rhymes with *house*)
**Русский:** вошь; подлец
**Plural:** *lice* /laɪs/ &middot; *lyse*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *head lice are common in schools*
**Pattern:** vowel change /aʊ/ → /aɪ/ &middot; **Group:** 1
**Type:** concrete
**Variants:** in the 'contemptible person' sense the plural is regular — *louses*
**Contrast:** The two senses take different plurals. Insects are *lice*; people are *louses*. The related phrasal verb *louse up* means to ruin something.

The parasitic insect — in practice almost always plural — and, informally, a contemptible person.

**1. The insect.** Nearly always encountered as the plural *lice*, and most often in the compound *head lice*. (вошь)

1. The school sent a note home about head *lice*.
2. A single *louse* can lay several eggs a day.
3. The shelter treated the dogs for *lice* and fleas.

**2. A contemptible person.** Informal and dated, but instantly understood. This sense takes the regular plural. (подлец, мерзавец)

4. That *louse* never paid back a cent.
5. The two of them are absolute *louses*.

**3. louse up — to ruin.** Informal, always with *up*. (испортить, завалить)

6. Don't *louse* up the interview by showing up late.

**See also:** [mouse](#mouse)

### mongoose

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmɑːŋɡuːs/ &middot; *MAHNG-goos*
**Русский:** мангуст
**Plural:** *mongooses* /ˈmɑːŋɡuːsɪz/
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *mongooses hunt snakes*
**Pattern:** regular *-es* despite the ending &middot; **Group:** 1 (trap)
**Type:** concrete
**Contrast:** ✗ *mongeese* is the classic false analogy and is always wrong. The word has nothing to do with *goose*: it came into English from Marathi *maṅgūs*, long after i-mutation had stopped operating.

A small carnivorous mammal of Africa and Asia, famous for killing snakes. Listed here only because it is the trap that the *goose → geese* pattern sets.

**1. The animal.** (мангуст)

1. A *mongoose* killed the cobra in the courtyard.
2. *Mongooses* were introduced to the islands and devastated the birds.
3. The zoo's two *mongooses* share an enclosure.
4. He wrote a story about a *mongoose* named Rikki-Tikki-Tavi.
5. Unlike *geese*, *mongooses* take a perfectly regular plural.

**See also:** [goose](#goose)
