# Greek *-is* → *-es*

> *Analysis → analyses*, *crisis → crises*. The spelling change is small; the sound change is not — the plural ends in /-siːz/, *SEEZ*.

[← The grammar of nouns](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

This is the most **regular** of the foreign plural patterns and the most **mispronounced**. Every noun here ends in *-sis* /-sɪs/ in the singular and *-ses* /-siːz/ in the plural. Nothing else changes.

The trap is that the two forms look almost identical on the page while sounding clearly different in speech:

- *analysis* /əˈnæləsɪs/ → *analyses* /əˈnæləsiːz/ — *uh-NAL-uh-sis* → *uh-NAL-uh-seez*
- *crisis* /ˈkraɪsɪs/ → *crises* /ˈkraɪsiːz/ — *KRY-sis* → *KRY-seez*
- *thesis* /ˈθiːsɪs/ → *theses* /ˈθiːsiːz/ — *THEE-sis* → *THEE-seez*

Unlike the Latin groups, this pattern admits **no regular alternative**. ✗ *Analysises*, ✗ *crisises*, and ✗ *thesises* are all wrong, and there is no context in which they are acceptable. Learn the sound and the whole group follows.

One further trap: *axes* is the plural of both *axis* /ˈæksiːz/ and *ax* /ˈæksɪz/, distinguished only in pronunciation.

### analysis

**Pronunciation:** /əˈnæləsɪs/ &middot; *uh-NAL-uh-sis*
**Русский:** анализ
**Plural:** *analyses* /əˈnæləsiːz/ &middot; *uh-NAL-uh-seez*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as an activity
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the analyses agree*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *analysises* is wrong. Note also the verb *analyze* and the adjective *analytical*, which change the *s* to a *z* sound.

Detailed examination of something; the result of that examination.

**1. Detailed examination.** (анализ)

1. Her *analysis* of the data took three weeks.
2. Both *analyses* reached the same conclusion.
3. Further *analysis* is needed.

**2. A written or spoken account of that examination.** (разбор)

4. The paper offers a careful *analysis* of the failure.
5. Independent *analyses* were commissioned from two firms.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. In the final *analysis*, it came down to money.

**See also:** [basis](#basis) &middot; [hypothesis](#hypothesis)

### basis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbeɪsɪs/ &middot; *BAY-sis*
**Русский:** основа, основание
**Plural:** *bases* /ˈbeɪsiːz/ &middot; *BAY-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the bases for the claim are weak*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** *Bases* is the plural of both *basis* /ˈbeɪsiːz/ and *base* /ˈbeɪsɪz/ — a genuine ambiguity in writing, resolved only by sound or context. ✗ *Basises* is wrong.

The foundation or starting point for something.

**1. A foundation or underlying reason.** (основание)

1. There is no *basis* for that accusation.
2. The *bases* of the two theories are quite different.
3. His argument has a solid factual *basis*.

**2. A regular arrangement — on a … basis.** Extremely common in business English. (на основе, на условиях)

4. She works there on a part-time *basis*.
5. Meetings are held on a monthly *basis*.
6. Employees are hired on a contract *basis*.

**See also:** [analysis](#analysis) &middot; [axis](#axis)

### crisis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkraɪsɪs/ &middot; *KRY-sis*
**Русский:** кризис
**Plural:** *crises* /ˈkraɪsiːz/ &middot; *KRY-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the crises overlapped*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *crisises* is wrong. The adjective is *critical*, from the same Greek root meaning 'to decide' — a crisis is originally the turning point at which a decision must be made.

A time of intense difficulty or danger.

**1. A time of serious difficulty.** (кризис)

1. The country faced its worst financial *crisis* in decades.
2. Two *crises* hit the company in the same quarter.
3. She handles a *crisis* better than anyone I know.

**2. A turning point in an illness or situation.** (перелом, критический момент)

4. The fever broke and the *crisis* passed.

**3. Attributive.** (кризисный)

5. They set up a *crisis* hotline within a day.
6. Both *crises* were handled by the same team.

**See also:** [thesis](#thesis) &middot; [diagnosis](#diagnosis)

### thesis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈθiːsɪs/ &middot; *THEE-sis*
**Русский:** тезис; диссертация
**Plural:** *theses* /ˈθiːsiːz/ &middot; *THEE-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both theses were defended*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *thesises* is wrong. In American universities a *thesis* is usually a master's project and a *dissertation* the doctoral one — the reverse of British usage.

A statement put forward as a proposition; a long piece of academic writing.

**1. A proposition to be defended.** (тезис)

1. His central *thesis* is that the war was avoidable.
2. The two *theses* contradict each other.

**2. A long academic paper.** (диссертация, дипломная работа)

3. She finished her master's *thesis* in April.
4. All *theses* are archived in the library.
5. He defends his *thesis* next month.

**3. In fixed expressions.** (в устойчивых выражениях)

6. The argument moves from *thesis* to antithesis to synthesis.

**See also:** [hypothesis](#hypothesis) &middot; [synopsis](#synopsis)

### hypothesis

**Pronunciation:** /haɪˈpɑːθəsɪs/ &middot; *hy-PAH-thuh-sis*
**Русский:** гипотеза
**Plural:** *hypotheses* /haɪˈpɑːθəsiːz/ &middot; *hy-PAH-thuh-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the hypotheses were tested separately*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *hypothesises* is wrong. Note the stress falls on the second syllable in both forms. The adjective is *hypothetical*.

A proposed explanation, put forward to be tested.

**1. A testable proposal.** (гипотеза)

1. The *hypothesis* was not supported by the data.
2. Several competing *hypotheses* remain.
3. We tested each *hypothesis* independently.
4. Her *hypothesis* turned out to be right.

**2. A guess or assumption.** More general. (предположение)

5. That's a reasonable working *hypothesis*.

**See also:** [thesis](#thesis) &middot; [analysis](#analysis)

### diagnosis

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdaɪəɡˈnoʊsɪs/ &middot; *dy-uhg-NOH-sis*
**Русский:** диагноз
**Plural:** *diagnoses* /ˌdaɪəɡˈnoʊsiːz/ &middot; *dy-uhg-NOH-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the two diagnoses conflicted*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *diagnosises* is wrong. The verb is *diagnose* and the adjective *diagnostic*.

Identification of an illness or a problem.

**1. Identification of a disease.** (диагноз)

1. The *diagnosis* took months to reach.
2. Both *diagnoses* pointed to the same condition.
3. She sought a second opinion on the *diagnosis*.
4. Early *diagnosis* improves survival dramatically.

**2. Identification of any problem.** Extended use. (постановка диагноза, определение проблемы)

5. The engineer's *diagnosis* was a failed sensor.

**See also:** [prognosis](#prognosis) &middot; [crisis](#crisis)

### prognosis

**Pronunciation:** /prɑːɡˈnoʊsɪs/ &middot; *prahg-NOH-sis*
**Русский:** прогноз (медицинский)
**Plural:** *prognoses* /prɑːɡˈnoʊsiːz/ &middot; *prahg-NOH-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the prognoses were guarded*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** A *diagnosis* says what is wrong; a *prognosis* says what will happen next. They are constantly confused.

A forecast of how an illness or situation will develop.

**1. A medical forecast.** (прогноз болезни)

1. The *prognosis* is good if treatment starts now.
2. Both *prognoses* were cautiously optimistic.
3. Doctors gave a poor *prognosis*.

**2. A forecast about anything.** (прогноз)

4. The *prognosis* for the industry is bleak.
5. Analysts offered wildly different *prognoses*.

**See also:** [diagnosis](#diagnosis)

### emphasis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛmfəsɪs/ &middot; *EM-fuh-sis*
**Русский:** акцент, ударение
**Plural:** *emphases* /ˈɛmfəsiːz/ &middot; *EM-fuh-seez*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable in general use
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the emphases differ between the two editions*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *emphasises* is wrong as a noun plural — *emphasises* is the British spelling of the verb *emphasizes*. Americans write *emphasizes*.

Special importance given to something; stress placed on a word.

**1. Special importance or attention.** Usually uncountable. (акцент, упор)

1. The school places great *emphasis* on writing.
2. The *emphasis* has shifted to prevention.
3. Different departments have different *emphases*.

**2. Stress on a syllable or word.** (ударение)

4. Put the *emphasis* on the second syllable.
5. The italics indicate *emphasis*.

**See also:** [analysis](#analysis) &middot; [synthesis](#synthesis)

### axis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæksɪs/ &middot; *AK-sis*
**Русский:** ось
**Plural:** *axes* /ˈæksiːz/ &middot; *AK-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *both axes are labeled*
**Pattern:** Greek *-is* → *-es* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** *Axes* is also the plural of *ax* — but pronounced differently: *AK-seez* for axes of rotation, *AK-siz* for the chopping tools. In writing only context tells them apart.

A line about which something rotates, or a reference line on a graph.

**1. A line of rotation.** (ось вращения)

1. The Earth turns on its *axis* once a day.
2. The planet's *axis* is tilted 23 degrees.

**2. A reference line on a graph.** (ось координат)

3. Label both *axes* before you plot anything.
4. Time goes on the horizontal *axis*.
5. The vertical *axis* shows temperature.

**3. An alliance between countries.** Historical and political. (ось, блок)

6. The *Axis* powers signed the pact in 1940.

**See also:** [basis](#basis)

### oasis

**Pronunciation:** /oʊˈeɪsɪs/ &middot; *oh-AY-sis*
**Русский:** оазис
**Plural:** *oases* /oʊˈeɪsiːz/ &middot; *oh-AY-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the oases are days apart*
**Pattern:** Greek *-is* → *-es* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *oasises* is wrong. Note the stress on the second syllable in both forms.

A fertile place in a desert; figuratively, any refuge.

**1. A watered place in a desert.** (оазис)

1. The caravan reached the *oasis* by nightfall.
2. Several *oases* lie along the old trade route.
3. Palm trees mark the *oasis* from miles away.

**2. A place of relief or calm.** Figurative and very common. (оазис, отдушина)

4. The garden is an *oasis* in the middle of the city.
5. Her office was an *oasis* of quiet.
6. These parks are *oases* for people with no yard.

**See also:** [axis](#axis)

### parenthesis

**Pronunciation:** /pəˈrɛnθəsɪs/ &middot; *puh-REN-thuh-sis*
**Русский:** скобка; вводное слово
**Plural:** *parentheses* /pəˈrɛnθəsiːz/ &middot; *puh-REN-thuh-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the parentheses are unbalanced*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** In everyday American speech the punctuation marks are usually just called *parens* or *round brackets*. The singular refers to one mark, so a pair is always *parentheses*.

A round bracket; also a word or phrase inserted as an aside.

**1. The punctuation mark.** Almost always plural, since they come in pairs. (скобка)

1. Put the date in *parentheses*.
2. Close the *parenthesis* after the citation.
3. The formula has three sets of *parentheses*.

**2. An inserted aside.** (вводное замечание)

4. The whole clause is a *parenthesis* and can be cut.
5. His speech was full of *parentheses* and digressions.

**See also:** [ellipsis](#ellipsis)

### ellipsis

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈlɪpsɪs/ &middot; *ih-LIP-sis*
**Русский:** многоточие; эллипсис
**Plural:** *ellipses* /ɪˈlɪpsiːz/ &middot; *ih-LIP-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the ellipses mark omitted text*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** *Ellipses* is also the plural of *ellipse*, the oval shape — and the two are pronounced differently: *ih-LIP-seez* for both, in fact, which makes them true homophones distinguished only by context.

The three dots marking omitted words; also the omission itself.

**1. The punctuation mark.** (многоточие)

1. Use an *ellipsis* to show you cut part of the quote.
2. The page is littered with *ellipses*.

**2. Omission of words that are understood.** Grammar. (эллипсис)

3. "Coming?" is an *ellipsis* of "Are you coming?"
4. English allows *ellipsis* after auxiliary verbs.
5. Both *ellipses* make the sentence hard to parse.

**See also:** [parenthesis](#parenthesis)

**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *synopsises* is wrong.

A brief summary of a book, film, or argument.

**1. A summary.** (краткое изложение)

1. Send a one-page *synopsis* with the manuscript.
2. All the *synopses* were written by the same intern.

### synthesis

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪnθəsɪs/ &middot; *SIN-thuh-sis*
**Русский:** синтез
**Plural:** *syntheses* /ˈsɪnθəsiːz/ &middot; *SIN-thuh-seez*
**Countability:** countable, and uncountable as a process
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the syntheses were repeated*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** ✗ *synthesises* is wrong as a noun. The verb is *synthesize*.

The combination of parts into a whole; in chemistry, the making of a compound.

**1. A combining of ideas.** (синтез)

1. The book is a *synthesis* of thirty years of research.
2. Both *syntheses* draw on the same sources.

**2. In chemistry.** (синтез вещества)

3. The *synthesis* takes four steps.
4. Several *syntheses* of the drug have been published.
5. Protein *synthesis* happens in the ribosome.

### neurosis

**Pronunciation:** /nʊˈroʊsɪs/ &middot; *nuu-ROH-sis*
**Русский:** невроз
**Plural:** *neuroses* /nʊˈroʊsiːz/ &middot; *nuu-ROH-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *his neuroses are well documented*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** abstract
**Contrast:** No longer a formal clinical term, but common in everyday and literary use. Compare *psychosis → psychoses*, which is still clinical.

A relatively mild mental or emotional disturbance.

**1. The condition.** (невроз)

1. The *neurosis* had built up over years.
2. Both *neuroses* trace back to the same event.
3. He writes about his *neuroses* with real humor.

**2. Loosely — an anxious obsession.** Informal. (навязчивая тревога)

4. Her *neurosis* about punctuality drives everyone crazy.
5. The whole industry runs on collective *neuroses*.

### metamorphosis

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmɛtəˈmɔːrfəsɪs/ &middot; *met-uh-MOR-fuh-sis*
**Русский:** метаморфоза, превращение
**Plural:** *metamorphoses* /ˌmɛtəˈmɔːrfəsiːz/ &middot; *met-uh-MOR-fuh-seez*
**Countability:** countable
**Agreement:** plural verb — *the metamorphoses take weeks*
**Pattern:** Greek *-sis* → *-ses* &middot; **Group:** 9
**Type:** concrete and abstract
**Contrast:** The stress is on the third syllable — *met-uh-MOR-fuh-sis* — not on *-morph-* alone.

A complete change of form; in biology, the transformation of a larva into an adult.

**1. In biology.** (метаморфоза)

1. The *metamorphosis* from caterpillar to butterfly takes two weeks.
2. Insect *metamorphoses* follow a fixed sequence.
3. The tadpole begins its *metamorphosis* in spring.

**2. A dramatic change in anything.** Figurative. (превращение)

4. The neighborhood has undergone a complete *metamorphosis*.
5. Her *metamorphosis* from intern to editor took four years.

**See also:** [crisis](#crisis)
