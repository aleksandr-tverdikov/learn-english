Core vocabulary: railways#

The everyday nouns of railways — pronunciation, Russian, plural, and three examples each.

← The grammar of nouns · All groups

Part of the core-vocabulary tier. Groups 1–23 cover the nouns that misbehave; this tier covers the nouns you simply need to know. Entries are deliberately lean, because a regular countable noun has no grammatical story to tell. A Contrast line appears only where the word hides a genuine trap.

Where a word in this field does misbehave, it lives in the grammar tier instead and is not repeated here.

railway#

Pronunciation
/ˈreɪlweɪ/ · RAYL-way
Русский
железная дорога
Plural
railways
Countability
countable
Contrast
Americans normally say railroad; railway survives mainly in company names and in British English.

A system of tracks and trains, or the company that operates it.

  1. The old railway ran straight through the middle of town.
  2. Several railways merged into one company in the 1970s.
  3. A narrow railway still climbs to the summit every summer.

rail#

Pronunciation
/reɪl/ · RAYL
Русский
рельс; железнодорожный транспорт; поручень; перекладина, штанга
Plural
rails
Countability
both — countable and uncountable with different meanings

Rail has 2 distinct senses.

1. One of the two steel bars a train runs on, or train transportation taken as a whole. (рельс; железнодорожный транспорт)

  1. A crew replaced a cracked rail just south of the bridge.
  2. Two steel rails ran straight out to the horizon.
  3. We shipped the whole load by rail instead of by truck.

2. A fixed horizontal bar for holding onto or for hanging things from. (поручень; перекладина, штанга)

  1. Hold the rail on your way down the stairs.
  2. Wet towels were hanging on the rail.
  3. A brass rail runs along the front of the bar.

Pronunciation

Русский

Plural

Countability
countable
Contrast
Americans call their own systems the subway; metro is used mostly for foreign systems and for the one in Washington.

Metro has 2 distinct senses.

1. An urban underground railroad system. (метро)

  1. We bought a day pass for the metro.
  2. The metro runs until one in the morning.
  3. Several European metros are older than ours.

2. A large city taken together with the suburbs around it. (агломерация, город с пригородами)

  1. Half a million people live in the Portland metro.
  2. Rents are climbing across the whole metro.
  3. The chain has stores in four metros.

  1. Take the tram from the parking lot to the terminal.
  2. We rode the tram around the zoo.
  3. The tram stops at every hotel on the strip.
