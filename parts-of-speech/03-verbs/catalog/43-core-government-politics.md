# Core verbs: government

> The everyday regular verbs of government — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary built entirely of irregular and phrasal verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### elect

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈlɛkt/ &middot; *i-LEKT*
**Русский:** избирать, избрать; выбирать; решать, решить (сделать что-л.); предпочитать, предпочесть
**Forms:** *elect* &middot; *elected* &middot; *elected*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *elects* &middot; **-ing form:** *electing*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** You are *elected* **to** an office and *elected* **as** something: elected to the council, elected as chairman.

Elect has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To choose someone for a public office by voting.** (избирать, избрать; выбирать)

1. Americans *elect* a president every four years.
2. She was *elected* to the state senate in 2022.
3. The district has never *elected* a Democrat.

**2. To choose to do something, especially in a formal or official situation.** (решать, решить (сделать что-л.); предпочитать, предпочесть)

4. She *elected* to take the earlier flight.
5. Employees may *elect* to receive the bonus as stock.
6. Having *elected* to represent himself, he lost the case.

1. Each party will *nominate* a candidate in the summer.

### poll

**Pronunciation:** /poʊl/ &middot; *POHL*
**Русский:** опрашивать, проводить опрос; набирать (голоса в опросах)
**Forms:** *poll* &middot; *polled* &middot; *polled*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *polls* &middot; **-ing form:** *polling*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (*poll* likely voters), intransitive (she is *polling* at 40 percent)

To ask a sample of people what they think, or to score a certain result in such a survey.

1. Networks *poll* voters right outside the precinct.
2. The paper *polled* a thousand adults last week.
3. He is *polling* well in the suburbs.

### govern

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡʌvərn/ &middot; *GUV-urn*
**Русский:** править, управлять (страной); регулировать, определять, управлять (о правилах)
**Forms:** *govern* &middot; *governed* &middot; *governed*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *governs* &middot; **-ing form:** *governing*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (*govern* a country), intransitive (it is harder to *govern*)
**Contrast:** The o sounds like the u in *cup*: GUV-urn, not GOH-vurn.

Govern has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To officially control and run a country, a state, or a city.** (править, управлять (страной))

1. It is easier to campaign than to *govern*.
2. The party *governed* for twelve years.
3. The island has been *governed* from the mainland since 1898.

**2. To control or decide how something works, said of a rule, a law, or a force.** (регулировать, определять, управлять (о правилах))

4. Federal law *governs* how the grant money can be spent.
5. The terms *governing* the loan are in section four.
6. For years, habit *governed* everything he did.

### reign

**Pronunciation:** /reɪn/ &middot; *RAYN*
**Русский:** царствовать, править (о монархе); царить, воцариться; господствовать; быть действующим чемпионом, удерживать титул
**Forms:** *reign* &middot; *reigned* &middot; *reigned*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *reigns* &middot; **-ing form:** *reigning*
**Transitivity:** intransitive
**Contrast:** *reign* (rule) sounds exactly like *rain* and *rein*, and it is spelled with -eign.

Reign has 3 distinct senses.

**1. To rule as a king or a queen.** (царствовать, править (о монархе))

1. Few monarchs *reign* for more than fifty years.
2. She *reigned* for seven decades.
3. He had *reigned* only a year when the war began.

**2. To be the main feature of a situation for a period of time.** (царить, воцариться; господствовать)

4. Silence *reigns* in the reading room.
5. Chaos *reigned* for the rest of the afternoon.
6. Confusion has *reigned* since the announcement.

**3. To hold a championship or a title at the present time.** (быть действующим чемпионом, удерживать титул)

7. She *reigned* as world champion for five years.
8. The *reigning* Super Bowl champions open the season at home.
9. No one has *reigned* that long in the sport.

### empower

**Pronunciation:** /ɪmˈpaʊər/ &middot; *im-POW-ur*
**Русский:** уполномочивать, уполномочить; наделять правом; давать уверенность в своих силах, расширять возможности
**Forms:** *empower* &middot; *empowered* &middot; *empowered*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *empowers* &middot; **-ing form:** *empowering*
**Transitivity:** transitive

Empower has 2 distinct senses.

**1. To give a person or a body the legal power to do something.** (уполномочивать, уполномочить; наделять правом)

1. The law *empowers* the governor to call out the guard.
2. Congress *empowered* the agency to write the rules.
3. Local boards have been *empowered* to set their own budgets.

**2. To make someone feel confident and in control of their own life.** (давать уверенность в своих силах, расширять возможности)

4. Learning to drive *empowered* her.
5. Good teachers *empower* students to think for themselves.
6. She felt *empowered* after the first week of classes.

### control

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtroʊl/ &middot; *kuhn-TROHL*
**Русский:** контролировать, управлять; владеть большинством; управлять (машиной, устройством), вести; сдерживать, контролировать (себя, эмоции); держать под контролем, сдерживать (болезнь, расходы)
**Forms:** *control* &middot; *controlled* &middot; *controlled*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *controls* &middot; **-ing form:** *controlling*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** The l doubles before an ending, in American English too: *control → controlled → controlling*.

Control has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To have the power to decide what an institution or a place does.** (контролировать, управлять; владеть большинством)

1. One party will *control* both chambers next year.
2. Rebels *controlled* the capital for a week.
3. Prices here are *controlled* by the state.

**2. To make a machine or a vehicle do what you want.** (управлять (машиной, устройством), вести)

4. A single chip *controls* the whole system.
5. The pilot *controlled* the plane with one hand.
6. He was *controlling* the drone from the parking lot.

**3. To hold your own feelings or behavior in check.** (сдерживать, контролировать (себя, эмоции))

7. Try to *control* your temper.
8. She *controlled* herself and said nothing.
9. He has trouble *controlling* his spending.

**4. To keep something within safe limits so that it does not get worse.** (держать под контролем, сдерживать (болезнь, расходы))

10. Diet and exercise can *control* blood pressure.
11. Firefighters *controlled* the blaze by midnight.
12. The infection is being *controlled* with antibiotics.

### support

**Pronunciation:** /səˈpɔːrt/ &middot; *suh-PORT*
**Русский:** поддерживать, поддержать; выступать за; поддерживать, подпирать; выдерживать вес; содержать, обеспечивать (семью); поддерживать (формат, функцию), быть совместимым с
**Forms:** *support* &middot; *supported* &middot; *supported*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *supports* &middot; **-ing form:** *supporting*
**Transitivity:** transitive

Support has 4 distinct senses.

**1. To be in favor of a person, a policy, or a plan.** (поддерживать, поддержать; выступать за)

1. Most voters *support* the measure.
2. She *supported* the bill in committee and on the floor.
3. The proposal is *supported* by both parties.

**2. To hold the weight of something and keep it from falling.** (поддерживать, подпирать; выдерживать вес)

4. Four stone columns *support* the roof.
5. The old shelf *supported* far more weight than it should have.
6. The walkway is *supported* by steel cables.

**3. To provide someone with the money they need to live.** (содержать, обеспечивать (семью))

7. He *supports* a family of five on one salary.
8. She *supported* herself through college by waiting tables.
9. Their parents have *supported* them for years.

**4. For a device or a program to be able to work with something.** (поддерживать (формат, функцию), быть совместимым с)

10. My phone doesn't *support* that app anymore.
11. The older model never *supported* wireless charging.
12. That file type is no longer *supported*.

### influence

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪnfluəns/ &middot; *IN-floo-uhns*
**Русский:** влиять, повлиять; оказывать влияние
**Forms:** *influence* &middot; *influenced* &middot; *influenced*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *influences* &middot; **-ing form:** *influencing*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** The verb takes a direct object: you *influence* someone, not "influence on" someone.

To have an effect on what someone decides or how something turns out.

1. Big donors *influence* which bills get a hearing.
2. Foreign money *influenced* the last campaign.
3. The vote was *influenced* by one long speech.
