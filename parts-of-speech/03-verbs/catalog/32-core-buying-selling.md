# Core verbs: money and trade

> The everyday regular verbs of money and trade — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### shop

**Pronunciation:** /ʃɑːp/ &middot; *SHAHP*
**Русский:** делать покупки, ходить по магазинам
**Forms:** *shop* &middot; *shopped* &middot; *shopped*
**Third person:** *shops* &middot; **-ing form:** *shopping*
**Transitivity:** intransitive
**Contrast:** Double the *p*: *shopped*, *shopping*. You *shop* **for** what you are looking for.

To go to stores looking for things to buy.

1. We usually *shop* on Saturday mornings.
2. They *shopped* for a used car all weekend.
3. I hate *shopping* for clothes.

### afford

**Pronunciation:** /əˈfɔːrd/ &middot; *uh-FORD*

**Forms:** *afford* &middot; *afforded* &middot; *afforded*
**Third person:** *affords* &middot; **-ing form:** *affording*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Almost always with *can*, *can't*, *could* or *be able to*: *I can't afford it*, not *I don't afford it*.

3. He works two jobs and still barely *affords* the rent.

### double

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʌbəl/ &middot; *DUB-uhl*
**Русский:** удваивать; увеличиваться вдвое
**Forms:** *double* &middot; *doubled* &middot; *doubled*
**Third person:** *doubles* &middot; **-ing form:** *doubling*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (double the price), intransitive (the rent doubled)

To make an amount twice as large, or to become twice as large.

1. Shipping costs can *double* around the holidays.
2. The rent *doubled* in six years.
3. They're *doubling* the size of the store.
