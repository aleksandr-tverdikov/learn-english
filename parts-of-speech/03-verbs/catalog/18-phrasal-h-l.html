Phrasal verbs: hang — lose#

Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs hang, have, hear… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

← Irregular verbs · The grammar of verbs

A phrasal verb is an irregular verb welded to a small word — up, out, on, behind — and the weld changes the meaning past recognition: hold up has nothing to do with holding, hang out has nothing to do with hanging, and lay off has nothing to do with lying down. You cannot take these apart and add up the pieces, so you learn each one whole, the way you learn a single word. The other half of the job is separability: whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle (hang the phone up) or must follow the whole phrase (hide behind the rules). It matters because a separable verb with a pronoun object has to split — hang it up, never ✗ hang up it — while an inseparable one never may, so every entry below states plainly which kind it is.

hang around#

Pronunciation
/ˌhæŋ əˈraʊnd/ · hang-uh-ROUND · the stress lands on the particle — hang aROUND, not HANG around
Русский
слоняться, болтаться, торчать; водиться с
Base verb
hang
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
no — the place or the person always follows the whole phrase. We hung around the parking lot, never ✗ we hung the parking lot around; with a pronoun, hang around them, never ✗ hang them around.
Transitive
either — it takes no object when it means "wait idly," and a place or (with with) a person otherwise
Register
informal
Verb type
action — dynamic and atelic, an activity that fills time (we hung around for an hour); sense 4 (that cough has hung around since January) shades toward a state, but it still takes the progressive freely.
Passive
no — the "wait" sense has no object at all, and the place and person senses are prepositional: ✗ the parking lot was hung around, ✗ she was hung around with.

Spend time somewhere or with someone without any particular purpose.

1. Wait or linger with nothing to do. No object. The idea is aimlessness — time passing without a plan, often with a hint of impatience.

  1. I hung around for an hour and she never showed up.
  2. Don't just hang around — grab a broom.

2. Spend time in a place. With a place as the object. American English also says hang out at.

  1. Kids hang around the 7-Eleven all summer.
  2. He hung around the courthouse hoping for a quote.

3. hang around with — keep company with. About the people someone chooses, and usually a little disapproving when a parent says it.

  1. She started hanging around with a rough crowd.
  2. I don't hang around with them anymore.

4. Stay in place longer than expected. Of a thing, a smell, or a feeling that refuses to go away.

  1. That cough has hung around since January.

hang on#

Pronunciation

Русский
подождать; держаться; зависеть от
Base verb
hang
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
no — nothing ever comes between hang and on. In the "depend" sense on is a preposition and keeps its object behind it: it hangs on the weather, ✗ it hangs the weather on; with a pronoun, it hangs on that, ✗ it hangs that on.
Transitive
either — no object in the "wait" and "grip" senses, an object after on in the "depend" sense
Register
everyday
Verb type
action in the "wait" and "grip" senses — dynamic (hang on a second, hang on tight). Stative in the "depend" sense: it hangs on the weather is a relation, not an event.
Passive
no — the wait and grip senses take no object, and the stative on-phrase resists the prepositional passive: ✗ the weather is hung on.

Wait, keep your grip, or depend on something, according to the sense.

1. Wait a moment. Informal and nearly always an imperative, on the phone or in person. Hold on is the interchangeable twin.

  1. Hang on — I've got another call.
  2. Hang on a second, that's not what the invoice says.

2. Keep a tight grip. Physical, and no object: what you grip goes after to.

  1. Hang on, this road gets rough.
  2. She hung on until the rescue crew reached her.

3. Depend on, be determined by. Here on is a preposition and the subject is usually a decision or an outcome.

  1. The whole season hangs on Sunday's game.
  2. Everything hung on whether the bank called the loan.

4. Listen with total attention. Fixed in hang on someone's every word.

  1. The students hung on his every word.

Pronunciation
/ˌhæŋ ˈʌp/ · hang-UP · the particle takes the stress — hang it UP, never HANG it up
Русский
вешать трубку; вешать (на крючок); завершить карьеру
Base verb
hang
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
yes — hang up your coat or hang your coat up, and with a pronoun it must split: hang it up, never ✗ hang up it. The phone sense is often used with no object at all: he hung up on me.
Transitive
either — no object in the phone sense, an object in the others
Register
everyday
Verb type
action — dynamic and telic in every live sense: ending a call, putting a coat on a hook, retiring the cleats. Sense 4 (be hung up on someone) is an adjectival state, not a verb use.

yes in the object senses — the coats were hung up by the door, his skates were hung up years ago. The fixed adjectival passive she's hung up on him is the ordinary form of sense 4.

End a phone call, put something on a hook, or retire from an activity.

1. End a phone call. Usually with no object; hang up on someone means ending it rudely, mid-conversation.

  1. She said goodbye and hung up.
  2. Don't hang up — I'm putting you on speaker.
  3. He hung up on me twice.

2. Put on a hook or hanger. Separable, and the split order is at least as common.

  1. Hang up your coat before you sit down.
  2. He hung the wet towel up on the rail.
  3. It's a nice jacket — hang it up instead of dropping it there.

3. Retire from a sport or a job. Fixed with the tool of the trade: hang up his cleats, her skates, the badge.

  1. After twenty seasons he finally hung up his skates.

4. be hung up — be stuck or obsessed. Passive or adjectival. Hung up on something means unable to stop thinking about it; hung up alone means delayed.

  1. The permit is hung up in the county office.
  2. She's still hung up on her ex.
