Core verbs: speech and communication#

The everyday regular verbs of speech and communication — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

add#

Pronunciation
/æd/ · AD
Русский
добавлять, добавить (сказать ещё)
Forms
add · added · added
Third person
adds · -ing form: adding
Transitivity
both — transitive (add a comment), intransitive ("That's all," he added)

To say something more after what has already been said.

  1. "And bring your passport," she added.
  2. Would you like to add anything before we finish?
  3. He has added a note at the bottom of the letter.

Forms

Third person
addresses · -ing form: addressing
Transitivity
transitive
Contrast

To speak formally to a person or a group.

  1. The mayor will address the city council tonight.
  2. She addressed the crowd from the steps of the library.
  3. He was addressing the students when the fire alarm went off.

Contrast

Pronunciation

Forms

Third person
agrees · -ing form: agreeing
Transitivity
both — transitive (I agree that it's late), intransitive (we all agree)
Contrast

To have the same opinion as someone else, or to say yes to a plan.

  3. We have never agreed about politics.
