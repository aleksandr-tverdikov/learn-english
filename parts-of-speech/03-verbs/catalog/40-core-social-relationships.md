# Core verbs: relationships

> The everyday regular verbs of relationships — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### acclaim

**Pronunciation:** /əˈkleɪm/ &middot; *uh-KLAYM*
**Русский:** восторженно принимать, высоко оценивать
**Forms:** *acclaim* &middot; *acclaimed* &middot; *acclaimed*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *acclaims* &middot; **-ing form:** *acclaiming*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Most often used in the passive: *a widely acclaimed writer*.

To praise someone or something publicly and enthusiastically.

1. Critics *acclaimed* the young pianist after her first concert.
2. Readers still *acclaim* him as the finest writer of his generation.
3. Her second novel has been widely *acclaimed*.

### admonish

**Pronunciation:** /ədˈmɑːnɪʃ/ &middot; *uhd-MAH-nish*
**Русский:** делать замечание, увещевать
**Forms:** *admonish* &middot; *admonished* &middot; *admonished*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *admonishes* &middot; **-ing form:** *admonishing*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Ends in -sh, so the third person adds -es: *admonishes*.

To tell someone firmly but not angrily that they have done something wrong.

1. Teachers often *admonish* students who talk during a test.
2. The judge *admonished* him for interrupting.
3. She has *admonished* the boys twice about their language.

### aggravate

**Pronunciation:** /ˈæɡrəveɪt/ &middot; *AG-ruh-vayt*
**Русский:** раздражать, злить; усугублять
**Forms:** *aggravate* &middot; *aggravated* &middot; *aggravated*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *aggravates* &middot; **-ing form:** *aggravating*
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** In American English it means both *annoy a person* and *make a problem worse* — the context decides which.

To annoy someone, or to make a bad situation worse.

1. His constant humming *aggravates* everyone in the office.
2. She *aggravated* her sister by reading her messages.
3. He was *aggravating* the neighbors with loud music all weekend.
