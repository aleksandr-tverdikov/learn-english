# Phrasal verbs: take — write

> Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs take, teach, tear… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

[← Irregular verbs](README.md) &middot; [The grammar of verbs](../README.md)

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle whose meaning you cannot reach by adding the two halves together: *take after* has nothing to do with following, *tell off* has nothing to do with counting, and *write off* has nothing to do with writing. The second thing to know about each one is separability — whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle. Some let it go either way (*take your coat off* or *take off your coat*), some force it inside as soon as it is a pronoun (*take it off*, never ✗ *take off it*), and prepositional and three-part verbs never let it in at all (*take after him*, never ✗ *take him after*). Every entry below spells out which, because that one line is the difference between sounding native and sounding wrong.

### take aback

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk əˈbæk/ &middot; *take-uh-BAK* &middot; the particle carries the stress — *took me aBACK*
**Русский:** ошеломить, застать врасплох
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — and the object almost always sits inside the phrase: *her answer took me aback*. With a pronoun it **must** split: ✗ *took aback me*. A full noun after the particle (*took aback the whole room*) is possible but rare.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object — and usually in the passive, *be taken aback*
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, but a momentary event of surprising someone, so the progressive is odd: ✗ *her answer is taking me aback*. It has no linking or stative use.
**Passive:** **yes**, and the passive is the usual form — *I was taken aback by how blunt her answer was*; *she looked taken aback*. The active *her answer took me aback* is the marked one.

Surprise someone so completely that they are briefly lost for words.

1. I was *taken aback* by how blunt her answer was.
2. His question *took* me *aback*, and I just stared at him.
3. She looked *taken aback* when I offered to pay for everything.
4. Don't be *taken aback* if he turns the offer down.
5. The size of the repair bill *took* us *aback*.
6. Everyone was *taken aback* by how quickly he agreed.

**See also:** [throw off](#throw-off), [take in](#take-in)

### take after

**Pronunciation:** /ˈteɪk ˌæf.tɚ/ &middot; *TAKE-af-ter* &middot; in a prepositional verb the verb keeps the stress; the preposition stays weak
**Русский:** быть похожим на, пойти в (кого-то из родни)
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object always follows the whole phrase: *takes after her mother*, *takes after her*. ✗ *takes her mother after*, ✗ *takes her after*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — a resemblance, like *resemble* itself, not anything the subject does. It rejects the progressive: ✗ *she is taking after her mother*.
**Passive:** **no** — a stative resemblance verb, and the prepositional object cannot be promoted: ✗ *her mother is taken after by her*, ✗ *his grandfather was taken after*.

Resemble an older relative in looks, character, or habits. It runs one way only: a child takes after a parent, never the other way around.

1. She *takes after* her mother — same laugh, same temper.
2. He *takes after* his grandfather more than anyone else in the family.
3. Both boys *take after* their dad.
4. I don't *take after* either of my parents.
5. If she *takes after* him, she'll be tall.
6. People say the youngest one *took after* his uncle.

**See also:** [take to](#take-to), [take on](#take-on)

### take apart

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk əˈpɑrt/ &middot; *take-uh-PART* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** разбирать (на части); разгромить; разнести в пух и прах
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take apart the engine* or *take the engine apart*, and with a pronoun the object **must** split the phrase: *take it apart*, never ✗ *take apart it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all three senses: dismantling (*he's taking the carburetor apart*), defeating an opponent, and pulling an argument to pieces.
**Passive:** **yes** in every sense — *the carburetor was taken apart on the kitchen table*; *we were taken apart, 42 to 3*; *the whole third chapter was taken apart*.

Separate something into its pieces — and, figuratively, do the same to an opponent or an argument.

**1. Dismantle.** Undo something into the parts it was built from. The opposite is *put back together*, not *take together*. (разбирать на части)

1. He *took* the carburetor *apart* on the kitchen table.
2. She can *take apart* a laptop in about four minutes.
3. Don't *take* it *apart* unless you know how it goes back together.

**2. Defeat completely.** Of a team or a fighter: beat the other side so thoroughly that they never get going. (разгромить, разнести)

4. Our defense *took* them *apart* in the second half.
5. The visiting team *took* us *apart*, 42 to 3.

**3. Pull an argument to pieces.** Analyze something hostile line by line until nothing is left standing. (раскритиковать, разобрать по косточкам)

6. The reviewer *took* the argument *apart* point by point.
7. My advisor *took apart* the whole third chapter.

**See also:** [tear apart](#tear-apart), [take down](#take-down)

### take aside

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk əˈsaɪd/ &middot; *take-uh-SIDE* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** отвести в сторону (для разговора наедине)
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes, and in practice it is nearly always split: *took me aside*. A full noun can follow the particle (*took aside the two students who were talking*), but a pronoun must go inside: ✗ *took aside me*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: a single act of leading someone away for a private word. Progressive is fine — *he's taking her aside now*.
**Passive:** **yes** — *I was taken aside after practice*; *the two students who had been whispering were taken aside*.

Lead someone away from a group for a private word, usually a serious one.

1. The coach *took* me *aside* after practice.
2. She *took* him *aside* and told him to stop.
3. Let me *take* you *aside* for a second.
4. He *took aside* the two students who had been whispering.
5. My boss *took* me *aside* to warn me before the meeting.

**See also:** [tell off](#tell-off), [take on](#take-on)

### take away

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk əˈweɪ/ &middot; *take-uh-WAY* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** убирать, забирать; отнимать; вычитать
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take away his phone* or *take his phone away*, and a pronoun must split the phrase: *take it away*, never ✗ *take away it*. The three-part *take away from* is a different verb and never splits — nothing may come between *away* and *from*: *the noise takes away from the meal*, ✗ *the noise takes the meal away from*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the remove sense (*they're taking his license away*). The arithmetic sense is a frozen classroom formula that does not inflect — *six take away two is four*, ✗ *six is taking away two* — and *take away from* (lessen) is closer to **stative**.
**Passive:** **restricted** — full passive in the remove sense: *his license was taken away for six months*, *our plates were taken away*. None in the other senses: ✗ *two is taken away from six*, ✗ *the food is taken away from by the loud music*.

Remove something or someone; in arithmetic, subtract; and with *from*, reduce the effect of something.

**1. Remove, confiscate, carry off.** The everyday sense. The American noun for restaurant food eaten elsewhere is *takeout*; *takeaway* in that meaning is British. (забирать, уносить, отнимать)

1. They *took* his license *away* for six months.
2. The waiter *took* our plates *away* before we finished.
3. *Take* it *away* — I can't look at it anymore.

**2. Subtract.** Classroom arithmetic. Outside school an American says *minus* or *subtract*. (вычитать)

4. Six *take away* two is four.
5. If you *take away* the tax, it's about forty dollars.

**3. Take away from — lessen.** Three-part and inseparable: the thing diminished always follows *from*. (умалять, портить впечатление)

6. The loud music *takes away from* the food.
7. Nothing can *take away from* what she accomplished.

**4. Take away from — come out with.** What you leave an experience holding: a lesson, an impression. The noun *takeaway* comes from here. (вынести (урок, впечатление))

8. What I *took away from* the meeting was that nobody had a plan.
9. The main *takeaway* was that we need more time.

**See also:** [take back](#take-back), [take out](#take-out), [throw away](#throw-away)

### take back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈbæk/ &middot; *take-BAK* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** брать назад (слова); возвращать (покупку); принять обратно; переносить в прошлое
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take back the sweater* or *take the sweater back*, and a pronoun must split: *take it back*, never ✗ *take back it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic throughout: retracting, returning a purchase, readmitting a person, and (of a song or a smell) sending someone back in memory.
**Passive:** **restricted** — senses 1–3 passivize freely: *the sweater was taken back*, *not one word of it was taken back*, *she was taken back two years later*. The memory sense resists it: ✗ *I am taken back to high school by that song*.

Retract, return, readmit, or transport someone mentally to an earlier time.

**1. Retract what you said.** Withdraw a statement, usually an unfair one. (брать слова обратно)

1. I *take* it *back* — you were right.
2. He refused to *take back* a single word of it.

**2. Return a purchase.** Bring something back to the store. (возвращать в магазин)

3. She *took* the sweater *back* because it didn't fit.
4. You can *take* it *back* within thirty days with the receipt.

**3. Accept someone back.** Let a partner or an employee return after a break. (принять обратно)

5. After everything, she still *took* him *back*.
6. The paper *took* her *back* two years later.

**4. Bring the past vividly to mind.** A smell or a song does this to you. (переносить в прошлое, навевать воспоминания)

7. That song *takes* me *back* to high school.
8. The smell of chalk *took* her *back* thirty years.

**See also:** [take away](#take-away), [think back](#think-back), [win back](#win-back)

### take down

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈdaʊn/ &middot; *take-DOWN* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** записывать; снимать, разбирать; свалить, одолеть
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take down the address* or *take the address down*, and a pronoun must split: *take it down*, never ✗ *take down it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all four senses: writing down, dismantling, defeating or arresting, and the idiomatic *take down a peg*.
**Passive:** **yes** — *my name and address were taken down*; *the tent was taken down in the rain*; *he was taken down in the parking lot*; *he needs to be taken down a peg*.

Write something down, remove something from where it stands, or bring down a person or an organization.

**1. Write down what is said.** Record details as you hear them. (записывать)

1. The officer *took down* my name and address.
2. Let me *take* that *down* before I forget it.

**2. Remove or dismantle.** Get something off a wall, or take a structure apart. (снимать, разбирать)

3. They *took* the tent *down* in the rain.
4. He *took* the posters *down* the day after the election.

**3. Defeat, arrest, or destroy.** Bring down someone powerful, or physically subdue a suspect. (свалить, задержать, покончить с)

5. Two agents *took* him *down* in the parking lot.
6. It took one whistleblower to *take* the company *down*.

**4. Take down a peg.** Deflate someone's self-importance. Also *take down a notch*. (сбить спесь)

7. Somebody needs to *take* him *down* a peg.
8. That loss *took* them *down* a notch.

**See also:** [take apart](#take-apart), [tear down](#tear-down), [write down](#write-down)

### take for

**Pronunciation:** /ˈteɪk fɔr/ &middot; *TAKE-for* &middot; the verb keeps the stress; *for* is weak, usually /fɚ/
**Русский:** принимать за (кого-то другого); считать (кем-то)
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition), with the object fixed in the middle
**Separable:** yes, and obligatorily so — the object sits between *take* and *for*, and there is no joined order at all: *I took him for a security guard*, ✗ *I took for him a security guard*. A pronoun goes in the same slot and nowhere else: *take it for granted*, never ✗ *take for granted it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — a judgment or a mistaken belief about identity, not an act, so the progressive is out: ✗ *I am taking him for a security guard*. The fixed *take for granted* is the same.
**Passive:** **yes**, unusually for a stative verb — *he was taken for a security guard because of the jacket*; *it was taken for granted that the room would be free*; *she doesn't want to be taken for granted*.

Wrongly believe someone or something to be something else — and, in the fixed phrase *take for granted*, to stop noticing what you have.

**1. Mistake one thing for another.** Usually about identity or role. (принимать за)

1. I *took* him *for* a security guard because of the jacket.
2. Everyone *takes* her *for* the boss.

**2. What do you take me for?.** A challenge to someone's low opinion of you. (за кого ты меня принимаешь?)

3. Do you *take* me *for* an idiot?
4. What do you *take* us *for*?

**3. Take for granted.** Assume something without checking, or stop appreciating someone. (принимать как должное)

5. He *takes* his wife *for granted*.
6. We *took* it *for granted* that the room would be free.

**See also:** [take in](#take-in), [tell apart](#tell-apart)

### take in

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈɪn/ &middot; *take-IN* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** понимать, усваивать; приютить; обмануть; ушить (одежду); сходить (на фильм)
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — both orders work with a full noun, *take in a movie* or *take a movie in*, *take in the waist* or *take the waist in*, and a pronoun must split the phrase: *take it in*, never ✗ *take in it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all five senses: absorbing information (*she sat taking it all in*), sheltering, deceiving, narrowing a garment, and going to see a show.
**Passive:** **yes** — *two foster kids were taken in last spring*; *the waist was taken in two inches*. The deceive sense lives mostly in the passive: *don't be taken in by the discount*.

Absorb, shelter, deceive, narrow, or go and see — one of the busiest phrasal verbs in the language.

**1. Absorb and understand.** Register information, or simply look at something at length. (усваивать, воспринимать)

1. There was too much on the slide to *take* it all *in*.
2. She sat quietly, *taking in* everything that was said.

**2. Give shelter to.** Let someone live with you. (приютить, взять к себе)

3. They *took in* two foster kids last spring.
4. A neighbor *took* us *in* after the fire.

**3. Deceive.** Usually passive: *be taken in by*. (обмануть, провести)

5. Don't be *taken in* by the discount — it isn't one.
6. He *took* a lot of smart people *in*.

**4. Make a garment narrower.** The tailor's sense; the opposite is *let out*. (ушить)

7. The tailor *took* the waist *in* two inches.

**5. Go and see something.** A movie, a game, a show. (сходить на, посмотреть)

8. We *took in* a movie after dinner.
9. The tour *takes in* four cities in six days.

**See also:** [take on](#take-on), [take up](#take-up), [take for](#take-for)

### take off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈɔf/ &middot; *take-AWF* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** снимать (одежду); взлетать; сорваться и уйти; пойти в гору; взять отгул
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes in the transitive senses — *take off your coat* or *take your coat off*, and a pronoun must split: *take it off*, never ✗ *take off it*. In the intransitive senses (a plane, a business) there is no object to place.
**Transitive:** either — transitive when you remove something or deduct time, intransitive when a plane or a business takes off
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic everywhere: removing something worn, leaving the ground, bolting, suddenly succeeding, and deducting time or money.
**Passive:** **restricted** — the transitive senses passivize: *shoes must be taken off at the door*, *ten dollars was taken off the bill*. The intransitive senses have no passive at all: ✗ *it was taken off by the plane*, ✗ *the business was taken off*.

Remove something worn, leave the ground or the scene, suddenly succeed, or subtract time or money.

**1. Remove something worn or attached.** Clothes, glasses, a lid, a label. (снимать)

1. *Take* your shoes *off* at the door.
2. She *took off* her coat and hung it by the door.
3. It's hot — *take* it *off*.

**2. Leave the ground.** Of aircraft; the noun is *takeoff*. (взлетать)

4. The plane *took off* forty minutes late.

**3. Leave suddenly.** Informal: go, fast, often without explaining. (сорваться, умчаться)

5. He *took off* down the alley before anyone could stop him.

**4. Suddenly succeed.** Of a product, a career, an idea. (пойти в гору, набрать популярность)

6. The business really *took off* after the article ran.

**5. Take time off; deduct.** Not work for a stretch, or knock money off a price. (взять отгул; скинуть, вычесть)

7. I'm *taking* Friday *off*.
8. She *took off* three days to move.
9. The manager *took* ten dollars *off* the bill.

**6. Imitate mockingly.** British. Americans say *do an impression of*. (пародировать)

10. In Britain they'd say she *takes* the boss *off* perfectly; in the US we'd say she does a great impression of him.

**See also:** [take on](#take-on), [take out](#take-out), [throw on](#throw-on)

### take on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈɑn/ &middot; *take-AWN* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** браться за, взваливать на себя; нанимать; приобретать (черты); сразиться с
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take on more work* or *take more work on*, and a pronoun must split: *take it on*, never ✗ *take on it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the accept-work, hire, and fight senses. Sense 3 (*his voice took on an edge*) is an inchoative change of state — still dynamic, but with an inanimate, non-agentive subject.
**Passive:** **restricted** — the agentive senses passivize: *three new clients were taken on*, *he was taken on as an apprentice*, *the state was taken on and beaten*. Sense 3 does not: ✗ *an edge was taken on by his voice*.

Accept work, hire someone, acquire a quality, or square up to an opponent bigger than you.

**1. Accept work or responsibility.** Agree to carry something. (браться за, брать на себя)

1. She *took on* three new clients this month.
2. Don't *take* any more *on* — you're already buried.

**2. Hire.** Add someone to a payroll. (нанимать)

3. The warehouse is *taking on* seasonal workers.
4. They *took* him *on* as an apprentice.

**3. Acquire a quality.** Of a voice, a story, a color: come to have it. (приобретать (черты, оттенок))

5. His voice *took on* an edge I hadn't heard before.
6. The whole story *takes on* a different meaning once you know that.

**4. Fight or compete with.** Especially against a stronger side. (сразиться с, бросить вызов)

7. A small firm *took on* the state and won.
8. Nobody wanted to *take* them *on* in court.

**See also:** [take up](#take-up), [take over](#take-over), [thrust upon](#thrust-upon)

### take out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈaʊt/ &middot; *take-OWT* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** вынимать; пригласить куда-то; оформить (кредит, страховку); устранить
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *take out the trash* or *take the trash out*, and a pronoun must split: *take it out*, never ✗ *take out it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all four verbal senses: removing, escorting someone out, obtaining something official, and destroying a target. (*Takeout*, the food, is a noun.)
**Passive:** **yes** in every sense — *the trash was taken out before dinner*; *I was taken out for my birthday*; *a loan was taken out against the house*; *the bridge was taken out by one missile*.

Remove something, escort someone out for an evening, obtain something official, or destroy a target.

**1. Remove from a place.** The literal core. (вынимать, доставать)

1. He *took* the trash *out* before dinner.
2. *Take* the batteries *out* if you're storing it.

**2. Escort someone out and pay.** Dinner, a movie, a birthday. (пригласить, сводить куда-то)

3. She *took* me *out* for my birthday.
4. He's *taking* her *out* on Friday.

**3. Obtain something official.** A loan, an insurance policy, an ad, a permit. (оформить, взять (кредит), поместить (объявление))

5. They *took out* a loan against the house.
6. We *took out* a full-page ad in the paper.

**4. Destroy or disable.** Informal and military: kill, knock out, put out of action. (устранить, вывести из строя)

7. One missile *took out* the bridge.
8. The flu *took* half the office *out* for a week.

**5. Food to eat elsewhere.** The American noun is *takeout*; Britain says *takeaway*. (еда навынос)

9. We ordered Chinese *takeout* and ate it on the floor.

**See also:** [take out on](#take-out-on), [take in](#take-in), [throw out](#throw-out)

### take out on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌteɪk ˈaʊt ɑn/ &middot; *take-OWT-on* &middot; the first particle carries the stress
**Русский:** срывать (злость, раздражение) на ком-то
**Base verb:** [take](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#take) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** yes, and with a pronoun obligatorily so — the feeling sits between *take* and *out* and the person follows *on*: *take your anger out on me*, *take it out on me*. A pronoun can go nowhere else: ✗ *take out it on me*. With a full noun the unsplit order is heard too: *take out your frustration on the kids*. Nothing may ever come between *out* and *on*: ✗ *take it out today on me*.
**Transitive:** yes — two objects, one before *out* and one after *on*
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: an act of venting, and progressive is normal — *you're taking it out on the kids again*.
**Passive:** **restricted** — the feeling can be promoted: *his frustration was taken out on whoever was closest*. The person after *on* cannot: ✗ *the kids were taken it out on*, ✗ *I was taken out on*.

Vent anger or frustration on someone who did nothing to cause it.

1. Don't *take* it *out on* the kids.
2. She *took* her bad day *out on* everybody in the office.
3. I'm sorry — I *took* my frustration *out on* you.
4. He *takes* everything *out on* whoever happens to be closest.
5. Whatever happened at work, don't *take* it *out on* me.

**See also:** [take out](#take-out), [tell off](#tell-off), [tear into](#tear-into)

### take over

**Separable:** no — the object always follows the whole phrase, and it is nearly always a reflexive pronoun or a word like *silence*: *withdrew into himself*. ✗ *withdrew himself into*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** formal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic and inchoative: entering a retreat from other people, not occupying one. *He was withdrawing into himself* is fine.
**Passive:** **no** — the object after *into* is reflexive or abstract and will not promote: ✗ *himself was withdrawn into*, ✗ *a silence was withdrawn into by her*.

Retreat from contact with other people, usually after a shock or a loss.

1. After the funeral he *withdrew into* himself for months.
2. She *withdrew into* a silence nobody could reach.
3. The boy *withdrew into* his own world at school.
4. He *withdraws into* work whenever things get hard.
5. Rather than argue, she *withdrew into* politeness.

### write back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈbæk/ &middot; *rite-BAK* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** написать в ответ, ответить (письмом)
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes in American English, and the person always goes inside, noun or pronoun alike: *write me back*, *he wrote her back*, *she wrote her cousin back*. ✗ *write back me*, ✗ *she wrote back her cousin*. With *to* the phrase stays whole: *write back to the office*.
**Transitive:** either — often with no object at all, or with the person written to inside the phrase
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic: sending a reply, with or without naming the person replied to.
**Passive:** **no** — the person sits inside the phrase but resists promotion: ✗ *I was written back*, ✗ *her cousin was written back the same night*. Say *she got a reply* instead.

Send a reply to a letter, an email, or a message.

1. She never *wrote back*.
2. *Write* me *back* when you get a chance.
3. He *wrote* her *back* the same night.
4. I *wrote back to* the office twice and got nothing.
5. If they *write back*, forward it to me.

### write down

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈdaʊn/ &middot; *rite-DOWN* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** записать; уценить, списать часть стоимости
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *write down the address* or *write the address down*, and a pronoun must split: *write it down*, never ✗ *write down it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic in both the everyday sense and the accounting sense.
**Passive:** **yes** — *the address was written down on the back of a receipt*, *the loan was written down by forty percent*.

Record something in writing — or, in accounting, cut the recorded value of an asset.

**1. Record in writing.** The everyday sense. (записать)

1. *Write* it *down* before you forget.
2. He *wrote down* the address on the back of a receipt.
3. She *wrote* every number *down* twice.
4. Nobody *wrote* anything *down*, and now nobody remembers.

**2. Reduce a recorded value.** Accounting; the noun is a *write-down*. The opposite is *write up*. (уценить, списать часть стоимости)

5. The bank *wrote down* the loan by forty percent.

### write in

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈɪn/ &middot; *rite-IN* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** вписать (кандидата, пункт); написать в редакцию
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes when there is an object — *write in her name* or *write her name in*, and a pronoun must split: *write it in*, never ✗ *write in it*. When it means writing to an organization there is no object: *listeners wrote in*.
**Transitive:** either — transitive when you insert something, intransitive when you write to an organization
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic; sense 1 (inserting a name or a clause) is transitive, sense 2 (writing to a paper or a station) intransitive.
**Passive:** **restricted** — yes for sense 1: *her name was written in on hundreds of ballots*, *the clause was written in by hand*. Sense 2 has no object: ✗ *the paper was written in to by listeners*.

Insert something into a document — in American elections, to vote for a candidate not printed on the ballot — or send a letter to a paper or a station.

**1. Insert into a document.** Add a name or a clause by hand. The noun *write-in* is an American election term. (вписать)

1. Hundreds of people *wrote in* her name.
2. He *wrote* his own name *in* as a protest.
3. *Write* it *in* under "other."

**2. Send a letter to an organization.** To a newspaper, a station, a company. (написать (в редакцию, на телеканал))

4. Listeners *wrote in* by the thousands.
5. If you disagree, *write in* to the paper.

**See also:** [write down](#write-down), [write out](#write-out), [weave in](#weave-in)

### write off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈɔf/ &middot; *rite-AWF* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** списать (долг, убыток); поставить крест на; (брит.) разбить машину
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *write off the debt* or *write the debt off*, and a pronoun must split: *write him off*, never ✗ *write off him*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic in all three senses: canceling a debt, giving up on someone, and (British) wrecking a car.
**Passive:** **yes** — *the balance was written off*, *he was written off too early*, *the album was written off when it came out*.

Cancel a debt, record a loss, or decide that someone or something is beyond hope.

**1. Cancel a debt or record a loss.** Accounting and tax; the noun is a *write-off*. (списать)

1. The bank *wrote off* the balance.
2. We *wrote* the whole quarter *off* as a loss.

**2. Dismiss as hopeless.** Give up on a person, a team, a project. (поставить крест на, махнуть рукой)

3. Don't *write* him *off* yet — he's still got a shot.
4. Critics *wrote* the album *off* when it came out.

**3. Wreck a vehicle.** British. Americans say *total*: *he totaled the car*. (разбить в хлам)

5. In Britain he *wrote off* his car on the motorway; in the US he'd have *totaled* it.

**See also:** [write down](#write-down), [throw away](#throw-away), [take off](#take-off)

### write out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈaʊt/ &middot; *rite-OWT* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** написать полностью, прописью; выписать (чек, рецепт); вывести (персонажа)
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *write out the number* or *write the number out*, and a pronoun must split: *write it out*, never ✗ *write out it*. In *write someone out of something* the person always sits inside: *wrote her out of the show*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic: setting something down in full, producing a document, or removing a character or an heir.
**Passive:** **yes** — *a prescription was written out*, *the number should be written out in words*, *she was written out of the show after season three*.

Set something down in full, produce a written document, or remove a character or an heir from a story.

**1. Write in full.** Spell out what is usually abbreviated or spoken. (написать полностью, прописью)

1. *Write* the number *out* in words on the second line.
2. She *wrote out* the whole speech by hand.

**2. Produce a written document.** A check, a prescription, a citation. (выписать)

3. The doctor *wrote out* a prescription.
4. He *wrote* a check *out* for the full amount.

**3. Write someone out of.** Remove a character from a script, or a person from a will. (вывести из сюжета, вычеркнуть из завещания)

5. They *wrote* her *out of* the show after season three.
6. His father *wrote* him *out of* the will.

**See also:** [write down](#write-down), [write up](#write-up), [write in](#write-in)

### write up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌraɪt ˈʌp/ &middot; *rite-UP* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** подробно описать, составить отчёт; сделать письменный выговор, выписать штраф
**Base verb:** [write](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#write) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *write up the results* or *write the results up*, and a pronoun must split: *write it up*, never ✗ *write up it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic in all three senses: turning notes into an account, filing a disciplinary report, and revaluing an asset upward.
**Passive:** **yes** — *the results were written up over the weekend*, *he was written up for being late three times*, *the asset was written up after the appraisal*.

Turn notes into a full written account — or, in American usage, file a formal report against someone.

**1. Produce a full written account.** The noun is a *write-up*. (составить отчёт, подробно описать)

1. I'll *write* the results *up* over the weekend.
2. She *wrote up* her notes into a full report.
3. *Write* it *up* and send it to legal.

**2. Formally report someone.** American: a disciplinary note at work, or a traffic ticket. (сделать письменный выговор, выписать штраф)

4. The manager *wrote* him *up* for being late three times.
5. A trooper *wrote* me *up* for doing 78.

**3. Increase a recorded value.** Accounting; the opposite of *write down*. (переоценить в сторону повышения)

6. The auditors *wrote* the asset *up* after the appraisal.

**See also:** [write down](#write-down), [write out](#write-out), [take down](#take-down)
