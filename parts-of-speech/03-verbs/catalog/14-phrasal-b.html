Phrasal verbs: be — buy#

Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs be, bear, beat… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

← Irregular verbs · The grammar of verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle whose meaning you cannot add up from the parts: break down has nothing to do with breaking, bring up a subject involves no upward motion, and be up to something is not about height. The other half of learning one is separability — whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle (break the numbers down) or has to follow the whole phrase (bear with me), because a separable verb with a pronoun object must split: blow it off, never ✗ blow off it. Every entry below is built on one of the irregular verbs from be to buy, and in speech the stress lands on the particle — bring it UP, not BRING it up — which is what fuses the two words into a single piece of vocabulary.

be after#

Pronunciation
/ˌbi ˈæftər/ · bee-AF-ter · the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
Русский
добиваться, охотиться за, преследовать
Base verb
be
Type
prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
Separable
no — the object always follows the preposition. ✗ be my job after, ✗ be it after.
Transitive
yes, always takes an object
Register
informal
Verb type
stative — copular be plus a preposition, naming a state of pursuit, not an act. It rejects the progressive: ✗ she is being after my job.
Passive
no — be has no passive of its own, so the prepositional passive is impossible: ✗ my job is been after by her, ✗ he was been after by the marshals.

To be trying to get hold of something, or to be chasing somebody down.

1. Be trying to get something. Used of a thing somebody wants — a job, money, a piece of information. Be stays simple here; the continuous ✗ is being after is impossible. (добиваться, охотиться за)

  1. I'm fairly sure she is after my job.
  2. He was after a raise, not a compliment.
  3. Whatever they were after, they didn't find it in the safe.

2. Be pursuing a person. Of police, creditors, or anyone chasing someone down. The person hunted comes straight after the preposition. (преследовать, разыскивать)

  1. The marshals have been after him for two years.
  2. The IRS is after my neighbor about a return he never filed.

be along#

Pronunciation
/ˌbi əˈlɔŋ/ · bee-uh-LONG · the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
Русский
подойти, подъехать, скоро прибыть
Base verb
be
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
not applicable — be along takes no object, so nothing can sit between the two words. ✗ be the doctor along.
Transitive
no, never takes an object
Register
everyday
Verb type
stative in form — the copula plus a particle, so no progressive (✗ the doctor is being along) — though what it reports is an arrival, which is why it lives in the future: will be along, should be along.
Passive
no — there is no object, and be cannot be passivized in any case: ✗ she was been along.

To arrive — said of a person or a vehicle that is expected shortly. It lives almost entirely in the future: will be along, should be along.

  1. The doctor will be along in a minute.
  2. Wait by the curb — your ride will be along soon.
  3. She said she would be along as soon as the meeting broke up.
  4. Another train will be along in twelve minutes.
  5. Someone from maintenance should be along before noon.

be around#

Pronunciation
/ˌbi əˈraʊnd/ · bee-uh-ROWND · the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
Русский
быть поблизости; существовать давно; быть тёртым калачом
Base verb
be
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ be him around.
Transitive
no, never takes an object
Register
everyday
Verb type
stative in all three senses — presence (I'll be around), long existence (that diner has been around since the fifties), and worldliness (he has been around). None of them takes the progressive: ✗ he is being around.
Passive
no — objectless and built on be: ✗ the fifties have been been around since.

To be present and available, to have existed for a long time, or — of a person — to have seen enough of life to be hard to fool.

1. Be present, be available. The commonest sense, usually about a stretch of time rather than a moment. (быть поблизости, быть рядом)

  1. Call if you need help — I will be around all afternoon.
  2. Nobody was around to sign for the package.

2. Have existed for a long time. Of institutions, products, and ideas. Almost always in the perfect. (существовать давно, быть известным)

  1. That diner has been around since the fifties.
  2. The idea has been around longer than any of us.

3. Be experienced, be worldly. Informal, and of a person. Said of a woman it can be a slur about her sex life, so Americans use it carefully. (быть тёртым калачом, повидать жизнь)

  1. He has been around — you won't sell him a story like that.
  2. She has been around long enough to spot a bad contract.

be
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ be the server down.
Transitive
no, never takes an object
Register
everyday
Verb type
stative — a condition, whether it is mood (she has been down), a dead server (the network is down), or a number (sales are down nine percent). ✗ the network is being down.
Passive
no — no object anywhere in the entry, so nothing can be promoted: ✗ the network was been down.

To be depressed, to be out of service, or to be lower or behind in a count.

1. Be depressed, be in low spirits. Informal, and about mood rather than illness. (быть в подавленном настроении, хандрить)

  1. She has been down since the layoffs.
  2. I was down for a week after the audition.

2. Be out of service. Of a network, a server, or a machine — the standard word in American offices. (не работать, лежать — о системе)

  1. The whole network is down again.
  2. Payroll was down for six hours yesterday.

3. Be lower, be behind. Of prices, sales, or a score. The amount follows: down nine percent, down fourteen. (снизиться; проигрывать, отставать)

  1. We were down fourteen at the half and still won.
  2. Sales are down nine percent from last quarter.

To be present at home or at work, to be in fashion, to be taking part, or — of results and arrivals — to have come in.

1. Be present, be at home or at the office. Of a person others are looking for. (быть на месте, быть дома)

  1. Is Dr. Alvarez in today?
  2. Nobody was in when the inspector came by.

2. Be in fashion. The opposite of be out. (быть в моде)

  1. Wide lapels are in again.
  2. Flip phones were in long before anyone called them retro.

3. Be taking part, be included. Usually the short answer I'm in. (участвовать, быть в деле)

  1. If you're putting a team together, I'm in.
  2. He was in until he saw the budget.

4. Have arrived, have come in. Of flights, harvests, votes, and lab results. (прибыть; поступить — о результатах)

  1. The flight is in early.
  2. The lab results are in, and they're clean.

be off#

Pronunciation
/ˌbi ˈɔf/ · bee-OFF · the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
Русский
уходить; отменяться; быть выключенным; быть неточным; не работать (о выходном)
Base verb
be
Type
phrasal verb (verb + particle)
Separable
not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ be the wedding off as a statement.
Transitive
no, never takes an object
Register
everyday
Verb type
stative in senses 2 to 6 — canceled (the wedding is off), switched off, inaccurate, on leave, spoiled. Sense 1 (I'm off, he was off before dawn) reports a departure but still takes plain be: ✗ I am being off.
Passive
no — no object in any sense, and no passive of be: ✗ the wedding was been off.

To leave, to be canceled, to be switched off, to be inaccurate, or to be away from work.

1. Leave, set out. Announcing your own departure or somebody else's. (уходить, отправляться)

  1. We are off to the airport at six.
  2. They were off before sunrise.

2. Be canceled. Of an event or a deal that had been arranged. The opposite is be on. (отменяться, расстроиться)

  1. The wedding is off.
  2. The deal was off by the time the lawyers called back.

3. Be switched off or disconnected. Of lights, engines, and utilities. (быть выключенным, отключённым)

  1. Make sure the burners are off.
  2. The power was off all night.

4. Be wrong, be inaccurate. Of figures, and of anything that feels not quite right. (быть неточным, не сходиться)

  1. Your totals are off by about two hundred dollars.
  2. Something about the story was off from the start.

5. Be away from work. Of a day, a week, or a leave. (быть свободным, не работать)

  1. I am off on Tuesday.
  2. She has been off since the baby came.

6. Be spoiled — British. Of food gone bad. An American says the milk has gone bad, not ✗ the milk is off. (испортиться — о продуктах)

  1. In London they'll tell you the milk is off; in Ohio it has gone bad.

To be going ahead as planned, to be switched on, to be performing or broadcasting, or to be taking a drug.

1. Be happening, be going ahead. The opposite of be off. (идти, состояться)

  1. The game is on despite the rain.

2. Be switched on. Of lights, appliances, and engines. (быть включённым)

  1. The porch light was on all night.
  2. Leave the fan on.

3. Be performing or broadcasting. Of a performer about to go up, or of a program showing. (выступать; идти — о передаче)

  1. You are on in five minutes.
  2. The news was on when I walked in.

4. Be taking a medication or a drug. The substance follows directly. (принимать, сидеть на)

  1. She is on antibiotics for another week.
  2. He has been on the same blood-pressure pill for years.

  1. Dinner is on me tonight.
  2. Fifty bucks says he misses. — You are on.
