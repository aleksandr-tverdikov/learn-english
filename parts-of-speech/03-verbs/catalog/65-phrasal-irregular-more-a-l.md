# More phrasal verbs on irregular bases: A-L

> Phrasal verbs on irregular bases that groups 14-22 missed — headwords A-L.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 14-22 cover the phrasal verbs on irregular bases that came from the original source material. Checking the catalog against a full WordNet verb list turned up a further set it had never included — *fall through*, *hang about*, *stand over*, *read up* — and these are those.

Each entry names its **base verb** and says whether it is **separable**. Because the base is irregular, the past and the participle are worth reading twice: *read up* → *read up* → *read up* looks unchanged but is not pronounced that way, and *sew up* → *sewed up* → *sewn up* changes only in the participle.

### bid up

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɪd ˌʌp/ &middot; *BID up*
**Русский:** взвинчивать цену, взвинтить цену; набавлять ставку, набавить ставку
**Base verb:** *bid*
**Separable:** separable — *bid up the price*, and with a pronoun it must split, *bid it up*
**Forms:** *bid up* &middot; *bid up* &middot; *bid up*
**Third person:** *bids up* &middot; **-ing form:** *bidding up*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To push the price of something higher by making one offer after another for it.

1. Collectors are *bidding up* anything that comes out of that studio.
2. Two rival funds *bid* the stock *up* forty percent in a single week.
3. Rumors of a shortage have *bid up* the price of copper.

### blow into

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbloʊ ˌɪntuː/ &middot; *BLOH in-too*
**Русский:** нагрянуть, заявиться; внезапно приезжать, внезапно приехать
**Base verb:** *blow*
**Separable:** inseparable — *blow into town*, never ✗ *blow town into*
**Forms:** *blow into* &middot; *blew into* &middot; *blown into*
**Third person:** *blows into* &middot; **-ing form:** *blowing into*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To arrive somewhere suddenly and without warning.

1. He *blows into* town twice a year and expects everyone to drop everything.
2. She *blew into* the office an hour late, still holding her coat.
3. A cousin I had never met had *blown into* town the night before.

### bring over

**Русский:** приносить, принести; привозить, привезти; приводить, привести (к кому-л.)
**Base verb:** *bring*

**Forms:** *bring over* &middot; *brought over* &middot; *brought over*
**Third person:** *brings over* &middot; **-ing form:** *bringing over*

To carry or take someone or something to the place where the other person is.

3. They have *brought over* half their furniture already.

### climb down

**Pronunciation:** /ˈklaɪm ˌdaʊn/ &middot; *KLYM down*
**Русский:** отступать, отступить (от требований); идти на попятную, пойти на попятную
**Base verb:** *climb*
**Separable:** no object — what is given up arrives through *from*: *climb down from that demand*
**Forms:** *climb down* &middot; *climbed down* &middot; *climbed down*
**Third person:** *climbs down* &middot; **-ing form:** *climbing down*
**Transitivity:** intransitive

To admit you were wrong and give up a demand or a claim you had insisted on.

1. Neither side wants to *climb down* first.
2. The company *climbed down* and paid the refunds.
3. They have *climbed down* from every threat they made.

### draw off

### fall away

**Pronunciation:** /ˈfɔːl əˌweɪ/ &middot; *FAWL uh-way*
**Русский:** круто спускаться, круто уходить вниз (о местности)
**Base verb:** *fall*
**Separable:** no object — *the hillside falls away*, never ✗ *fall the hillside away*
**Forms:** *fall away* &middot; *fell away* &middot; *fallen away*
**Third person:** *falls away* &middot; **-ing form:** *falling away*
**Transitivity:** intransitive

Of ground, to slope sharply downward from where you are standing.

1. Behind the cabin the hillside *falls away* into a ravine.
2. The path *fell away* so steeply that we went down on all fours.
3. We stood where the ground had *fallen away* to a beach two hundred feet below.
