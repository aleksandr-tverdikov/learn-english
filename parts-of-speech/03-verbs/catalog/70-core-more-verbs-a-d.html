More verbs A-D#

Further verbs A-D — what a coverage check against two large verb lists showed was still missing.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Gathered by checking the catalog against roughly 21,000 scraped verb lemmas and writing entries for what was genuinely absent.

Most of that list was not worth writing — archaic verbs no living American uses, British-only spellings, narrow technical coinages, and a fair number of words that were not verbs at all — so these are the survivors of a deliberate rejection pass that threw out roughly nine of every ten candidates.

abdicate#

Pronunciation
/ˈæbdɪkeɪt/ · AB-di-kayt
Русский
отрекаться от престола, отречься; слагать с себя (обязанности)
Forms
abdicate · abdicated · abdicated
Third person
abdicates · -ing form: abdicating
Verb class
regular
Transitivity
both — transitive (he abdicated the throne), intransitive (the king abdicated in 1936)

To give up a throne, a position of power, or a responsibility that belongs to you.

  1. A monarch may abdicate in favor of a younger heir.
  2. Edward VIII abdicated in order to marry an American.
  3. Critics say the agency has abdicated its duty to protect consumers.

Contrast

Contrast
