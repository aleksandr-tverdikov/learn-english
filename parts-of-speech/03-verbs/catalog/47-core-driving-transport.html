Core verbs: driving and transport#

The everyday regular verbs of driving and transport — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

honk#

Pronunciation
/hɑːŋk/ · HAHNK
Русский
сигналить, бибикать, посигналить
Forms
honk · honked · honked
Verb class
regular
Third person
honks · -ing form: honking
Transitivity
both — transitive (honk the horn), intransitive (the taxi honked at me)

To sound a vehicle horn.

  1. Do not honk in a hospital zone.
  2. The cab behind us honked twice.
  3. Someone has been honking at that stalled van for a minute.

idle#

Pronunciation
/ˈaɪdəl/ · EYE-duhl
Русский
работать на холостом ходу; держать двигатель включённым
Forms
idle · idled · idled
Verb class
regular
Third person
idles · -ing form: idling
Transitivity
both — transitive (do not idle your engine), intransitive (the bus idled at the curb)
Contrast
Sounds like idol; the silent -e drops before -ing: idling.

To run an engine while the vehicle is not moving.

  1. Diesel engines idle louder than gas engines.
  2. The bus idled at the curb for ten minutes.
  3. A sign asks drivers to stop idling near the school.
