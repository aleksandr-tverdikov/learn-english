More verbs F-I#

Further verbs F-I — what a coverage check against two large verb lists showed was still missing.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Gathered by checking the catalog against roughly 21,000 scraped verb lemmas and writing entries for what was genuinely absent.

Most of that list was not worth writing — archaic verbs no living American uses, British-only spellings, narrow technical coinages, and a fair number of words that were not verbs at all — so these are the survivors of a deliberate rejection pass that threw out roughly nine of every ten candidates.

Pronunciation
/feɪs/ · FAYSS
Русский
сталкиваться с (проблемой); быть обращённым к
Forms

Third person
faces · -ing form: facing
Verb class
regular
Transitivity
both — transitive (face a decision), intransitive (the house faces south)
Contrast
Drop the -e before -ing: facing. Face a problem takes no preposition, but face UP TO a problem is a separate, phrasal meaning.

To deal with something difficult, or to be turned toward something.

  1. They face a tough decision this week.
  2. The company faced three lawsuits at once.
  3. Our apartment is facing the courtyard, so it stays quiet.
