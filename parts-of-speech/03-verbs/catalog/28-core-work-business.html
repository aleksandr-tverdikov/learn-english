Core verbs: work and business#

The everyday regular verbs of work and business — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary built entirely of irregular and phrasal verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

work#

Pronunciation
/wɜːrk/ · WURK
Русский
работать, поработать; работать, функционировать; действовать, подействовать, сработать; управлять (механизмом), уметь обращаться с, работать на (станке)
Forms
work · worked · worked
Verb class
regular
Third person
works · -ing form: working
Transitivity
both — transitive (work long hours), intransitive (she works downtown)
Contrast
Work FOR a company, work AT a place, work ON a project, work WITH people. The preposition changes the meaning, not the verb.

Work has 3 distinct senses.

1. To do a job, especially one you are paid for. (работать, поработать)

  1. I work for a small firm downtown.
  2. She worked late three nights in a row.
  3. He has been working here since 2019.

2. For a machine, a system, or a plan to do what it is supposed to do. (работать, функционировать; действовать, подействовать, сработать)

  1. The elevator doesn't work again.
  2. The medicine worked within an hour.
  3. Their plan has worked better than anyone expected.

3. To operate a machine or a piece of equipment. (управлять (механизмом), уметь обращаться с, работать на (станке))

  1. Can you work the projector?
  2. She worked the register while the manager was out.
  3. He has been working that crane for twenty years.
Phrasal verbs
work around · work in · work off · work on, work for, work with · work out · work out · work out · work out · work out to · work through · work up · work up · work up to

Employ has 2 distinct senses.

  1. The team employs several different testing methods.
  2. Doctors employed a new technique to close the wound.
  3. The film is employing the same trick for the third time.

Fire has 3 distinct senses.

1. To make someone leave their job, usually for poor work or bad behavior. (увольнять, уволить)

  1. No boss enjoys having to fire anyone.
  2. They fired him for missing three deadlines.
  3. Two people were fired after the audit.

2. To shoot a gun, or to launch a weapon. (стрелять, выстрелить; вести огонь; запускать (ракету))

  1. Don't fire until I give the signal.
  2. The officer fired two warning shots.
  3. Missiles were fired from a ship offshore.

3. To bake clay in a very hot oven so that it hardens. (обжигать, обжечь (керамику))

  1. We'll fire the kiln on Saturday.
  2. She fired her first bowl last weekend.
  3. These tiles are fired at 2,200 degrees.

Fail has 3 distinct senses.

1. To not succeed, or to not do something that you should have done. (терпеть неудачу, провалиться; не суметь, не справиться)

  1. Most new restaurants fail in the first year.
  2. The supplier failed to deliver on time.

2. To stop working the way it should. (отказывать, отказать; выходить из строя, переставать работать)

  1. The brakes failed halfway down the hill.
  2. If the power fails, the generator starts automatically.
  3. Doctors told him his kidneys were failing.

3. To judge a student's work as not good enough to pass. (ставить неудовлетворительную оценку; заваливать, завалить (студента))

  1. The professor failed half the class.
  2. They'll fail you if you skip the final.
  3. She was failed for copying someone else's answers.
