# More verbs E-J

> Further verbs E-J — what a coverage check against two large verb lists showed was still missing.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Gathered by checking the catalog against roughly 21,000 scraped verb lemmas and writing entries for what was genuinely absent.

Most of that list was not worth writing — archaic verbs no living American uses, British-only spellings, narrow technical coinages, and a fair number of words that were not verbs at all — so these are the survivors of a deliberate rejection pass that threw out roughly nine of every ten candidates.

---
### earmark

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪrmɑːrk/ &middot; *EER-mark*
**Русский:** предназначать, выделять (средства)
**Forms:** *earmark* &middot; *earmarked* &middot; *earmarked*
**Third person:** *earmarks* &middot; **-ing form:** *earmarking*
**Verb class:** regular
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Earmark takes for: earmarked for research.

To set money or resources aside for one specific purpose.

1. Congress may *earmark* funds for rural clinics.
2. The city *earmarked* two million dollars for repairs.
3. That land has been *earmarked* for housing.

### effect

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈfɛkt/ &middot; *ih-FEKT*
**Русский:** осуществлять, осуществить; производить (изменение)
**Forms:** *effect* &middot; *effected* &middot; *effected*
**Third person:** *effects* &middot; **-ing form:** *effecting*
**Verb class:** regular
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Do not confuse with affect. To affect something is to influence it; to effect something is to make it happen. Effect is usually the noun (эффект) and only rarely this verb.

To bring something about, especially a change.

1. Only Congress can *effect* a change this large.
2. The new director *effected* a complete turnaround.
3. Reforms have been *effected* at every level.
