Core verbs: law and crime#

The everyday regular verbs of law and crime — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

/ˈpiːnəlaɪz/ · PEE-nuh-lyz
Русский
штрафовать, наказывать
Forms
penalize · penalized · penalized
Third person
penalizes · -ing form: penalizing
Transitivity
transitive
Contrast
The first syllable is PEE-, though the noun penalty starts PEN-. American spelling uses -ize.

To punish someone, usually with a fine or a loss of rights.

  1. The IRS can penalize you for filing late.
  2. The court penalized the firm with a heavy fine.
  3. Drivers are penalized for every point on their record.
