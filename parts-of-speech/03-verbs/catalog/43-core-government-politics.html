Core verbs: government#

The everyday regular verbs of government — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

elect#

Pronunciation
/ɪˈlɛkt/ · i-LEKT
Русский
избирать, избрать; выбирать
Forms
elect · elected · elected
Verb class
regular
Third person
elects · -ing form: electing
Transitivity
transitive
Contrast
You are elected to an office and elected as something: elected to the council, elected as chairman.

To choose someone for a public office by voting.

  1. Americans elect a president every four years.
  2. She was elected to the state senate in 2022.
  3. The district has never elected a Democrat.

/ˈɡʌvərn/ · GUV-urn
Русский

Forms

Verb class
regular
Third person

Transitivity
both — transitive (govern a country), intransitive (it is harder to govern)
Contrast

To officially control and run a country, a state, or a city.

  1. It is easier to campaign than to govern.
  2. The party governed for twelve years.
  3. The island has been governed from the mainland since 1898.

Contrast
