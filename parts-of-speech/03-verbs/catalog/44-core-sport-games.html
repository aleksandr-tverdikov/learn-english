Core verbs: sport and games#

The everyday regular verbs of sport and games — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary built entirely of irregular and phrasal verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

Contend has 2 distinct senses.

1. To compete seriously for something such as a title or a prize. (бороться (за титул), соперничать)

  1. Three clubs contend for the title almost every season.
  2. They contended for the championship until the final week.
  3. He has been contending with a knee injury all year.

2. To claim firmly that something is true, especially in an argument or in court. (утверждать, заявлять)

  1. The defense contends that the evidence was planted.
  2. Critics contended that the law was unconstitutional.
  3. He has long contended that the test is unfair.
