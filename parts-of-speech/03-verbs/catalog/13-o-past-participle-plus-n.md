# Past has "o", participle = past + -n

> The past takes an /oʊ/ vowel and the participle is that past form plus -n.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These verbs bend the same way twice. The past swaps the base vowel for an /oʊ/ or /ɔr/ sound, and then the participle is simply that past form with **-n** or **-en** glued on: *speak* → *spoke* → *spoken*, *freeze* → *froze* → *frozen*, *wear* → *wore* → *worn* (во 2-й звук "о", 3-я = 2-я + "n"). Once you know the past form, the participle costs you one letter, which is why this is the friendliest of the irregular families. Two members — *hide* → *hid* → *hidden* and *bite* → *bit* → *bitten* — keep the "+ -n" half of the rule but shorten the vowel instead of rounding it, and two more — *delve* and *cleave* — preserve the pattern only in archaic forms that modern Americans have replaced with regular ones.

### awake

**Pronunciation:** /əˈweɪk/ &middot; *uh-WAKE*
**Русский:** будить; просыпаться
**Forms:** *awake* /əˈweɪk/ &middot; *awoke* /əˈwoʊk/ &middot; *awoken* /əˈwoʊkən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** neutral to formal — ordinary in writing; everyday speech says *wake up*
**Pattern:** a-e /eɪ/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *awakes* &middot; **-ing form:** *awaking* (the silent e drops)
**Contrast:** *Awake* follows [wake](#wake) exactly, and carries the same traps. It is also an adjective — *I was awake at four* is a state, not a past tense — and it has a regular cousin *awaken/awakened/awakened*, which is what most American writers reach for. The past cannot serve as the participle: ✗ *had awoke*, ✓ *had awoken*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, and literary beside everyday *wake up*; keep the verb apart from the adjective *awake* (*I was awake at four* is a state, not a past tense)
**Transitivity:** **both** — intransitive when you stop sleeping (*she awoke to the sound of rain*), transitive when you rouse someone or stir a feeling (*the sirens awoke half the neighborhood*); *awake to* takes a prepositional object
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *half the neighborhood was awoken by the sirens*, *an old homesickness has been awoken*. No passive of the intransitive *she awoke*.

To stop sleeping, or to rouse someone from sleep — a more literary word than *wake up* — and, figuratively, to stir something that was dormant.

**1. Stop sleeping.** Intransitive and a little literary; everyday American speech says *wake up*. Keep it apart from the adjective *awake* — *I was awake at four* is a state, not a past tense. (просыпаться, пробуждаться)

1. I *awake* before the alarm most mornings.
2. She *awoke* to the sound of rain on the roof.
3. The whole house *had awoken* by six.

**2. Wake someone up.** Transitive, and rarer than the intransitive sense; most American writers reach for *wake* or the regular *awaken* here. (будить, разбудить)

4. Try not to *awake* the children when you come in.
5. The sirens *awoke* half the neighborhood.
6. The dog *has awoken* me at dawn every day this week.

**3. Stir up a feeling or memory.** Figurative and transitive, common in writing: a sight, a smell, or a story brings something dormant back to life. (пробуждать (чувства, воспоминания))

7. Something in the story *awoke* an old memory.
8. Small kindnesses *awake* loyalty in people.
9. The photographs *have awoken* a homesickness she thought was gone.

**4. awake to — become aware of.** Followed by a noun: to finally see something you had been ignoring. The adjective version, *be awake to*, is at least as common. (осознать, понять)

10. The country slowly *awoke* to the scale of the problem.
11. We *awake* to our mistakes only after the fact.
12. Investors *have awoken* to the risk.

**See also:** [wake](#wake), [break](#break)

### bear

**Pronunciation:** /bɛr/ &middot; *BAIR*
**Русский:** родить, нести; переносить; выносить
**Forms:** *bear* /bɛr/ &middot; *bore* /bɔr/ &middot; *born* /bɔrn/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — *can't bear it* and *was born* are in every American's mouth
**Pattern:** ear /ɛr/ — ore /ɔr/ — orn &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *bears* &middot; **-ing form:** *bearing*
**Contrast:** English keeps two participles here and they are not interchangeable. *Born* belongs to birth, and only in the passive: *She was born in Ohio*. Every other sense — carrying, enduring, producing — takes *borne*: *He has borne the cost alone*, *The plan has borne fruit*. The base is also a homophone of the adjective *bare*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the carrying, enduring, and producing senses; closer to **stative** in *bear a name / bear a resemblance / bear interest*, where the progressive is odd: ✗ *the building is bearing her name*
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive almost everywhere (*bear the cost*, *bear three children*, *bear fruit*), intransitive in the direction sense (*bear left where the road forks*)
**Passive:** **restricted** — natural for costs and burdens (*the legal fees have been borne by the company*), and *be born* is passive-only (*she was born in Ohio*; there is no active ✗ *she borned*). But the name and resemblance senses resist it: ✗ *her grandfather's name is borne by the building*, and ✗ *left was borne at the fork*.

To carry or support something, to endure it, or to bring it forth — plus the passive *be born*, the one sense that takes *born* instead of *borne*.

**1. Endure, put up with.** Very often negative or with *can't*: *I can't bear it*. The participle is *borne*, never *born*. (терпеть, выносить)

1. I can't *bear* the sound of a dentist's drill.
2. She *bore* the delay without a word of complaint.
3. He *has borne* worse news than this.

**2. be born (passive only).** Passive only, and the only sense spelled *born*. There is no active ✗ *she borned*; birth in the active takes *bear/bore/borne*. (родиться)

4. Their twins were *born* in a snowstorm.
5. He was *born* the year the mill closed.
6. All three of her children *had been born* by the time she turned thirty.

**3. Give birth to, bring forth.** The active birth sense, formal or literary; ordinary speech says *have a baby*. Here the participle is *borne*: *She has borne three children*. (рожать, родить)

7. Mares usually *bear* one foal at a time.
8. She *bore* three children in five years.
9. The queen *had borne* no heir.

**4. Carry the weight of, support.** The structural sense — of beams, walls, ice, or a person carrying a burden or a cost. (нести (нагрузку), выдерживать вес)

10. These beams *bear* the weight of the whole roof.
11. The ice *bore* his weight for two steps and then cracked.
12. The company *has borne* the legal fees since March.

**5. Carry, bring (formal).** Formal or literary for physically carrying something from one place to another — *bearing gifts*, *bearing a message*. (нести, приносить)

13. The servers *bear* trays over their heads.
14. The messengers *bore* the news from the capital.
15. Pilgrims *have borne* these relics along the same road for centuries.

**6. Produce, yield.** Of trees, investments, and efforts. *Bear fruit* works literally and figuratively; *bear interest* is the banking use. (приносить (плоды, доход, результат))

16. The old apple tree *bears* less fruit every year.
17. The account *bore* four percent interest.
18. Two years of talks *have borne* fruit at last.

**7. Have or show (a name, mark, resemblance).** Of documents, buildings, and faces: to carry a name, a signature, a scar, or a likeness. (носить (имя, знак), иметь)

19. The building *bears* her grandfather's name.
20. The check *bore* no signature.
21. The town *has borne* that name since 1802.

**8. bear in mind.** A fixed phrase, most often imperative, meaning to keep a fact in view while deciding something. (иметь в виду, учитывать)

22. *Bear* in mind that the office closes at four.
23. We *bore* in mind that she was new.
24. The committee *has borne* the deadline in mind throughout.

**9. bear with (someone).** A request for patience, usually imperative: *bear with me*. (потерпеть, отнестись с терпением)

25. *Bear* with me — this will take a minute.
26. She *bore* with his questions for a full hour.
27. Readers *have borne* with this digression long enough.

**10. Turn, veer in a direction.** Of a road, a vehicle, or a walker; also nautical. *Bear left*, *bear right*, *bear north*. (брать (влево, вправо), держать курс)

28. *Bear* left where the road forks.
29. The trail *bore* east along the ridge.
30. We *had borne* too far south and lost the river.

**11. bear out — confirm.** Of evidence, data, or events proving a claim or a person right. (подтверждать)

31. The data *bear* out her theory.
32. The audit *bore* out every complaint.
33. Events *have borne* him out.

### bespeak

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈspik/ &middot; *bih-SPEEK*
**Русский:** свидетельствовать, означать; предвещать
**Forms:** *bespeak* /bɪˈspik/ &middot; *bespoke* /bɪˈspoʊk/ &middot; *bespoken* /bɪˈspoʊkən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** rare — a prefixed, literary verb; *bespoke* is better known as the British tailoring adjective
**Pattern:** ea /i/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *bespeaks* &middot; **-ing form:** *bespeaking*
**Contrast:** A prefixed [speak](#speak) — the same three forms with *be-* on the front, so there is nothing new to memorize. In American English it is a formal, literary verb meaning "be evidence of," and the past form *bespoke* is far better known as a British adjective for custom tailoring (*a bespoke suit*); Americans say *custom-made*.
**Verb type:** **stative** in its one living American sense, "be evidence of" — the subject is a thing, not an actor, and the progressive is out: ✗ *the silence is bespeaking trouble*. The archaic "order in advance" sense is action
**Transitivity:** **transitive**, object obligatory: *cracked paint bespeaks neglect*, ✗ *the empty shelves bespoke*
**Passive:** **restricted** — fine in the archaic ordering sense (*the rooms had been bespoken months before the wedding*), but the evidence sense resists it like other stative verbs: ✗ *neglect is bespoken by the cracked paint*.

A formal, literary verb meaning to be evidence of something; its past form *bespoke* leads a second life as a British adjective for custom-made goods.

**1. Be a sign of, give evidence of.** The only living verb sense in American English, and formal even then. The subject is almost always a thing — a voice, a room, a detail — not a person. (свидетельствовать о, говорить о)

1. Cracked paint and a sagging porch *bespeak* neglect.
2. The empty shelves *bespoke* a store about to close.
3. The silence *had bespoken* trouble long before anyone admitted it.
4. Her steady voice *bespeaks* years of practice.

**2. Order or arrange in advance.** Archaic: to speak for something ahead of time — a suit, a room, a dinner. This is the sense that produced the tailoring adjective. (заказывать заранее, договариваться о)

5. He *bespoke* a suit from a tailor on Savile Row.
6. In older novels a traveler would *bespeak* dinner at the inn.
7. The rooms *had been bespoken* months before the wedding.

**3. bespoke — custom-made (adjective).** British, and not a verb use at all, though it is where most readers meet the form. Americans say *custom-made*, *made-to-order*, or *custom*. (сделанный на заказ, индивидуальный)

8. He wears a *bespoke* suit to every hearing.
9. The firm sells *bespoke* software to hospitals.

**See also:** [speak](#speak), [misspeak](#misspeak)

### bite

**Pronunciation:** /baɪt/ &middot; *BITE*
**Русский:** кусать
**Forms:** *bite* /baɪt/ &middot; *bit* /bɪt/ &middot; *bitten* /ˈbɪtən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** i-e /aɪ/ — i /ɪ/ — i + -ten &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *bites* &middot; **-ing form:** *biting* (the silent e drops)
**Contrast:** The past is *bit* and the participle is *bitten*: ✗ *I have bit my tongue*, ✓ *I have bitten my tongue*. Because *bit* is also an everyday noun (*a bit of sugar*) and a past tense that sounds complete on its own, the short form slips in after *have* more than almost any other verb in this group.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic throughout, from teeth (*the terrier bit the mail carrier*) to tires gripping snow to cold and tariffs taking hold
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive with an object (*bite your tongue*, *bite back a laugh*), intransitive with the object left out (*dogs bite*, *nobody bit*, *the wind bites*)
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *she was bitten by a spider in the garage*. No passive of the intransitive *trout bite best after sunrise*.

To cut or grip something with the teeth — and, by extension, to sting, to grip a surface, or to take hold painfully.

**1. Cut or grip with the teeth.** The core sense, transitive or intransitive. Remember the split: past *bit*, participle *bitten* — ✗ *I have bit my tongue*. (кусать, укусить)

1. Dogs *bite* when they are frightened.
2. The neighbor's terrier *bit* the mail carrier last spring.
3. I *have bitten* my nails since I was nine.

**2. Sting (of insects and snakes).** In American usage mosquitoes, spiders, ants, and snakes *bite*; bees and wasps *sting*. (кусать, жалить)

4. Mosquitoes *bite* worst at dusk.
5. The mosquito *bit* me twice before I felt it.
6. She was *bitten* by a spider in the garage.

**3. Grip a surface, take hold.** Of tires, blades, screws, and anchors — anything that digs in rather than slipping. (врезаться, впиваться, цепляться)

7. Winter tires *bite* into packed snow.
8. The blade *bit* deep into the log.
9. The anchor *had bitten* into the sand by then.

**4. Take effect painfully.** Of cold, wind, budget cuts, sanctions, or prices — the moment something stops being abstract and starts to hurt. (сказываться, бить (по кому-л.), пробирать)

10. The wind *bites* on that side of the building.
11. By then the cold *had bitten* through my gloves.
12. The new tariffs *bit* hardest in the smallest towns.

**5. Take the bait, respond to an offer.** Of fish, and figuratively of buyers or of anyone falling for a trick. Often negative: *nobody bit*. (клюнуть, попасться на удочку)

13. Trout *bite* best just after sunrise.
14. We advertised for a week and nobody *bit*.
15. Two buyers *have bitten* since we dropped the price.

**6. bite the bullet.** A fixed phrase: to accept something painful you have been putting off. (стиснуть зубы, решиться на неприятное)

16. You'll have to *bite* the bullet and tell her.
17. We finally *bit* the bullet and replaced the roof.
18. He *has bitten* the bullet on far worse repairs.

**7. bite your tongue, bite back.** To stop yourself from saying something. *Bite back* takes an object: *bite back a reply*. (прикусить язык, сдержаться)

19. I *bit* my tongue and said nothing.
20. She had to *bite* back a laugh.
21. He *has bitten* his tongue through three straight meetings.


**Phrasal verbs:** [bite back](14-phrasal-b.md#bite-back) &middot; [bite into](14-phrasal-b.md#bite-into) &middot; [bite off](14-phrasal-b.md#bite-off)

**See also:** [hide](#hide), [forget](#forget)

### break

**Pronunciation:** /breɪk/ &middot; *BRAKE*
**Русский:** ломать; разбивать
**Forms:** *break* /breɪk/ &middot; *broke* /broʊk/ &middot; *broken* /ˈbroʊkən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** ea /eɪ/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *breaks* &middot; **-ing form:** *breaking*
**Contrast:** ✗ *I have broke it* — after *have*, it is always *broken*. Bare *broke* doubles as an adjective meaning "out of money" (*I'm broke until Friday*), which is part of why the wrong form sounds passable to some ears. The base is a homophone of *brake*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, and ergative: the same event runs transitive or intransitive (*he broke the vase* / *the vase broke*). Bare *broke* also lives on as an adjective, "out of money"
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive when someone breaks a thing, a rule, or a record (*she broke the state record*), intransitive of things that break, fail, or begin (*my phone broke*, *day breaks around five*, *war broke out*)
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *the window had been broken before we arrived*, *the story was broken on Sunday*. No passive of the intransitive *day breaks* or *the talks broke down*.

To separate something into pieces or stop it working — and, figuratively, to violate a rule, interrupt a state, or surpass a limit.

**1. Break into pieces, smash.** The literal core, transitive or intransitive. After *have* it is always *broken*: ✗ *I have broke it*. (ломать, разбивать; ломаться)

1. Glass jars *break* if you stack them.
2. He *broke* the vase reaching for the light.
3. Someone *had broken* the window before we arrived.

**2. Stop working, fail.** Of machines and devices. For cars and larger machinery Americans usually add *down*. (ломаться, выходить из строя)

4. Cheap chargers *break* within a month.
5. My phone *broke* the day the warranty ended.
6. The washing machine *has broken* twice this year.

**3. Fracture a bone.** Used of the person and of the bone alike: *he broke his wrist*, *his wrist broke*. (сломать (кость))

7. Old bones *break* easily.
8. He *broke* his wrist on the ice last winter.
9. She *has broken* the same finger twice.

**4. Violate a rule, law, or promise.** Figurative but completely ordinary — *break the law*, *break a contract*, *break your word*. (нарушать (закон, обещание, правило))

10. Don't *break* your promise to her.
11. The company *broke* the terms of the lease.
12. He *has broken* every rule in the handbook.

**5. Interrupt, put an end to a continuous state.** Of silence, a habit, a tie, a heat wave, a fall, a journey — anything that has been running unbroken. (прерывать, нарушать (тишину, привычку, ничью))

13. Nobody wanted to *break* the silence.
14. A branch *broke* his fall.
15. The rain *has broken* the heat wave at last.

**6. Beat a record or a limit.** Of records, thresholds, and numbers: *break a record*, *break four minutes*, *break a hundred degrees*. (побить (рекорд), преодолеть (порог))

16. Records *break* at every Olympics.
17. She *broke* the state record in the 400.
18. Three runners *have broken* four minutes on this track.

**7. break the news, break a story.** To be the one who tells something for the first time — gently, in the case of bad news; first, in the case of journalism. (сообщить (новость), первым опубликовать)

19. She *breaks* the news gently whenever she can.
20. The paper *broke* the story on Sunday.
21. Someone *had broken* the news to him already.

**8. Of dawn, weather, or a voice.** A cluster of intransitive uses: day breaks, a storm breaks, and a voice breaks either with emotion or in adolescence. (наступать (о рассвете); разразиться; ломаться (о голосе))

22. Day *breaks* around five in June.
23. His voice *broke* when he thanked her.
24. The storm *had broken* before we reached the car.

**9. break down.** Three common uses: a vehicle or negotiation fails, a person loses composure, or something is separated into parts for analysis. (сломаться; сорваться, разрыдаться; разбить на части)

25. *Break* the costs down by department.
26. Our car *broke* down on the interstate.
27. The talks *have broken* down completely.

**10. break up.** To end a relationship, to disperse a gathering, or to come apart into pieces. Of a phone call it means the signal is cutting out. (расстаться; разойтись; распадаться)

28. Ice on the river *breaks* up in April.
29. They *broke* up after four years.
30. The meeting *had broken* up by noon.

**11. break in, break into.** *Break into* a building or a car is burglary; *break in* also means to soften new shoes or train a newcomer, and *break into* a market or a laugh starts something abruptly. (вломиться, проникнуть; разносить (обувь))

31. It takes a week to *break* in new boots.
32. Someone *broke* into the garage last night.
33. Thieves *have broken* into three cars on this block.

**12. break out.** Of fires, wars, arguments, and epidemics starting suddenly; also of escaping from confinement, and of skin — *break out in a rash*. (вспыхнуть (о войне, пожаре); сбежать)

34. Fires *break* out in that canyon every August.
35. War *broke* out that September.
36. Two inmates *had broken* out through the laundry.

### choose

**Pronunciation:** /tʃuz/ &middot; *CHOOZ*
**Русский:** выбирать
**Forms:** *choose* /tʃuz/ &middot; *chose* /tʃoʊz/ &middot; *chosen* /ˈtʃoʊzən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** oo /u/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *chooses* &middot; **-ing form:** *choosing* (the silent e drops)
**Contrast:** Two o's in the present, one in the past — and completely different vowels: *choose* /tʃuz/ but *chose* /tʃoʊz/. Since the spellings differ by a single letter, the common error is a misspelling rather than a tense mistake: ✗ *I chose to walk to work every day* when you mean *choose*. There is no ✗ *choosed*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic mental verb; the progressive is fine (*I'm still choosing*). The participle *chosen* also stands alone as an elevated adjective: *the chosen one*
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive with the thing picked (*they chose the cheaper apartment*), intransitive when the object is understood (*you choose this time*) or replaced by *between / from* or a *to*-infinitive (*she chose to walk*)
**Passive:** **yes** — *she was chosen from a field of forty*, *a captain is chosen every September*. The *to*-infinitive pattern has no passive: ✗ *to walk was chosen by her*.

To pick one thing out of several, or to decide on a course of action.

**1. Pick one out of several.** The core sense. Watch the spelling more than the tense: present *choose* /tʃuz/, past *chose* /tʃoʊz/, and there is no ✗ *choosed*. (выбирать, выбрать)

1. You *choose* the restaurant this time.
2. They *chose* the cheaper apartment.
3. We *have chosen* a name for the dog.

**2. choose to — decide to do something.** With an infinitive, meaning to do something deliberately rather than by default. Often used to underline that it was a free decision. (предпочесть, решить)

4. People *choose* to live here for the quiet.
5. She *chose* to walk rather than wait for the bus.
6. He *has chosen* not to comment.

**3. Select someone for a role.** Of committees, voters, and teams. Very often passive: *she was chosen from a field of forty*. (избирать, отбирать, назначать)

7. Each team *chooses* a captain in September.
8. Voters *chose* him by a wide margin.
9. The committee *had chosen* her before the interviews ended.

**4. choose between, choose from.** *Between* takes two options, *among* or *from* takes more. *There is nothing to choose between them* means they are equally good. (выбирать между; выбирать из)

10. You have to *choose* between the two offers.
11. She *chose* from a list of six.
12. I *have chosen* from this menu so often I know it by heart.

**5. chosen — selected, elect (adjective).** As an adjective the participle carries a religious or elevated tone: *the chosen people*, *the chosen one*. (избранный)

13. He acts like the *chosen* one.
14. They saw themselves as a *chosen* people.

### cleave

**Pronunciation:** /kliv/ &middot; *KLEEV*
**Русский:** раскалывать; прилипать; оставаться верным

**Transitivity:** **both** — usually intransitive (*if I misspeak, please correct me*), transitive in the older plain sense of getting a word out wrong (*the announcer misspoke the winner's name*)
**Passive:** **restricted** — marginal even with an object (?*the winner's name was misspoken on live television*), and the ordinary intransitive use has no passive at all: ✗ *it was misspoken by the senator*.

To say the wrong thing by mistake — in American public life, the standard way to take a statement back without calling it a lie.

**1. Misstate something, say the wrong thing.** Nearly always about oneself and nearly always in the past: *I misspoke* concedes an error while denying intent. (оговориться, сказать не то)

1. If I *misspeak*, please correct me.
2. The senator *misspoke* about the date of the vote.
3. He admits he *has misspoken* twice this week.

**2. Say or pronounce badly.** The older, plainer sense — getting a word, a name, or a line out wrong. Rarer than the political use above. (говорить или произносить неправильно)

4. Even careful people *misspeak* when they are tired.
5. The announcer *misspoke* the winner's name on live television.
6. She realized she *had misspoken* the moment the words left her mouth.

### speak

**Русский:** говорить
**Forms:** *speak* /spik/ &middot; *spoke* /spoʊk/ &middot; *spoken* /ˈspoʊkən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** ea /i/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *speaks* &middot; **-ing form:** *speaking*
**Contrast:** ✗ *I have spoke to her* — the participle is *spoken*. This is the model verb for the whole group, so fixing it here fixes *bespeak*, *misspeak*, and by analogy *break*, *steal*, and *freeze*. Note that *spoke* is also a noun (a wheel's spoke) and *spoken* runs loose as an adjective in *spoken English*, *soft-spoken*, *well-spoken*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the talking senses (*he spoke for twenty minutes*), but **stative** when it means knowing a language, where the progressive is out: ✗ *I am speaking Spanish* to mean you know it
**Transitivity:** **both** — intransitive at the core (*please speak more slowly*, *nobody had spoken for a minute*), transitive with a language or a fixed object (*we speak Spanish at home*, *she speaks her mind*)
**Passive:** **restricted** — natural with a language or a fixed object (*Spanish is spoken here*, *not a word was spoken*), but the ordinary intransitive and prepositional uses have none: ✗ *the landlord was spoken*, and ✗ *she was spoken to slowly* only works as the separate prepositional passive.

To use the voice to say words — to talk, to hold a conversation, to use a language, or to address an audience.

**1. Say words, talk.** The bare sense, usually with an adverb or in the negative. This is the model verb for the whole group: ✗ *I have spoke*. (говорить, разговаривать)

1. Please *speak* more slowly.
2. He *spoke* very quietly.
3. Nobody *had spoken* for a full minute.

**2. speak to, speak with — talk to someone.** *Speak to* and *speak with* are both American; *with* sounds a shade more mutual, *to* a shade more one-directional. (поговорить с, обратиться к)

4. *Speak* with your advisor before you drop the class.
5. I *spoke* to the landlord yesterday.
6. She *has spoken* with all three references.

**3. Know and use a language.** Transitive, with a language as the object, and never progressive in this sense: ✗ *I am speaking Spanish* to mean you know it. (говорить на (языке), владеть языком)

7. We *speak* Spanish at home.
8. She *spoke* almost no English when she arrived.
9. I *have spoken* Russian since childhood.

**4. Give a talk, address an audience.** Intransitive, usually with *at*, *to*, or *on*. The related noun is *speaker*. (выступать, произносить речь)

10. The dean *speaks* at every graduation.
11. He *spoke* for twenty minutes without notes.
12. She *has spoken* at three conferences this year.

**5. speak up.** Two everyday uses: talk louder so you can be heard, and volunteer an opinion or an offer in a group. (говорить громче; подать голос)

13. *Speak* up, please — we can't hear you in back.
14. Nobody *spoke* up when he asked for volunteers.
15. One student *has spoken* up at every meeting.

**6. speak out.** Stronger than *speak up*: to state a public objection, usually at some risk. Often *speak out against*. (открыто высказываться, выступать против)

16. Few employees *speak* out in meetings like that.
17. Teachers *spoke* out against the new schedule.
18. Several officers *have spoken* out since the report.

**7. speak your mind.** A fixed phrase for saying exactly what you think, with the implication that most people would not. (высказать свое мнение прямо)

19. She *speaks* her mind at every meeting.
20. He *spoke* his mind and lost the account.
21. I *have spoken* my mind often enough here.

**8. speak for — represent; vouch for.** To act as someone's voice, or to attest to their quality. *Speak for yourself* is a mild rebuke. (говорить от имени; ручаться за)

22. I *speak* for the whole department here.
23. She *spoke* for the family at the hearing.
24. He *has spoken* for that community for twenty years.

25. The numbers *speak* for themselves.
26. Her silence *spoke* volumes.
27. The results *have spoken* for themselves all season.

**10. so to speak, generally speaking.** Frozen phrases used as sentence adverbs — hedging a metaphor, framing a generalization, or changing the subject with *speaking of*. (так сказать; вообще говоря)

To take what is not yours — and by extension to move quietly, to snatch a small pleasure, or to take the attention away from someone else.

**1. Take without permission.** The core sense. You *steal* the thing and *rob* the person or place: ✗ *they stole the bank*. The passive is where the participle error shows up: ✗ *my bike has been stole*. (красть, воровать)

5. Big labels *steal* from small ones all the time.
6. He *stole* the line from an old movie.
7. She claims they *have stolen* her design.

**3. Take quickly and quietly.** Of a glance, a kiss, a nap, an hour — something small taken when nobody is looking or when there is no time for it. (урвать, сделать украдкой)

8. I *steal* twenty minutes with a book after lunch.
9. He *stole* a look at his phone under the table.
10. We *have stolen* whole afternoons this way.

11. Fog *steals* up the valley every evening.
12. She *stole* out of the house before dawn.
13. He *had stolen* back to his seat unnoticed.

11. He *tore* a ligament in the second half.

13. The town was *torn* between two proposals.
14. I am *torn* between the two offers.

**Pronunciation:** /wɛr/ &middot; *WAIR*
**Русский:** носить (одежду и т. п.)
**Forms:** *wear* /wɛr/ &middot; *wore* /wɔr/ &middot; *worn* /wɔrn/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** ear /ɛr/ — ore /ɔr/ — orn &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *wears* &middot; **-ing form:** *wearing*
**Contrast:** ✗ *She has wore that coat all winter* — the participle is *worn*. *Worn* also stands alone as an adjective meaning shabby or tired (*worn shoes*, *a worn face*), which is a useful reminder that it, not *wore*, is the form that follows *have* and *be*. The base is a homophone of *where* and *ware*.
**Verb type:** **both** — the erosion senses are action and dynamic (*water wore a channel through the rock*), while having clothes on is closer to **stative**, a state that happens to allow the progressive for temporary wear (*she's wearing boots today*). *Worn* is also an adjective meaning shabby or tired
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive with clothing, a look, or a surface (*he wore the same tie*, *traffic wears the paint off*), intransitive when the thing itself deteriorates or an effect fades (*cheap fabric wears badly*, *the numbness wears off*)
**Passive:** **restricted** — free in the erosion senses (*the stairs had been worn smooth*, *he was worn down by the campaign*), but the clothing sense is stilted as a full passive (✗ *the same tie was worn by him to every interview*) and survives mainly as a reduced participle: *the coat worn by the bride*.

To have clothing or an accessory on your body — and, in a second family of senses, to reduce or damage something gradually through use.

**1. Have clothing on.** The core sense, and one of the highest-frequency verbs in the language. After *have* it is *worn*: ✗ *she has wore that coat*. (носить (одежду), быть одетым в)

1. Most people here *wear* boots all winter.
2. He *wore* the same tie to every interview.
3. She *has worn* those earrings every day this month.

**2. Have a hairstyle, expression, or look.** Extends past clothes to anything you carry on your person or your face: a beard, a smile, a look of confusion, cologne. (носить (прическу); иметь (выражение лица))

4. She *wears* her hair short now.
5. He *wore* a look of complete confusion.
6. He *has worn* a beard since college.

**3. Damage or reduce through use.** Transitive, of friction over time: *wear a hole*, *wear a groove*, *wear something smooth*. (изнашивать, стирать, протирать)

7. Traffic *wears* the paint off the crosswalk every year.
8. Water *wore* a channel through the rock.
9. The stairs *had worn* smooth in the middle.

**4. Become thin or damaged with use.** The intransitive half. *Wear well* is a compliment — it means something (or someone) ages without deteriorating. (изнашиваться, протираться)

10. Cheap fabric *wears* badly.
11. The soles *wore* out in a single season.
12. The rug *has worn* through by the door.

**5. wear out.** Of an object used until it is useless, and of a person exhausted by something. Also *wear out your welcome*. (изнашивать(ся); измотать)

13. Those kids *wear* me out.
14. He *wore* out three pairs of shoes on the trail.
15. I *have worn* myself out arguing about this.

**6. wear off.** Of an effect fading: medication, novelty, shock, the numbness after the dentist. (проходить, ослабевать (об эффекте))

16. Wait until the numbness *wears* off.
17. The novelty *wore* off in a week.
18. The excitement *has worn* off completely.

**7. wear down.** To weaken someone's resistance by persistence rather than force; also literal, of surfaces ground away. (измотать, сломить сопротивление)

19. Constant delays *wear* down even patient clients.
20. They *wore* him down with phone calls.
21. The campaign *had worn* her down by October.

**8. wear on.** Two uses: of time passing slowly — *as the evening wore on* — and of something grating on a person. (тянуться (о времени); действовать на нервы)

22. His whistling *wears* on me.
23. As the afternoon *wore* on, fewer people stayed.
24. The wait *has worn* on everyone.

**9. worn — shabby, tired (adjective).** The participle stands alone as an adjective, of objects and of faces — a useful reminder that *worn*, not *wore*, is the form that follows *have* and *be*. (потертый, изношенный; измученный)

25. He came in with a *worn* jacket and a *worn* face.
26. The book is *worn* at the spine.

### weave

**Pronunciation:** /wiv/ &middot; *WEEV*
**Русский:** ткать; плести
**Forms:** *weave* /wiv/ &middot; *wove* /woʊv/ &middot; *woven* /ˈwoʊvən/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — ordinary in speech and writing, above all the participle *woven*
**Pattern:** ea /i/ — o /oʊ/ — o + -n &middot; **Group:** 13
**Third person:** *weaves* &middot; **-ing form:** *weaving* (the silent e drops)
**Contrast:** The meaning picks the forms. Weaving thread, baskets, or storylines takes *wove/woven*; moving in and out through traffic or a crowd takes the regular *weaved*: ✓ *The cab weaved across three lanes*, ✗ *The cab wove across three lanes*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, and the sense picks the forms: making cloth or storylines takes *wove/woven*, while winding through traffic is regular — ✓ *the cab weaved across three lanes*, ✗ *the cab wove across three lanes*
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive in the making senses (*her grandmother wove blankets*, *he weaves jokes into serious lectures*), intransitive in the movement sense (*cyclists weave through stopped traffic*)
**Passive:** **yes** in the making senses — *the scarf is woven from two kinds of wool*, *the letters had been woven into the narrative*. No passive of the movement sense: ✗ *through the pack was weaved*.

To make cloth or baskets by crossing strands — and, figuratively, to work several elements into one whole. Winding through traffic is the regular *weaved*.

**1. Make cloth on a loom.** The literal textile sense, and the one that firmly takes *wove/woven*. (ткать)

1. They *weave* rugs on a loom older than the house.
2. Her grandmother *wove* blankets for the whole family.
3. Villagers here *have woven* silk for six generations.
4. The scarf is *woven* from two kinds of wool.

**2. Make by interlacing strands.** Baskets, wreaths, fences, hair — anything built by crossing flexible material. (плести (корзину, венок))

5. Children *weave* wreaths out of clover.
6. She *wove* a basket out of river reeds.
7. He *had woven* a fence out of willow branches.

**3. Work several elements into a whole.** Figurative, of stories, arguments, music, and lectures. Usually *weave something into something*. (вплетать, сплетать (в повествование))

8. He *weaves* jokes into serious lectures.
9. The author *had woven* the letters into the narrative.
10. She *has woven* three timelines into one story.

**4. Move winding through.** This sense is regular — *weaved, weaved*: ✓ *the cab weaved across three lanes*, ✗ *the cab wove across three lanes*. (лавировать, петлять)

11. Cyclists *weave* through stopped traffic all day.
12. The runner *weaved* through the pack on the last lap.
13. He *has weaved* in and out of that lane for a mile.
