# Core verbs: learning and teaching

> The everyday regular verbs of learning and teaching — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### educate

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɛdʒəkeɪt/ &middot; *EJ-uh-kayt*
**Русский:** обучать, дать образование; просвещать
**Forms:** *educate* &middot; *educated* &middot; *educated*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *educates* &middot; **-ing form:** *educating*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To teach someone over a long period, especially in a school or college.

1. Public schools *educate* nearly fifty million American children.
2. She was *educated* at a small college in Vermont.
3. The campaign is *educating* parents about online safety.
