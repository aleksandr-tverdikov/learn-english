# Phrasal verbs on regular bases: G-L

> Phrasal verbs built on regular verbs — *check in*, *look up*, *figure out* — headwords G-L.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

The catalog's other 925 phrasal verbs are all built on **irregular** bases — *get*, *give*, *take*, *put*, *come*, *go* — because that is how the source material was organized. That left out the whole regular-base family, including some of the commonest phrasal verbs in English.

Each entry names its **base verb** and says whether it is **separable**, which is the hardest thing about phrasal verbs: *turn it off* and *turn off the light* are both fine, but *look after the kids* can never become ✗ *look the kids after*. A phrasal verb with several distinct meanings gets one entry per meaning, because they are different verbs.

### gain on

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡeɪn ɑːn/ &middot; *GAYN ahn*
**Русский:** догонять, догнать; нагонять, нагнать
**Base verb:** *gain*
**Separable:** inseparable — the object follows *on*: *gaining on us*, never ✗ *gain us on*.
**Forms:** *gain on* &middot; *gained on* &middot; *gained on*
**Third person:** *gains on* &middot; **-ing form:** *gaining on*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To get closer to someone or something ahead of you.

1. That blue car has been *gaining on* us since the last exit.
2. She *gained on* the leader with every lap.
3. By mile ten he had *gained on* the whole front pack.

### gang up on

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæŋ ˌʌp ɑːn/ &middot; *GANG up ahn*
**Русский:** ополчаться, ополчиться (на кого-л.); наваливаться всем вместе
**Base verb:** *gang*
**Separable:** inseparable — the target comes after *on*: *they ganged up on him*, never ✗ *they ganged him up on*. Without a target it stands alone: *they all ganged up*.

**Third person:** *gangs up on* &middot; **-ing form:** *ganging up on*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To join with others against one person.

1. Don't all *gang up on* me at once.
2. The other kids *ganged up on* him at recess.
3. Three networks are *ganging up on* the same story.

### gas up

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡæs ˌʌp/ &middot; *GAS up*
**Русский:** заправлять, заправить (машину); заправляться, заправиться
**Base verb:** *gas*
**Separable:** separable — *gas up the car* or *gas the car up*, and with a pronoun it must split: *gas it up*. Just as often there is no object at all: *we stopped to gas up*.
**Forms:** *gas up* &middot; *gassed up* &middot; *gassed up*
**Third person:** *gasses up* &middot; **-ing form:** *gassing up*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (gas up the truck), intransitive (stop and gas up)

To fill a vehicle's tank with gasoline.

1. I need to *gas up* before we get on the highway.
2. He *gassed up* the truck at the station on Route 9.
3. We're *gassing up* and then heading straight out.
