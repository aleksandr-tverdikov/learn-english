# Core verbs: damage and repair

> The everyday regular verbs of damage and repair — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### damage

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdæmɪdʒ/ &middot; *DAM-ij*
**Русский:** повреждать, повредить; наносить ущерб
**Forms:** *damage* &middot; *damaged* &middot; *damaged*
**Third person:** *damages* &middot; **-ing form:** *damaging*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To harm something so that it works less well or looks worse.

1. Heavy rain *damaged* the roof over the porch.
2. Smoking *damages* your lungs a little at a time.
3. Two of the paintings were badly *damaged* in the fire.

**Русский:** осушать, осушить; истощать, истощить
**Forms:** *drain* &middot; *drained* &middot; *drained*
**Third person:** *drains* &middot; **-ing form:** *draining*
**Transitivity:** both

To empty liquid out of something, or to use up strength or money.

1. He *drained* the radiator before the first freeze.
2. The tank *drains* in about ten minutes.
3. The long repair had *drained* their savings.
