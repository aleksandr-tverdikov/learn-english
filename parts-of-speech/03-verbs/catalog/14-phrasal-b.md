# Phrasal verbs: be — buy

> Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs be, bear, beat… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

[← Irregular verbs](README.md) &middot; [The grammar of verbs](../README.md)

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle whose meaning you cannot add up from the parts: *break down* has nothing to do with breaking, *bring up* a subject involves no upward motion, and *be up to* something is not about height. The other half of learning one is **separability** — whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle (*break the numbers down*) or has to follow the whole phrase (*bear with me*), because a separable verb with a pronoun object *must* split: *blow it off*, never ✗ *blow off it*. Every entry below is built on one of the irregular verbs from *be* to *buy*, and in speech the stress lands on the particle — *bring it UP*, not *BRING it up* — which is what fuses the two words into a single piece of vocabulary.

### be after

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈæftər/ &middot; *bee-AF-ter* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** добиваться, охотиться за, преследовать
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object always follows the preposition. ✗ *be my job after*, ✗ *be it after*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **stative** — copular *be* plus a preposition, naming a state of pursuit, not an act. It rejects the progressive: ✗ *she is being after my job*.
**Passive:** **no** — *be* has no passive of its own, so the prepositional passive is impossible: ✗ *my job is been after by her*, ✗ *he was been after by the marshals*.

To be trying to get hold of something, or to be chasing somebody down.

**1. Be trying to get something.** Used of a thing somebody wants — a job, money, a piece of information. *Be* stays simple here; the continuous ✗ *is being after* is impossible. (добиваться, охотиться за)

1. I'm fairly sure she *is after* my job.
2. He *was after* a raise, not a compliment.
3. Whatever they *were after*, they didn't find it in the safe.

**2. Be pursuing a person.** Of police, creditors, or anyone chasing someone down. The person hunted comes straight after the preposition. (преследовать, разыскивать)

4. The marshals *have been after* him for two years.
5. The IRS *is after* my neighbor about a return he never filed.

**See also:** [be up to](#be-up-to), [be on to](#be-on-to), [be in on](#be-in-on)

### be along

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi əˈlɔŋ/ &middot; *bee-uh-LONG* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** подойти, подъехать, скоро прибыть
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — *be along* takes no object, so nothing can sit between the two words. ✗ *be the doctor along*.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** in form — the copula plus a particle, so no progressive (✗ *the doctor is being along*) — though what it reports is an arrival, which is why it lives in the future: *will be along*, *should be along*.
**Passive:** **no** — there is no object, and *be* cannot be passivized in any case: ✗ *she was been along*.

To arrive — said of a person or a vehicle that is expected shortly. It lives almost entirely in the future: *will be along*, *should be along*.

1. The doctor *will be along* in a minute.
2. Wait by the curb — your ride *will be along* soon.
3. She said she *would be along* as soon as the meeting broke up.
4. Another train *will be along* in twelve minutes.
5. Someone from maintenance *should be along* before noon.

**See also:** [be around](#be-around), [be in](#be-in), [blow in](#blow-in)

### be around

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi əˈraʊnd/ &middot; *bee-uh-ROWND* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** быть поблизости; существовать давно; быть тёртым калачом
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be him around*.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** in all three senses — presence (*I'll be around*), long existence (*that diner has been around since the fifties*), and worldliness (*he has been around*). None of them takes the progressive: ✗ *he is being around*.
**Passive:** **no** — objectless and built on *be*: ✗ *the fifties have been been around since*.

To be present and available, to have existed for a long time, or — of a person — to have seen enough of life to be hard to fool.

**1. Be present, be available.** The commonest sense, usually about a stretch of time rather than a moment. (быть поблизости, быть рядом)

1. Call if you need help — I *will be around* all afternoon.
2. Nobody *was around* to sign for the package.

**2. Have existed for a long time.** Of institutions, products, and ideas. Almost always in the perfect. (существовать давно, быть известным)

3. That diner *has been around* since the fifties.
4. The idea *has been around* longer than any of us.

**3. Be experienced, be worldly.** Informal, and of a person. Said of a woman it can be a slur about her sex life, so Americans use it carefully. (быть тёртым калачом, повидать жизнь)

5. He *has been around* — you won't sell him a story like that.
6. She *has been around* long enough to spot a bad contract.

**See also:** [be in](#be-in), [be along](#be-along), [be out](#be-out)

### be down

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈdaʊn/ &middot; *bee-DOWN* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** быть подавленным; не работать (о системе); проигрывать, снизиться
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be the server down*.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — a condition, whether it is mood (*she has been down*), a dead server (*the network is down*), or a number (*sales are down nine percent*). ✗ *the network is being down*.
**Passive:** **no** — no object anywhere in the entry, so nothing can be promoted: ✗ *the network was been down*.

To be depressed, to be out of service, or to be lower or behind in a count.

**1. Be depressed, be in low spirits.** Informal, and about mood rather than illness. (быть в подавленном настроении, хандрить)

1. She *has been down* since the layoffs.
2. I *was down* for a week after the audition.

**2. Be out of service.** Of a network, a server, or a machine — the standard word in American offices. (не работать, лежать — о системе)

3. The whole network *is down* again.
4. Payroll *was down* for six hours yesterday.

**3. Be lower, be behind.** Of prices, sales, or a score. The amount follows: *down nine percent*, *down fourteen*. (снизиться; проигрывать, отставать)

5. We *were down* fourteen at the half and still won.
6. Sales *are down* nine percent from last quarter.

**See also:** [be up](#be-up), [be off](#be-off), [be down with](#be-down-with)

### be down with

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˌdaʊn ˈwɪθ/ &middot; *bee-down-WITH* &middot; the stress falls on the last word, with a lighter beat on *down*
**Русский:** слечь с (болезнью); быть за, поддерживать (сленг)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. ✗ *be the flu down with*, ✗ *be it down with*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday — sense 2 is slang
**Verb type:** **stative** — both the illness sense (*half the office is down with the flu*) and the slang approval sense (*I'm down with that*) name a state. ✗ *he is being down with the flu*.
**Passive:** **no** — *be* takes no passive, so the object of *with* stays put: ✗ *the flu is been down with by half the office*.

To be sick with a particular illness; in slang, to approve of something or be willing to join in.

**1. Be sick with.** The illness follows *with*. This is the ordinary American way to report what somebody has. (слечь с, болеть)

1. Half the office *is down with* the flu.
2. He *was down with* strep for most of spring break.

**2. Approve of, be willing.** Slang, and dated enough now that it sounds like the nineties. (быть за, одобрять)

3. I'm *down with* that plan.
4. She said she *was down with* whatever we decided.
5. Nobody *was down with* moving the meeting to seven.

**See also:** [be down](#be-down), [be up for](#be-up-for), [be into](#be-into)

### be in

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɪn/ &middot; *bee-IN* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** быть на месте; быть в моде; участвовать; поступить (о результатах)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be the doctor in* as a statement; the question *Is the doctor in?* is ordinary inversion, not separation.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — presence (*the doctor is in*), fashion (*wide lapels are in*), participation (*I'm in*), and arrival of results (*the votes are in*) are all states. ✗ *the doctor is being in*.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive throughout, and the copula has no passive: ✗ *the votes were been in*.

To be present at home or at work, to be in fashion, to be taking part, or — of results and arrivals — to have come in.

**1. Be present, be at home or at the office.** Of a person others are looking for. (быть на месте, быть дома)

1. Is Dr. Alvarez *in* today?
2. Nobody *was in* when the inspector came by.

**2. Be in fashion.** The opposite of *be out*. (быть в моде)

3. Wide lapels *are in* again.
4. Flip phones *were in* long before anyone called them retro.

**3. Be taking part, be included.** Usually the short answer *I'm in*. (участвовать, быть в деле)

5. If you're putting a team together, I'm *in*.
6. He *was in* until he saw the budget.

**4. Have arrived, have come in.** Of flights, harvests, votes, and lab results. (прибыть; поступить — о результатах)

7. The flight *is in* early.
8. The lab results *are in*, and they're clean.

**See also:** [be out](#be-out), [be around](#be-around), [be in on](#be-in-on)

### be in for

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɪn fər/ &middot; *bee-IN-fer* &middot; the stress falls on *in*; *for* is reduced to a schwa
**Русский:** не миновать чего-то, ждёт (обычно неприятное)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. ✗ *be a surprise in for*, ✗ *be it in for*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — it reports what is in store for someone, not anything the subject does: *we are in for a rough winter*. ✗ *we are being in for a rough winter*.
**Passive:** **no** — the object of *for* cannot move to subject: ✗ *a surprise is been in for by him*.

To be about to experience something, almost always something unpleasant.

1. If he thinks that drive is easy, he *is in for* a surprise.
2. We *are in for* a rough winter.
3. She *was in for* a long wait at the DMV.
4. Anyone who skipped the reading *is in for* trouble tomorrow.
5. They didn't know they *were in for* three more hours of delays.

**See also:** [be up for](#be-up-for), [be in](#be-in), [be out of](#be-out-of)

### be in on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɪn ɑn/ &middot; *bee-IN-on* &middot; the stress falls on *in*, the first of the two particles
**Русский:** быть в курсе, участвовать в (тайном)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. ✗ *be the joke in on*, ✗ *be it in on*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **stative** — being one of the people who know or take part is a standing condition: *she has been in on the planning since the first week*. ✗ *she is being in on it*.
**Passive:** **no** — built on *be*, so no passive at all: ✗ *the joke was been in on by everybody*.

To be one of the people who know about or take part in something, usually something private.

1. Only three people *were in on* the surprise.
2. Was the manager *in on* it, or did he really not know?
3. I want to *be in on* the next round of hiring.
4. Everybody at that table *was in on* the joke but me.
5. She *has been in on* the planning since the first week.

**See also:** [be in](#be-in), [be up to](#be-up-to), [be on to](#be-on-to)

### be into

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɪntu/ &middot; *bee-IN-too* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** увлекаться, интересоваться; быть неравнодушным к
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object always follows the preposition: *he is into synthesizers*, never ✗ *he is synthesizers into*; with a pronoun, *she is into it*, never ✗ *she is it into*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **stative** — a state of interest or attraction, whether the object is a hobby (*he is into synthesizers*) or a person (*she is into him*). ✗ *he is being into synthesizers*.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *synthesizers are been into by him*. The copula blocks it, however transitive the phrase looks.

To be keenly interested in something, or attracted to someone.

**1. Be keen on an activity or subject.** The everyday way an American says what a hobby is. (увлекаться, интересоваться)

1. He *is into* vintage synthesizers.
2. She *was into* rock climbing long before the gyms opened.
3. I'm not *into* podcasts about podcasts.

**2. Be attracted to a person.** Of romantic interest, and usually negative. (быть неравнодушным к, нравиться)

4. I don't think she *is into* me.
5. They dated for a month before he admitted he wasn't *into* it.

**See also:** [be up for](#be-up-for), [be down with](#be-down-with), [be after](#be-after)

### be off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɔf/ &middot; *bee-OFF* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** уходить; отменяться; быть выключенным; быть неточным; не работать (о выходном)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be the wedding off* as a statement.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** in senses 2 to 6 — canceled (*the wedding is off*), switched off, inaccurate, on leave, spoiled. Sense 1 (*I'm off*, *he was off before dawn*) reports a departure but still takes plain *be*: ✗ *I am being off*.
**Passive:** **no** — no object in any sense, and no passive of *be*: ✗ *the wedding was been off*.

To leave, to be canceled, to be switched off, to be inaccurate, or to be away from work.

**1. Leave, set out.** Announcing your own departure or somebody else's. (уходить, отправляться)

1. We *are off* to the airport at six.
2. They *were off* before sunrise.

**2. Be canceled.** Of an event or a deal that had been arranged. The opposite is *be on*. (отменяться, расстроиться)

3. The wedding *is off*.
4. The deal *was off* by the time the lawyers called back.

**3. Be switched off or disconnected.** Of lights, engines, and utilities. (быть выключенным, отключённым)

5. Make sure the burners *are off*.
6. The power *was off* all night.

**4. Be wrong, be inaccurate.** Of figures, and of anything that feels not quite right. (быть неточным, не сходиться)

7. Your totals *are off* by about two hundred dollars.
8. Something about the story *was off* from the start.

**5. Be away from work.** Of a day, a week, or a leave. (быть свободным, не работать)

9. I *am off* on Tuesday.
10. She *has been off* since the baby came.

**6. Be spoiled — British.** Of food gone bad. An American says *the milk has gone bad*, not ✗ *the milk is off*. (испортиться — о продуктах)

11. In London they'll tell you the milk *is off*; in Ohio it has gone bad.

**See also:** [be on](#be-on), [be out](#be-out), [be over](#be-over)

### be on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɑn/ &middot; *bee-ON* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** идти, состояться; быть включённым; выступать; принимать (лекарство)
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be the game on* as a statement.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — happening (*the game is on*), switched on, showing, or taking a drug are all states, as are the fixed *it's on me* and *you're on*. ✗ *the game is being on*.
**Passive:** **no** — objectless and copular: ✗ *the game was been on*. Even sense 4, *she is on lithium*, cannot invert.

To be going ahead as planned, to be switched on, to be performing or broadcasting, or to be taking a drug.

**1. Be happening, be going ahead.** The opposite of *be off*. (идти, состояться)

1. The game *is on* despite the rain.
2. Is the meeting still *on* for Thursday?

**2. Be switched on.** Of lights, appliances, and engines. (быть включённым)

3. The porch light *was on* all night.
4. Leave the fan *on*.

**3. Be performing or broadcasting.** Of a performer about to go up, or of a program showing. (выступать; идти — о передаче)

5. You *are on* in five minutes.
6. The news *was on* when I walked in.

**4. Be taking a medication or a drug.** The substance follows directly. (принимать, сидеть на)

7. She *is on* antibiotics for another week.
8. He *has been on* the same blood-pressure pill for years.

**5. Fixed uses: it's on me, you're on.** *It's on me* offers to pay; *you're on* accepts a bet or a challenge. (я угощаю; идёт, по рукам)

9. Dinner *is on* me tonight.
10. Fifty bucks says he misses. — You *are on*.

**See also:** [be off](#be-off), [be up](#be-up), [be out](#be-out)

### be on to

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈɑn tu/ &middot; *bee-ON-too* &middot; the stress falls on *on*, the first of the two particles
**Русский:** раскусить, разгадать; напасть на верный след
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. ✗ *be him on to*, ✗ *be it on to*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **stative** — a state of knowledge: having seen through someone (*the cops are on to him*) or having a promising lead (*you might be on to something*). ✗ *they are being on to him*.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *he is been on to by the cops*. Compare the ordinary verb *suspect*, which does passivize.

To have figured out what someone is up to, or — with *something* — to have hit on a promising idea. Often written as one word: *onto*.

**1. Have seen through someone.** Of anybody who has caught on to a scheme. (раскусить, разгадать)

1. Don't try it twice — they *are on to* you.
2. By March the auditors *were on to* him.

**2. Be onto something.** Have a promising lead or idea. Almost always with the vague word *something*. (напасть на что-то стоящее)

3. Keep digging — I think you *are on to* something.
4. She *was on to* something big and knew it.
5. The first draft was noise, but the second *was on to* something.

**See also:** [be after](#be-after), [be in on](#be-in-on), [be up to](#be-up-to)

### be out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈaʊt/ &middot; *bee-OWT* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** отсутствовать; выйти (о книге); выйти из моды; погаснуть; отпасть
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — there is no object to place. ✗ *be the book out* as a statement.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** across all seven senses — absent, published, unfashionable, extinguished, ruled out, openly gay, unconscious. Each is a resulting condition, never an act: ✗ *the fire is being out*.
**Passive:** **no** — nothing to promote, and *be* has no passive: ✗ *the book was been out in March*.

To be absent, to have been released, to be unfashionable, to be extinguished, to be ruled out, or to be openly gay.

**1. Be absent, be away.** Of a person away from a desk, a class, or a job. (отсутствовать, выйти)

1. She *is out* until Thursday.
2. The whole crew *was out* with food poisoning.

**2. Be published or released.** Of books, albums, films, and reports. (выйти, поступить в продажу)

3. The new edition *is out* in April.
4. His album *has been out* for a month.

**3. Be unfashionable.** The opposite of *be in*. (выйти из моды)

5. Cargo shorts *are out* again, apparently.

**4. Be extinguished.** Of fires, candles, and lights. (погаснуть, потухнуть)

6. The fire *was out* by midnight.

**5. Be ruled out, be no longer possible.** Of a plan that has stopped being an option. (отпадать, исключаться)

7. A June wedding *is out* — the venue is booked.

**6. Be openly gay.** Of a person who no longer hides it. (не скрывать ориентацию, быть открытым геем)

8. He *has been out* since college.

**7. Be unconscious.** Usually intensified: *out cold*. (быть без сознания)

9. He *was out* before the medics reached him.

**See also:** [be in](#be-in), [be off](#be-off), [be out of](#be-out-of)

### be out of

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈaʊt əv/ &middot; *bee-OWT-uv* &middot; the stress falls on *out*, the first of the two particles
**Русский:** закончиться (у кого-либо), не иметь больше
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. ✗ *be milk out of*, ✗ *be it out of*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — having none of something left is a state of the subject, not an event: *the store has been out of that model since June*. ✗ *we are being out of milk*.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *milk is been out of by us*. The thing you have run out of can never become the subject.

To have no more of something left.

1. We *are out of* milk again.
2. The pharmacy *was out of* the generic, so I paid for the brand name.
3. By Friday they *were out of* clean uniforms.
4. Tell me before we *are out of* printer paper, not after.
5. The store *has been out of* that model since June.

**See also:** [be out](#be-out), [be in for](#be-in-for), [be down with](#be-down-with)

### be over

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbi ˈoʊvər/ &middot; *bee-OH-ver* &middot; the stress falls on the particle, not the verb
**Русский:** закончиться; пережить, разлюбить
**Base verb:** [be](10-participle-base-plus-n.md#be) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle); prepositional verb in sense 2
**Separable:** no — sense 1 has no object at all, and in sense 2 the person follows the whole phrase: *she is over him*, never ✗ *she is him over*.
**Transitive:** either — no object in sense 1, always an object in sense 2
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** in both senses — an end reached (*the meeting is over*) and an attachment given up (*she is over him*). ✗ *the meeting is being over*, ✗ *she is being over him*.
**Passive:** **no** — sense 1 has no object, and sense 2's object resists promotion: ✗ *he is been over by her*.

To be finished; or, of a person, to have gotten past a hurt or an attachment.

**1. Be finished, be at an end.** Of meetings, games, seasons, and relationships seen from outside. (закончиться, кончиться)

1. The meeting *is over*.
2. By the time we parked, the first period *was over*.
3. It *was over* before anyone understood what had happened.

**2. No longer be upset about or attached to someone.** The object is a person or a hurt. (пережить, разлюбить, забыть)

**1. Accept or believe an idea.** Usually with a hint that the idea was oversold. (поверить в, купиться на)

1. I never *bought into* the whole story.
2. Half the country *bought into* it for about a year.
3. You don't have to *buy into* the theory to use the method.

**2. Purchase a stake.** Of practices, franchises, and funds. (войти в долю, купить долю)

4. She *bought into* the practice in 2016.
5. Two associates *bought into* the firm the year it expanded.
