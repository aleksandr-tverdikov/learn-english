# Phrasal verbs on regular bases: M-P

> Phrasal verbs built on regular verbs — *check in*, *look up*, *figure out* — headwords M-P.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

The catalog's other 925 phrasal verbs are all built on **irregular** bases — *get*, *give*, *take*, *put*, *come*, *go* — because that is how the source material was organized. That left out the whole regular-base family, including some of the commonest phrasal verbs in English.

Each entry names its **base verb** and says whether it is **separable**, which is the hardest thing about phrasal verbs: *turn it off* and *turn off the light* are both fine, but *look after the kids* can never become ✗ *look the kids after*. A phrasal verb with several distinct meanings gets one entry per meaning, because they are different verbs.

### mail in

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪl ˌɪn/ &middot; *MAYL in*
**Русский:** отправлять по почте, отправить по почте; высылать, выслать
**Base verb:** *mail*
**Separable:** separable — *mail in the form*, *mail the form in*, and with a pronoun it must split: *mail it in*, never ✗ *mail in it*.
**Forms:** *mail in* &middot; *mailed in* &middot; *mailed in*
**Third person:** *mails in* &middot; **-ing form:** *mailing in*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To send something to an office or a company by mail.

1. *Mail in* the rebate form within thirty days or you lose it.
2. She *mailed* her ballot *in* two weeks early.
3. Half the applications were *mailed in* after the deadline.

### mail it in

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪl ɪt ˌɪn/ &middot; *MAYL it in*
**Русский:** халтурить, схалтурить; делать спустя рукава
**Base verb:** *mail*
**Separable:** fixed — the object is always *it* and nothing else can go there: *they mailed it in*, never ✗ *mailed the concert in*. Informal.
**Forms:** *mail it in* &middot; *mailed it in* &middot; *mailed it in*
**Third person:** *mails it in* &middot; **-ing form:** *mailing it in*
**Transitivity:** transitive — but only with the fixed object *it*

To do something with no real effort, just enough to get through it.

1. The band *mailed it in* for the last three songs.
2. You can tell he's *mailing it in* until his contract runs out.
3. They *mail it in* every year against the weaker teams.
