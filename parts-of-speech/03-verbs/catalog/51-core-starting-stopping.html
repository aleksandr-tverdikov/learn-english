Core verbs: starting and stopping#

The everyday regular verbs of starting and stopping — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

end#

Pronunciation
/ɛnd/ · END
Русский
заканчивать, закончить; заканчиваться, кончаться
Forms
end · ended · ended
Verb class
regular
Third person
ends · -ing form: ending
Transitivity
both — transitive (end the meeting), intransitive (the movie ends late)

To stop, or to bring something to its finish.

  1. Let us end the meeting on time for once.
  2. The concert ended just after eleven.
  3. The strike has ended after nine long weeks.

Pronunciation
/kəˈmɛns/ · kuh-MENS
Русский
начинать, начать; начинаться
Forms
commence · commenced · commenced
Verb class
regular
Third person
commences · -ing form: commencing
Transitivity
both — transitive (commence an investigation), intransitive (the trial commences Monday)
Contrast
Commence is the formal word — a schedule or a court uses it. In ordinary speech Americans say start or begin.

To begin, especially something formal or official.

  1. Boarding will commence in ten minutes.
  2. The trial commenced on a rainy Tuesday in March.
  3. Work on the bridge is commencing next spring.
