# Phrasal verbs: make — run

> Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs make, mean, meet… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

[← Irregular verbs](README.md) &middot; [The grammar of verbs](../README.md)

A phrasal verb is an ordinary verb plus a small word that together mean something you could not have worked out from either half: *put off* has nothing to do with putting, and you *run into* an old friend without touching her (фразовые глаголы). Two things decide whether you sound like a native speaker. The first is stress — with a true particle you lean on the little word, *put it OFF*, not *PUT it off*, which is what welds the pair into a single word; with a preposition the stress stays back on the verb, *RUN into her*. The second is separability: a particle lets the object sit on either side (*put off the meeting*, *put the meeting off*) and a pronoun object **must** come in the middle (*put it off*, never ✗ *put off it*), while a preposition never lets go of its object (*run into her*, never ✗ *run her into*). Every entry below tells you which kind you are holding.

*Mean* is the one base here that builds nothing: *mean well* and *what do you mean by that* are the plain verb doing plain work, so no *mean* entry appears below.

### make for

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪk fɔr/ &middot; *MAKE-for* &middot; a preposition, so the stress stays back on the verb and *for* is reduced to /fɚ/
**Русский:** направляться к; способствовать, приводить к
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb
**Separable:** no — *for* never lets go of its object. ✗ *made the door for*, and with a pronoun the object still follows the whole phrase: *made for it*, never ✗ *made it for*.
**Transitive:** yes, there is always something after *for*
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **both** — action in sense 1, dynamic physical movement toward a goal (*she made for the exit*). Sense 2 is **stative**, a situation standing in a relation to its result, and it resists the progressive: ✗ *rain is making for a sloppy game*.
**Passive:** **no** — the prepositional passive fails in both senses: ✗ *the exit was made for*, ✗ *a long day is made for by two connecting flights*.

To head straight toward something, or to be the thing that produces a certain result.

**1. Head toward, move in the direction of.** Physical movement with a destination in mind, and usually in a hurry — you *make for* an exit, a door, higher ground. (направляться к, устремляться к)

1. As soon as the credits rolled she *made for* the exit.
2. The dog *made* straight *for* the water.
3. We *made for* higher ground as soon as the river came up.

**2. Produce a result, contribute to.** The subject is a situation rather than a person, and what follows is the outcome: *makes for a long day*, *makes for good television*. (способствовать, приводить к, обеспечивать)

4. Two connecting flights *make for* a very long day.
5. Rain in the fourth quarter *made for* a sloppy game.
6. Cheap parts *make for* expensive repairs later.

**See also:** [make off](#make-off), [run for](#run-for)

### make of

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmeɪk əv/ &middot; *MAKE-of* &middot; stress on the verb; *of* is unstressed and said /əv/
**Русский:** понимать, истолковывать
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb, in the frame *make something of something*
**Separable:** no — *of* stays glued to its object, but the frame always puts a word in front of it: *make nothing of it*, *what do you make of her?* ✗ *make of it nothing*.
**Transitive:** yes, and doubly so — it needs both an object and an *of* phrase
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic but mental, the act of forming or voicing an opinion. No stative or linking use; the frame needs both a *what / much / nothing* object and an *of* phrase.
**Passive:** **restricted** — natural only in sense 2, where the quantity is promoted: *a great deal was made of one careless sentence*. Sense 1 has no passive at all: ✗ *what is made of the new manager?*

To form an opinion about something, or to treat it as significant.

**1. Interpret, form an opinion.** Nearly always a question or a negative — *what do you make of…*, *I don't know what to make of…* You cannot use it to report an opinion you already hold. (понимать, думать о, истолковывать)

1. What do you *make of* the new manager?
2. I didn't know what to *make of* her answer.
3. Nobody could *make* much *of* the handwriting on the envelope.
4. The jury will *make* what it likes *of* his silence.

**2. Make something of it — treat as an issue.** Confrontational in the first shape (*want to make something of it?*) and simply "give weight to" in *make much of*. (раздувать, придавать значение; «имеешь что-то против?»)

5. "You're late." "You want to *make* something *of* it?"
6. The papers *made* a great deal *of* one careless sentence.
7. He never *made* anything *of* the fact that he had paid for it himself.

**See also:** [make out](#make-out), [read into](#read-into)

### make off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈɔf/ &middot; *make-OFF* &middot; the particle carries the stress, not the verb
**Русский:** удирать, скрываться, уносить ноги
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** nothing to separate — the verb takes no object, so no word can come between *make* and *off*.
**Transitive:** no, never takes an object; for the thing carried away use [make off with](#make-off-with)
**Register:** everyday — very common in news writing about crime
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, a verb of hurried escape, always with a human or animal subject. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, so there is nothing to promote: ✗ *was made off*, ✗ *the alley was made off down*.

To leave in a hurry, especially in order to escape.

1. The two of them *made off* before anyone thought to call the police.
2. He *made off* down the alley with the alarm still ringing.
3. The raccoons *make off* the second the porch light comes on.
4. Whoever hit the mailbox had *made off* by the time we got outside.
5. She *made off* in the middle of the ceremony and nobody noticed until dessert.

**See also:** [make off with](#make-off-with), [run away](#run-away), [run off](#run-off)

### make off with

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈɔf wɪθ/ &middot; *make-OFF-with* &middot; the stress falls on the middle particle
**Русский:** украсть и скрыться, унести
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the object always follows the whole phrase, pronoun or not. ✗ *made the bike off with*, ✗ *made it off with*; it is *made off with it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic and agentive: taking something and fleeing with it. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **restricted** — the three-part passive is marginal in American English: ?*my bike was made off with*, ✗ *it was made off with by a kid*. Say *was stolen* instead.

To steal something and get away with it.

1. Someone *made off with* my bike right off the porch.
2. Two men *made off with* the register and a box of receipts.
3. The gulls *made off with* half of my sandwich.
4. He left the laptop on the seat and a kid *made off with* it.
5. A former partner had *made off with* most of the deposits before the audit.

**See also:** [make off](#make-off), [run off with](#run-off-with), [run away with](#run-away-with)

### make out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈaʊt/ &middot; *make-OUT* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *make it OUT*, not *MAKE it out*
**Русский:** разобрать, различить; справляться; выписывать (чек); целоваться
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *make out the address* or *make the address out*, and with a pronoun the object **must** split the pair: *make it out*, never ✗ *make out it*. Senses 2 and 5 take no object at all, so nothing separates there.
**Transitive:** either, depending on the sense
**Register:** everyday; the kissing sense is informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in senses 2–5 (faring, writing out a check, claiming, kissing). Sense 1 is a **perception** verb close to stative: it lives with *can / could* and avoids the progressive, ✗ *I am making out a figure*.
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *the signature could barely be made out*, *the check was made out to the county clerk*, *he was made out to be some kind of expert*. Intransitive senses 2 and 5 have none: ✗ *was made out at the DMV*.

To manage to see, hear, or understand something; and, in other senses, to fare, to write out, to claim, or to kiss.

**1. Discern, manage to see or hear.** Almost always with *can/could*, and very often negative — the thing is at the edge of what your eyes or ears can handle. (разобрать, различить, расслышать)

1. I could just *make out* a figure at the end of the dock.
2. The signature was so faded that I couldn't *make* it *out*.
3. Read the sign and tell me whether you can *make* the street name *out*.

**2. Manage, fare, get on.** Intransitive and thoroughly American, usually in a question about how something went. (справляться, преуспевать)

4. How did you *make out* at the DMV?
5. Their first store *made out* better than anyone expected.

**3. Write out a check or a form.** The money sense; the payee follows *to*. (выписывать (чек, документ))

6. *Make* the check *out* to the county clerk.
7. She *made* the receipt *out* for the full amount.

**4. Claim, represent as.** With *that* or with *out to be*, and always with a hint that the claim is false. (изображать, выставлять (кем-л.))

8. He *makes* himself *out* to be some kind of expert.
9. The article *made out* that the whole delay was the mayor's fault.

**5. Kiss and grope.** Informal American, intransitive, often *make out with someone*. Not used in careful writing. (целоваться, обжиматься)

10. They were *making out* in the back row of the theater.
11. She admitted she had *made out* with him at the party.

**See also:** [make of](#make-of), [make up](#make-up), [read off](#read-off), [put together](#put-together)

### make over

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈoʊvɚ/ &middot; *make-OH-ver* &middot; the particle carries the stress; the noun *a makeover* moves it to the front, *MAKE-oh-ver*
**Русский:** переделать, преобразить; переписать (имущество на кого-л.)
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *make over the basement* or *make the basement over*, and a pronoun **must** go in the middle: *make it over*, never ✗ *make over it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday; the property sense is formal and legal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in both senses, the physical transformation and the legal transfer of property. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **yes**, and common in both — *the basement was made over into a rec room*; *the farm had been made over to a trust years before he died*.

To change how something looks from top to bottom, or to sign property over to somebody.

**1. Transform, redo completely.** Rooms, wardrobes, images, brands. The noun *makeover* is heard more often than the verb. (переделать, преобразить, переоборудовать)

1. They *made over* the basement into a rec room.
2. We *made* the whole front porch *over* in one weekend.
3. The agency *made* him *over* before the first debate.

**2. Transfer ownership to.** Legal and formal, always with *to*. (переписать, передать (имущество))

4. She *made over* the deed to her daughter in 1998.
5. The farm had been *made over* to a trust years before he died.
6. He *made* the whole account *over* to his brother and moved to Tucson.

**See also:** [make up](#make-up), [put up](#put-up)

### make up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈʌp/ &middot; *make-UP* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *make it UP*; the noun *makeup* is stressed on the front, *MAKE-up*
**Русский:** выдумывать; составлять; мириться; навёрстывать
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes in most senses — *make up a story* or *make the story up*, and a pronoun **must** separate them: *make it up*, never ✗ *make up it*. Two exceptions worth memorizing: the "constitute" sense never separates (*women make up half the class*, ✗ *make half the class up*), and the "reconcile" sense has no object at all.
**Transitive:** either, depending on the sense
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in senses 1 and 3–6 (inventing, reconciling, catching up, applying cosmetics, preparing). Sense 2, 'constitute', is **stative** and refuses the progressive: ✗ *volunteers are making up half the staff*.
**Passive:** **restricted** — fine in the dynamic senses: *the story was made up on the drive over*, *the prescription was made up in ten minutes*, *she was made up for the stage lights*. Sense 2 passivizes only in the fixed *be made up of* — *the district is made up of six counties*, never ✗ *made up by six counties* — and sense 3 is intransitive: ✗ *she was made up with*.

To invent something, to form a whole, to settle a quarrel, or to make good on what was missed.

**1. Invent, fabricate.** Stories, excuses, names, rules. The implication is that none of it is true. (выдумывать, сочинять, придумывать)

1. He *made up* a story about a flat tire.
2. She *made* the whole thing *up* on the drive over.
3. Don't *make* it *up* — just say you forgot.

**2. Constitute, form.** Statistical and structural: the parts are the subject and the whole follows. This sense never separates. (составлять, образовывать)

4. Volunteers *make up* almost half of the staff.
5. Six counties *make up* the district.

**3. Reconcile after a quarrel.** Intransitive, often *make up with someone*. (мириться, помириться)

6. They fought all week and *made up* on Sunday.
7. She has never *made up* with her sister.

**4. Make good on what was missed.** Hours, work, a test, lost ground. (навёрстывать, отрабатывать, восполнять)

8. I'll *make up* the hours on Saturday.
9. He *made* the missed quiz *up* after school.
10. The runner *made* it *up* on the last lap.

**5. Apply cosmetics.** Usually reflexive or passive — *made up*, *make yourself up*. (краситься, гримировать)

11. She was *made up* for the stage lights, not for daylight.

**6. Prepare, put together.** Beds, prescriptions, lists, packages. (готовить, составлять, приготовлять)

12. The pharmacy *made up* the prescription in ten minutes.
13. Could you *make* the guest bed *up* before they get here?

**See also:** [make up for](#make-up-for), [make out](#make-out), [make over](#make-over), [put together](#put-together)

### make up for

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈʌp fɔr/ &middot; *make-UP-for* &middot; the stress falls on the middle particle
**Русский:** компенсировать, возмещать, навёрстывать
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the object follows the whole phrase. ✗ *make lost time up for*, ✗ *make it up for*; it is *make up for it*. Do not confuse it with the four-word *make it up to someone*, where *it* really does sit in the middle: *I'll make it up to you*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **both** — action when a person compensates (*she worked doubles to make up for the time off*), **stative** when a thing simply offsets another, where the progressive is odd: *the view makes up for the stairs*, ✗ *the view is making up for the stairs*.
**Passive:** **restricted** — stiff at best, and only with the loss as subject: ?*the lost time will be made up for*. ✗ *the stairs are made up for by the view*.

To offset something bad or missing with something good.

1. The view *makes up for* the stairs.
2. No bonus is going to *make up for* three lost weekends.
3. She worked doubles all month to *make up for* the time off.
4. Nothing *makes up for* it, but the apology helped.
5. Good coffee *made up for* a thin breakfast.
6. He has been trying to *make up for* that one remark ever since.

**See also:** [make up](#make-up), [pay back](#pay-back), [pay off](#pay-off)

### make up to

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk ˈʌp tu/ &middot; *make-UP-to* &middot; the stress falls on the middle particle
**Русский:** заискивать перед, подлизываться к
**Base verb:** [make](07-plus-d.md#make) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the person follows the whole phrase: *making up to the boss*, *making up to him*. ✗ *make the boss up to*.
**Transitive:** yes, and the object is always a person
**Register:** British — an American would say *suck up to* or *butter up*. Note that American *make it up to someone* looks similar but means to compensate them, which is a different verb entirely.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic and agentive, flattering somebody for gain; the object is always a person. British, where American English says *suck up to* or *butter up*.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *the department head was made up to*, ✗ *she was made up to by the neighbors*. The prepositional passive is simply unavailable here.

To flatter somebody in order to get something out of them.

1. He spent the whole term *making up to* the department head.
2. She was *making up to* the neighbors long before she asked about the fence.
3. You can tell he wants something by the way he *makes up to* her.
4. Nobody who *made up to* the old man was in the will.
5. Stop *making up to* me and just ask.

**See also:** [make up for](#make-up-for), [pay off](#pay-off)

### meet up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmit ˈʌp/ &middot; *meet-UP* &middot; the particle carries the stress; the noun *a meetup* moves it forward, *MEET-up*
**Русский:** встретиться, пересечься
**Base verb:** [meet](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#meet) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** nothing to separate — no object follows, so nothing can sit between *meet* and *up*. For the person you are meeting, use [meet up with](#meet-up-with).
**Transitive:** no, takes no object
**Register:** everyday — casual and social; a business appointment is a *meeting*, not a *meetup*
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, an arranged social get-together. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, so no passive exists: ✗ *was met up at the taco place*. Use plain *meet* if you need one: *we were met at the gate*.

To get together with somebody by arrangement, usually informally.

1. Let's *meet up* after work.
2. We *met up* at the taco place on Third.
3. The two groups *meet up* every spring in Denver.
4. They had already *met up* by the time I got the text.
5. If the flight lands early we can *meet up* before the game.

**See also:** [meet up with](#meet-up-with), [meet with](#meet-with), [run into](#run-into)

### meet up with

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmit ˈʌp wɪθ/ &middot; *meet-UP-with* &middot; the stress falls on the middle particle
**Русский:** встретиться с, пересечься с
**Base verb:** [meet](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#meet) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the person follows the whole phrase. ✗ *met my cousin up with*, ✗ *met him up with*; it is *met up with him*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday — casual, and only for planned meetings; a chance encounter is [run into](#run-into)
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, a planned joining-up with somebody; never a chance encounter, which is *run into*.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *my cousin was met up with in Chicago*, ✗ *they had been met up with twice*. Recast with plain *meet*: *she was met at baggage claim*.

To join somebody somewhere by arrangement.

1. I'm going to *meet up with* my cousin in Chicago.
2. She *met up with* the rest of the crew at the trailhead.
3. We'll *meet up with* you after the second set.
4. He had *met up with* them twice that week already.
5. Text me when you land and I'll *meet up with* you at baggage claim.

**See also:** [meet up](#meet-up), [meet with](#meet-with), [run into](#run-into)

### meet with

**Pronunciation:** /ˈmit wɪθ/ &middot; *MEET-with* &middot; a preposition, so the stress stays on the verb
**Русский:** встречаться с (по делу); встретить, натолкнуться на (одобрение, сопротивление)
**Base verb:** [meet](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#meet) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb
**Separable:** no — *with* never leaves its object: *met with the union*, *met with them*. ✗ *met the union with*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday in American English; British English drops the *with* for the appointment sense and simply says *meet the client*
**Verb type:** **both** — action in sense 1, an agentive scheduled meeting (*the governor met with tribal leaders*). Sense 2 is non-agentive and near-stative: the subject undergoes a reception rather than doing anything.
**Passive:** **no** in either sense — ✗ *tribal leaders were met with on Monday*, ✗ *stiff resistance was met with by the proposal*. Do not be fooled by *the proposal was met with resistance*: that is the passive of plain transitive *meet* plus a *with* phrase, not of this verb.

To hold a formal meeting with somebody, or to receive a particular kind of response.

**1. Hold a meeting with.** The standard American business and political use, where the meeting is scheduled and has a purpose. (встречаться с, проводить встречу с)

1. The governor *met with* tribal leaders on Monday.
2. She *meets with* her advisor every other Thursday.
3. We have *met with* three contractors and liked none of them.

**2. Receive, be greeted by (a response).** A fixed set of nouns follows: *approval*, *resistance*, *opposition*, *success*, *an accident*. The subject is a plan or a person, and the noun is what came back. (встретить, натолкнуться на; потерпеть (неудачу))

4. The proposal *met with* stiff resistance from the neighbors.
5. His first novel *met with* almost no success.
6. Her plan finally *met with* the board's approval.
7. Should the shipment *meet with* an accident, call the insurer that day.

**See also:** [meet up with](#meet-up-with), [run into](#run-into), [put forward](#put-forward)

### pay back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpeɪ ˈbæk/ &middot; *pay-BACK* &middot; the particle carries the stress; the noun *payback* moves it forward, *PAY-back*
**Русский:** вернуть долг, отдать деньги; отомстить, отплатить
**Base verb:** [pay](07-plus-d.md#pay) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *pay back the loan* or *pay the loan back*, and a pronoun **must** go in the middle: *pay it back*, *pay me back*, never ✗ *pay back it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object — either the money or the person
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in both senses, repaying money and getting even. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **yes**, with either object promoted — *the loan was paid back two years early*; *she was paid back for every remark she ever made*.

To return money you owe, or to get even with somebody.

**1. Repay money.** The object can be the sum, the loan, or the person you owe. (вернуть долг, отдать деньги)

1. I'll *pay back* the forty dollars on Friday.
2. She *paid* the loan *back* two years early.
3. *Pay* me *back* whenever you can.

**2. Get even with somebody.** The object is a person, and *for* introduces the offense. Often in the noun *payback*. (отомстить, отплатить)

4. He *paid* her *back* for every remark she ever made about his driving.
5. They *paid* the whole department *back* by leaking the memo.
6. She swore she would *pay* him *back* someday.

**See also:** [pay off](#pay-off), [pay up](#pay-up), [make up for](#make-up-for)

### pay down

**Pronunciation:** /ˌpeɪ ˈdaʊn/ &middot; *pay-DOWN* &middot; the particle carries the stress
**Русский:** погашать, выплачивать (часть долга)
**Base verb:** [pay](07-plus-d.md#pay) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *pay down the mortgage* or *pay the mortgage down*, and a pronoun **must** split the pair: *pay it down*, never ✗ *pay down it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday — American money talk; a British paper is likelier to write *pay off* or *reduce*
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, and gradual by definition, so the progressive is natural: *we're paying the card down before the rate resets*.
**Passive:** **yes** — *the balance was paid down to under a thousand*, *half of the pension shortfall has been paid down*.

To reduce a debt by paying part of it, without necessarily clearing it.

1. We're trying to *pay down* the credit card before the rate resets.
2. She *paid* the balance *down* to under a thousand.
3. Every extra check *pays* it *down* a little faster.
4. The city has *paid down* half of its pension shortfall.
5. He *paid* the principal *down* for six years and then refinanced.

**See also:** [pay off](#pay-off), [pay back](#pay-back), [run up](#run-up)

### pay for

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪ fɔr/ &middot; *PAY-for* &middot; a preposition, so the stress stays on the verb and *for* reduces to /fɚ/
**Русский:** платить за; поплатиться за
**Base verb:** [pay](07-plus-d.md#pay) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb
**Separable:** no — *for* keeps its object: *paid for the tickets*, *paid for them*. ✗ *paid the tickets for*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday

**Русский:** сбежать с (кем-л.); унести, украсть; легко выиграть; захлестнуть (о чувствах)
**Base verb:** [run](09-participle-equals-base.md#run) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the object follows the whole phrase: *ran away with the money*, *ran away with it*. ✗ *ran the money away with*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action — dynamic** in all four senses. Sense 4 is the odd one: the feeling is the subject and the person the object — *don't let your imagination run away with you*.
**Passive:** **restricted**, and marginal in practice: ✗ *the whole tip jar was run away with*, ✗ *he was run away with by a bass player*. Sports writing will occasionally allow *the game was run away with in the third quarter*, but the active is the safe choice.

To leave with somebody or something you were not entitled to, to win something easily, or, of a feeling, to take charge of a person.

**1. Elope with, leave with somebody.** (сбежать с, убежать с)

1. She *ran away with* a bass player from Tulsa.

**2. Take and go, steal.** (унести, украсть, скрыться с)

2. Somebody *ran away with* the whole tip jar.
3. The tide *ran away with* both of our chairs.

**3. Win easily.** Sports and awards writing: the margin was never in doubt. (легко выиграть, унести (победу))

4. The visiting team *ran away with* it in the third quarter.
5. Her short film *ran away with* every prize that year.

**4. Of a feeling, to take over.** The fixed shape is *let your imagination / your temper run away with you*. (захлестнуть, взять верх над)

6. Don't let your imagination *run away with* you.

**Separable:** yes — *run down the list* or *run the list down*, and a pronoun **must** go in the middle: *run it down*, never ✗ *run down it*. Sense 3 has no object at all.
**Transitive:** either, depending on the sense
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action — dynamic** throughout: hitting somebody with a vehicle, belittling them, tracking a thing down, or going through a list. Sense 3 (*the battery had run down*) is a dynamic change of state with the battery as subject.
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses, and common in senses 1 and 4: *two cyclists were run down on the shoulder*, *the original invoice was finally run down*, *she was run down by her own department*. **No** passive of sense 3: ✗ *the battery was run down overnight* can only mean somebody drained it.

To knock somebody down with a vehicle, to belittle them, to lose power, to track something down, or to go through a list.

2. He *ran* the mailbox *down* backing out.

**2. Belittle, criticize.** The object is a person or their work. (ругать, принижать, охаивать)

3. He *runs* everybody *down* and wonders why nobody calls.
4. She never *ran* her old firm *down* in an interview.

**3. Lose power, wind down.** Intransitive, of batteries, clocks, and businesses. (разряжаться, садиться; приходить в упадок)

5. The battery had *run down* overnight.
6. The plant slowly *ran down* through the eighties.

**4. Track down, locate.** A source, a lead, a spare part. (разыскать, отследить)

7. It took a week to *run down* the original invoice.
8. She *ran* the rumor *down* to a single email.

**5. Go through item by item.** Usually a list, an agenda, or the numbers; the noun *rundown* is the summary itself. (перечислить, пробежаться по (списку))

9. Let me *run down* what we know so far.
10. He *ran* the schedule *down* twice and still forgot Thursday.
11. *Run* it *down* for me one more time — I lost the thread.

**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object after *into*
**Register:** everyday

3. The truck *ran into* a guardrail in the fog.
4. He *ran into* the doorframe in the dark.

**3. Hit a problem.** Trouble, delays, resistance, a bug. (столкнуться с, наткнуться на (проблему))

5. We have *run into* the same problem every quarter.
6. If you *run into* trouble, call the desk.

**4. Reach a figure.** Of costs and casualties: *run into the millions*. (достигать, доходить до (суммы))

7. Repairs could *run into* six figures.

**Register:** everyday

To hit something with a vehicle, to go past a time limit, to review something quickly, or to overflow.

2. He *ran* the hose *over* with the mower.
3. She braked hard so she wouldn't *run* it *over*.

4. The meeting *ran over* by twenty minutes.
5. Every panel he chairs *runs over*.

**3. Review quickly.** Prepositional, and inseparable: the notes, the agenda, the plan. (бегло просмотреть, пройтись по)

6. Let's *run over* the agenda one more time.
7. She *ran over* it twice on the way in.

**4. Overflow.** Of a tub, a gutter, or a cup. (перелиться через край)

8. The sink *ran over* while she was on the phone.

Sense 3, *a streak of stubbornness runs through that family*, is **stative**: it describes a standing property, not an event, and rejects the progressive (✗ *is running through that family*).
**Passive:** **yes** in senses 1 and 4, and the entry's own example is one: *he was run through in the second act*, *the safety checks were run through twice*. Sense 2 is acceptable but stiff (?*the whole advance was run through in four months*), and sense 3 has no passive: ✗ *that family is run through by a streak of stubbornness*.

To go over something from start to finish, to spend money fast, or, of a theme, to be present all the way along.

**1. Rehearse, go over.** Lines, a routine, a checklist, a plan. The noun *a run-through* is the rehearsal itself. (прогнать, отрепетировать, пройтись по)

1. Let's *run through* it once before they get here.
2. The crew *ran through* the safety checks twice.
3. She *runs through* her opening in the car every morning.

**2. Spend or use up quickly.** Money, savings, an inheritance, a box of tissues. (промотать, быстро израсходовать)

4. He *ran through* the whole advance in four months.
5. They *had run through* two boxes of paper by noon.

**3. Be present throughout.** Of a theme, a vein, a streak. (проходить через, пронизывать)

6. A streak of stubbornness *runs through* that entire family.

**4. Pierce with a blade.** Archaic, and now only in historical writing. (проткнуть, пронзить)

7. He was *run through* in the second act and died beautifully.

**See also:** [run over](#run-over), [read through](#read-through), [run by](#run-by)

### run up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌrʌn ˈʌp/ &middot; *run-UP* &middot; the particle carries the stress; the noun *the run-up* moves it forward, *RUN-up*
**Русский:** накопить (счета, долги); поднять (флаг); быстро сшить
**Base verb:** [run](09-participle-equals-base.md#run) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *run up a bill* or *run a bill up*, and a pronoun **must** go in the middle: *run it up*, never ✗ *run up it*. Compare [run up against](#run-up-against), which never separates at all.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action — dynamic** in all three senses — piling up charges, hoisting a flag, and sewing something quickly. Sense 1 is gradual and takes the progressive well: *he was running up a tab all night*.
**Passive:** **yes**, and idiomatic in every transitive sense: *a four-hundred-dollar tab was run up in one evening*, *the flag is run up at seven every morning*, *the whole dress was run up in an afternoon*.

To accumulate a large amount of something owed, to hoist something, or to sew something quickly.

**1. Accumulate charges or debt.** Bills, tabs, legal fees, mileage. (накопить, набрать (счета, долги))

1. He *ran up* a four-hundred-dollar tab.
2. They *ran* enormous legal bills *up* fighting the permit.
3. She *ran* it *up* on a card she was supposed to have cut in half.

**2. Hoist.** A flag, a sail. (поднять (флаг, парус))

4. They *run* the flag *up* at seven every morning.

**3. Sew quickly.** Slightly dated, and more common in British English. (быстро сшить, смастерить)

5. Her grandmother could *run up* a dress in an afternoon.

**4. Run up to — the approach to an event.** In the noun *the run-up to* an election or a holiday. (преддверие, период перед)

6. Prices climb in the *run-up* to the holidays.

**See also:** [run up against](#run-up-against), [pay down](#pay-down), [ring up](#ring-up)

### run up against

**Pronunciation:** /ˌrʌn ˌʌp əˈɡɛnst/ &middot; *run-up-uh-GENST* &middot; the last particle carries the stress
**Русский:** столкнуться с (препятствием), упереться в
**Base verb:** [run](09-participle-equals-base.md#run) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the obstacle follows the whole phrase: *ran up against the zoning board*, *ran up against it*. ✗ *ran the zoning board up against*. Contrast [run up](#run-up), which separates freely: *ran a bill up*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action — dynamic** — meeting an obstacle at a point in time, with the project or the person as subject: *the project ran up against the zoning board in its first month*.
**Passive:** **no** — the obstacle stays where it is: ✗ *the zoning board was run up against by the project*, ✗ *the same objection was run up against at every meeting*.

To meet an obstacle you cannot simply go around.

1. The project *ran up against* the zoning board in its first month.
2. She *ran up against* the same objection at every meeting.
3. We *ran up against* a wall of paperwork.
4. Anybody who tries this *runs up against* the same math.
5. They had *run up against* it before and knew what was coming.

**See also:** [run into](#run-into), [run up](#run-up), [run across](#run-across)

### run with

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrʌn wɪθ/ &middot; *RUN-with* &middot; a preposition, so the stress stays on the verb
**Русский:** взяться за, развить (идею) и довести до конца
**Base verb:** [run](09-participle-equals-base.md#run) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb
**Separable:** no — *with* keeps its object: *run with the idea*, *run with it*. ✗ *run the idea with*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object after *with*
**Register:** informal — American workplace speech, usually approving
**Verb type:** **action — dynamic** — taking an idea and developing it, usually over a stretch of time: *she was running with the suggestion before anyone approved it*.
**Passive:** **no** — the idea cannot be promoted: ✗ *the suggestion was run with by her*, ✗ *the joke was run with by somebody in marketing*. Keep it active, or say *the suggestion was taken up*.

To take an idea and develop it on your own without waiting for more instruction.

1. It's a rough draft — take it and *run with* it.
2. She *ran with* the suggestion and had a prototype by Friday.
3. They gave him one sentence of direction and he *ran with* it.
4. Somebody in marketing *ran with* the joke and put it on a billboard.
5. We can *run with* the cheaper version if the budget stays flat.
