# Core verbs: motion and travel

> The everyday regular verbs of motion and travel — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### accelerate

**Pronunciation:** /əkˈsɛləreɪt/ &middot; *uhk-SEL-uh-rayt*
**Русский:** ускорять, ускорить; разгоняться
**Forms:** *accelerate* &middot; *accelerated* &middot; *accelerated*
**Third person:** *accelerates* &middot; **-ing form:** *accelerating*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (accelerate the car), intransitive (the train accelerated)

To go faster, or to make something go faster.

1. This car *accelerates* from zero to sixty in six seconds.
2. She *accelerated* to pass the truck.
3. He was *accelerating* when a deer stepped onto the road.
