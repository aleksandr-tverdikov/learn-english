# Past = participle, add -d

> The past and participle add a -d to the unchanged base.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

These verbs use one form for both the past and the participle, and they build it by adding a -d to the base: *pay* → *paid* → *paid*, *lay* → *laid* → *laid*, *have* → *had* → *had*. The spelling adjusts a little where the base ends in -ay or -ake — the y becomes i (*say* → *said*, *mislay* → *mislaid*), and the k drops out of *make* → *made* — but there is only ever one form to learn past the base. Most of the group is just four verbs (*have*, *say*, *make*, *pay*, plus *lay*) wearing a prefix, so *repay*, *overlay*, *gainsay* and *unmake* cost you nothing extra once the base verb is in place. (In the original Russian list this is the group отличаются от 1-й добавлением "d".)

The one thing to watch is sound, not spelling: *said* rhymes with *bed*, not with *paid*.

### belay

**Pronunciation:** /bɪˈleɪ/ &middot; *bih-LAY*
**Русский:** мор. закрепить (канат); завёртывать (снасть)
**Forms:** *belay* /bɪˈleɪ/ &middot; *belayed* /bɪˈleɪd/ &middot; *belayed* /bɪˈleɪd/ — the older nautical *belaid* /bɪˈleɪd/ is the form the source list records
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** rare — a climbing and sailing term; outside those trades Americans meet it only in the Navy's *Belay that!*
**Pattern:** ay — ayed — ayed &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *belays* &middot; **-ing form:** *belaying*
**Contrast:** This is the odd one out among the -lay verbs here. *Lay*, *mislay* and *overlay* all give *-laid*, but *belay* went regular centuries ago: modern American English says *belayed*, and every climbing manual prints it that way. *Belaid* survives only in old sailing texts. Note also the Navy idiom *Belay that!*, meaning "cancel that" — it has no past tense at all.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic throughout: managing a rope (*belay your partner*), making a line fast (*belay the halyard*), and the Navy imperative *Belay that!*, which exists only as a command.
**Transitivity:** **both** — transitive with a climber or a line as object (*she belayed me on every pitch*), intransitive when the partner is understood (*he has belayed for beginners for six years*).
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *I was belayed by my partner on every pitch*; *the halyard is belayed to a pin on the rail*. The imperative *Belay that!* has no passive.

To hold and control a climbing rope for a partner, or, at sea, to make a line fast around a cleat — and in Navy usage, to cancel an order.

**1. Belay a climber.** The everyday modern sense: to manage the rope so that a partner's fall is caught. Fully regular in American English — *belayed*, never the old ✗ *belaid*. (страховать (в альпинизме))

1. Always *belay* your partner before they leave the ground.
2. She *belayed* me on every pitch of the route.
3. He *has belayed* for beginners at the gym for six years.
4. By noon they *had belayed* each other up four routes.

**2. Make a rope fast (nautical).** At sea: to secure a line by winding it around a cleat or a belaying pin. This is the original sense, and the archaic past *belaid* belongs to it. (закреплять (канат, снасть))

5. The bosun *belays* the halyard to a pin on the rail.
6. They *belayed* the line and let the sail hold.
7. The crew *had belayed* every sheet before the squall hit.

**3. Belay that! — cancel an order.** Navy and Coast Guard usage, and it exists only as an imperative — there is no past tense in practice. *Belay that* means disregard what was just said. (отставить!, отменить (приказ))

8. *Belay* that — we're taking the other truck.
9. *Belay* my last transmission; the coordinates were wrong.

**See also:** [lay](#lay), [mislay](#mislay)

### clip

**Pronunciation:** /klɪp/ &middot; *KLIP*
**Русский:** стричь; подрезать
**Forms:** *clip* /klɪp/ &middot; *clipped* /klɪpt/ &middot; *clipped* /klɪpt/ — the archaic participle *clipt* /klɪpt/ is what the source list records
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — clipping coupons, nails, or a curb is ordinary American conversation
**Pattern:** p — pped — pped &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *clips* &middot; **-ing form:** *clipping* (the p doubles)
**Contrast:** *Clip* is a fully regular verb in American English: *clipped* for both the past and the participle. *Clipt* is an old poetic spelling — Shakespeare and hymn books have it — and it sounds exactly the same, so nothing is lost by ignoring it. Do not write ✗ *He has clipt the hedge* today.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in every sense: cutting (*clip the dog*), snipping out of print (*clip a coupon*), fastening (*clip the badge on*), striking in passing (*clip the curb*), and figurative curtailing (*clip his wings*).
**Transitivity:** **transitive** in all five senses, and the object is normally obligatory — ✗ *The truck clipped* is incomplete. The fastening sense adds a goal phrase: *clip A to B*.
**Passive:** **yes** — *his hair has been clipped the same way since 1998*; *a mic was clipped to his collar*; *his wings have been clipped since the merger*.

To cut something short with scissors or clippers, to cut a piece out of print, to fasten with a clip, or to strike something a glancing blow.

**1. Cut with scissors or clippers.** The core sense: trimming hair, nails, a hedge, or an animal's coat. Regular in modern usage; the poetic participle *clipt* is dead. (стричь, подрезать)

1. I *clip* the dog myself to save eighty dollars.
2. She *clipped* her nails over the trash can.
3. The barber *has clipped* his hair the same way since 1998.
4. We *had clipped* the hedge back before the storm came through.

**2. Cut something out of print.** To snip an article, coupon, or photo out of printed material — still alive in *clip coupons*. Extended now to saving a short piece of video or audio. (вырезать (из газеты, журнала))

5. My grandmother still *clips* recipes out of the Sunday paper.
6. He *clipped* the obituary and mailed it to me.
7. Fans *have clipped* the best thirty seconds of the interview.

**3. Fasten with a clip.** To attach one thing to another with a clip or clasp. The pattern is *clip A to B*. (прикреплять, пристёгивать)

8. *Clip* the badge to your jacket before you go in.
9. The sound tech *clipped* a mic to his collar.
10. Someone *had clipped* the pages together in the wrong order.

**4. Hit with a glancing blow.** To strike something in passing, usually by accident and usually at speed. Common in driving and in contact sports. (задеть, зацепить (вскользь))

11. If you *clip* the curb at that angle you'll blow a tire.
12. The truck *clipped* our mirror and kept going.
13. He *has clipped* the same doorframe twice this month.

**5. Cut short, curtail.** Figurative: to reduce or limit. Fixed in *clip someone's wings*, meaning restrict their freedom, and in *clipped speech*, meaning terse and brisk. (укорачивать, урезать, ограничивать)

14. The new rules *clip* the mayor's authority considerably.
15. Budget cuts *clipped* the program back to two days a week.
16. The board *has clipped* his wings since the merger.

**See also:** [ski](#ski)

### gainsay

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡeɪnˈseɪ/ &middot; *gayn-SAY*
**Русский:** отрицать; противоречить
**Forms:** *gainsay* /ˌɡeɪnˈseɪ/ &middot; *gainsaid* /ˌɡeɪnˈsɛd/ &middot; *gainsaid* /ˌɡeɪnˈsɛd/ — the spelling pronunciation /ˌɡeɪnˈseɪd/ is also accepted for the past and participle
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** rare — formal and nearly frozen in *no one can gainsay it*; almost never produced in speech
**Pattern:** ay — aid — aid &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *gainsays* /ˌɡeɪnˈseɪz/ &middot; **-ing form:** *gainsaying*
**Contrast:** The past follows *said*, so it is normally /ˌɡeɪnˈsɛd/, rhyming with *bed*; the spelling pronunciation /ˌɡeɪnˈseɪd/ is also heard and accepted. The third person, though, keeps the /eɪ/ — *gainsays* is not pronounced like *says* /sɛz/. Note too that this verb is formal and lives almost entirely in negative frames: *no one can gainsay it*, not ✗ *I gainsay you*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, a verb of speech, but so formal and frozen that it barely appears outside negative frames: *no one can gainsay it*, *there is no gainsaying it*.
**Transitivity:** **transitive**, object obligatory — a statement (*gainsay the numbers*) or a person (*gainsay the bishop*). ✗ *Nobody gainsaid* is incomplete.
**Passive:** **restricted** — natural only in the negative frames the verb lives in: *the finding has not been gainsaid in twenty years*. A plain positive passive sounds wrong: ✗ *Every point was gainsaid by the committee.*

To deny or contradict something — formal, and almost always in a negative frame.

**1. Deny, contradict a statement.** Formal and largely written. It lives in negative frames — *no one can gainsay it*, *there is no gainsaying it* — and sounds wrong in a plain positive sentence. (отрицать, оспаривать)

1. No one can *gainsay* the numbers in her report.
2. The committee *gainsaid* every point he made.
3. Nobody *has gainsaid* the finding in twenty years.

**2. Oppose, speak against someone.** With a person or an authority as the object: to speak in open opposition to them. Archaic in tone and rare outside legal or literary prose. (противоречить, перечить (кому-л.))

4. Few of his officers dared *gainsay* him.
5. She *gainsaid* the bishop in open council.
6. Until last week no one *had gainsaid* the old theory.

**See also:** [say](#say), [unsay](#unsay)

### have

**Pronunciation:** /hæv/ &middot; *HAV*
**Русский:** иметь, обладать
**Forms:** *have* /hæv/ &middot; *had* /hæd/ &middot; *had* /hæd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking, and the auxiliary behind every perfect tense
**Pattern:** ave — ad — ad &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *has* /hæz/ (not ✗ *haves*) &middot; **-ing form:** *having* (drop the e)
**Contrast:** *Have* is both a full verb and the auxiliary that builds every perfect tense, so the two stack: *I have had three cups of coffee*, *she had had enough*. In speech the auxiliary weakens to /əv/, which sounds exactly like *of* — that is why people write ✗ *would of gone*. It is always *would have gone* or *would've gone*. Also watch the contraction *'d*, which can be either *had* or *would*: *I'd finished* is *had*, *I'd finish* is *would*.
**Verb type:** **stative** in the core senses — possession (*we have two cars*), experience (*I had a headache*), obligation (*have to*) — and there it rejects the progressive: ✗ *I am having two cars*. **Dynamic** in the eat/drink, hold-an-event, give-birth and causative senses, where the progressive is fine: *we're having lunch*, *she's having a baby*. Also the **auxiliary** behind every perfect tense (*they have finished*).
**Transitivity:** **transitive** in every full-verb sense, object obligatory — ✗ *We have* is incomplete. The causative takes object + complement: *have the roof replaced*, *have the office send you the file*. Never ditransitive. As auxiliary it takes no object at all, only a participle.
**Passive:** **no** for the stative senses — this is the classic learner trap: ✗ *Two cars are had by us*, ✗ *A headache was had by me*, ✗ *A meeting is had every Monday*. The auxiliary has no passive either. The one live passive is the fixed idiom *be had* 'be cheated': *if you paid full price, you have been had*.

To possess or hold something; to eat, experience, receive, or arrange something; and, as an auxiliary, to build every perfect tense in English.

**1. Possess, own.** The basic full-verb sense. In informal American speech it usually appears as *have got*: *I've got two brothers* means the same as *I have two brothers*. (иметь, обладать)

1. We *have* two cars and no garage.
2. They *had* a house on the lake before the divorce.
3. She *has had* that jacket since college.

**2. Auxiliary of the perfect tenses.** *Have/has* + participle makes the present perfect, *had* + participle the past perfect. In speech it weakens to /əv/, which is why people misspell it ✗ *would of gone* for *would have gone*. (вспомогательный глагол перфекта)

4. They *have* finished the roof already.
5. By the time we sat down, they *had* eaten.
6. She *has had* three interviews this week.

**3. Eat or drink something.** *Have* replaces *eat* and *drink* in most ordinary sentences: you *have* lunch, a coffee, a beer. (есть, пить, принимать (пищу))

7. I usually *have* oatmeal before a run.
8. We *had* pizza twice last week.
9. He *has had* nothing but coffee all day.

**4. Experience, undergo.** Used of illnesses, feelings, and things that happen to you: *have a headache*, *have a good time*, *have an accident*. (испытывать, переживать)

10. Did you *have* a good flight?
11. I *had* a headache all afternoon.
12. She *has had* a rough year.

**5. Hold an event; take part in one.** *Have a party, a meeting, a conversation, an argument* — the subject organizes it or is in it. (устраивать, проводить)

13. We *have* a staff meeting every Monday at nine.
14. They *had* a barbecue for the whole street.
15. The club *has had* the same fundraiser for twelve years.

**6. Have to — be obliged.** *Have to* + base verb expresses obligation, and it is what English uses in the past, where *must* has no form: *I had to leave*. In fast speech it becomes *hafta*. (быть должным, приходиться)

16. You *have to* show ID at the door.
17. I *had to* leave before the second half.
18. We *have had to* rewrite the contract twice.

**7. Have got — informal possession and obligation.** Spoken American English adds *got*: *I've got a meeting* (possession) and *I've got to go* (obligation, reduced to *gotta*). Present tense only — the past is plain *had* and *had to*. (разг.: иметь; быть должным)

19. I *have got* a meeting at three I can't move.
20. You *have got* to see this.
21. She *has got* two brothers in Chicago.

**8. Have something done — causative.** *Have* + object + participle means you arranged for someone else to do it: *have the car fixed*. With a bare infinitive — *have someone do it* — you are directing the person yourself. (поручать, делать (чужими руками))

22. I'll *have* the office send you the file.
23. We *had* the roof replaced last spring.
24. She *has had* her hair cut short since June.

**9. Give birth to.** Of people and animals alike: *have a baby*, *have puppies*. Neutral, and by far the commonest way to say it. (рожать, родить)

25. She's going to *have* a baby in March.
26. Our cat *had* five kittens under the porch.
27. They *have had* three children in five years.

**10. Receive, get.** Somewhat formal or written: *have news*, *have word from someone*, *may I have your attention*. (получать)

28. May I *have* your attention for a moment?
29. We *had* word from the embassy this morning.
30. I *have had* no answer from them at all.

**11. Allow, tolerate.** Negative only, and emphatic: *I won't have it*, *she wouldn't have any of that*. It means refuse to permit. (терпеть, допускать)

31. I will not *have* shouting in this house.
32. The principal wouldn't *have* any of it.
33. They *had* enough sense not to argue with her about it.

**12. Be had — be cheated.** Usually passive: *you've been had* means you were tricked or overcharged. British *have someone on* means to tease them; Americans say *put someone on*. (быть обманутым, попасться на удочку)

34. If you paid full price for that, you *have been had*.
35. The dealer *had* us on the trade-in value.
36. They realized they *had been had* only after the check cleared.

### inlay

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈleɪ/ &middot; *in-LAY*
**Русский:** вкладывать; выстилать
**Forms:** *inlay* /ɪnˈleɪ/ &middot; *inlaid* /ɪnˈleɪd/ &middot; *inlaid* /ɪnˈleɪd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** rare — a craft and dental term; most Americans know only the noun *an inlay*
**Pattern:** ay — aid — aid &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *inlays* &middot; **-ing form:** *inlaying*
**Contrast:** Stress moves between the verb and the noun: the verb is *in-LAY* /ɪnˈleɪ/, but the noun — a piece of inlaid work, or a dental filling — is *IN-lay* /ˈɪnˌleɪ/. Everything else follows [lay](#lay).
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: setting one material flush into the surface of another (*inlay mother-of-pearl by hand*), including the dental sense (*inlay gold*).
**Transitivity:** **transitive**, object obligatory, in two frames — *inlay A with B* (*inlay the wood with brass*) or *inlay B into A* (*inlay brass into the floor*).
**Passive:** **yes**, and the passive is the commoner form — *every door in the house had been inlaid with ivory*; *brass letters have been inlaid into the lobby floor*. The participle doubles as an adjective: *an inlaid box*.

To set pieces of one material into the surface of another so that the finished surface stays flush.

**1. Set material into a surface.** Woodworking, jewelry, and instrument making: shell, metal, or stone let into wood or silver. The pattern is *inlay A with B* or *inlay B into A*. (инкрустировать, вкладывать)

1. Craftsmen still *inlay* mother-of-pearl by hand in that shop.
2. The luthier *inlaid* tiny stars along the neck of the guitar.
3. Someone *has inlaid* brass letters into the lobby floor.
4. By 1890 the builders *had inlaid* every door in the house with ivory.

**2. Fit a dental inlay.** Dentistry: to fit a shaped filling into a prepared cavity. Most people meet the noun *an inlay*, but the verb is standard in clinical writing. (ставить вкладку (в зуб))

5. This dentist *inlays* gold in cases where a crown would be too much.
6. They *inlaid* porcelain rather than amalgam.
7. By the second visit he *had inlaid* both molars.

### lay

**Pronunciation:** /leɪ/ &middot; *LAY*
**Русский:** класть
**Forms:** *lay* /leɪ/ &middot; *laid* /leɪd/ &middot; *laid* /leɪd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — you lay something down a dozen times a day without noticing
**Pattern:** ay — aid — aid &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *lays* &middot; **-ing form:** *laying*
**Contrast:** This is the classic trap in English. *Lay* takes an object — you lay *something* down — and goes *lay, laid, laid*. *Lie* takes no object — you lie down yourself — and goes *lie, lay, lain*. The collision is that *lay* is also the past of *lie*: ✗ *I laid down for a nap* should be *I lay down for a nap*. And the spelling of the past is *laid*, never ✗ *layed*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in every sense, from putting something down flat to installing (*lay tile*), preparing (*lay a trap*), imposing (*lay blame*), and wagering (*lay fifty dollars*).
**Transitivity:** **transitive** — the object is what separates it from intransitive *lie*: ✗ *I laid down for a nap* should be *I lay down for a nap*. The one object-dropping sense is egg-laying: *the hens aren't laying*.
**Passive:** **yes** — *the tile had been laid before the inspector arrived*; *three charges were laid against the company*; *two hundred workers were laid off in March*.

To put something down flat — and from there, to install, prepare, establish, impose, or wager it. Always transitive; the intransitive verb is *lie*.

**1. Put something down flat.** The core sense, and it always takes an object. Do not confuse it with intransitive *lie*: ✗ *I laid down for a nap* should be *I lay down for a nap*. (класть, положить)

1. Please *lay* the baby in the crib on her back.
2. He *laid* his keys on the counter and forgot them there.
3. She *has laid* a blanket over him.

**2. Lay eggs.** Of birds, fish, and insects. It works without an object too: *the hens aren't laying*. (нести (яйца), откладывать яйца)

4. That turtle *lays* her eggs on the same beach every June.
5. The salmon *laid* their eggs and died.
6. The hens *have laid* six eggs this week.

**3. Install, put in place.** The building trades: *lay tile, lay carpet, lay pipe, lay track, lay brick*. (укладывать, прокладывать)

7. They *lay* about four hundred feet of pipe a day.
8. We *had laid* the tile before the inspector arrived.
9. The crew *has laid* new cable down the whole street.

**4. Set out, prepare.** *Lay the table*, *lay a fire*, *lay a trap*, *lay plans*. British English says *lay the table* where Americans usually say *set the table*. (накрывать (на стол); разводить (огонь); готовить (ловушку))

10. *Lay* the table for six, please.
11. He *laid* a fire in the grate before the guests arrived.
12. They *had laid* a trap and he walked right into it.

**5. Lay a foundation, lay the groundwork.** Figurative and very common in writing about projects and arguments: *lay a foundation*, *lay the groundwork*, *lay the basis for*. (закладывать (основу, фундамент))

13. These three chapters *lay* the foundation for everything after.
14. That treaty *laid* the groundwork for forty years of peace.
15. Her early work *has laid* the basis for the whole field.

**6. Impose, assign (blame, charges, a tax).** Formal and legal: *lay blame on someone*, *lay charges*, *lay claim to*, *lay a tax on*. (возлагать (вину); налагать (обвинение, налог))

16. Don't *lay* the blame on the intern.
17. Prosecutors *laid* three charges against the company.
18. Two countries *have laid* claim to the same reef.

**7. Bet, wager.** *Lay a bet*, *lay odds*, *lay money on something*. Informal; *I'd lay money that…* just means the speaker is confident. (ставить (в пари), держать пари)

19. I'd *lay* money that he shows up late.
20. He *laid* fifty dollars on the underdog.
21. She *has laid* a small bet on every Derby since 1990.

**8. Lay off — dismiss from a job.** To end someone's employment for economic reasons rather than for cause — quite different from *fire*. Informally, *lay off something* also means quit doing it: *lay off the coffee*. (увольнять (по сокращению))

22. Management may *lay off* more people after the audit.
23. The plant *laid off* two hundred workers in March.
24. They *have laid off* a third of the department since spring.

**9. Lay out — arrange, or explain in detail.** Two everyday uses: spreading things out in order (*lay out your clothes*), and presenting a plan or an argument point by point (*lay out the terms*). (раскладывать, размещать; излагать)

25. She *lays out* her clothes the night before every workday.
26. He *laid out* the whole proposal in fifteen minutes.
27. The memo *has laid out* exactly what happens next.

**10. Lay down — set a rule, or put aside.** *Lay down the law* means state the rules firmly; *lay down arms* means surrender. In music, *lay down a track* means record it. (устанавливать (правила); складывать (оружие))

28. The coach *lays down* the same three rules every season.
29. The rebels *laid down* their arms at dawn.
30. The band *has laid down* four tracks this week.

### make

**Pronunciation:** /meɪk/ &middot; *MAYK*
**Русский:** делать; производить; заставлять
**Forms:** *make* /meɪk/ &middot; *made* /meɪd/ &middot; *made* /meɪd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the handful of verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** ake — ade — ade &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *makes* &middot; **-ing form:** *making* (drop the e)
**Contrast:** In the "force someone to" sense, active *make* takes a bare infinitive but the passive takes *to*: *They made him apologize* becomes *He was made to apologize* — ✗ *He was made apologize*. Note also that *made* is a homophone of *maid*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the creating, forcing, earning, and light-verb senses (*make a mistake*, *make a living*). The *amount to* sense is **stative** and copula-like, joining subject to a complement rather than acting on an object: *four quarters make a dollar*, *that makes nine of us* — no progressive, ✗ *four quarters are making a dollar*.
**Transitivity:** **both**, and mostly transitive — plain transitive (*make coffee*), **ditransitive** (*make her a sandwich*), and complex-transitive with a complement (*make her chairman*, *make me happy*) or a bare infinitive (*they made him apologize*). Intransitive only inside the fixed *make do* and *make for*.
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *the same part has been made there since 1962*; *she was made chief financial officer*. Note the shift in the forcing sense: the passive requires *to* — *he was made to apologize*, ✗ *he was made apologize*. **No** passive of the *amount to* sense: ✗ *A dollar is made by four quarters.*

To create or produce something — and, in its many extended uses, to force, earn, reach, amount to, or manage something.

**1. Create, produce, build.** The core sense: bringing something into existence, from a sandwich to a spacecraft. (делать, изготавливать, производить)

1. I *make* coffee before anyone else in the house is up.
2. He *made* the whole dinner out of what was in the fridge.
3. That factory *has made* the same part since 1962.

**2. Force or cause someone to do something.** Active *make* takes a bare infinitive — *they made him apologize* — but the passive takes *to*: *he was made to apologize*, never ✗ *he was made apologize*. (заставлять, вынуждать)

4. That story *makes* me laugh every single time.
5. They *made* him apologize in front of the class.
6. Nobody *has made* her change her mind yet.

**3. Cause to become; appoint.** *Make* + object + adjective (*make me happy*) or + noun (*make her chairman*). The appointing use is formal. (делать (каким-л.), превращать; назначать)

7. The new lights *make* the room feel twice as big.
8. The board *made* her chief financial officer.
9. Two years abroad *had made* him almost unrecognizable.

**4. Earn money.** *Make a living*, *make money*, *make sixty thousand a year*. Completely neutral in American English, where *earn* sounds more formal. (зарабатывать)

10. She *makes* more than both of us combined.
11. He *made* a fortune on that one contract.
12. They *have made* nothing on the store in three years.

**5. Light verb: make a decision, a mistake, an offer.** *Make* carries dozens of fixed noun combinations where it means little more than 'do': *make a decision, a mistake, an effort, a promise, a call, progress, an offer*. Learn them as units. (совершать, принимать, делать (в устойчивых сочетаниях))

13. Try not to *make* the same mistake twice.
14. They *made* three offers on the same house.
15. The team *has made* real progress this quarter.

**6. Amount to, equal.** Arithmetic and totals: *two and two make four*, *that makes eleven of us*. (составлять, равняться)

16. Four quarters *make* a dollar.
17. With Dave, that *makes* nine people in a six-person van.
18. The last donation *made* it an even ten thousand.

**7. Reach, arrive at, manage to attend.** *Make the train*, *make the deadline*, *make it to the airport*. Very often with *it*: *we made it*. (успевать, добираться, поспевать)

19. If we leave now we can *make* the six o'clock train.
20. He *made* it to the hospital with ten minutes to spare.
21. She *has made* every practice this season.

**8. Make up — invent, reconcile, or constitute.** Three unrelated meanings share one phrase: invent a story, end a quarrel, and form the whole of something (*volunteers make up most of the staff*). *Make up for* means compensate. (выдумывать; мириться; составлять)

22. He *made up* the entire story on the drive over.
23. They fought for a week and then *made up*.
24. Volunteers *make up* most of the staff.
25. She *has made up* for the missed weeks with extra hours.

**9. Make out — discern, or manage.** To *make out* a shape or a signature is to perceive it with difficulty; *how did you make out?* asks how you did. In American slang it also means to kiss at length. (разобрать (разглядеть, расслышать); справляться)

26. I can barely *make out* the signature.
27. We *made out* fine without the generator.
28. Nobody *has made out* what the last line of the letter says.

**10. Make do — manage with what's available.** A fixed phrase, always *make do (with)*. There is no ✗ *make due*. (обходиться (тем, что есть))

29. We'll *make do* with what's in the pantry.
30. They *made do* on one income for years.
31. He *has made do* without a car since he moved downtown.

**11. Make for; make off with.** *Make for* a place means head toward it, and *make for* + noun also means produce a result (*that makes for a long day*). *Make off with* something means steal it and go. (направляться к; способствовать; удрать (с чем-л.))

32. The crowd *made for* the exits as soon as it started raining.
33. Thin walls *make for* awkward conversations.
34. Someone *had made off with* the tip jar.

**12. Make up one's mind — decide.** A fixed idiom separate from the other *make up* senses: to arrive at a decision, often after hesitating. (решаться, принимать решение)

35. *Make up* your mind — the table is only held until seven.
36. She *had made up* her mind long before the meeting started.

**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — sinks, rivers, and parking lots overflow in ordinary speech
**Pattern:** ow — owed — owed &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *overflows* &middot; **-ing form:** *overflowing*
**Contrast:** In current American English *overflow* is a plain regular verb: ✗ *The sink has overflown* should be *The sink has overflowed*. The form *overflown* does exist, but it belongs to a different verb — *overfly*, to fly over: *the border has been overflown by drones*. The verb is *oh-ver-FLOH*; the noun (*an overflow pipe*) is *OH-ver-floh* /ˈoʊvərˌfloʊ/.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, and mostly a process rather than a deed: liquid spilling over (*the tub overflowed*), a space too full (*the meeting overflowed into the hallway*), feeling brimming over (*overflowing with gratitude*), and a buffer exceeding its space.
**Transitivity:** **both** — normally intransitive, often with *into* (*the shelter has overflowed every night*), but transitive with the thing exceeded as object: *the creek overflowed its banks*, *the input overflowed the buffer*.

To spill over the edge of a container, or to be too full to hold everything that is in it.

**1. Spill over the edge.** Of liquid and of the things that hold it. Regular in modern American English: ✗ *the sink has overflown* should be *has overflowed* — *overflown* belongs to *overfly*. (переливаться через край, выходить из берегов)

1. The gutters *overflow* every time it rains hard.
2. The tub *overflowed* while I was on the phone.
3. The creek *has overflowed* its banks twice this spring.
4. By dawn the river *had overflowed* the levee at three points.

**2. Be too full; spill into another space.** Of a room, a container, or a schedule that cannot hold what it is given. Often *overflow into*, meaning the surplus goes somewhere else. (переполняться, не вмещаться)

5. Her inbox *overflows* by Monday afternoon.
6. The meeting *overflowed* into the hallway.
7. The shelter *has overflowed* every night this winter.

**3. Be filled with feeling.** Figurative and warm, usually in the -ing form: *overflowing with gratitude*, *overflowing with enthusiasm*. (быть переполненным (чувством))

8. She *overflows* with enthusiasm for every new project.
9. His letter *overflowed* with apologies.
10. The town *has overflowed* with visitors since the film came out.

**4. Exceed capacity (computing).** Technical: when a value or a memory buffer exceeds the space allotted to it. *Buffer overflow* is the standard security term. (переполняться (о буфере, счётчике))

11. The counter *overflows* after 65,535 events.
12. The input *overflowed* the buffer and crashed the service.
13. Attackers *had overflowed* the stack to run code of their own.

To cover a surface with a layer, or to place one layer, image, or set of data on top of another.

4. Craftsmen *overlay* the cheap wood with walnut veneer.

To pay more than something is worth, more than a person's work is worth, or more than the amount actually owed.

### pay

**Pronunciation:** /peɪ/ &middot; *PAY*
**Русский:** платить
**Forms:** *pay* /peɪ/ &middot; *paid* /peɪd/ &middot; *paid* /peɪd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the verbs an American uses without thinking
**Pattern:** ay — aid — aid &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *pays* &middot; **-ing form:** *paying*
**Contrast:** The y becomes i in the past: *paid*, not ✗ *payed*. The spelling *payed* is real but nautical only — you *pay out* a rope and you *payed* it out. For money, favors, compliments and attention, it is always *paid*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: handing over money (*pay the rent*), settling a debt, giving what is owed in attention or respect (*pay attention*, *pay a visit*), suffering consequences (*you'll pay for that*), and yielding a return (*the bond pays four percent*).
**Transitivity:** **ditransitive** at its core — *they paid her more than she asked for*, *pay him forty dollars* — and plain transitive with either object alone (*pay the fine*, *pay the servers*). **Intransitive** in the 'be profitable' sense: *it pays to read the fine print*, *the store never really paid*.
**Passive:** **yes**, with the recipient as the natural passive subject — *the servers are paid weekly*, *she was paid more than she asked for*; the sum promotes too: *the fine was paid without appeal*. **No** passive of the profitable sense: ✗ *It is paid to read the fine print.*

To give money for goods, work, or a debt — and, in its wider senses, to be worth doing, to suffer for something, or to give what is owed in attention and respect.

**1. Give money for goods or work.** The core sense. The y becomes i in the past: *paid*, never ✗ *payed*. (платить, оплачивать)

1. I *pay* the rent on the first of every month.
2. He *paid* cash for the whole thing.
3. We *had paid* for the tickets before the show sold out.

**2. Settle a debt or a bill.** *Pay a bill*, *pay a loan*, *pay taxes*, *pay a fine*. With *off*, it means clear the debt completely. (погашать (долг), рассчитываться)

4. She *pays* her credit card in full every month.
5. The company *paid* its fine without appealing.
6. They *have paid* off the car loan early.

**3. Pay someone wages.** With a person as the object, and two objects are normal: *pay him forty dollars*. (платить (кому-л.), выплачивать зарплату)

7. The restaurant *pays* its servers weekly.
8. They *paid* her more than she asked for.
9. The district *has paid* substitutes late three times this year.

**4. Be profitable, be worth doing.** No object: *it pays to arrive early*, *crime doesn't pay*, *the store never really paid*. (окупаться, быть выгодным)

10. It *pays* to read the fine print.
11. The store never really *paid*.
12. Patience *has paid* for them more than once.

**5. Suffer the consequences.** *Pay for* a mistake, *pay the price*, *you'll pay for this*. Nothing to do with money — this is threat or moral reckoning. (поплатиться, расплачиваться)

13. You'll *pay* for that remark.
14. He *paid* for the sprint with two days of sore legs.
15. The team *has paid* dearly for that one bad trade.

**6. Give what is owed: attention, respects, a visit.** A fixed set of combinations where nothing is bought: *pay attention*, *pay a compliment*, *pay a visit*, *pay your respects*, *pay tribute*. (оказывать, уделять (внимание, почтение))

16. She *pays* attention to details nobody else notices.
17. He *paid* her a real compliment for once.
18. Thousands *have paid* their respects at the memorial.

**7. Yield a return (finance).** Of a bond, a stock, or an account: to produce interest or a dividend at a stated rate. (приносить доход, давать проценты)

19. The bond *pays* four percent a year.
20. That fund *paid* nothing for two straight quarters.
21. The account *has paid* interest every quarter since 2019.

**8. Pay off — succeed after effort, or bribe.** Beyond clearing a debt, *pay off* means an effort produced its reward — and, quite separately, that someone was bribed into silence. (оправдать себя, принести плоды; дать взятку)

22. All that studying *has paid off*.
23. The extra rehearsal really *paid off*.
24. Someone *had paid off* the inspector.

**9. Pay back — return money, or retaliate.** Money returned to the person who lent it, or, figuratively, revenge: *pay someone back for* something. (возвращать (долг); отплатить, отомстить)

25. I'll *pay* you *back* on Friday.
26. He *paid* them *back* by leaking the memo.
27. She *has paid back* every dollar she borrowed.

**10. Pay out — disburse money, or let out rope.** Of an insurer or a bet, to hand the money over. In the nautical sense of letting a rope run out, the spelling of the past really is *payed* — the one place that spelling is correct. (выплачивать (страховку); травить (канат))

28. The policy *pays out* only after a thirty-day wait.
29. The insurer *paid out* less than half the claim.
30. They *payed* out the anchor chain slowly.

To make something again in a new form — a film, a kitchen, a career.

**2. Rebuild, redo from scratch.** Of physical things and plans: to make something over completely rather than repair it. (переделывать, перестраивать)

4. She *remakes* her budget from scratch every January.

7. Every new dean tries to *remake* the college in his own image.

To pay money back, to return a kindness — or, in a bitter twist on the same frame, to return an injury.

1. I'll *repay* you on Friday, I promise.
2. He *repaid* the loan in eighteen months.
3. She *has repaid* every dollar she borrowed from her sister.

5. They *repaid* the neighbors with a week of free babysitting.
6. By spring they *had repaid* every favor the family had done them.

**3. Requite with harm.** Formal, and almost always in the frame *repay X with Y*, where Y is worse than X deserved. (отплатить (злом), воздать)

7. He *repaid* their trust with a lawsuit.
8. History *has repaid* that generosity poorly.

**4. Be worth the effort.** Formal and written, with a thing as the subject: *the book repays careful reading*. (оправдывать (усилия), вознаграждать)

9. Nothing *repays* effort quite like daily practice.
10. The archive *repaid* three days of digging.
11. The detour *has repaid* us with the best view on the trip.

### say

**Pronunciation:** /seɪ/ &middot; *SAY*
**Русский:** сказать
**Forms:** *say* /seɪ/ &middot; *said* /sɛd/ &middot; *said* /sɛd/
**Verb class:** irregular
**Register:** everyday — one of the commonest verbs in the language
**Pattern:** ay — aid — aid &middot; **Group:** 7
**Third person:** *says* /sɛz/ &middot; **-ing form:** *saying*
**Contrast:** The spelling adds -d like *paid*, but the sound does not follow. *Say* rhymes with *day*; *said* rhymes with *bed*, not with *paid*; and *says* rhymes with *fez*, not with *pays*. Separately, *say* and *tell* split by object: you *say something to someone* but you *tell someone something* — ✗ *He said me the news*.
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, the basic verb of speech (*say something*), extended to what a text or instrument indicates (*the sign says the pool closes at nine*), to reported common belief (*they say the pass is closed*), and to what something conveys (*her silence said more than an argument*).
**Transitivity:** **transitive**, and the object is normally a clause (*she said she would call back*) or a noun (*say nothing*, *say grace*). Never ditransitive: the addressee takes *to* — *say something to someone*, ✗ *He said me the news* (that frame belongs to *tell*).
**Passive:** **yes** — *nothing was said about a deadline*; and the impersonal raising passive is standard journalism: *he is said to have turned down the job twice*. The addressee can never be the passive subject: ✗ *I was said the news.*

To put something into words — and, by extension, what a text indicates, what people report, and what a thing conveys without speaking.

**1. Utter words, speak.** The basic verb of speech. Watch the objects: you *say something to someone*, but you *tell someone something* — ✗ *He said me the news*. (говорить, сказать, произносить)

1. Don't *say* anything until the lawyer gets here.
2. She *said* she would call back after lunch.
3. He *has said* the same thing in every meeting this month.

**2. Of a text, sign, or instrument: indicate.** With an inanimate subject: what a written or displayed thing conveys. *The sign says*, *my watch says nine*, *the label says dry clean only*. (гласить; показывать (о надписи, приборе))

4. The sign *says* the pool closes at nine.
5. The letter *said* nothing about a deadline.
6. The forecast *has said* rain all week.

**3. Report, claim (they say, is said to).** The impersonal report of common belief. The passive frame *is said to* is formal and standard in journalism. (говорят, утверждают; считается)

7. They *say* the pass is closed until May.
8. He is *said* to have turned down the job twice.
9. Nobody *had said* a word about the layoffs.

**4. Suppose, assume (let's say).** Used to set up a hypothetical or an estimate: *say it rains — then what?*, *let's say fifty people*. Imperative in form, and fixed. (допустим, скажем, предположим)

10. *Say* it rains on Saturday — what's the backup plan?
11. Let's *say* fifty people show up.
12. *Say* you're right; it still doesn't help us.

**5. Recite (a prayer, a line, a word).** *Say grace*, *say a prayer*, *say your lines*, *say a few words*. Also used of pronouncing a word: *say it in Russian*. (произносить, читать (молитву, реплику))

13. He *says* grace before every meal.
14. She *said* a few words at the service.
15. The priest *has said* mass in that chapel for thirty years.

**6. Convey, mean (what something says about you).** Of art, evidence, or behavior — what it communicates without words. *That says a lot about him.* (выражать, свидетельствовать, говорить о (чём-л.))

16. The fact that he left early *says* everything.
17. Her silence *said* more than an argument would have.
18. That one decision *has said* more about the company than any press release.

**7. Say so; say the word.** Two fixed uses: *say so* means state it openly (*if you don't like it, say so*), and *just say the word* means give the signal and it will happen. (сказать прямо; дать знать)

19. Just *say* the word and I'll book it.
20. If you'd wanted help, you should have *said* so.
21. He never *said* so out loud, but everyone knew.

**See also:** [gainsay](#gainsay), [unsay](#unsay), [tell](04-past-equals-participle-plus-d.md#tell)
