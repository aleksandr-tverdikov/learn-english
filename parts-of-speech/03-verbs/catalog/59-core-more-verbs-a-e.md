# More verbs A-E

> Further verbs A-E — what a coverage check against two large verb lists showed was still missing.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Gathered by checking the catalog against roughly 21,000 scraped verb lemmas and writing entries for what was genuinely absent.

Most of that list was not worth writing — archaic verbs no living American uses, British-only spellings, narrow technical coinages, and a fair number of words that were not verbs at all — so these are the survivors of a deliberate rejection pass that threw out roughly nine of every ten candidates.

### armor

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɑːrmər/ &middot; *AHR-mur*
**Русский:** покрывать бронёй, бронировать
**Forms:** *armor* &middot; *armored* &middot; *armored*
**Third person:** *armors* &middot; **-ing form:** *armoring*
**Verb class:** regular
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** American English spells it *armor, armored, armoring*; British English writes *armour, armoured, armouring*.

To cover something with a protective layer of metal or other hard material.

1. The company *armors* trucks for use in war zones.
2. They *armored* the embassy doors after the attack.
3. Every vehicle in the convoy has been *armored*.

### arouse

**Forms:** *arouse* &middot; *aroused* &middot; *aroused*
**Third person:** *arouses* &middot; **-ing form:** *arousing*
**Verb class:** regular
**Transitivity:** transitive
**Contrast:** Do not confuse *arouse*, which is regular (*aroused, aroused*), with *arise*, which is irregular (*arose, arisen*).

To stir up a feeling or reaction in someone.

3. The story has *aroused* strong feelings on both sides.
