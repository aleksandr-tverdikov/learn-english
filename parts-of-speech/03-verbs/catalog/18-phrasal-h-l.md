# Phrasal verbs: hang — lose

> Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs hang, have, hear… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

[← Irregular verbs](README.md) &middot; [The grammar of verbs](../README.md)

A phrasal verb is an irregular verb welded to a small word — *up*, *out*, *on*, *behind* — and the weld changes the meaning past recognition: *hold up* has nothing to do with holding, *hang out* has nothing to do with hanging, and *lay off* has nothing to do with lying down. You cannot take these apart and add up the pieces, so you learn each one whole, the way you learn a single word. The other half of the job is **separability**: whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle (*hang the phone up*) or must follow the whole phrase (*hide behind the rules*). It matters because a separable verb with a pronoun object *has* to split — *hang it up*, never ✗ *hang up it* — while an inseparable one never may, so every entry below states plainly which kind it is.

### hang around

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ əˈraʊnd/ &middot; *hang-uh-ROUND* &middot; the stress lands on the particle — hang aROUND, not HANG around
**Русский:** слоняться, болтаться, торчать; водиться с
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** no — the place or the person always follows the whole phrase. *We hung around the parking lot*, never ✗ *we hung the parking lot around*; with a pronoun, *hang around them*, never ✗ *hang them around*.
**Transitive:** either — it takes no object when it means "wait idly," and a place or (with *with*) a person otherwise
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic and atelic, an activity that fills time (*we hung around for an hour*); sense 4 (*that cough has hung around since January*) shades toward a state, but it still takes the progressive freely.
**Passive:** **no** — the "wait" sense has no object at all, and the place and person senses are prepositional: ✗ *the parking lot was hung around*, ✗ *she was hung around with*.

Spend time somewhere or with someone without any particular purpose.

**1. Wait or linger with nothing to do.** No object. The idea is aimlessness — time passing without a plan, often with a hint of impatience.

1. I *hung around* for an hour and she never showed up.
2. Don't just *hang around* — grab a broom.

**2. Spend time in a place.** With a place as the object. American English also says *hang out at*.

3. Kids *hang around* the 7-Eleven all summer.
4. He *hung around* the courthouse hoping for a quote.

**3. hang around with — keep company with.** About the people someone chooses, and usually a little disapproving when a parent says it.

5. She started *hanging around with* a rough crowd.
6. I don't *hang around with* them anymore.

**4. Stay in place longer than expected.** Of a thing, a smell, or a feeling that refuses to go away.

7. That cough has *hung around* since January.

**See also:** [hang out](#hang-out), [hang back](#hang-back)

### hang back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈbæk/ &middot; *hang-BAK* &middot; the particle takes the stress — hang BACK, never HANG back
**Русский:** держаться позади, мяться, не решаться
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** n/a — there is no object to move; the particle sits right after the verb.
**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic, but about withholding action: the subject is doing something (declining to step forward), not being something.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, so there is no object to promote: ✗ *the front row was hung back*.

Hold yourself back from acting or moving forward, out of shyness or doubt.

1. The new hires *hung back* while the veterans took the front row.
2. Don't *hang back* — ask her yourself.
3. He *hung back* at the door, unsure whether he was welcome.
4. Investors have *hung back* since the rates went up.
5. She *hangs back* in meetings and then emails her best idea an hour later.

**See also:** [hold back](#hold-back), [hang around](#hang-around)

### hang in

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈɪn/ &middot; *hang-IN* &middot; the particle takes the stress — hang IN there, never HANG in there
**Русский:** держаться, не сдаваться
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** n/a — no object; *there* in *hang in there* is not an object but part of the fixed phrase.
**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic, durative: enduring over a stretch of time (*she hung in through two rounds of layoffs*).
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, and *there* in *hang in there* is part of the fixed phrase, not an object: ✗ *there was hung in*.

Keep going through something difficult; almost always heard as *hang in there*.

1. *Hang in there* — the results come back Friday.
2. She *hung in* through two rounds of layoffs.
3. If you can *hang in* for one more semester, the hard part is over.
4. He *hung in there* long after the rest of the team quit.
5. Tell him to *hang in there*; we're two votes short.

**See also:** [hang on](#hang-on), [hold out](#hold-out)

### hang on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈɑn/ &middot; *hang-AHN* &middot; particle-stressed when it means wait or grip — hang ON; but verb-stressed when *on* is a true preposition, *everything HANGS on the vote*
**Русский:** подождать; держаться; зависеть от
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** no — nothing ever comes between *hang* and *on*. In the "depend" sense *on* is a preposition and keeps its object behind it: *it hangs on the weather*, ✗ *it hangs the weather on*; with a pronoun, *it hangs on that*, ✗ *it hangs that on*.
**Transitive:** either — no object in the "wait" and "grip" senses, an object after *on* in the "depend" sense
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** in the "wait" and "grip" senses — dynamic (*hang on a second*, *hang on tight*). **Stative** in the "depend" sense: *it hangs on the weather* is a relation, not an event.
**Passive:** **no** — the wait and grip senses take no object, and the stative *on*-phrase resists the prepositional passive: ✗ *the weather is hung on*.

Wait, keep your grip, or depend on something, according to the sense.

**1. Wait a moment.** Informal and nearly always an imperative, on the phone or in person. *Hold on* is the interchangeable twin.

1. *Hang on* — I've got another call.
2. *Hang on* a second, that's not what the invoice says.

**2. Keep a tight grip.** Physical, and no object: what you grip goes after *to*.

3. *Hang on*, this road gets rough.
4. She *hung on* until the rescue crew reached her.

**3. Depend on, be determined by.** Here *on* is a preposition and the subject is usually a decision or an outcome.

5. The whole season *hangs on* Sunday's game.
6. Everything *hung on* whether the bank called the loan.

**4. Listen with total attention.** Fixed in *hang on someone's every word*.

7. The students *hung on* his every word.

**See also:** [hang on to](#hang-on-to), [hold on](#hold-on)

### hang on to

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈɑn tə/ &middot; *hang-AHN-tuh* &middot; the middle particle carries the stress — hang ON to it; the *to* reduces to /tə/
**Русский:** держаться за, сохранять, не отдавать
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — three-part verbs never split. *Hang on to the receipt*, ✗ *hang the receipt on to*; with a pronoun, *hang on to it*, ✗ *hang it on to*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic when it means gripping (*hang on to the railing*); closer to a maintained state when it means keeping (*we hung on to the deed for forty years*).
**Passive:** **restricted** — the three-part prepositional passive is awkward at best: ✗ *the railing was hung on to*. Say *the deed was kept* instead.

Keep something rather than give it up, sell it, or throw it away; also grip it tightly. Often written *hang onto*.

1. *Hang on to* that receipt in case the drill quits.
2. They *hung on to* the house through the whole downturn.
3. I've *hung on to* his letters for thirty years.
4. *Hang on to* me — the ice is worse than it looks.
5. She *hung on to* her seat by fewer than three hundred votes.
6. It's ugly, but *hang on to it*; those parts are impossible to find.

**See also:** [hold on to](#hold-on-to), [hang on](#hang-on)

### hang out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈaʊt/ &middot; *hang-OWT* &middot; the particle takes the stress — hang OUT, and *hang the wash OUT*
**Русский:** тусоваться, зависать; вывешивать (бельё)
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes in the laundry sense — *hang out the wash* or *hang the wash out*, and with a pronoun it must split: *hang it out*, never ✗ *hang out it*. The socializing sense takes no object at all, so nothing can come between.
**Transitive:** either — no object when it means "spend time," an object in the laundry sense
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic throughout: an activity in the socializing sense (*we hung out at the diner*), a telic act in the laundry sense (*hang the wash out*).
**Passive:** **yes** in the laundry sense — *the sheets were hung out to dry*, and the idiom *he was left hung out to dry*. **No** passive of the socializing sense: ✗ *I was hung out with by them*.

Spend time somewhere in a relaxed way; also, put washing outside to dry.

**1. Spend time relaxing, socialize.** Informal and very American. With people it takes *with*; with a place, *at*. No object follows the particle itself.

1. We just *hung out* at her place and watched movies.
2. Who does he *hang out with* these days?
3. They've been *hanging out* together since middle school.

**2. Put washing outside to dry.** Separable, and the split order is the everyday one.

4. She *hung* the sheets *out* before the rain came.
5. He *hangs out* the laundry every Saturday.
6. Bring the towels in — I *hung them out* this morning.

**3. Lean or stick out.** Of a tongue, a shirttail, or anything protruding.

7. The dog rode with his tongue *hanging out*.

**See also:** [hang around](#hang-around), [hang up](#hang-up)

### hang over

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhæŋ ˌoʊvər/ &middot; *HANG-oh-ver* &middot; verb-stressed, because *over* here is a preposition with an object — HANGS over us, not hangs OVer us
**Русский:** нависать над, висеть над (об угрозе)
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — what it hangs over always follows the preposition. *The debt hangs over the family*, ✗ *the debt hangs the family over*; with a pronoun, *it hangs over us*, ✗ *it hangs us over*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative in effect** — it names a condition that persists rather than an event (*a lawsuit hangs over the company*), though unlike most statives it takes the progressive: *a deadline is hanging over her*.
**Passive:** **no** — *over* is a preposition and the subject is the threat, not an agent: ✗ *the family is hung over by the debt*.

Loom over someone as a threat or an unfinished worry. The noun *hangover* comes from it.

1. The audit has *hung over* the department all year.
2. A possible strike *hangs over* the negotiations.
3. That conversation *hung over* dinner like weather.
4. For months the verdict *hung over* him and he barely slept.
5. There is a deadline *hanging over* us, so let's be quick.

**See also:** [hold over](#hold-over), [hang on](#hang-on)

### hang together

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ təˈɡɛðər/ &middot; *hang-tuh-GETH-er* &middot; the stress lands on the particle — hang toGETHer
**Русский:** быть связным, сходиться; держаться вместе
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** n/a — there is no object to move.
**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** in sense 1 — coherence is a property of the story, not something it does (✗ *the alibi is hanging together*). **Action** (durative) in sense 2, of people staying united under pressure.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive in both senses: ✗ *the story was hung together*.

Make sense as a whole, or stay united.

**1. Be coherent, make sense.** Of a story, an argument, or an alibi whose parts fit each other.

1. His story doesn't *hang together* — the times are wrong.
2. The essay is well written, but it doesn't quite *hang together*.
3. Once we saw the receipts, the whole account *hung together*.

**2. Stay united.** Of people who stick with each other under pressure.

4. The band *hung together* for twenty years.
5. If we don't *hang together*, they'll pick us off one by one.

**See also:** [hold together](#hold-together)

### hang up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæŋ ˈʌp/ &middot; *hang-UP* &middot; the particle takes the stress — hang it UP, never HANG it up
**Русский:** вешать трубку; вешать (на крючок); завершить карьеру
**Base verb:** [hang](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#hang) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *hang up your coat* or *hang your coat up*, and with a pronoun it must split: *hang it up*, never ✗ *hang up it*. The phone sense is often used with no object at all: *he hung up on me*.
**Transitive:** either — no object in the phone sense, an object in the others
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic and telic in every live sense: ending a call, putting a coat on a hook, retiring the cleats. Sense 4 (*be hung up on someone*) is an adjectival state, not a verb use.
**Passive:** **yes** in the object senses — *the coats were hung up by the door*, *his skates were hung up years ago*. The fixed adjectival passive *she's hung up on him* is the ordinary form of sense 4.

End a phone call, put something on a hook, or retire from an activity.

**1. End a phone call.** Usually with no object; *hang up on someone* means ending it rudely, mid-conversation.

1. She said goodbye and *hung up*.
2. Don't *hang up* — I'm putting you on speaker.
3. He *hung up on* me twice.

**2. Put on a hook or hanger.** Separable, and the split order is at least as common.

4. *Hang up* your coat before you sit down.
5. He *hung* the wet towel *up* on the rail.
6. It's a nice jacket — *hang it up* instead of dropping it there.

**3. Retire from a sport or a job.** Fixed with the tool of the trade: *hang up his cleats*, *her skates*, *the badge*.

7. After twenty seasons he finally *hung up* his skates.

**4. be hung up — be stuck or obsessed.** Passive or adjectival. *Hung up on* something means unable to stop thinking about it; *hung up* alone means delayed.

8. The permit is *hung up* in the county office.
9. She's still *hung up on* her ex.

**See also:** [hang out](#hang-out), [hold up](#hold-up)

### have at

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhæv ət/ &middot; *HAV-uht* &middot; verb-stressed — HAVE at it; *at* reduces to /ət/, as prepositions do
**Русский:** приняться за, наброситься на
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object always follows *at*. *Have at the leftovers*, ✗ *have the leftovers at*; with a pronoun, *have at it*, ✗ *have it at*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic, and nearly frozen in the imperative *have at it*; the archaic "attack" sense is likewise a dynamic act.
**Passive:** **no** — *at* is a preposition and the phrase is fixed: ✗ *the leftovers were had at*, ✗ *it was had at*.

Go ahead and tackle something with energy; in older writing, attack.

**1. Go ahead, help yourself, get to work on it.** Nearly always the fixed *have at it*, offered as permission.

1. The keyboard's yours — *have at it*.
2. If you think you can fix the carburetor, *have at it*.
3. There's half a pie in the fridge. *Have at it*.

**2. Attack.** Archaic outside historical fiction and jokes about it.

4. The knight cried "*Have at* thee!" and charged.
5. The puppy *had at* the couch cushions while we were out.

**See also:** [lay into](#lay-into), [have it out](#have-it-out)

### have in

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæv ˈɪn/ &middot; *hav-IN* &middot; the particle takes the stress — have them IN, not HAVE them in
**Русский:** приглашать к себе, вызывать (мастера)
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes, and in practice it must split: *have the neighbors in*, *have them in*. ✗ *Have in the neighbors* is not idiomatic, and ✗ *have in them* is impossible.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic *have*, meaning "host," not stative possession, so the progressive is fine: *we're having the neighbors in on Friday*.
**Passive:** **no** — like other *have* idioms it refuses the passive: ✗ *the neighbors were had in*, ✗ *a plumber was had in*.

Bring someone into your home — guests for the evening, or a worker for a job.

1. We *had* the Kaminskis *in* for coffee.
2. They're *having* a plumber *in* on Thursday.
3. She *had them in* for an hour and sent them home with pie.
4. We haven't *had* anyone *in* since the baby was born.
5. If the wiring smells hot, *have* an electrician *in* today.

**See also:** [have over](#have-over), [have on](#have-on)

### have it in for

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæv ɪt ˈɪn fər/ &middot; *hav-it-IN-fer* &middot; the stress falls on *in*, the particle in the middle — have it IN for me
**Русский:** иметь зуб на, точить зуб на
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the phrase is frozen. The *it* never changes and never moves, and the person always follows *for*: *he has it in for me*, ✗ *he has me in for it*.
**Transitive:** yes — the person always follows *for*
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **stative** — a standing attitude, not an act, and it rejects the progressive: ✗ *he is having it in for me*.
**Passive:** **no** — the frozen *it* cannot move and stative *have* blocks the passive anyway: ✗ *I am had it in for*.

Bear a lasting grudge against someone and look for chances to hurt them.

1. That umpire *has it in for* our catcher.
2. She was sure the scheduler *had it in for* her.
3. Ever since the complaint, he's *had it in for* me.
4. I don't think the professor *has it in for* anyone; he grades everyone that hard.
5. You'd swear the weather *has it in for* this town.

**See also:** [hold against](#hold-against), [have it out](#have-it-out)

### have it out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæv ɪt ˈaʊt/ &middot; *hav-it-OWT* &middot; the particle takes the stress — have it OUT with him
**Русский:** выяснить отношения, объясниться начистоту
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** no — the *it* is frozen in place between the verb and the particle and can never be replaced by another noun: *have it out with your brother*, ✗ *have the argument out*. The person you argue with always follows *with*.
**Transitive:** yes — the fixed *it*, plus the person after *with*
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic and telic, a single confrontation with an endpoint: *we finally had it out in the parking lot*.
**Passive:** **no** — the *it* is frozen and cannot be promoted: ✗ *it was had out with him*.

Settle a quarrel by finally arguing it through face to face.

1. They finally *had it out* in the parking lot.
2. I'm going to *have it out with* my landlord about the heat.
3. We *had it out* over the will and haven't spoken since.
4. Stop sulking and *have it out with* her.
5. After twenty years of hints, the sisters *had it out*.

**See also:** [have it in for](#have-it-in-for), [hold out on](#hold-out-on)

### have on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæv ˈɑn/ &middot; *hav-AHN* &middot; the particle takes the stress — she had a red coat ON
**Русский:** быть одетым в, носить; иметь компромат на; разыгрывать (брит.)
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *he had on a heavy coat* or *he had a heavy coat on*, and with a pronoun it must split: *he had it on*, never ✗ *he had on it*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **stative** — "be wearing" is a state, and the entry notes it refuses the progressive: ✗ *he is having on a coat*. Sense 2 (*what they have on him*) is equally stative; only the British "tease" sense is dynamic.
**Passive:** **no** — this is the classic stative *have* that learners try to passivize: ✗ *a heavy coat was had on by him*, ✗ *nothing is had on me*. Use *wear* or *hold* if you need a passive.

Be wearing something; also, hold damaging information about someone.

**1. Be wearing.** The ordinary way to describe what someone is dressed in at a moment. Unlike *wear*, it does not take the progressive: ✗ *he is having on a coat*.

1. She *had on* the blue dress from the wedding.
2. He *had* nothing *on* but a towel.
3. That's my scarf — you *had it on* all day.

**2. Hold evidence against.** Usually negative, and common in police and legal talk.

4. They've got nothing *on* him.
5. What exactly does the prosecutor *have on* your client?

**3. Have scheduled.** Of commitments; *have a lot on* is more British than American, where people say *have a lot going on*.

6. I *have* a lot *on* this week.

**4. Tease, fool.** British. An American would say *pull someone's leg* or *mess with someone*.

7. You're *having me on* — she never said that.

**See also:** [have over](#have-over), [have in](#have-in)

### have over

**Pronunciation:** /ˌhæv ˈoʊvər/ &middot; *hav-OH-ver* &middot; the particle takes the stress — have them Over, not HAVE them over
**Русский:** приглашать в гости
**Base verb:** [have](07-plus-d.md#have) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes, and it must split in practice: *have the Nguyens over*, *have them over*. ✗ *Have over the Nguyens* is not idiomatic and ✗ *have over them* is impossible.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic *have*, meaning "host": *we're having the Nguyens over Saturday* is perfectly natural in the progressive.
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *the Nguyens were had over*; English simply has no passive for this idiom. Say *they were invited over*.

Invite someone to your home. The American default; British English says *have someone round*.

1. We're *having* my parents *over* on Sunday.
2. *Have them over* — the grill is already going.
3. She *had* the whole team *over* after the game.
4. They haven't *had* us *over* once since they moved.
5. Why don't you *have* him *over* and just talk it through?

**See also:** [have in](#have-in), [have on](#have-on)

### hear about

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɪr əˌbaʊt/ &middot; *HEER-uh-bowt* &middot; verb-stressed — HEARD about it; the preposition stays weak
**Русский:** узнавать о, слышать о; получить нагоняй
**Base verb:** [hear](04-past-equals-participle-plus-d.md#hear) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the news always follows *about*. *I heard about the fire*, ✗ *I heard the fire about*; with a pronoun, *I heard about it*, ✗ *I heard it about*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** **action** — dynamic, an achievement: news reaches you at a moment (*I heard about the fire last night*), though the progressive works for repeated report: *we've been hearing about it all week*.
**Passive:** **restricted** — the prepositional passive is awkward: ✗ *the fire was heard about*, and the threat sense has none at all: ✗ *it will be heard about*.

Learn a piece of news; in a warning, be scolded for something.

**1. Get news of.** The everyday sense: information reaches you, usually secondhand.

1. I *heard about* the layoffs from a friend in payroll.
2. Have you *heard about* the new bridge?
3. We only *heard about* it after the funeral.

**2. Be scolded, face the consequences.** Always in the future and always a threat: *you'll hear about it*.

4. If your mother finds that dent, you'll *hear about it*.
5. The mayor is going to *hear about* this at the next meeting.

**See also:** [hear of](#hear-of), [hear from](#hear-from)

### hear from

**Pronunciation:** /ˈhɪr frəm/ &middot; *HEER-frum* &middot; verb-stressed — HEAR from her; *from* reduces to /frəm/
**Русский:** получать известия от
**Base verb:** [hear](04-past-equals-participle-plus-d.md#hear) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the person always follows *from*. *I heard from my sister*, ✗ *I heard my sister from*; with a pronoun, *we heard from them*, ✗ *we heard them from*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday

3. He didn't *let on* how sick he was.
4. If they ask, don't *let on* — act surprised.
5. The kids figured it out but never *let on* to their parents.

### let out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌlɛt ˈaʊt/ &middot; *let-OWT* &middot; the particle takes the stress — let it OUT
**Русский:** выпускать; расставлять (одежду); издавать (крик); заканчиваться (о занятиях)
**Base verb:** [let](01-all-three-same.md#let) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes, and with a pronoun it must split: *let it out*, never ✗ *let out it*. With a noun both orders work: *let out the dog* or *let the dog out*. In *school lets out at three* there is no object at all.
**Transitive:** either — an object in most senses, none when a class or a movie ends
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all four senses — releasing, widening a garment, producing a sound, ending for the day.
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *the prisoners were let out*, *the waist was let out an inch*, *a scream was let out* (stiff; the active is better). No passive of sense 4: ✗ *three-fifteen is let out at* for *school lets out at three-fifteen*.

Release something, make a garment wider, produce a sound, or come to an end.

**1. Release, allow out.** Separable.

1. *Let* the dog *out* before you leave.
2. They *let out* the prisoners who had served their time.
3. The cat's crying at the door — *let her out*.

**2. Make a garment wider.** The opposite of *take in*.

4. The tailor *let out* the waist an inch.

**3. Produce a sound.** Of a scream, a sigh, a laugh.

5. She *let out* a scream that woke the block.
6. He *let out* a long breath and sat down.

**4. End, finish for the day.** No object. American, of school, class, church, and movies.

7. School *lets out* at three-fifteen.

### let up

**Transitive:** no — it never takes a direct object
**Register:** everyday

Become less intense, or ease the pressure on someone.

**1. Become less intense.** Of rain, wind, pain, pressure, work.

1. The rain *let up* just after noon.
2. The headaches haven't *let up* all week.
3. Business won't *let up* until January.

**2. let up on — go easier on.** Of a person under pressure.

4. *Let up on* him — he's been here two days.
5. The defense never *let up on* the quarterback.

### lie around

**Pronunciation:** /ˌlaɪ əˈraʊnd/ &middot; *lie-uh-ROUND* &middot; the particle takes the stress — lie aROUND all day
**Русский:** валяться (о вещах); бездельничать
**Base verb:** [lie](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#lie) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** n/a — there is no object; this *lie* never takes one. Careful: the transitive verb is *lay*, so a person *lies around* but *lays* the tools around.
**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in sense 2 (a person doing nothing all weekend); sense 1, of objects left out, is closer to **stative** — it reports where things are, like locative *be*, and *tools were lying around* names a state, not an event.
**Passive:** **no** — this *lie* never takes an object at all (the transitive verb is *lay*), so there is nothing to promote: ✗ *the garage was lain around*. Contrast the genuine passive of the transitive verb: *the tools were laid around the garage*.

Be left untidily in the open, or spend time doing nothing. Many Americans say *lay around* in speech; it is very common and still counted as nonstandard.

**1. Be left lying about.** Of objects nobody has put away.

1. There were tools *lying around* the whole garage.
2. Don't leave cash *lying around*.

**2. Do nothing.** Of people, usually with a note of disapproval.

3. He *lay around* the house all weekend.
4. I'd love a Saturday to just *lie around* and read.
5. They *lay around* the pool until the sun went down.

**See also:** [lie low](#lie-low), [hang around](#hang-around), [lay down](#lay-down)

### lie behind

**Pronunciation:** /ˈlaɪ bɪˌhaɪnd/ &middot; *LIE-bih-hynd* &middot; verb-stressed — what LIES behind it; *behind* is a preposition and keeps its object
**Русский:** стоять за, лежать в основе
**Base verb:** [lie](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#lie) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object always follows *behind*. *What lies behind the decision*, ✗ *what lies the decision behind*; with a pronoun, *what lies behind it*, ✗ *what lies it behind*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** formal
**Verb type:** **stative** — a relation of cause that simply holds; nobody performs it. It resists the progressive except as a stylistic flourish (*a longer story is lying behind that photograph* is odd; *lies behind* is what you write).
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *the decision is lain behind by a grudge*, ✗ *the closure was lain behind*. Note too that the participle of this *lie* is *lain*, which never appears in a passive at all; use *was caused by* or *was driven by*.

Be the hidden reason or cause of something.

1. Nobody has explained what *lies behind* the sudden closure.
2. A long grudge *lay behind* the lawsuit.
3. The economics that *lie behind* the policy are never discussed.
4. Fear, not principle, *lay behind* the vote.
5. There's a longer story *lying behind* that photograph.

**See also:** [lead to](#lead-to), [lie with](#lie-with)

**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** British — Americans say *sleep in*; the British noun *lie-in* is an American *sleep-in*
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, of staying in bed deliberately. British; the American equivalent is *sleep in*. The noun *lie-in* comes from it.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, with no object to promote: ✗ *Sunday was lain in*. Only the noun is available: *she had a long lie-in*.

Stay in bed later than usual in the morning.

1. On Sundays they *lie in* until ten.
2. I was hoping to *lie in* for once.
3. She had a long *lie-in* after the night shift.
4. An American would not say *lie in*; he would say he slept in.

### lie low

**Pronunciation:** /ˌlaɪ ˈloʊ/ &middot; *lie-LOH* &middot; the second word takes the stress — lie LOW
**Русский:** залечь на дно, затаиться
**Base verb:** [lie](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#lie) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** n/a — there is no object to move.
**Transitive:** no — it never takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic as a course of behavior (*he lay low for a month*), but grammatically *lie* here is **linking**: *low* is an adjective complement describing the subject, exactly as in *lie still* or *lie quiet*, not an object or a particle.
**Passive:** **no** — there is no object, and a complement can never become a passive subject: ✗ *low was lain*, ✗ *he was lain low* in this sense. (*He was laid low by the flu* is a different verb, transitive *lay*.)

Stay out of sight and avoid attention until trouble passes. Americans very often say *lay low*, which is nonstandard but everywhere.

1. He *lay low* for a month after the story broke.
2. *Lie low* until the auditors are finished.
3. The suspects were *lying low* somewhere in the valley.
4. After the argument she *lay low* and let him cool off.
5. In American speech you will hear *lay low* far more often than *lie low*.

### lie with

**Русский:** лежать на (об ответственности), быть за (кем-то)

**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** formal
**Verb type:** **stative** — a relation of responsibility that holds rather than happens; the subject is the decision or the blame, never an agent. It resists the progressive: ✗ *the decision is lying with the board*.
**Passive:** **no** — stative and prepositional, so the object cannot be promoted: ✗ *the board is lain with by the decision*, ✗ *the contractor is lain with*. Recast as *the board decides* or *the contractor is responsible*. The archaic sense 2 has no passive either.

Be the responsibility or the choice of a particular person or body.

**1. Be the responsibility of.** Of decisions, blame, and duties. The subject is the decision, not the person.

1. The final decision *lies with* the board.
2. Responsibility for the delay *lies with* the contractor.
3. The choice *lies with* you and nobody else.
4. Whatever fault there is *lies with* me.

**2. Have sex with.** Archaic and biblical; you meet it only in older translations.

5. He shall not *lie with* his neighbor's wife.

Come upon something by chance, or settle on it as a bird does.

### light up

**Separable:** yes — *light up the room* or *light the room up*, and with a pronoun it must split: *light it up*, never ✗ *light up it*. In the "face brightens" sense there is no object.
**Transitive:** either — an object when you illuminate something, none when a face brightens or a smoker lights up
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in every sense. Sense 2 is inchoative and intransitive — a face becomes bright — which is a change of state, not a linking use: *her face lit up*, not ✗ *her face lit up bright*.
**Passive:** **yes** in the transitive senses — *the whole field was lit up by floodlights*, *the Celtics were lit up for forty points*. Make something bright, become bright with feeling, or start smoking. American English prefers the past *lit*.

**1. Illuminate.** Separable.

1. Floodlights *lit up* the whole field.
2. One candle *lit* the room *up* more than you'd expect.

**2. Become bright with feeling.** No object, of a face or eyes.

4. Her face *lit up* when she saw the dog.
5. The kids *lit up* at the word snow.

**3. Start smoking.** Informal, usually with no object.

6. He *lit up* the moment he got outside.

**4. Dominate an opponent.** American sports slang.

7. The rookie *lit up* the Celtics for forty points.

End up without the benefit that others get.

Miss a chance to get something worth having.
