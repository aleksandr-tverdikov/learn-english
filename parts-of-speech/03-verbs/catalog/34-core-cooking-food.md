# Core verbs: cooking

> The everyday regular verbs of cooking — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### braise

**Pronunciation:** /breɪz/ &middot; *BRAYZ*
**Русский:** тушить, потушить (в небольшом количестве жидкости)
**Forms:** *braise* &middot; *braised* &middot; *braised*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *braises* &middot; **-ing form:** *braising*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To cook meat slowly in a covered pot with a little liquid.

1. *Braise* the short ribs for three hours.
2. We *braised* the lamb in red wine.
3. The beef has been *braising* all afternoon.

### barbecue

**Pronunciation:** /ˈbɑːrbɪkjuː/ &middot; *BAR-bi-kyoo*
**Русский:** жарить на барбекю, готовить на углях
**Forms:** *barbecue* &middot; *barbecued* &middot; *barbecued*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *barbecues* &middot; **-ing form:** *barbecuing*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (barbecue ribs), intransitive (they barbecue every weekend)
**Contrast:** Spelled barbecue (short form BBQ); barbeque is nonstandard. The -e drops before -ing: barbecuing.

To cook food slowly outdoors over smoke or hot coals.

1. They *barbecue* every Fourth of July.
2. We *barbecued* ribs all afternoon.
3. He is *barbecuing* chicken in the backyard.

### caramelize

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkɛrəməlaɪz/ &middot; *KER-uh-muh-lyz*
**Русский:** карамелизовать, карамелизоваться
**Forms:** *caramelize* &middot; *caramelized* &middot; *caramelized*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *caramelizes* &middot; **-ing form:** *caramelizing*
**Transitivity:** both — transitive (caramelize the sugar), intransitive (the onions caramelized)

To cook sugar, or the sugar in a food, until it turns brown and sweet.

2. She *caramelized* the sugar for the flan.
3. The shallots have *caramelized* beautifully.
