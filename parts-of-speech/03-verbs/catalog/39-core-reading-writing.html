Core verbs: reading and writing#

The everyday regular verbs of reading and writing — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary built entirely of irregular and phrasal verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

Click has 4 distinct senses.

1. To press a button on a mouse or a spot on a screen to choose something. (щёлкать, кликать (мышью))

  1. Click the blue link at the bottom of the page.
  2. I clicked on the ad by mistake.

2. To make a short, sharp sound. (щёлкать, щёлкнуть (о звуке))

  1. The lock clicks when it is fully shut.
  2. Her heels clicked down the hallway.
  3. Something was clicking under the hood the whole way home.

3. To suddenly become clear or make sense. (доходить, дойти (о смысле); осенять, осенить)

  1. A rule doesn't always click until you use it.
  2. Then it clicked: I had met him at the wedding.
  3. Nothing was clicking for me in that class.

4. To like someone immediately and get along with them easily. (сразу находить общий язык, сойтись (характерами))

  1. Some roommates just click.
  2. We clicked the first time we met.
