# More phrasal verbs on irregular bases: M-Z

> Phrasal verbs on irregular bases that groups 14-22 missed — headwords M-Z.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 14-22 cover the phrasal verbs on irregular bases that came from the original source material. Checking the catalog against a full WordNet verb list turned up a further set it had never included — *fall through*, *hang about*, *stand over*, *read up* — and these are those.

Each entry names its **base verb** and says whether it is **separable**. Because the base is irregular, the past and the participle are worth reading twice: *read up* → *read up* → *read up* looks unchanged but is not pronounced that way, and *sew up* → *sewed up* → *sewn up* changes only in the participle.

### make believe

**Pronunciation:** /ˌmeɪk bɪˈliːv/ &middot; *mayk bi-LEEV*
**Русский:** притворяться, притвориться; играть в воображаемое; воображать
**Base verb:** *make*
**Separable:** fixed — *believe* is part of the verb and never moves; what you pretend comes after as a clause: *make believe you're asleep*
**Forms:** *make believe* &middot; *made believe* &middot; *made believe*
**Third person:** *makes believe* &middot; **-ing form:** *making believe*
**Transitivity:** intransitive — it takes a clause, not an object: *made believe he hadn't heard*

To pretend that something is true, especially as a game.

1. The kids *make believe* the couch is a pirate ship.
2. We *made believe* we hadn't seen him come in.
3. She spent the whole afternoon *making believe* she was a doctor.

### pay in

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpeɪ ˌɪn/ &middot; *PAY in*
**Русский:** вносить, внести (деньги); отчислять, отчислить; делать взносы
**Base verb:** *pay*
**Separable:** separable, but it very often stands alone: *you pay in for forty years*. With an amount both orders work — *pay in two hundred a month*, *pay it in*
**Forms:** *pay in* &middot; *paid in* &middot; *paid in*
**Third person:** *pays in* &middot; **-ing form:** *paying in*
**Transitivity:** both — intransitive (*we both pay in every month*), transitive (*paid in another five hundred*)

To put money into a fund, a plan, or a shared pot.

1. Everybody in the office *pays in* ten dollars a week.
2. He *paid in* for thirty years and drew his first check at sixty-five.
3. By then she had *paid in* more than the policy was ever going to return.

### read in

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːd ˌɪn/ &middot; *REED in*
**Русский:** посвящать, посвятить (в курс дела); допускать, допустить к закрытой информации
**Base verb:** *read*
**Separable:** separable, and usually split — the person goes in the middle: *read her in*, *read the new analyst in*; what she is told follows *on*: *read him in on the plan*
**Forms:** *read in* &middot; *read in* &middot; *read in*
**Third person:** *reads in* &middot; **-ing form:** *reading in*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To give someone official access to information they were not allowed to have before.

1. Only four people have been *read in* on the project.
2. They *read* her *in* the day she transferred.
3. I'll *read in* the rest of the team on Monday.

### read up

**Pronunciation:** /ˈriːd ˌʌp/ &middot; *REED up*
**Русский:** изучать, изучить (материал); читать, почитать о чём-л.; подчитывать
**Base verb:** *read*
**Separable:** no object of its own — the subject follows *on* or *about*: *read up on the case*; never ✗ *read the case up*
**Forms:** *read up* &middot; *read up* &middot; *read up*
**Third person:** *reads up* &middot; **-ing form:** *reading up*
**Transitivity:** intransitive — always with *on* or *about*

To learn about a subject by reading as much as you can about it.

1. *Read up* on the company before the interview.
2. I *read up* on Texas water law all weekend.
3. She's been *reading up* about adoption for months.

### run to

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrʌn ˌtuː/ &middot; *RUN too*
**Русский:** бежать жаловаться (кому-л.); обращаться, обратиться за помощью
**Base verb:** *run*
**Separable:** inseparable — the person follows *to*: *run to her lawyer*, *run to me*; never ✗ *run me to*
**Forms:** *run to* &middot; *ran to* &middot; *run to*
**Third person:** *runs to* &middot; **-ing form:** *running to*
**Transitivity:** transitive

To go to someone for help or sympathy instead of handling something yourself.

1. Don't *run to* me when the landlord calls.
2. He *ran to* his father the second things got hard.
3. She's *run to* the same friend with every problem since college.

### see over

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsiː ˌoʊvər/ &middot; *SEE oh-vur*
**Русский:** осматривать, осмотреть (дом, помещение); обходить с осмотром

To walk through a building or property and look at all of it.

1. We drove out to *see over* the barn before making an offer.
2. They *saw over* three apartments in one afternoon.
3. The place had already been *seen over* by two other buyers.

### shoot out

### shoot through

**Русский:** пронзать, пронзить; простреливать (о боли); проноситься, пронестись сквозь

### sit down

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪt ˌdaʊn/ &middot; *SIT down*
**Русский:** садиться, сесть; усаживать, усадить
**Base verb:** *sit*
**Separable:** usually no object — *sit down and eat*. With an object it splits: *sit the kids down*, *sat him down*; never ✗ *sit down him*
**Forms:** *sit down* &middot; *sat down* &middot; *sat down*
**Third person:** *sits down* &middot; **-ing form:** *sitting down*

To lower yourself into a seated position, or to put someone else into one.

1. Please *sit down* — the movie is about to start.
2. We had barely *sat down* when the power went out.
