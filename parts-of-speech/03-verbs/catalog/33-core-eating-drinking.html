Core verbs: eating and drinking#

The everyday regular verbs of eating and drinking — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

← The grammar of verbs · All groups

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that misbehave: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no work, no need, no ask and no explain.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: carry → carried, stop → stopped, agree → agreed. Every entry spells all four out, and a Contrast line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

chew#

Pronunciation
/tʃuː/ · CHOO
Русский
жевать, пожевать
Forms
chew · chewed · chewed
Third person
chews · -ing form: chewing
Transitivity
both — transitive (chew your food), intransitive (chew slowly)

To break food up with your teeth before swallowing it.

  1. Chew your food slowly — it helps digestion.
  2. The puppy chewed the corner off my shoe.
  3. She sat chewing gum and staring out the window.
