# Phrasal verbs: get — grow

> Phrasal verbs built on the irregular verbs get, give, go… — where the meaning is in the particle, not the verb.

[← Irregular verbs](README.md) &middot; [The grammar of verbs](../README.md)

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle that behaves as one word, and its meaning is almost never the sum of its parts: *give up* has nothing to do with giving, and *get over the flu* has nothing to do with being above anything. The second thing to learn about each one is **separability** — whether the object may sit between the verb and the particle (*get the message across*, *get it across*) or must follow the whole phrase (*get over the flu*, never ✗ *get the flu over*) — because a separable verb with a pronoun object **has** to split: *give it up*, never ✗ *give up it*. Stress is the audible clue to which kind you are hearing: a true particle takes the stress (*give it UP*, *get it aCROSS*), while a preposition leans on the verb and stays weak (*GO after him*, *GET to me*). The five bases below are among the most productive in English — *get* alone accounts for more than a third of this page, and *grind* for four entries, which is all it honestly has.

### get across

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt əˈkrɔs/ &middot; *get-uh-CROSS* &middot; *across* is an adverb particle here, so it takes the stress — *get it aCROSS*, never *GET it across*
**Русский:** донести (мысль), объяснить доходчиво
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *get your point across* or *get across your point*, and with a pronoun the object **must** split: *get it across*, never ✗ *get across it*.
**Transitive:** either — usually takes an object (*get the idea across*), but the message itself can be the subject: *the warning got across*.
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic and causative: you cause an idea to land (*she gets her ideas across in meetings*), and the progressive is normal — *I'm still not getting it across*. No stative or linking use.
**Passive:** **restricted.** Causative *get* fights the passive even where there is an object: *the point was finally gotten across* is possible but stiff, and Americans say *the point finally got across* instead. The object-less *the joke got across* has no passive at all.

Make an idea land — to say it in a way the other person actually takes in.

1. She never quite *gets* her ideas *across* in meetings.
2. He *got across* the danger in three sentences.
3. I don't think the joke *got across*.
4. Try a diagram if the numbers won't *get* the point *across*.
5. I said it twice and still couldn't *get* it *across*.
6. The ad *gets across* one message and one only.

**See also:** [get through to](#get-through-to), [get at](#get-at), [get over](#get-over)

### get after

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɛt ˌæftər/ &middot; *GET-af-ter* &middot; *after* is a preposition, so the verb keeps the main stress — *GET after him*
**Русский:** подгонять, дёргать (чтобы сделал); взяться за дело
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the object follows the whole phrase: *get after your brother*. ✗ *get your brother after*, ✗ *get him after*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal — very common in American speech, rare in writing
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in both senses, nagging a person (*my mother got after me for weeks*) and attacking a job (*let's get after it*); the progressive is at home in each — *somebody's getting after the landlord about the heat*.
**Passive:** **no.** ✗ *the landlord was gotten after about the heat* is not English, and *get after it* is a frozen idiom with nothing to promote: ✗ *it was gotten after*.

Keep at somebody until they do the thing; or, of a job, attack it with energy.

**1. Nag somebody into acting.** American and informal, usually with *about* or *to do*. You get after a person, not after a task. (подгонять, дёргать)

1. You need to *get after* him about that permission slip.
2. My mother *got after* me for weeks until I called the dentist.
3. Somebody should *get after* the landlord about the heat.

**2. get after it — throw yourself into the work.** A fixed American expression, at home in gyms, locker rooms, and job sites. (поднажать, взяться за дело)

4. Practice starts at six, so let's *get after it*.
5. They came out after halftime and really *got after it*.

**See also:** [get on with](#get-on-with), [get at](#get-at), [go at](#go-at)

### get ahead

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt əˈhɛd/ &middot; *get-uh-HED* &middot; the particle carries the stress — *get aHEAD*
**Русский:** продвигаться (по службе), преуспевать; опережать
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — *get ahead* has no object to place. A target arrives through *of*: *get ahead of the competition*, never ✗ *get the competition ahead*.
**Transitive:** no — intransitive; *get ahead of* takes its object through the preposition
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic. Both the *rise at work* sense (*she works two jobs just to get ahead*) and *get ahead of* (*we got ahead of the story*) describe progress being made, not a state held.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive, so there is no object to promote, and the prepositional passive fails too: ✗ *we were gotten ahead of on price*, ✗ *ourselves shouldn't be gotten ahead of*.

Make progress — rise at work, or climb out from under what you owe.

**1. Do well, move up.** The career and money sense, often with *in* or a bare *get ahead*. (продвигаться, преуспевать)

1. You can't *get ahead* at that company without a degree.
2. She works two jobs just to *get ahead*.
3. Nobody *gets ahead* by keeping quiet in meetings.

**2. get ahead of — outpace or anticipate.** With *of*, either beating a rival or jumping too far forward in an argument. (опережать, забегать вперёд)

4. Let's not *get ahead of* ourselves.
5. Two of our rivals have *gotten ahead of* us on price.
6. We *got ahead of* the story by announcing it ourselves.

**See also:** [get by](#get-by), [get on](#get-on), [get through](#get-through)

### get along

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt əˈlɔŋ/ &middot; *get-uh-LAWNG* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get aLONG*
**Русский:** ладить (с кем-то); справляться, обходиться
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — no object. The other person arrives through *with*: *get along with my sister*, never ✗ *get my sister along*.
**Transitive:** no — intransitive
**Register:** everyday — the standard American choice; British English prefers *get on with*
**Verb type:** action — dynamic, though the *be friendly* sense names a relationship that holds over time; the progressive still works (*they've been getting along better since the move*), and the *be on your way* sense lives only there: *I should be getting along*.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive in all three senses. The other person arrives through *with*, never as an object: ✗ *my first boss was never gotten along with*.

Have a friendly relationship with somebody; also, manage well enough with what you have.

**1. Have a good relationship.** The main American sense. The other person comes in after *with*. (ладить, уживаться)

1. The two of them *get along* fine now.
2. I never *got along* with my first boss.
3. They have *gotten along* much better since the move.

**2. Manage, cope.** Usually with *on* or *without*, about money, health, or age. (справляться, обходиться)

4. She *gets along* on very little money.
5. How is your father *getting along* after the surgery?

**3. Be on your way.** Dated in America, and always in the progressive: *I should be getting along*. (отправляться, идти)

6. It's late — I should *be getting along*.

**See also:** [get on with](#get-on-with), [get by](#get-by), [get on](#get-on)

### get around

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt əˈraʊnd/ &middot; *get-uh-ROWND* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get aROUND*
**Русский:** передвигаться; обходить (правило); расходиться (о слухах)
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle); prepositional when an obstacle follows
**Separable:** no — when there is an object it follows the whole phrase: *get around the rules*, never ✗ *get the rules around*.
**Transitive:** either — no object in the travel and rumor senses, an object in the evasion sense
**Register:** everyday — British English says *get round*
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all three senses: moving about (*he gets around town on an old bicycle*), evading (*their lawyers found a way to get around the ban*), and spreading (*word got around fast*).
**Passive:** **restricted.** Only the evasion sense has an object, and even there the passive is stiff: *the ban was gotten around by their lawyers* is possible, but *their lawyers got around the ban* is what people actually say. ✗ *town is gotten around by bicycle*.

Move about from place to place; find a way past something in your way; or, of news, spread.

**1. Move about, travel.** About mobility rather than distance: how a person navigates a town or a house. (передвигаться)

1. He *gets around* town on an old bicycle.
2. Since the hip replacement she *gets around* with a cane.

**2. Evade something standing in your way.** Rules, laws, and paperwork are the usual objects. (обходить (закон, правило))

3. There is no *getting around* the paperwork.
4. Their lawyers found a way to *get around* the ban.

**3. Spread, of news.** The subject is *word*, *it*, or a rumor; no object at all. (распространяться, расходиться)

5. Word *got around* fast.
6. It *got around* that he was leaving.

**See also:** [get around to](#get-around-to), [get out of](#get-out-of), [go around](#go-around)

### get around to

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɛt əˈraʊnd tə/ &middot; *get-uh-ROWND-tuh* &middot; the particle *around* carries the stress and *to* stays weak — *get aROUND tuh*
**Русский:** собраться (наконец) сделать, дойти до
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the object follows the whole phrase, pronoun and all: *get around to the dishes*, *get around to it*. ✗ *get the dishes around to*, ✗ *get around the dishes to*. (Nearly every three-part verb behaves this way; *get over with*, below, is the one exception on this page.)
**Transitive:** yes — the object is usually an *-ing* form
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic; it names the moment a long-postponed action finally starts (*I finally got around to fixing the fence*), usually with an *-ing* form.
**Passive:** **no.** The three-part verb refuses it: ✗ *the fence was finally gotten around to*, ✗ *my email was never gotten around to*.

Finally do the thing you have been putting off.

1. I finally *got around to* fixing the fence.
2. She never *got around to* answering my email.
3. We'll *get around to* it eventually.
4. He has been meaning to call, but he hasn't *gotten around to* it.
5. If I *get around to* the attic this weekend, I'll look for the box.

**See also:** [get down to](#get-down-to), [get to](#get-to), [put off](19-phrasal-m-r.md#put-off)

### get at

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɡɛt ət/ &middot; *GET-at* &middot; *at* is a preposition and normally reduces to /ət/, with the stress on the verb — though it takes the stress when it ends a question: *What are you getting AT?*
**Русский:** добраться до; клонить к; придираться к
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — *get at the truth*, never ✗ *get the truth at*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday — the criticizing sense is informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in the *reach*, *criticize*, and *tamper* senses (*the raccoons got at the trash*, *his manager is always getting at him*). The *be driving at* sense is a verb of meaning and lives almost entirely in the progressive: *what are you getting at?*
**Passive:** **restricted.** The tamper sense passivizes naturally — *the witness had been gotten at before the trial*. Elsewhere it is marginal or impossible: *the shelf can't be gotten at without a ladder* is stiff, and ✗ *what was being gotten at?* fails outright.

Reach something hard to reach, or drive at a point, or keep picking at a person.

**1. Reach, gain access to.** Physical or informational: a high shelf, a locked file, the facts. (добраться до, достать)

1. I can't *get at* the shelf without a ladder.
2. The raccoons *got at* the trash again.

**2. Be driving at, imply.** Almost always in a question or a negative, and usually in the progressive. (клонить к, иметь в виду)

3. What exactly are you *getting at*?
4. I couldn't tell what she was *getting at*.

**3. Keep criticizing somebody.** Informal, and always about a person. (придираться к, доставать)

5. He feels like his manager is always *getting at* him.

**4. Tamper with, reach corruptly.** Of witnesses, juries, and officials — someone has bribed or threatened them. (подкупить, повлиять на)

6. Somebody had *gotten at* the witness before the trial.

**See also:** [get to](#get-to), [get through to](#get-through-to), [get after](#get-after)

### get away

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt əˈweɪ/ &middot; *get-uh-WAY* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get aWAY*
**Русский:** сбежать, вырваться; уехать отдохнуть
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — *get away* itself takes no object, and what you escape arrives through *from*: *get away from the office*, *get away from it*. ✗ *get the office away*. (Moving somebody else out of danger is the causative *get the kids away from the window*, where the object belongs to *get* — a different pattern.)
**Transitive:** no — intransitive
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic verb of escape and departure (*two of them got away*, *we're trying to get away for a few days*). The fixed *there's no getting away from the fact* presents a truth rather than a movement.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive; what you escape arrives through *from*, and the prepositional passive fails: ✗ *the office couldn't be gotten away from until eight*.

Escape, or simply manage to leave — including leaving town for a break.

**1. Escape.** Of suspects, animals, and anything that slips your grip. (сбежать, скрыться)

1. Two of them *got away* before the police arrived.
2. The dog *got away* from the sitter on Tuesday.

**2. Manage to leave; take a break.** Often about work you cannot walk out of, or a short vacation. (вырваться, уехать)

3. I couldn't *get away* from the office until eight.
4. We're trying to *get away* for a few days in October.

**3. there's no getting away from — you cannot dodge the fact.** A fixed frame introducing something inconvenient and true. (от этого никуда не деться)

5. There's no *getting away* from the fact that we're two months late.

**See also:** [get away with](#get-away-with), [get out](#get-out), [get off](#get-off)

### get away with

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɛt əˈweɪ wɪθ/ &middot; *get-uh-WAY-with* &middot; the particle *away* takes the stress and *with* stays weak — *get aWAY with*
**Русский:** сойти с рук, остаться безнаказанным
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the object follows the whole phrase, pronoun and all: *get away with murder*, *get away with it*. ✗ *get murder away with*, ✗ *get away murder with*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: doing something wrong and escaping the consequence (*he got away with it because nobody checked the receipts*).
**Passive:** **no.** Despite the obligatory object, the three-part verb has no passive: ✗ *murder is gotten away with every day*, ✗ *lying to her twice won't be gotten away with*.

Do something wrong and go unpunished.

1. He *got away with* it because nobody checked the receipts.
2. You won't *get away with* lying to her twice.
3. They have *gotten away with* far worse than this.
4. Kids will *get away with* whatever you let them.
5. I *got away with* wearing jeans to the interview.

**See also:** [get off](#get-off), [get out of](#get-out-of), [get by](#get-by)

### get back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt ˈbæk/ &middot; *get-BACK* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get BACK*
**Русский:** вернуться; вернуть себе; отойти
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes in the *recover* sense — *get your deposit back* or *get back your deposit*, and a pronoun **must** split: *get it back*, never ✗ *get back it*. In the *return home* sense there is no object at all.
**Transitive:** either — no object when it means *return*, an object when it means *recover*
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: returning (*call me when you get back*), recovering something (*she never got her books back*), and the shouted imperative *get back — that wire is live*.
**Passive:** **no** in practice. The recover sense is causative *get*, which blocks it: ✗ *her books were never gotten back*, ✗ *every dollar was gotten back*. English keeps the active — *he got back every dollar he put in*.

Return from somewhere; or get something back into your hands.

**1. Return, come home.** No object; the place arrives through *from* or *to*. (вернуться, возвратиться)

1. We *got back* from Denver on Sunday.
2. Call me when you *get back*.

**2. Recover something lost or lent.** Here the object can sit on either side of *back*, and a pronoun must sit in front of it. (вернуть себе, получить обратно)

3. She never *got* her books *back*.
4. I finally *got* it *back* after three emails.
5. He *got back* every dollar he put in.

**3. Move away — imperative.** A shouted warning: step back from something dangerous. (отойди, назад)

6. *Get back* — that wire is live.

**See also:** [get back at](#get-back-at), [get back to](#get-back-to), [give back](#give-back)

### get back at

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɛt ˈbæk æt/ &middot; *get-BACK-at* &middot; the particle *back* carries the stress — *get BACK at him*
**Русский:** отомстить, поквитаться с
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — the person follows the whole phrase: *get back at your brother*, ✗ *get your brother back at*, ✗ *get him back at*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic verb of revenge (*she spread the story to get back at her ex*), often in the progressive: *he's just trying to get back at me*.
**Passive:** **no.** ✗ *her ex was gotten back at*, ✗ *the coach was gotten back at by the players* — the pattern has no passive despite always taking an object.

Do something to hurt somebody who hurt you first.

1. She spread the story to *get back at* her ex.
2. He's just trying to *get back at* me for the parking thing.
3. Don't post anything to *get back at* them; it always backfires.
4. They *got back at* the coach by refusing to play the second half.
5. There's no point *getting back at* people who have already moved on.

**See also:** [get back](#get-back), [get off](#get-off), [go after](#go-after)

### get back to

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɛt ˈbæk tə/ &middot; *get-BACK-tuh* &middot; the particle *back* carries the stress and *to* stays weak — *get BACK tuh you*
**Русский:** связаться позже, ответить; вернуться к
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — *get back to the customer*, ✗ *get the customer back to*, ✗ *get back the customer to*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in both senses, promising a later answer (*let me check the calendar and get back to you*) and resuming a task or topic (*I should get back to work*).
**Passive:** **no.** ✗ *I was never gotten back to about the refund*, ✗ *that question will be gotten back to at the end*.

Answer somebody later; or return to a task or a subject you left.

**1. Reply later.** The standard American way to promise an answer you do not have yet. (связаться позже, ответить)

1. Let me check the calendar and *get back to* you.
2. Nobody ever *got back to* me about the refund.

**2. Return to a task or a topic.** The object is the work, the subject, or the routine you had stepped away from. (вернуться к)

3. I should *get back to* work.
4. We'll *get back to* that question at the end.
5. It took her a month to *get back to* running.

**See also:** [get back](#get-back), [get around to](#get-around-to), [get down to](#get-down-to)

### get behind

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt bɪˈhaɪnd/ &middot; *get-bih-HYND* &middot; the stress falls on *behind* — *get beHIND*
**Русский:** отставать (по срокам, платежам); поддержать
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle); prepositional in the support sense
**Separable:** no — nothing goes between the verb and *behind*: *get behind the candidate*, ✗ *get the candidate behind*.
**Transitive:** either — no object when it means *fall behind*, an object when it means *support*
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic: falling behind is a change of state (*we got behind on the rent in March*), and backing somebody is the moment support is thrown (*the union got behind the proposal*).
**Passive:** **no.** ✗ *the proposal was gotten behind by the union*, and the *fall behind* sense has no object to promote at all.

Fall behind where you should be; or throw your weight behind somebody.

**1. Fall behind schedule or payments.** No object, usually with *on* or *with*. (отставать, запаздывать)

1. We *got behind* on the rent in March.
2. Two rainy weeks and the whole crew *got behind*.

**2. Support, back.** The American idiom for public, active support — of a candidate, a bill, a plan. (поддержать, встать за)

3. The union *got behind* the proposal.
4. Once the mayor *got behind* it, the money appeared.
5. If everyone *gets behind* her, she can win the seat.

**See also:** [get ahead](#get-ahead), [get through](#get-through), [go along with](#go-along-with)

### get by

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt ˈbaɪ/ &middot; *get-BY* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get BY*
**Русский:** перебиваться, сводить концы с концами; обходиться
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — no object. What you manage on arrives through *on* or *with*: *get by on one salary*, ✗ *get one salary by*.
**Transitive:** no — intransitive
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic; it describes coping over a stretch of time, so the progressive is natural: *they're all just getting by*, *we got by for years without a car*.
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive; what you manage on follows *on* or *with*, so there is nothing to promote: ✗ *one salary is gotten by on*.

Manage — to have just barely enough money, skill, or time to keep going.

1. They *get by* on one salary.
2. My Spanish is bad, but I *get by*.
3. We *got by* for years without a car.
4. She *gets by* with a lot of help from her sister.
5. Nobody in that department is thriving; they're all just *getting by*.

**See also:** [get along](#get-along), [get through](#get-through), [go without](#go-without)

### get down

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɛt ˈdaʊn/ &middot; *get-DOWN* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *get it DOWN*
**Русский:** записать; угнетать; пригнуться
**Base verb:** [get](03-past-equals-participle-vowel.md#get) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes — *get the address down* or *get down the address*, and a pronoun **must** split: *get it down*, never ✗ *get down it*.
**Transitive:** either — an object in the *write* and *depress* senses, none in the *duck* and *dance* senses
**Register:** everyday — the dancing sense is informal
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all four senses: recording (*let me get the address down*), depressing (*the gray February weather gets me down*), ducking (*get down — someone's at the window*), and the dated *dance* sense (*the whole room got down*).
**Passive:** **no** in practice. Both transitive senses are causative *get*, which blocks it: ✗ *every word he said was gotten down*, ✗ *I am gotten down by February*. English says *don't let them get you down*, never a passive.

Write something down before you lose it; wear somebody's spirits down; or lower yourself.

**1. Write down, record.** Usually in a hurry: an address, a plate number, a quote. (записать, зафиксировать)

1. Wait — let me *get* the address *down*.
2. I *got* it *down* on the back of an envelope.
3. She *got down* every word he said.

**2. Depress somebody.** The subject is the weather, the news, the job. The person always sits between. (угнетать, вгонять в тоску)

4. The gray February weather *gets* me *down*.
5. Don't let them *get* you *down*.

**3. Lower yourself, duck.** No object; often an urgent imperative. (пригнуться, спуститься)

6. *Get down* — someone's at the window.
7. He couldn't *get down* off the ladder by himself.

5. It's easy to *grow apart* when you live two time zones away.

**See also:** [grow up](#grow-up), [grow out of](#grow-out-of), [go back](#go-back)

### grow back

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡroʊ ˈbæk/ &middot; *groh-BACK* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *grow it BACK*
**Русский:** отрасти заново, вырасти снова; отрастить обратно
**Base verb:** [grow](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#grow) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** yes when there is an object — *grow its tail back* or *grow back its tail*, and a pronoun **must** split: *grow it back*, never ✗ *grow back it*. Most of the time, though, what returns is simply the subject and there is nothing to place: *his hair grew back*.
**Transitive:** either — no object when the thing returns on its own, an object when somebody regrows it
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic; sense 1 is a process the subject undergoes (*his hair grew back*), sense 2 an act with an object (*he grew the beard back by August*)
**Passive:** **restricted** — acceptable only in generic statements about what can regrow: *a tail can be grown back; a finger cannot*. Awkward for a real event: ✗ *the beard was grown back by August*, and sense 1 is intransitive with no passive at all.

Return by growing after being cut, lost, or damaged — or regrow the part you lost.

**1. Return by growing.** No object; the hair, the grass, or the tail is the subject. (отрасти заново, вырасти снова)

1. His hair *grew back* thicker after the treatment.
2. Cut the basil hard and it *grows back* within a week.
3. The lawn never *grew back* where the pool had been.
4. Her nails had *grown back* by the spring.

**2. Regrow something you lost.** Now there is an object, and it may sit on either side of *back*; a pronoun sits in front of it. (отрастить обратно, восстановить)

5. A lizard *grows* its tail *back*; a person cannot *grow back* a finger.
6. He shaved in June and *grew* the beard *back* by August.
7. It took two years to *grow* it *back* after the chemo.

**See also:** [grow into](#grow-into), [grow up](#grow-up), [get back](#get-back)

### grow into

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡroʊ ˈɪntə/ &middot; *groh-IN-tuh* &middot; *into* is a preposition; the stress sits on its first syllable — *grow INtuh it*
**Русский:** дорасти до (одежды); войти в (роль); перерасти в
**Base verb:** [grow](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#grow) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — *grow into the coat*, ✗ *grow the coat into*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic; every sense is a gradual development, which is why the perfect is so common: *he has grown into a careful, patient teacher*
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *the coat will be grown into*, ✗ *the job was grown into in about a year*. Say *she settled into the job*.

Become big enough for something, or develop until you fit a role.

**1. Become big enough for.** Of children's clothes bought a size too large. (дорасти до (одежды))

1. Buy the coat a size up — he'll *grow into* it.

**2. Develop into a role.** Of jobs and responsibilities that fit badly at first. (войти в роль, дорасти до)

2. She *grew into* the job in about a year.
3. He has *grown into* a careful, patient teacher.

**3. Develop into something larger.** Of small projects that turn into big ones. (перерасти в, превратиться в)

4. The reading group *grew into* a small press.
5. What started as a hobby *grew into* a business.

**See also:** [grow out of](#grow-out-of), [grow up](#grow-up), [get into](#get-into)

### grow on

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡroʊ ˈɑn/ &middot; *groh-AWN* &middot; the stress falls on *on* — *it grew ON me*
**Русский:** постепенно начать нравиться
**Base verb:** [grow](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#grow) &middot; **Type:** prepositional verb (verb + preposition)
**Separable:** no — the person always follows the whole phrase: *the song grew on me*, ✗ *the song grew me on*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object — the person who comes to like it
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic but gradual, with the thing as subject and the person as object; the progressive works: *the town is growing on me*
**Passive:** **no** — the experiencer has to stay the object: ✗ *I was grown on by the album*, ✗ *everybody in the office has been grown on*. English says *I came to like it*.

Come to be liked by somebody gradually, after a poor or flat first impression.

1. I hated the album at first, but it *grew on* me.
2. Give the town a year — it *grows on* you.
3. The name has *grown on* everybody in the office.
4. That painting never *grew on* me.
5. His sense of humor *grows on* people slowly.

**See also:** [grow into](#grow-into), [get into](#get-into), [go for](#go-for)

### grow out of

**Pronunciation:** /ɡroʊ ˈaʊt əv/ &middot; *groh-OWT-uv* &middot; the particle *out* carries the stress and *of* reduces to /əv/ — *grow OUT uv it*
**Русский:** вырасти из (одежды); перерасти (привычку); вырасти из (возникнуть)
**Base verb:** [grow](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#grow) &middot; **Type:** three-part phrasal verb
**Separable:** no — *grow out of those boots*, ✗ *grow those boots out of*.
**Transitive:** yes, always takes an object
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in senses 1 and 2 (outgrowing boots, outgrowing tantrums); **stative** in sense 3, a standing fact of origin: ✗ *the company is growing out of a college project*
**Passive:** **no** — ✗ *those boots were grown out of in one winter*, ✗ *a college project was grown out of by the company*.

Get too big for something, leave a habit behind, or originate in something smaller.

**1. Get too big for.** Of clothes and shoes outgrown in a season. (вырасти из (одежды))

1. She *grew out of* those boots in one winter.

**2. Leave a habit or a phase behind.** Of tantrums, allergies, and phases parents wait out. (перерасти, избавиться с возрастом)

2. Most kids *grow out of* it by six.
3. He never *grew out of* correcting people.

**3. Originate in.** Of companies, laws, and books that began as something else. (вырасти из, возникнуть из)

4. The company *grew out of* a college project.
5. The law *grew out of* a single case in Ohio.

**See also:** [grow into](#grow-into), [grow up](#grow-up), [get over](#get-over)

### grow up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡroʊ ˈʌp/ &middot; *groh-UP* &middot; the particle takes the stress — *grow UP*
**Русский:** расти, взрослеть; повзрослеть (веди себя как взрослый)
**Base verb:** [grow](12-participle-plus-n-vowel-change.md#grow) &middot; **Type:** phrasal verb (verb + particle)
**Separable:** not applicable — no object. Raising a child is *bring up* or *raise*, not ✗ *grow a child up*.
**Transitive:** no — intransitive
**Register:** everyday
**Verb type:** action — dynamic in all three senses; the imperative *Grow up!* is a rebuke, not a separate class, and *grown up* in *she's all grown up* is an adjective, not a passive participle
**Passive:** **no** — intransitive: ✗ *Cleveland was grown up outside of*, ✗ *the mill was grown up around*. Raising a child uses another verb: *she was brought up in Ohio*.

Become an adult; spend your childhood somewhere; or, as a rebuke, start acting like one.

**1. Become an adult; spend childhood somewhere.** The core sense, with a place or a period after it. (расти, взрослеть)

1. I *grew up* just outside Cleveland.
2. Kids *grow up* faster than their parents expect.

**2. Grow up! — act your age.** An imperative, sharp and dismissive. (повзрослей!, хватит ребячиться)

3. *Grow up* and apologize to her.

**3. Develop, of a place or a practice.** Of towns and markets that form around something. (вырасти, сложиться)

4. A whole town *grew up* around the mill.
5. A market for used parts has *grown up* online.

**See also:** [grow apart](#grow-apart), [grow into](#grow-into), [go up](#go-up)
