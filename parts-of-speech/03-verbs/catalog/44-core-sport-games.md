# Core verbs: sport and games

> The everyday regular verbs of sport and games — all four forms, pronunciation, Russian, and three examples that use more than one form.

[← The grammar of verbs](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

Groups 1–22 cover the verbs that **misbehave**: irregular forms, phrasal verbs, and verb + preposition patterns. This tier covers the regular verbs, which were never a category here — which is why a dictionary of 1,291 verbs had no *work*, no *need*, no *ask* and no *explain*.

Regular does not mean effortless. The forms are still where learners fail: *carry → carried*, *stop → stopped*, *agree → agreed*. Every entry spells all four out, and a **Contrast** line appears wherever the spelling or a required preposition is the trap.

### contend

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈtɛnd/ &middot; *kuhn-TEND*
**Русский:** бороться (за титул), соперничать
**Forms:** *contend* &middot; *contended* &middot; *contended*
**Verb class:** regular
**Third person:** *contends* &middot; **-ing form:** *contending*
**Transitivity:** intransitive
**Contrast:** Contend **for** a prize; contend **with** a problem or a rival.

To compete seriously for something such as a title or a prize.

1. Three clubs *contend* for the title almost every season.
2. They *contended* for the championship until the final week.
3. He has been *contending* with a knee injury all year.
