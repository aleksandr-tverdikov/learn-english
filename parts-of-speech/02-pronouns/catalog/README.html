Categories#

#CategoryWhat it coversEntries
1Personal pronounsThe twelve forms that point at the speaker, the listener, and everyone else — with who/whom, the last words in English that still change shape depending on their job in the sentence.50
2Possessive pronounsThe words that answer whose? with no noun attached — mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs, and every noun phrase in English that can take 's and then stand on its own.48
3Reflexive, intensive, and reciprocal pronounsThe -self words and the two reciprocals — the pronouns that bend a sentence back onto its own subject, or pass the action around a group.35
4Demonstrative pronounsThe pointing pronouns — the words that stand in for a noun and at the same time say where it is: near the speaker or away from the speaker, in the room, in time, or in the conversation itself.44
5Relative pronounsThe words that hook a clause onto a noun already standing in the sentence, pointing back at it and filling a slot inside the new clause at the same time.20
6Interrogative pronounsThe five wh- words that stand in for the answer you are asking for — who, whom, whose, what, which — plus how many and how much, and the emphatic and elliptical forms built on them.43
7Compound indefinite pronounsThe pronouns built by welding some-, any-, every-, or no- onto -body, -one, or -thing — the words that name a person or a thing without saying which one.50
8Quantifying and distributive pronounsThe pronouns of amount — the words that answer how much? and how many? without naming what they count, and the distributives that take a set apart one member at a time.77
9Fused relatives, generic pronouns, and dummy pronounsThe pronouns that point at nobody in particular — the ones that swallow their own antecedent, the ones that stand for people in general, and the ones that fill a subject slot English refuses to leave empty.37
10Archaic, literary, and dialectal pronounsThe pronouns no American writes in an email but every American runs into — thou and ye in scripture and Shakespeare, thereof and same in a contract, y'all and yinz and hisself in the speech of half the country.108
