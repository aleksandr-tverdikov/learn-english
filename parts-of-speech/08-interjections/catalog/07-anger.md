# Anger, frustration, and minced oaths

> The noises, curses, and polite substitutes Americans reach for when patience runs out.

[← Back to the interjections index](../README.md) &middot; [All categories](README.md)

What unites this group is pressure escaping. Some entries are raw noises (*argh*, *grr*), some are real profanity (*damn*, *hell*), and a large share are **minced oaths** — deliberate distortions that keep the rhythm and the opening sound of a taboo word while dropping the offense: *gosh* for *God*, *darn* for *damn*, *heck* for *hell*, *shoot* for *shit*. Register is the whole risk here, so the labels below are blunt about which forms are safe at work and which are not, and every minced oath names the word it is standing in for — a learner who cannot see the original has no way to judge the substitute. The full inventory of taboo words, with offensiveness ratings for each, lives on the [profanity page](08-profanity.md); the register ladder on the [main interjections page](../README.md) places them against everything else. One punctuation quirk runs through the last section: the sarcastic entries (*great*, *perfect*, *nice*, *fine*) are marked by a flat, falling tone, which in writing usually means a period — an exclamation point would make them sincere again.

## Raw sounds of frustration

These are not words so much as noises with a spelling. They carry no taboo at all, so they are safe everywhere, and their length on the page is a volume dial: *argh* is a grumble, *aaargh* is a howl.

### argh

**Pronunciation:** /ɑrɡ/, /ɑr/ &middot; *AHRG, AHR*
**Русский:** аргх / а-а-а — стон досады и бессилия, когда всё пошло не так
**Type:** primary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *aargh*, *arrgh*, *aaargh*, *arg*

A groan of frustration or defeat, aimed at a situation that has gone wrong rather than at a person. Extra letters in writing mean a longer, louder groan.

1. "*Argh!* I just lost an hour of work."
2. *Argh* — the store closes in five minutes and I'm still ten minutes away.
3. He looked at the flat tire and let out a long *argh*.
4. "*Aaargh.* Why does this printer hate me?"
5. *Argh,* I knew I should have left earlier.

**See also:** [ugh](#ugh), [gah](#gah), [grr](#grr)

### gah

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɑ/ &middot; *GAH*
**Русский:** а-а! / тьфу ты — короткий резкий возглас раздражения, вырывается прежде слов
**Type:** primary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *gaah*, *gahh*

A short, sharp bark of irritation, quicker and more sudden than *ugh*. It usually escapes before you have found words for whatever is annoying you.

1. "*Gah!* This zipper is stuck again."
2. *Gah,* I hate group projects.
3. She opened an inbox full of spam and muttered a quiet *gah*.
4. "*Gah!* I said the wrong name in the meeting."
5. *Gah* — do not talk to me before coffee.

**See also:** [argh](#argh), [ugh](#ugh), [grr](#grr)

### grr

**Pronunciation:** no standard IPA — a growl with no vowel of its own: the r is held and carries the whole syllable, roughly /ɡr̩ː/ &middot; *GRR (a low animal growl, teeth together, r drawn out)*
**Русский:** р-р-р — рычание в знак досады, чаще в переписке и полушутя
**Type:** onomatopoeic &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *grrr*, *grrrr*

An imitation of an animal growl used to show annoyance, most often in writing or in a half-joking tone. Said out loud it is usually playful rather than genuinely angry.

1. "*Grr.* The website logged me out again."
2. She texted back a single *grr* and a photo of the parking ticket.
3. *Grrr* — somebody took my lunch out of the fridge.
4. "*Grr!*" he said, wrestling with the jar lid.
5. *Grr,* I have to redo the whole slide deck.

**See also:** [argh](#argh), [hmph](#hmph), [ugh](#ugh)

### hmph

**Pronunciation:** no standard IPA — a hum with the lips shut, pushed out through the nose and cut off, roughly /hm̩f/ &middot; *HMF (mouth never opens; the sound is all nose and breath)*
**Русский:** хмф / пф — обиженное фырканье носом: не впечатлён и слегка задет
**Type:** onomatopoeic &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *humph*, *harrumph*, *hmpf*

A short snort through the nose that signals offended disapproval or grudging skepticism. It tells everyone you are unimpressed and a little insulted without committing you to words. The spelled-out *harrumph* is the one form that has a real vowel: /həˈrʌmf/.

1. "*Hmph.* I wasn't even invited."
2. She crossed her arms and gave a loud *hmph*.
3. *Hmph* — that is not what he told me yesterday.
4. "*Humph!* Fine, do it your way."
5. He listened to the excuse, said *hmph*, and walked out.

**See also:** [grr](#grr), [oh brother](#oh-brother), [honestly](#honestly)

### ugh

**Pronunciation:** /ʌɡ/ &middot; *UHG*
**Русский:** фу / у-у — стон отвращения, усталости или досады
**Type:** primary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *uggh*, *ughhh*, *ugghh*

The all-purpose groan of disgust, weariness, or annoyance. It covers a bad smell, a tedious chore, and news you did not want to hear, and it is one of the most common interjections in American speech.

1. "*Ugh,* Monday already."
2. *Ugh* — the milk went bad.
3. "Traffic on the bridge is stopped." "*Ugh.*"
4. She read the group chat and typed *ugh* before she typed anything else.
5. *Ughhh,* I have three more hours of this.

**See also:** [argh](#argh), [gah](#gah), [sheesh](#sheesh)

## Profanity and blasphemy

The real thing rather than a substitute. Everything in this section is fine among friends and wrong in a job interview, a client meeting, or a classroom; the religious ones (*God*, *Jesus*, *Christ*) also give genuine offense to many believers, which is exactly why the minced oaths in the next section exist.

### Christ

**Pronunciation:** /kraɪst/ &middot; *KRYST*
**Русский:** Господи! / Боже мой! — богохульный возглас раздражения, груб.
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** vulgar
**Variants:** *oh Christ*, *Christ almighty*

A blasphemous exclamation of exasperation, said when a person or a situation has pushed you past patience. This is real profanity, not a soft oath: it is a step stronger than *jeez*, which is its minced form, and a religious listener hears it as breaking a commandment rather than as ordinary swearing.

1. "*Christ,* would you slow down?"
2. *Christ* — how many times do I have to explain this?
3. He looked at the repair bill and said, "*Oh, Christ.*"
4. "*Christ almighty,* the meeting is three hours long."
5. *Christ,* I just mopped that floor.

**See also:** [Jesus](#jesus), [jeez](#jeez), [for Christ's sake](#for-christs-sake)

### crap

**Pronunciation:** /kræp/ &middot; *KRAP*
**Русский:** блин! / чёрт! — лёгкое ругательство при неудаче, приличная замена shit (разг.)
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *oh crap*, *aw crap*

A mild swear for something that has just gone wrong, and a family-safe stand-in for *shit*. Most Americans hear it as mild, but it is still a swear word: keep it away from clients, strangers, and other people's children.

1. "*Crap,* I left my badge at home."
2. *Crap* — the game went to overtime and I have to leave.
3. He dropped the tray and said *crap* under his breath.
4. "*Crap!* That's the second flat tire this month."
5. *Crap,* I forgot to hit send.

**See also:** [oh crap](#oh-crap), [crud](#crud), [shoot](#shoot)

### damn

**Pronunciation:** /dæm/ &middot; *DAM*
**Русский:** чёрт! / проклятье — самое частое лёгкое американское ругательство досады (разг.)
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *damn!*

The most common mild curse in American speech, used when something disappoints or irritates you. Said flat and clipped it is pure frustration; drawn out with a rising tone it flips to admiration, as in "Damn, that's good." It is mild to most ears and still a genuine curse to religious ones, who hear the original wish for damnation in it.

1. "*Damn,* the flight got canceled."
2. *Damn* — I meant to pick that up on the way home.
3. He read the rejection letter and muttered *damn*.
4. "*Damn!* I hit reply-all again."
5. *Damn,* this line hasn't moved in ten minutes.

**See also:** [damn it](#damn-it), [dang](#dang), [darn](#darn)

### damn it

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdæmɪt/ &middot; *DAM-it*
**Русский:** чёрт возьми! / да чёрт побери — резче damn, направлено на ситуацию
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *dammit*, *damnit*, *damn it all*

A sharper, more directed *damn*, aimed at a situation that keeps going wrong. It is said as one word with no pause, which is why *dammit* is the ordinary written spelling.

1. "*Damn it,* the file didn't save."
2. *Dammit* — I told you to check the oil.
3. He slammed the drawer shut. "*Damn it all.*"
4. "Answer the phone, *damn it*."
5. *Dammit,* I was two minutes from finishing.

**See also:** [damn](#damn), [damn you](#damn-you), [goddamn it](#goddamn-it)

### damn you

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdæm ju/ &middot; *DAM-yoo*
**Русский:** будь ты проклят! / чтоб тебя — проклятие в адрес человека, а не ситуации
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *damn him*, *damn her*, *damn them all*

A curse aimed at a person rather than at a situation — literally a wish for their damnation, which is why it still lands harder than *damn it* even when it is half a joke. In the third person (*damn him*, *damn them*) it is a complaint about somebody who is not in the room.

1. "*Damn you,* you knew and you let me walk in there anyway."
2. *Damn him* — he borrowed the good ladder again and didn't bring it back.
3. "You were right." "*Damn you.* I hate it when you're right."
4. She read the letter a second time and said it quietly. "*Damn you.*"
5. *Damn them all,* I am not paying a forty-dollar change fee.

**See also:** [damn it](#damn-it), [go to hell](#go-to-hell), [screw you](#screw-you)

### go to hell

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡoʊ tə ˈhɛl/ &middot; *goh-tuh-HEL*
**Русский:** иди к чёрту! / катись к чёрту — враждебный посыл, почти всегда всерьёз
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *go straight to hell*, *the hell with you*, *tell him to go to hell*

A hostile dismissal telling someone they are finished as far as you are concerned. Unlike *screw you*, which friends trade as a joke, this one is almost always meant, and it is the line people remember years later.

1. "*Go to hell.* I am not apologizing for something I didn't do."
2. "He wants us to eat the shipping cost." "Tell him to *go to hell*."
3. *Go straight to hell* — you promised me that was over.
4. She hung up, looked at the phone, and said, "*Go to hell.*"
5. *The hell with you,* then. I'll move it myself.

**See also:** [screw you](#screw-you), [damn you](#damn-you), [to hell with it](#to-hell-with-it)

### God

**Pronunciation:** /ɡɑd/ &middot; *GAHD*
**Русский:** Господи! / Боже мой! — раздражённый возглас; верующие слышат имя всуе
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *oh God*, *my God*, *God almighty*

An exasperated appeal used when someone is wearing your patience thin. In this angry sense it is flat and clipped, unlike the drawn-out *oh God* of shock or dread. Most Americans treat it as mild; devout listeners do not, and to them it is the commandment about taking the name in vain.

1. "*God,* you are impossible."
2. *God* — just pick a restaurant already.
3. "*Oh God,* not this argument again."
4. She rubbed her eyes. "*God almighty,* it's only Tuesday."
5. *God,* I wish this elevator would hurry up.

**See also:** [Jesus](#jesus), [gosh](#gosh), [for God's sake](#for-gods-sake)

### goddamn it

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɑdˈdæmɪt/ &middot; *gahd-DAM-it*
**Русский:** да чтоб оно всё! / чёрт бы всё побрал — сильнейшее в семье damn, груб.
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** vulgar
**Variants:** *goddammit*, *god damn it*, *goddamnit*

The strongest member of the *damn* family, adding blasphemy to the curse. It signals real anger, it is fully taboo at work, in front of children, and around anyone you have just met, and religious listeners object to it twice over.

1. "*Goddamn it,* I asked for this yesterday."
2. *Goddammit* — the power went out right before I saved.
3. He kicked the bumper. "*God damn it.*"
4. "Would you listen to me, *goddamn it*?"
5. *Goddamn it,* that's the third time this week.

**See also:** [damn it](#damn-it), [gosh darn it](#gosh-darn-it), [dagnabbit](#dagnabbit)

### hell

**Pronunciation:** /hɛl/ &middot; *HEL*
**Русский:** чёрт! — само по себе лёгкая досада; внутри вопроса усилитель: какого чёрта
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *oh hell*, *hell no*

A mild swear used alone for resigned annoyance, and used inside questions as an intensifier that sharpens the whole sentence (*where the hell*, *why the hell*). Alone it is milder than *damn it*; inside a question it is noticeably hotter. It is common enough to pass unnoticed among friends and still counts as swearing in a classroom or a client meeting.

1. "*Hell,* I don't know why the server keeps crashing."
2. Where the *hell* did I put my keys?
3. "*Hell* — I might as well start over."
4. Why the *hell* would he say that to her?
5. *Hell,* at this point just cancel the whole thing.

**See also:** [what the hell](#what-the-hell), [heck](#heck), [oh hell](#oh-hell)

### Jesus

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒizəs/ &middot; *JEE-zuhs*
**Русский:** Господи Иисусе! / Боже мой — раздражённый богохульный возглас, груб.
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** vulgar
**Variants:** *Jesus Christ*, *Jesus H. Christ*, *oh Jesus*

An exasperated blasphemous exclamation, usually aimed at a person who has just done something careless or maddening. *Jesus Christ* is the fuller and angrier form. Both are moderate as swearing and strong as blasphemy, so the room, not the word, decides how badly it lands.

1. "*Jesus,* watch where you're going."
2. *Jesus Christ* — who parked like that?
3. He saw the estimate and said, "*Jesus.*"
4. "*Jesus H. Christ,* would you two stop?"
5. *Jesus,* I've told him four times.

**See also:** [Christ](#christ), [jeez](#jeez), [jiminy Christmas](#jiminy-christmas)

### oh crap

**Pronunciation:** /oʊ ˈkræp/ &middot; *oh-KRAP*
**Русский:** ой, блин! / вот чёрт — досада в момент, когда понял, что влип
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *aw crap*, *oh crud*

*Crap* with the sinking realization built into it: you have just noticed the problem, and it is yours. It leans more toward dismay than toward anger.

1. "*Oh crap,* I double-booked myself."
2. *Oh crap* — the dog got into the trash.
3. She checked the clock. "*Oh, crap.* I'm late."
4. "*Oh crap!* I sent that to the wrong Sarah."
5. *Oh crap,* the tickets were for last night.

**See also:** [crap](#crap), [oh hell](#oh-hell), [shoot](#shoot)

### oh hell

**Pronunciation:** /oʊ ˈhɛl/ &middot; *oh-HEL*
**Русский:** ох, чёрт! / да чёрт возьми — стон досады при плохой новости
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *aw hell*, *oh hell no*

A groan of dismayed frustration at bad news or an unwelcome sight. The longer *oh hell no* is a separate and very emphatic refusal.

1. "*Oh hell,* the basement is flooded."
2. *Oh hell* — he brought his brother with him.
3. "*Aw hell,* I left the tickets on the counter."
4. She saw the check engine light come on. "*Oh hell.*"
5. "They want us there at five in the morning." "*Oh hell* no."

**See also:** [oh crap](#oh-crap), [hell](#hell), [to hell with it](#to-hell-with-it)

### screw it

**Pronunciation:** /ˈskru ɪt/ &middot; *SKROO-it*
**Русский:** да ну его! / плевать — бросить дело от досады; приличная замена fuck it
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *screw this*, *screw that*

A declaration that you are giving up on something out of frustration and moving on — the printable form of *fuck it*. *Screw this* aims the same feeling at the situation you are stuck in right now.

1. "*Screw it,* I'm ordering pizza."
2. *Screw this* — I've been on hold for forty minutes.
3. He looked at the instructions, then at the pile of parts. "*Screw it.*"
4. "*Screw it,* we'll fix it in the next release."
5. *Screw that,* I'm not driving four hours for a one-hour meeting.

**See also:** [to hell with it](#to-hell-with-it), [whatever](#whatever), [fine](#fine)

### screw you

**Pronunciation:** /skru ˈju/ &middot; *skroo-YOO*
**Русский:** да пошёл ты! / иди ты — смягчённое fuck you, но всё равно оскорбление
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *screw him*, *screw them*, *screw off*

A hostile insult aimed straight at a person, and the softened form of *fuck you*. Softened is relative: it still ends conversations and starts arguments. Close friends sometimes use it jokingly, but never assume that.

1. "*Screw you,* I did all the work on that."
2. *Screw you* — you knew and you didn't say anything.
3. "You're being dramatic." "*Screw you.*"
4. He told his brother *screw you* and hung up.
5. "*Screw him,* we'll find another contractor."

**See also:** [go to hell](#go-to-hell), [shut up](#shut-up), [whatever](#whatever)

### shit

**Pronunciation:** /ʃɪt/ &middot; *SHIT*
**Русский:** вот дерьмо! / бля! — сильное обиходное ругательство досады, груб., табу при чужих
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** vulgar
**Variants:** *oh shit*, *aw shit*

The strongest of the everyday one-word swears of frustration, and genuinely taboo in public, professional, and family settings. *Shoot*, *sugar*, and *crap* are the polite substitutes built from it.

1. "*Shit,* I locked my keys in the car."
2. *Shit* — that's a police car behind us.
3. He read the number on the screen and said *shit* very quietly.
4. "*Oh shit,* the deadline was yesterday."
5. *Shit,* I think I deleted the wrong folder.

**See also:** [shoot](#shoot), [sugar](#sugar), [crap](#crap)

### to hell with it

**Pronunciation:** /tə ˈhɛl wɪð ɪt/ &middot; *tuh-HEL-with-it*
**Русский:** да ну его к чёрту! / чёрт с ним — бросить дело от злости или усталости
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *the hell with it*, *to hell with this*

A more emphatic *screw it*: you are abandoning the effort out of anger or exhaustion. It can also be pointed at a person or a rule you have decided to stop respecting.

1. "*To hell with it,* I'm going home."
2. *The hell with it* — I'll just buy a new one.
3. She stared at the spreadsheet another minute, then said, "*To hell with it.*"
4. "*To hell with this,* the line isn't moving."
5. *To hell with it,* let's just tell them the truth.

**See also:** [screw it](#screw-it), [fine](#fine), [that does it](#that-does-it)

### what the hell

**Pronunciation:** /ˌwʌt ðə ˈhɛl/ &middot; *wut-thuh-HEL*
**Русский:** какого чёрта? — гневный вопрос; 2. сказанное ровно — «а, была не была»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *what the hell?*, *the hell*

Two distinct uses. **1.** An angry or bewildered demand for an explanation, with the stress on *hell*. **2.** Said flat, with a shrug, a decision to take the risk anyway — roughly "why not."

1. "*What the hell* were you thinking?"
2. *What the hell* — my car was right here an hour ago.
3. "*What the hell* is that noise?"
4. He opened the box. "*What the hell?* These are the wrong size."
5. "Should we split the last piece?" "Ah, *what the hell* — take it."
6. It's my birthday, so *what the hell,* I'm getting the expensive one.

**See also:** [what the heck](#what-the-heck), [hell](#hell), [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me)

## Minced oaths

Every item here is a taboo word with the offense filed off, usually by keeping the first sound and changing everything after it. They are the safe register for classrooms, workplaces, and grandparents, and a few of them now sound so old-fashioned that Americans use them mainly for comic effect.

### cheese and crackers

**Pronunciation:** /ˌtʃiz ən ˈkrækərz/ &middot; *cheez-uhn-KRAK-erz*
**Русский:** ёлки-палки! / ёшкин кот — нарочито дурашливая замена Jesus Christ
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *cheese and rice*, *oh cheese and crackers*

A deliberately silly stand-in for *Jesus Christ*, built to match its rhythm and opening sounds. It is used to be funny about being annoyed rather than to hide real anger.

1. "*Cheese and crackers,* look at this mess."
2. *Cheese and crackers* — who let the dog on the couch?
3. "*Cheese and rice,* that's expensive."
4. Grandma says *cheese and crackers* so she doesn't swear in front of the kids.
5. *Cheese and crackers,* I forgot the tickets again.

**See also:** [jiminy Christmas](#jiminy-christmas), [jeez Louise](#jeez-louise), [mother of pearl](#mother-of-pearl)

### criminy

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkrɪməni/ &middot; *KRIM-uh-nee*
**Русский:** батюшки мои! / ёлки зелёные — устар., старомодная замена Christ
**Type:** primary &middot; **Register:** dated
**Variants:** *crimeny*, *criminey*, *criminy sakes*

An old-fashioned minced form of *Christ*, used for mild exasperation. It sounds gently folksy today and comes mostly from older speakers or from people reaching for a laugh.

1. "*Criminy,* how much did they charge you?"
2. *Criminy* — that dog next door barks all night long.
3. "*Criminy sakes,* slow down."
4. He came in stamping the snow off his boots. "*Criminy,* it is cold out there."
5. *Criminy,* these instructions make no sense at all.

**See also:** [cripes](#cripes), [jiminy Christmas](#jiminy-christmas), [drat](#drat)

### cripes

**Pronunciation:** /kraɪps/ &middot; *KRYPS*
**Русский:** ёкарный бабай! / господи прости — устар., смягчённое Christ, как из старых комиксов
**Type:** primary &middot; **Register:** dated
**Variants:** *oh cripes*, *for cripes' sake*, *cripes almighty*

A minced form of *Christ*, used for mild exasperation or startled annoyance. It peaked in mid-century American speech and now sounds like dialogue out of an old comic strip, which is mostly why people reach for it.

1. "*Cripes,* would you look at this traffic."
2. *Cripes* — the dog just ate an entire stick of butter.
3. "*Oh cripes,* I left the water running upstairs."
4. He got the estimate back from the mechanic. "*Cripes.*"
5. Hold still, *for cripes' sake,* I am almost done.

**See also:** [criminy](#criminy), [jeez](#jeez), [jiminy Christmas](#jiminy-christmas)

### crud

**Pronunciation:** /krʌd/ &middot; *KRUHD*
**Русский:** блин! / ёлки — мягкая замена crap, годится при детях и коллегах
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh crud*, *aw crud*

A soft substitute for *crap*, safe around children and coworkers. It marks small annoyances rather than real anger.

1. "*Crud,* I forgot my lunch."
2. *Crud* — the pen leaked all over my bag.
3. "*Oh, crud.* The store already closed."
4. She caught a typo in the last line and said *crud*.
5. *Crud,* I think I left the light on in the garage.

**See also:** [crap](#crap), [darn](#darn), [shoot](#shoot)

### dagnabbit

**Pronunciation:** /dæɡˈnæbɪt/ &middot; *dag-NAB-it*
**Русский:** ах ты ж, едрёна корень! — устар., деревенская замена goddamn it, ныне только в шутку
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** dated
**Variants:** *dagnab it*, *dad gum it*, *dadgummit*

A rural, old-fashioned scrambling of *goddamn it*, now heard mainly in cartoons, Westerns, and self-aware humor. Almost nobody says it with a straight face anymore.

1. "*Dagnabbit,* the fence is down again."
2. *Dagnabbit* — those kids cut through my yard every morning.
3. "*Dad gum it,* where did I put my hat?"
4. He kicked the tire and hollered *dagnabbit*.
5. *Dagnabbit,* I can never get this lid off.

3. "*Sugar!*" she said, dropping the lid of the casserole dish.
4. He caught himself mid-word and finished with *sugar*.
5. *Sugar,* the sitter just canceled on us.

**See also:** [shoot](#shoot), [fudge](#fudge), [shit](#shit)

### tarnation

**Pronunciation:** /tɑrˈneɪʃən/ &middot; *tahr-NAY-shuhn*
**Русский:** какого лешего! — устар., замена damnation, почти только в what in tarnation
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** dated
**Variants:** *what in tarnation*, *who in tarnation*, *consarn it*

A frontier-era minced *damnation*, almost always used inside *what in tarnation*. Like *Sam Hill* it survives mainly as costume — cartoons, Westerns, and people doing an impression of a cranky old farmer.

1. "*What in tarnation* is going on out here?"
2. *Tarnation* — the raccoons got into the feed again.
3. "*Who in tarnation* parked in the middle of the road?"
4. He opened the bill for the new well pump. "*Tarnation.*"
5. Get down off that roof, *tarnation,* before you break your neck.

**See also:** [Sam Hill](#sam-hill), [dagnabbit](#dagnabbit), [doggone](#doggone)

### what the heck

**Pronunciation:** /ˌwʌt ðə ˈhɛk/ &middot; *wut-thuh-HEK*
**Русский:** какого фига? — приличный what the hell; ровным тоном — «а, была не была»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *what the heck?*, *what in the heck*

Two uses, exactly parallel to *what the hell* but clean. **1.** A demand for an explanation. **2.** With a shrug, a decision to go ahead and do the thing anyway.

1. "*What the heck* happened to my sandwich?"
2. *What the heck* — the door was locked five minutes ago.
3. "*What the heck* is he doing up there?"
4. She opened the package. "*What the heck?* I ordered blue."
5. "Do you want dessert?" "*What the heck,* sure."
6. It's Friday, so *what the heck,* let's leave early.

**See also:** [what the hell](#what-the-hell), [heck](#heck), [H-E-double-hockey-sticks](#h-e-double-hockey-sticks)

## Oath phrases: *for ___'s sake*

A single fixed frame with a swappable holy name. The strength of the phrase depends entirely on which name goes in the slot: *Christ* and *God* are the real oaths, while *Pete*, *heaven*, and *crying out loud* are the substitutions that make the same exasperation printable.

### for Christ's sake

**Pronunciation:** /fər ˈkraɪsts ˌseɪk/ &middot; *fer-KRYSTS-sayk*
**Русский:** ради Христа! / да господи ж ты — самое резкое в этой группе, богохульно, груб.
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** vulgar
**Variants:** *for Christ sake*, *for Christ's sakes*

A blasphemous plea for someone to stop being unreasonable. It is the sharpest member of the *for ...'s sake* family, and to a religious listener it is not exasperation but profanity.

1. "*For Christ's sake,* just answer the question."
2. *For Christ's sake* — the meeting was supposed to start an hour ago.
3. "Pick up the phone, *for Christ's sake*."
4. He threw up his hands. "*For Christ's sake,* I already told you."
5. *For Christ's sake,* it isn't that complicated.

**See also:** [for God's sake](#for-gods-sake), [Christ](#christ), [for Pete's sake](#for-petes-sake)

### for crying out loud

**Pronunciation:** /fər ˌkraɪɪŋ aʊt ˈlaʊd/ &middot; *fer-KRY-ing-owt-LOWD*
**Русский:** да сколько же можно! / ну ёлки-палки — вполне приличное «я дошёл до предела»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh for crying out loud*

A wholesome expression of exasperation that stands in for the religious oaths it imitates. It is the safest way to announce to a room that you have had enough.

1. "*For crying out loud,* shut the door."
2. *For crying out loud* — I've called three times already.
3. "It's the fourth reschedule, *for crying out loud*."
4. She dropped the folder twice in a row. "*For crying out loud.*"
5. *For crying out loud,* would somebody answer that phone?

**See also:** [for Pete's sake](#for-petes-sake), [good grief](#good-grief), [for heaven's sake](#for-heavens-sake)

### for God's sake

**Pronunciation:** /fər ˈɡɑdz ˌseɪk/ &middot; *fer-GAHDZ-sayk*
**Русский:** ради бога! / господи ты боже мой — раздражённый призыв сделать или прекратить
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *for God's sakes*, *for gosh sakes*

An exasperated appeal, normally urging someone to act or to stop. It is mild for most listeners and moderately offensive to religious ones. *For gosh sakes* is the minced version and carries none of the offense.

1. "*For God's sake,* be careful with that."
2. *For God's sake* — he's only eight years old.
3. "Let her finish, *for God's sake*."
4. He read the email twice. "*For God's sake,* that is not what I asked for."
5. *For God's sake,* put on a coat.

**See also:** [for Christ's sake](#for-christs-sake), [for heaven's sake](#for-heavens-sake), [for the love of God](#for-the-love-of-god)

### for heaven's sake

**Pronunciation:** /fər ˈhɛvənz ˌseɪk/ &middot; *fer-HEV-uhnz-sayk*
**Русский:** ради всего святого! — мягкий, безобидный вариант for God's sake, часто у старших
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *for heaven's sakes*, *for the love of heaven*

A gentle, church-safe version of *for God's sake*, common with older speakers and around children. It scolds without offending anyone.

1. "*For heaven's sake,* wipe your feet."
2. *For heaven's sake* — the poor thing hasn't eaten all day.
3. "*For heaven's sake,* it's only a scratch."
4. She looked down the muddy hallway. "*For heaven's sakes.*"
5. *For heaven's sake,* call your mother back.

**See also:** [for God's sake](#for-gods-sake), [for Pete's sake](#for-petes-sake), [good grief](#good-grief)

### for Pete's sake

**Pronunciation:** /fər ˈpits ˌseɪk/ &middot; *fer-PEETS-sayk*
**Русский:** ну ради бога же! / ёлки-палки — смягчённая клятва: «Пит» вместо святого имени
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *for Pete's sakes*, *for pity's sake*

A minced oath in which "Pete" quietly replaces a holy name. It is exasperated but never rude, which is why parents and teachers reach for it first.

1. "*For Pete's sake,* hurry up."
2. *For Pete's sake* — the recycling goes in the blue bin.
3. "Just ask him, *for Pete's sake*."
4. He waited another ten minutes. "*For Pete's sake,* where is she?"
5. *For Pete's sake,* turn the music down.

**See also:** [for crying out loud](#for-crying-out-loud), [for heaven's sake](#for-heavens-sake), [for God's sake](#for-gods-sake)

### for the love of God

**Pronunciation:** /fər ðə ˌlʌv əv ˈɡɑd/ &middot; *fer-thuh-luhv-uhv-GAHD*
**Русский:** ради всего святого, умоляю! — мольба прекратить; варианты с Mike и Pete безобидны
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *for the love of Mike*, *for the love of Pete*, *for the love of all that's holy*

A pleading, dramatic version of *for God's sake*, used when you are begging someone to stop or to act. The *Mike* and *Pete* versions are the minced forms and are safe anywhere.

1. "*For the love of God,* don't tell her I said that."
2. *For the love of God* — somebody turn off that alarm.
3. "*For the love of Mike,* just read the instructions."
4. She had been on hold for an hour. "*For the love of God,* pick up."
5. *For the love of all that's holy,* stop clicking that pen.

**See also:** [for God's sake](#for-gods-sake), [for crying out loud](#for-crying-out-loud), [come on](#come-on)

## Telling someone to stop

Volitive interjections: short commands whose job is to end a behavior. They are ordered here roughly from a protest (*come on*) to a genuine warning (*that does it*), and all of them get their force from a falling, clipped delivery rather than from volume.

### back off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbæk ˈɔf/ &middot; *bak-AWF*
**Русский:** отстань! / не наседай — предупреждение перестать давить, жёстче cut it out
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *back off!*, *back the heck off*, *back way off*

A warning to stop pushing — physically, or more often with questions, pressure, or criticism. It is more confrontational than *cut it out* because it treats the other person as a threat rather than as a nuisance.

1. "*Back off.* I said I would handle it."
2. *Back off* — she has had a rough enough week already.
3. He was standing about six inches away, so I told him to *back off*.
4. The two of them squared up until the bartender said, "*Back off,* both of you."
5. *Back off* a little and let the kid figure it out himself.

**See also:** [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [cut it out](#cut-it-out), [give it a rest](#give-it-a-rest)

### come on

**Pronunciation:** /kəˈmɑn/ &middot; *kuh-MAHN*
**Русский:** да ладно! / да брось — протест против несправедливого; коротко — «давай быстрее»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *c'mon*, *oh come on*, *aw come on*

A protest that something is unfair, unreasonable, or obviously wrong. Stretched out with a falling pitch it means "you cannot be serious"; short and clipped it simply means "hurry up."

1. "*Come on,* that was clearly a foul."
2. *Oh come on* — you promised you'd be there.
3. "*C'mon,* we're going to miss the previews."
4. He watched a third car cut him off. "*Come on!*"
5. *Come on,* nobody actually believes that.

**See also:** [give me a break](#give-me-a-break), [oh please](#oh-please), [seriously](#seriously)

### cut it out

**Pronunciation:** /ˌkʌt ɪt ˈaʊt/ &middot; *kut-it-OWT*
**Русский:** прекрати! / хватит — твёрдый, но не злой приказ прекратить надоедливое
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *cut that out*, *cut it*

A direct order to stop an annoying behavior, most often aimed at children, siblings, or friends. It is firm without being hostile.

1. "*Cut it out,* both of you."
2. *Cut it out* — I'm trying to listen.
3. "You keep kicking my seat. *Cut it out.*"
4. She heard the giggling start up again. "*Cut that out.*"
5. *Cut it out,* that stopped being funny an hour ago.

**See also:** [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [quit it](#quit-it), [enough](#enough)

### drop it

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdrɑp ɪt/ &middot; *DRAHP-it*
**Русский:** хватит об этом! / оставь эту тему — приказ прекратить разговор, а не действие
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *just drop it*, *drop the subject*, *let it drop*

An order to stop talking about one particular thing. Where *cut it out* is about behavior, *drop it* is about a topic, and the sharpness usually means the topic hurts.

1. "*Drop it,* Dad. I don't want to talk about it."
2. *Just drop it* — we are not relitigating the seating chart.
3. "So about the car—" "*Drop it.*"
4. He kept circling back to it until she finally said, "*Drop it.* Please."
5. I asked him twice to *drop it* and he brought it up again at dinner.

**See also:** [cut it out](#cut-it-out), [enough](#enough), [knock it off](#knock-it-off)

### enough

**Pronunciation:** /ɪˈnʌf/ &middot; *ih-NUHF*
**Русский:** хватит! / довольно — властное «всё, прекратили», уместно у родителя или начальника
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** neutral
**Variants:** *enough already*, *that's enough*, *enough is enough*

A flat command to stop, said when patience has run out. It carries real authority and works from a parent, a teacher, or a manager without sounding coarse.

1. "*Enough.* Go to your rooms."
2. *Enough already* — I have heard this story four times.
3. "*That's enough,* we are not discussing it any further."
4. The room kept arguing until she said, "*Enough.*"
5. *Enough,* I don't want to hear another word about it.

**See also:** [that does it](#that-does-it), [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [cut it out](#cut-it-out)

### give it a rest

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɪv ɪt ə ˈrɛst/ &middot; *giv-it-uh-REST*
**Русский:** да уймись уже! / хватит уже об этом — устало, с закатыванием глаз
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal
**Variants:** *just give it a rest*, *give that a rest*

A weary demand that somebody stop repeating something — a complaint, a joke, a piece of nagging. It is blunter than *drop it* and comes with an audible eye-roll.

1. "*Give it a rest,* you've told that story three times tonight."
2. *Give it a rest* — nobody here cares who won in 2003.
3. "You could have taken the other exit." "*Give it a rest.*"
4. He hummed the same eight bars for an hour before somebody said, "*Give it a rest.*"
5. Would you *give it a rest* for five minutes so I can think?

**See also:** [drop it](#drop-it), [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [enough](#enough)

### give me a break

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɡɪv mi ə ˈbreɪk/ &middot; *giv-mee-uh-BRAYK*
**Русский:** да ладно тебе! / не смеши — «это несправедливо» или «не поверю»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *gimme a break*

An exasperated objection that something is unfair or that you are simply not buying it. Depending on the situation it means "stop bothering me" or "that excuse is ridiculous."

1. "*Give me a break,* I've been here since six."
2. *Gimme a break* — he has never done a day of work on that project.
3. "Traffic again?" "*Give me a break.*"
4. She read the fine print. "*Give me a break,* there's a fee for everything."
5. *Give me a break,* it's only my first week.

**See also:** [come on](#come-on), [oh please](#oh-please), [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me)

### knock it off

**Pronunciation:** /ˌnɑk ɪt ˈɔf/ &middot; *nahk-it-AWF*
**Русский:** а ну прекрати! / завязывай — резче cut it out, после первой вежливой просьбы
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *knock that off*

A blunt order to stop, a notch harder than *cut it out*. It usually arrives after you have already asked politely once.

1. "*Knock it off* before somebody gets hurt."
2. *Knock it off* — I can hear you two from downstairs.
3. "Hey. *Knock it off.*"
4. The coach blew his whistle. "*Knock it off,* all of you."
5. *Knock it off,* I'm on the phone.

**See also:** [cut it out](#cut-it-out), [quit it](#quit-it), [enough](#enough)

### quit it

**Pronunciation:** /ˈkwɪt ɪt/ &middot; *KWIT-it*
**Русский:** отстань! / перестань — детское «не трогай», классический протест младшего в семье
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** childish
**Variants:** *quit that*

A short complaint telling someone to stop poking, teasing, or otherwise pestering you. It is the classic sibling protest and sounds young in an adult's mouth.

1. "*Quit it,* you're getting sand everywhere."
2. *Quit it* — that's my chair.
3. "He keeps touching my stuff." "*Quit it,* Danny."
4. She swatted his hand away. "*Quit that.*"
5. *Quit it,* I'm trying to concentrate.

**See also:** [cut it out](#cut-it-out), [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [shut up](#shut-up)

### shut up

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʃʌt ˈʌp/ &middot; *shut-UHP*
**Русский:** заткнись! / замолчи — груб.; между близкими бывает шуткой, с чужими — оскорбление
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** very informal (rude)
**Variants:** *shut it*, *oh shut up*

A rude command to stop talking. Said to a stranger, a coworker, or anyone with authority over you it is a genuine insult and can end the relationship; between close friends and siblings it can be a joking dismissal, and with a sharp rise in pitch it turns into "no way, really?"

1. "*Shut up* and let her finish."
2. *Shut up* — I am not having this conversation again.
3. "You sing worse than I do." "*Oh, shut up.*"
4. The two of them argued until their father yelled, "*Shut up,* both of you."
5. *Shut up,* I can't hear the announcement.

**See also:** [knock it off](#knock-it-off), [cut it out](#cut-it-out), [enough](#enough)

### that does it

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðæt ˌdʌz ɪt/ &middot; *THAT-duhz-it*
**Русский:** ну всё! / это последняя капля — терпение кончилось, сейчас последуют меры
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *that's it*, *that tears it*, *that's the last straw*

The announcement that your patience has just run out and you are about to do something about it. It is almost always followed by what that something is.

1. "*That does it,* I'm calling the landlord."
2. *That's it* — everybody out of the pool.
3. "*That does it.* We're leaving."
4. The alarm went off a fourth time. "*That tears it,* I'm unplugging it."
5. *That's it,* I am never ordering from them again.

**See also:** [enough](#enough), [to hell with it](#to-hell-with-it), [screw it](#screw-it)

## Exasperated disbelief

Questions and adverbs that have stopped asking anything. Every one of these is a complaint dressed as a question, so the answer is never expected, and the pitch usually falls at the end even when there is a question mark on the page.

### are you kidding me

**Pronunciation:** /ɑr ju ˈkɪdɪŋ mi/ &middot; *ahr-yoo-KID-ing-mee*
**Русский:** ты шутишь?! / да ты издеваешься — возмущённый вопрос без ожидания ответа
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *are you kidding*, *are you serious*, *are you freaking kidding me*

An outraged question that expects no answer — you are protesting that something is absurd or unfair. The stress lands hard on *kidding*.

1. "*Are you kidding me?* They canceled it again?"
2. *Are you kidding* — forty-five dollars for parking?
3. "The game's rained out." "*Are you kidding me?*"
4. She stared at the empty pan. "*Are you serious?* I left for one minute."
5. *Are you kidding me,* the deadline moved up a week?

**See also:** [you gotta be kidding me](#you-gotta-be-kidding-me), [seriously](#seriously), [unbelievable](#unbelievable)

### honestly

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɑnəstli/ &middot; *AHN-ist-lee*
**Русский:** ну честное слово! / боже мой — усталый вздох досады: «ну как так можно»
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** neutral
**Variants:** *oh honestly*

Said alone with a sigh, a small expression of despairing exasperation at someone's behavior — roughly "I cannot believe you." It is mild enough for almost any setting, including the office.

1. "*Honestly.* You'd lose your head if it weren't attached."
2. *Honestly* — I turn my back for one minute.
3. "He did it again." "*Honestly!*"
4. She picked the wet towel up off the bed. "*Honestly,* every single day."
5. *Honestly,* I don't know why I bother.

**See also:** [really](#really), [oh brother](#oh-brother), [good grief](#good-grief)

### oh brother

**Pronunciation:** /oʊ ˈbrʌðər/ &middot; *oh-BRUHTH-er*
**Русский:** ну вот, опять! / господи — озвученное закатывание глаз, беззлобно
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *brother*

A weary eye-roll put into words, used when someone is being dramatic, ridiculous, or entirely predictable. It is dismissive but harmless.

1. "*Oh brother,* here we go again."
2. *Oh brother* — he's telling the fishing story.
3. "She says the whole thing is my fault." "*Oh brother.*"
4. He watched the fine print scroll past. "*Oh, brother.*"
5. *Oh brother,* not another software update.

**See also:** [good grief](#good-grief), [honestly](#honestly), [oh please](#oh-please)

### oh please

**Pronunciation:** /oʊ ˈpliz/ &middot; *oh-PLEEZ*
**Русский:** да брось! / ой, ну хватит — презрительное «не верю, это смешно»
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *please*, *oh, please*

A scornful rejection of what someone just said: you find it exaggerated, insincere, or ridiculous. The tone is flat and falling, nothing like the pleading *please*.

1. "*Oh please,* you were nowhere near the finish line."
2. *Oh please* — he's been saying that for years.
3. "I would have won if the wind hadn't picked up." "*Oh, please.*"
4. She rolled her eyes at the commercial. "*Please.* Nothing works that fast."
5. *Oh please,* like anybody's going to notice.

**See also:** [come on](#come-on), [give me a break](#give-me-a-break), [whatever](#whatever)

### really

**Pronunciation:** /ˈrili/, /ˈrɪli/ &middot; *REE-lee, RIL-ee*
**Русский:** ну серьёзно? / вот как — ровным тоном упрёк; с повышением — обычное удивление
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** neutral
**Variants:** *really?*, *oh really*

Said flat, a rebuke: you are pointing out that someone has just done something thoughtless. Said with a rising pitch the same word is ordinary surprise instead, so the tone carries the entire meaning.

1. "*Really.* You parked across two spaces."
2. *Really* — you're going to eat that over my keyboard?
3. "I told her you said it." "*Really?* Thanks a lot."
4. He watched his roommate leave the dish in the sink. "*Really,* man?"
5. *Really,* after everything I did for him?

**See also:** [seriously](#seriously), [honestly](#honestly), [unbelievable](#unbelievable)

### seriously

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsɪriəsli/ &middot; *SEER-ee-uhs-lee*
**Русский:** серьёзно?! / да ты что — раздражённый вызов «ты правда это делаешь?»
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *seriously?*, *are you serious*

An exasperated challenge meaning "you cannot possibly be doing this." Flat and falling it is a complaint; rising, it is disbelief.

1. "*Seriously?* You ate the last one?"
2. *Seriously* — it has been down all morning.
3. "The meeting moved to five o'clock." "*Seriously.*"
4. She found the printer jammed for the third time. "*Seriously?*"
5. *Seriously,* who leaves a shopping cart in the middle of the lot?

**See also:** [really](#really), [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me), [unbelievable](#unbelievable)

### the nerve

**Pronunciation:** /ðə ˈnɜrv/ &middot; *thuh-NURV*
**Русский:** какая наглость! / ну и наглец — возмущённый приговор, произносимый за глаза
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *the nerve of him*, *the nerve of some people*, *of all the nerve*

An outraged verdict on somebody's gall. It is said *about* the offender rather than to them, and it usually arrives about ten seconds after they have left the room.

1. "*The nerve.* He asked me to cover his shift on my day off."
2. *The nerve of some people* — she took the spot I was clearly waiting for.
3. "And then he asked for a refund." "*The nerve of him.*"
4. She read the email twice, just to be sure she had it right. "*Of all the nerve.*"
5. Charging eight dollars for a bottle of water — *the nerve.*

**See also:** [unbelievable](#unbelievable), [honestly](#honestly), [oh brother](#oh-brother)

### unbelievable

**Pronunciation:** /ˌʌnbɪˈlivəbəl/ &middot; *un-bih-LEE-vuh-buhl*
**Русский:** невероятно! / уму непостижимо — приговор возмутительному; ровным тоном — злость
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** neutral
**Variants:** *absolutely unbelievable*, *un-be-lievable*

A one-word verdict on something outrageous. In the right context it can be admiring, but said flatly about a problem it is pure anger.

1. "*Unbelievable.* They towed my car."
2. *Unbelievable* — three hours late and not one phone call.
3. "He denied the whole thing." "*Unbelievable.*"
4. She looked at the bill. "*Absolutely unbelievable.*"
5. *Unbelievable,* I fixed that yesterday and it's already broken.

**See also:** [seriously](#seriously), [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me), [you gotta be kidding me](#you-gotta-be-kidding-me)

### what is wrong with you

**Pronunciation:** /ˌwʌt ɪz ˈrɔŋ wɪð ju/ &middot; *wut-iz-RAWNG-with-yoo*
**Русский:** ты что, с ума сошёл? / что с тобой не так — упрёк не здоровью, а рассудку
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *what's wrong with you*, *what is wrong with people*, *what the hell is wrong with you*

An angry question about somebody's judgment, not their health: you are saying that what they did was so thoughtless it needs explaining. Aimed at a stranger it is close to a challenge; aimed at a friend it is a scolding.

1. "*What is wrong with you?* That's a fire exit."
2. *What's wrong with you* — you cannot say that to a customer.
3. "He posted the whole conversation online." "*What is wrong with people?*"
4. She found the crayon mural in the hallway. "*What is wrong with you two?*"
5. Honestly, *what is wrong with you,* letting the dog drink out of the pool.

**See also:** [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me), [seriously](#seriously), [honestly](#honestly)

### you gotta be kidding me

**Pronunciation:** /ju ˌɡɑtə bi ˈkɪdɪŋ mi/ &middot; *yoo-gah-tuh-bee-KID-ing-mee*
**Русский:** да вы издеваетесь! / этого не может быть — театральнее are you kidding me
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *you've got to be kidding me*, *you have got to be kidding me*, *you gotta be kidding*

A stronger, more theatrical *are you kidding me*, aimed at the universe as much as at any person. It marks the moment something goes wrong on top of something that was already wrong.

1. "*You gotta be kidding me* — a second flat tire?"
2. *You've got to be kidding me.* The reservation was for tomorrow?
3. "They lost the paperwork." "*You have got to be kidding me.*"
4. He watched the elevator doors close in his face. "*You gotta be kidding me.*"
5. *You gotta be kidding me,* I just paid that off.

**See also:** [are you kidding me](#are-you-kidding-me), [unbelievable](#unbelievable), [seriously](#seriously)

## Sarcasm and grudging surrender

Ordinary positive words that mean their exact opposite when the pitch falls flat at the end. This is the single most common place learners take an American literally, and in writing the tell is the punctuation: a period keeps the sarcasm, an exclamation point would make the praise sincere.

### big whoop

**Pronunciation:** /ˌbɪɡ ˈwup/ &middot; *big-WOOP*
**Русский:** подумаешь! / вот уж радость — саркастический отказ впечатляться
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *big whoop-de-doo*, *big deal*, *whoop-de-doo*

A flat, sarcastic refusal to be impressed by whatever somebody has just announced. The *whoop* is a mock cheer, and delivering it without any energy at all is the entire joke.

1. "*Big whoop.* I've been doing that job for six years."
2. "He got a certificate." "*Big whoop.*"
3. *Big whoop* — a ten-dollar gift card for working every weekend in December.
4. She read the employee-of-the-month plaque and muttered, "*big whoop*."
5. So he apologized. *Big deal.* He should never have said it in the first place.

**See also:** [whatever](#whatever), [oh joy](#oh-joy), [thanks a lot](#thanks-a-lot)

### fine

**Pronunciation:** /faɪn/ &middot; *FYN*
**Русский:** ну и ладно! / ладно, сквозь зубы — сдача с явным недовольством, пассивная агрессия
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *fine!*, *oh fine*, *well fine*

A clipped surrender that means the opposite of fine: you are giving in while making sure everyone knows you are unhappy about it. The short, falling *fine* is one of the most passive-aggressive words in American English.

1. "*Fine.* Do it your way."
2. *Fine* — I'll drive.
3. "You have to be there at eight." "*Fine.*"
4. She grabbed her coat off the hook. "*Fine,* I'm going."
5. *Fine,* don't listen to me.

**See also:** [whatever](#whatever), [screw it](#screw-it), [great](#great)

### great

**Pronunciation:** /ɡreɪt/ &middot; *GRAYT*
**Русский:** ну прекрасно! / замечательно — сказано ровно, значит саркастическое «вот беда»
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh great*, *that's great*

Said flat and falling, the sarcastic opposite of its literal meaning: something has just gone wrong. Learners regularly take this one at face value.

1. "*Great.* The car won't start."
2. *Oh great* — now it's raining.
3. "Your flight is delayed four hours." "*Great.*"
4. He found the milk carton empty in the fridge. "*Great.*"
5. *Great,* now I have to do the whole thing over.

**See also:** [just great](#just-great), [terrific](#terrific), [perfect](#perfect)

### just great

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʌst ˈɡreɪt/ &middot; *just-GRAYT*
**Русский:** ну просто замечательно! — тот же сарказм, но уже без всяких сомнений
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *that's just great*, *oh just great*

*Great* with the sarcasm made unmistakable by the *just*. It is almost never sincere.

1. "*Just great.* The Wi-Fi is out again."
2. *Oh, just great* — I left the tickets at home.
3. "That's *just great,* now we're both stuck here."
4. She read the cancellation notice on the door. "*Just great.*"
5. *Just great,* the one day I forget my umbrella.

**See also:** [great](#great), [perfect](#perfect), [terrific](#terrific)

### nice

**Pronunciation:** /naɪs/ &middot; *NYS*
**Русский:** ну молодец! / красиво — ровным тоном сарказм; с повышением — настоящая похвала
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *real nice*, *nice going*, *nice one*

With flat intonation, a sarcastic jab at something clumsy or inconsiderate that somebody just did. With rising intonation the same word is a real compliment, so tone is everything.

1. "*Nice.* You just spilled it on the rug."
2. *Nice going* — you woke the baby.
3. "I told her about the party." "*Real nice.*"
4. He watched the driver take up two spots. "*Nice.*"
5. *Nice one,* now the whole file is gone.

**See also:** [great](#great), [thanks a lot](#thanks-a-lot), [terrific](#terrific)

### oh joy

**Pronunciation:** /oʊ ˈdʒɔɪ/ &middot; *oh-JOY*
**Русский:** какое счастье! / вот радость-то — сухой сарказм о том, чего вы заранее боитесь
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh joy of joys*, *what joy*

Sarcastic delight at something you are dreading. It is drier and more theatrical than *great*, and it usually introduces the bad news instead of reacting to it.

1. "*Oh joy.* Another all-hands meeting."
2. *Oh joy* — my in-laws are staying for two weeks.
3. "You're on the schedule for New Year's Eve." "*Oh joy.*"
4. He opened the envelope and found a jury summons. "*Oh, joy.*"
5. Three connecting flights, *oh joy,* and not one of them on the same airline.

**See also:** [great](#great), [terrific](#terrific), [big whoop](#big-whoop)

### perfect

**Pronunciation:** /ˈpɜrfɪkt/ &middot; *PUR-fikt*
**Русский:** ну просто отлично! / прекрасно — сарказм о новой беде в худший момент
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh perfect*, *just perfect*

A sarcastic verdict on a new problem arriving at the worst possible moment. Flat delivery is the only thing marking it as sarcasm.

1. "*Perfect.* The printer is out of toner."
2. *Oh perfect* — a flat tire on the way to the airport.
3. "They moved the meeting to Friday at five." "*Perfect.*"
4. She looked at the coffee stain on her white shirt. "*Just perfect.*"
5. *Perfect,* now the neighbors are mowing.

**See also:** [great](#great), [just great](#just-great), [terrific](#terrific)

### swell

**Pronunciation:** /swɛl/ &middot; *SWEL*
**Русский:** чудненько! / расчудесно — устар. «отлично», сегодня почти только с иронией
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** dated
**Variants:** *oh swell*, *just swell*, *that's swell*

A mid-century word for *excellent* that almost nobody uses sincerely anymore, which makes it permanently available for sarcasm. Said flat it works exactly like sarcastic *great*, with an extra layer of irony from the word itself sounding old.

1. "*Swell.* The heat is out and it's twelve degrees."
2. *Oh swell* — they gave away our table.
3. "How's the new job?" "*Just swell.*"
4. He watched the tow truck pull away with his car. "*Swell.*"
5. So now I'm paying for both of them. *Swell.*

**See also:** [great](#great), [terrific](#terrific), [perfect](#perfect)

### terrific

**Pronunciation:** /təˈrɪfɪk/ &middot; *tuh-RIF-ik*
**Русский:** великолепно! / чудесно — сарказм, суше и старомоднее, чем great
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh terrific*, *just terrific*

Another positive word turned sour by flat delivery, a little drier and more dated than sarcastic *great*. It usually greets news you did not want.

1. "*Terrific.* Now we're both late."
2. *Oh terrific* — the elevator is broken again.
3. "They want the report by noon." "*Terrific.*"
4. He read the notice taped to the door. "*Just terrific.*"
5. *Terrific,* the game is blacked out in our area.

**See also:** [great](#great), [perfect](#perfect), [just great](#just-great)

### thanks a lot

**Pronunciation:** /ˌθæŋks ə ˈlɑt/ &middot; *thangks-uh-LAHT*
**Русский:** спасибо большое! / вот спасибо — саркастическая благодарность за подставу
**Type:** phrase &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *thanks a bunch*, *thanks for nothing*

Sarcastic thanks for something that made your life harder. Only the flat, clipped delivery separates it from real gratitude.

1. "*Thanks a lot,* now I have to redo the whole thing."
2. *Thanks a lot* — you told her before I got the chance.
3. "I ate the last slice." "*Thanks a lot.*"
4. She wiped the mud off her seat. "*Thanks a bunch.*"
5. *Thanks for nothing,* I waited an hour.

**See also:** [nice](#nice), [great](#great), [whatever](#whatever)

### whatever

**Pronunciation:** /wʌtˈɛvər/ &middot; *wut-EV-er*
**Русский:** да как хочешь / плевать — пренебрежительное закрытие спора, звучит грубовато
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *whatevs*, *yeah whatever*

A dismissive surrender that ends a conversation without conceding anything. It reads as rude to most listeners and is the classic teenage response to being corrected.

1. "*Whatever.* I'm done arguing about it."
2. *Whatever* — do what you want.
3. "You're the one who forgot." "*Yeah, whatever.*"
4. He shrugged at the criticism. "*Whatever,* man."
5. *Whatever,* it's not my problem anymore.

**See also:** [fine](#fine), [oh please](#oh-please), [screw it](#screw-it)

### wonderful

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwʌndərfəl/ &middot; *WUHN-der-fuhl*
**Русский:** чудесно! / великолепно — сарказм о свежей катастрофе, горше и книжнее great
**Type:** secondary &middot; **Register:** informal
**Variants:** *oh wonderful*, *just wonderful*, *that's just wonderful*

Sarcastic praise for a fresh disaster, a little more formal and a little more bitter than *great*. The extra syllables give the flat delivery more room to work.

1. "*Wonderful.* The dog found the birthday cake."
2. *Oh wonderful* — the hotel gave our room away.
3. "The whole system is down until Monday." "*Wonderful.*"
4. She found that the leak had reached the carpet. "*Just wonderful.*"
5. And now the sitter is sick. *Wonderful.*

**See also:** [great](#great), [terrific](#terrific), [perfect](#perfect)
