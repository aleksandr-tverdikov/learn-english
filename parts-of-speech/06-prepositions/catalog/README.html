#CategoryWhat it coversEntries
1The core three: in, on, atThe three highest-frequency and highest-error prepositions in English — the container, the surface, and the point — and the whole system of time and place they divide between them.6
2The core relational six: of, to, for, from, by, withThe six workhorses that hold English sentences together — the words that mark possession, direction, purpose, source, agency, and company, and that shrink to a single unstressed vowel the moment anyone speaks at normal speed.6
3Place and positionThe prepositions that answer where? — the ones that fix a thing in space relative to another thing without moving it.68
4Movement and directionThe prepositions that trace a path — where motion starts, what route it takes, and where it ends up.44
5TimeThe prepositions that put an event on the clock and the calendar — when it happened, how long it lasted, and what it came before, after, or inside of.61
6Cause, reason, purpose, and resultThe prepositions that answer why — what made it happen, what somebody was aiming at, and what came of it.58
7Agent, instrument, means, manner, and measureThe prepositions that answer by whom, with what, how, and how much — the words that attach a doer, a tool, a method, a style, or a number to an event.55
8Accompaniment, addition, exception, and restrictionThe prepositions that add something to a set, subtract something from it, or report that a piece of it is missing.51
9Comparison, similarity, contrast, opposition, and supportThe prepositions that hold two things up against each other — to say they are alike, to say they are different, to name the benchmark one of them is measured by, or to take a side.66
10Topic, reference, respect, role, and capacityThe prepositions that name what something is about, restrict a claim to one domain, or say which hat a person is wearing.61
11Complex (multi-word) prepositionsThe fixed groups of two, three, and four words that behave as a single preposition — in front of, according to, because of, with regard to — and the rules that keep them frozen.186
12Marginal, participial, and postpositive prepositionsThe words that are prepositions only by the job they do — verbs, adjectives, and adverbs drafted into prepositional service, plus the handful that follow their object instead of preceding it.85
13Formal, legal, Latin, and archaic prepositionsThe prepositions that live outside ordinary conversation — in contracts, statutes, hymns, and Shakespeare — and the ones borrowed whole from Latin and French.61
14Dependent prepositions: adjective patternsThe prepositions you do not get to choose — the ones an adjective drags along behind it, so that good takes at, afraid takes of, and married takes to, for no reason anyone can give.248
15Dependent prepositions: noun patternsThe prepositions that a noun drags along behind it — a reason for, a solution to, an increase in — chosen not by the writer but by the noun itself.241
