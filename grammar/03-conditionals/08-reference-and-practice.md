## The master table

Read a row as a sentence frame. The arrow separates the **condition** (the *if*-clause) from the **result** (the main clause); either half may be written first, and the order changes nothing but the comma. The column that matters most is **time referred to** — not the name of the tense in the frame, which is regularly a lie. *If I had a car* is past tense about this afternoon.

| # | Pattern | Structure | Time referred to | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Zero** | *if* + present simple → present simple | any time — always | a result that follows every time, automatically | *If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.* |
| 2 | **Zero, habitual** | *if* + present simple → present simple | repeated, now | a routine rather than a law of nature | *If she **works** late, she **takes** a cab home.* |
| 3 | **Zero, past habitual** | *if* + past simple → past simple | repeated, past | what always used to happen | *If it **rained**, the game **was** canceled.* |
| 4 | **First** | *if* + present simple → *will* + base | future, real | a likely result of a condition that may well happen | *If it **rains**, we**'ll cancel**.* |
| 5 | **First, imperative result** | *if* + present simple → imperative | future, real | an instruction attached to a trigger | *If he **calls**, **tell** him I'm out.* |
| 6 | **First, *going to*** | *if* + present simple → *be going to* + base | future, real | a plan already made, not a decision on the spot | *If the loan **comes through**, we**'re going to move**.* |
| 7 | **First, perfect condition** | *if* + present perfect → *will* + base | future, after completion | the result waits for the condition to be finished | *If you**'ve finished**, we**'ll start**.* |
| 8 | **First, continuous condition** | *if* + present continuous → *will* + base | future, real | the condition is an activity in progress | *If you**'re driving**, I**'ll take** the train.* |
| 9 | **First, modal result** | *if* + present simple → *may / might / can / should / must* + base | future, real | a result that is hedged, permitted, or required | *If the fever **returns**, you **should call** us.* |
| 10 | ***if … should*** | *if* + *should* + base → *will* + base or imperative | future, unlikely but possible | "in the remote case that" — distances the condition | *If you **should see** Dana, **give** her my number.* |
| 11 | ***if … happen to*** | *if* + *happen to* + base → any real result | future, by chance | "if it turns out that" — no plan involved | *If you **happen to pass** a drugstore, grab some aspirin.* |
| 12 | ***if … will*** (willingness) | *if* + *will / would* + base → result | present, real | *will* means "be willing to"; this is a request, not a future | *If you**'ll take** a seat, the doctor **will be** right with you.* |
| 13 | **Second** | *if* + past simple → *would* + base | present or future, unreal | imagined, unlikely, or flatly untrue right now | *If I **had** a car, I**'d drive** you.* |
| 14 | **Second with *were*** | *if* + *were* → *would* + base | present, unreal | the same thing, with the subjunctive *were* | *If I **were** you, I**'d wait**.* |
| 15 | **Second, continuous** | *if* + past continuous → *would be* + `-ing` | present, unreal | an imagined activity in progress | *If I **were sitting** where you are, I**'d say** the same thing.* |
| 16 | **Second with *were to*** | *if* + *were to* + base → *would* + base | future, unreal | pure supposition — the speaker is not predicting anything | *If the dam **were to fail**, the valley **would flood**.* |
| 17 | **Second, *could* condition** | *if* + *could* + base → *would* + base | present or future, unreal | an ability the subject does not actually have | *If I **could afford** it, I**'d go**.* |
| 18 | **Third** | *if* + past perfect → *would have* + past participle | past, unreal | how the past could have gone — and did not | *If I**'d known**, I **would have called**.* |
| 19 | **Third, continuous** | *if* + past perfect continuous → *would have been* + `-ing` | past, unreal | an imagined past activity in progress | *If I**'d been paying** attention, I **would have caught** it.* |
| 20 | **Third, modal result** | *if* + past perfect → *could / might have* + past participle | past, unreal | the other past was possible, not guaranteed | *If we**'d left** earlier, we **might have made** the flight.* |
| 21 | **Mixed: past → present** | *if* + past perfect → *would* + base | past condition, present result | a change in the past that would still be showing today | *If I**'d taken** the job, I**'d be living** in Denver.* |
| 22 | **Mixed: present → past** | *if* + past simple or *were* → *would have* + past participle | standing condition, past result | a permanent fact explains a particular past outcome | *If she **weren't** so stubborn, she **would have apologized** years ago.* |
| 23 | **Mixed: past → future** | *if* + past perfect → *would be* + `-ing` with a future adverbial | past condition, future result | a past change that would have been paying off later | *If they**'d approved** the permit, we**'d be breaking** ground next month.* |
| 24 | **Mixed: present → future** | *if* + past simple → *would* + base with a future adverbial | present condition, future result | an unreal present fact with a consequence still ahead | *If I **spoke** Spanish, I**'d be** in Bogotá next week.* |
| 25 | ***if it weren't for*** + noun | → *would* + base | present, unreal | one thing is the only obstacle | *If it **weren't for** the traffic, I**'d walk**.* |
| 26 | ***if it hadn't been for*** + noun | → *would have* + past participle | past, unreal | one thing is the only reason it turned out that way | *If it **hadn't been for** Marta, we**'d have lost** the account.* |
| 27 | **Inverted second** | ***Were*** + subject + complement → *would* + base | present or future, unreal | a formal second conditional with no *if* | ***Were** I in charge, I**'d cancel** the whole program.* |
| 28 | **Inverted *were to*** | ***Were*** + subject + *to* + base → *would* + base | future, unreal | the formal counterpart of row 16 | ***Were** the dam **to fail**, the valley **would flood**.* |
| 29 | **Inverted third** | ***Had*** + subject + past participle → *would have* + past participle | past, unreal | a formal third conditional with no *if* | ***Had** I **known**, I **would have called**.* |
| 30 | **Inverted *should*** | ***Should*** + subject + base → *will* + base or imperative | future, unlikely | the formal counterpart of row 10 — standard in instructions | ***Should** you **need** anything, **dial** zero.* |
| 31 | **Inverted *were it not for*** | ***Were it not for*** + noun → *would* + base | present, unreal | the formal counterpart of row 25 | ***Were it not for** the rain, we**'d be** on the water.* |
| 32 | **Inverted *had it not been for*** | ***Had it not been for*** + noun → *would have* + past participle | past, unreal | the formal counterpart of row 26 | ***Had it not been for** the backup, we **would have lost** everything.* |

### How to read the table without memorizing it

Thirty-two rows is too many to hold in your head, and you do not have to. **The two halves of a conditional are chosen independently.** Pick the *if*-clause by the time and reality of the condition; pick the result clause by the time and reality of the result. Combine freely.

| Time and reality of the **condition** | The *if*-clause takes | | Time and reality of the **result** | The result clause takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real, always true | present simple | | real, always true | present simple |
| real, future | present simple | | real, future | *will* + base |
| unreal, present or future | past simple or *were* | | unreal, present or future | *would* + base |
| unreal, past | past perfect | | unreal, past | *would have* + past participle |

Line up a row on the left with the row directly opposite and you get zero, first, second, and third. Line up a row on the left with any *other* row on the right and you get a **mixed conditional**. That is all a mixed conditional is: there is no fifth type to learn, only the four halves recombining. Rows 21 to 24 of the master table are the four crossings that come up often enough to be worth naming, and the reasoning behind the whole grid is worked out in [Real and unreal](06-real-unreal-and-time.md).

The one thing that never moves: **the unreal marking goes on both halves, once each**. The *if*-clause carries it as a tense shifted one step back from real time; the result clause carries it as *would*. Neither half carries both, which is why ✗ *If I would have known* and ✗ *If I would be rich* are ungrammatical — they put the *would* in the wrong half, or in both.

### The six sentences to remember

> **Zero.** *If you press this, the light comes on.* (always)

> **First.** *If you press that, the alarm will go off.* (might well happen)

> **Second.** *If you pressed that, the alarm would go off.* (you are not going to)

> **Third.** *If you had pressed that, the alarm would have gone off.* (you did not)

> **Mixed, past to present.** *If you had pressed that, we would all be outside now.*

> **Mixed, present to past.** *If you were more careful, you wouldn't have pressed it.*

## The connectors

*If* is one of about thirty words and phrases that introduce a condition. They differ on three axes: whether they can carry an unreal condition, how formal they are, and whether a clause or a bare noun phrase follows. The fourth column is the one most often got wrong — several of these take a noun phrase and cannot take a clause at all.

| Connector | Real or unreal | Register | What follows | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [*if*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#if) | both | neutral | clause; present tense for future time, never *will* | *If it **rains**, we'll cancel.* |
| *if … not* | both | neutral | clause | *If you **don't hurry**, we'll miss it.* |
| [*unless*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#unless) | **real only** | neutral | clause; names the single exception | *We'll be there by noon **unless** traffic is bad.* |
| [*even if*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#even-if) | both | neutral | clause; concedes the condition | ***Even if** she apologizes, I'm not going.* |
| [*only if*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#only-if) | both | neutral | clause; when fronted, the **main clause inverts** | ***Only if** you ask **will I tell** you.* |
| [*as long as*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#as-long-as) / *so long as* | real | neutral to informal | clause; the condition is a limit or a price | *You can stay **as long as** you pay rent.* |
| [*provided (that)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#provided-that) / *providing (that)* | real | formal | clause; a term somebody imposed | ***Provided that** the check clears, the unit is yours.* |
| [*on condition that*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#on-condition-that) | real | formal, legal | clause, often with a bare subjunctive | *He was released **on condition that** he **surrender** his passport.* |
| [*in case*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#in-case) | real | neutral | clause; a precaution taken **beforehand** | *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* |
| *in case of* / *in the event of* | real | formal — signs, policies | **noun phrase** | ***In case of** fire, use the stairs.* |
| [*in the event that*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#in-the-event-that) | real | formal | clause; for occurrences, not states | ***In the event that** the flight is canceled, the airline rebooks you.* |
| [*supposing (that)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#supposing-that) / *suppose* | both | informal | clause; opens a hypothetical | ***Suppose** they said no — then what?* |
| [*what if*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#what-if) | both | informal | clause; present = real, past = unreal | ***What if** we missed the deadline?* |
| [*say*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#say) | both | informal | clause or noun phrase; "let's imagine" | ***Say** you won — what would you buy first?* |
| [*assuming (that)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#assuming-that) | real | neutral | clause; grants the premise for the sake of argument | ***Assuming** the data holds, we'll publish in June.* |
| [*given (that)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#given-that) | real, treated as true | neutral to formal | clause or noun phrase | ***Given that** nobody objected, the motion carries.* |
| [*granted (that)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#granted-that) | conceded as true | formal | clause | ***Granted that** the cost is high, the alternative is worse.* |
| [*whether (or not)*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#whether) | both, **both branches** | neutral | clause; the condition turns out not to matter | *We're going **whether** it rains **or not**.* |
| [*regardless of whether*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#regardless-of-whether) | both branches | formal | clause | ***Regardless of whether** he agrees, the policy stands.* |
| [*otherwise*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/11-conjunctive-adverbs.md#otherwise) / [*or else*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/01-coordinating.md#or-else) | real | neutral | an independent clause; means "if that does not happen" | *Leave now; **otherwise** you'll miss it.* |
| [*barring*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#barring) | real | formal | **noun phrase**; "unless there is" | ***Barring** a last-minute objection, we close Friday.* |
| [*absent*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#absent) | real | formal, legal | **noun phrase** | ***Absent** a written agreement, the default terms apply.* |
| [*without*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#without) | both | neutral | **noun phrase** | ***Without** her signature, nothing moves.* |
| [*but for*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#but-for) | **unreal only** | formal | **noun phrase** | ***But for** the storm, we would have landed on time.* |
| [*if not for*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#if-not-for) | **unreal only** | neutral | **noun phrase** | ***If not for** the storm, we'd have landed on time.* |
| [*were it not for*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#were-it-not-for) / *had it not been for* | **unreal only** | formal | **noun phrase** | ***Were it not for** the storm, we'd be home by now.* |
| [*should*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#should) + subject | real, unlikely | formal | inverted clause, no *if* | ***Should** the alarm sound, exit by the rear door.* |
| [*if only*](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md#if-only) | **unreal** | neutral, emotional | clause; a wish, not a condition | ***If only** I'd listened.* |
| imperative + *and* / *or* | real | informal | an independent clause; the imperative **is** the condition | *Finish the report **and** I'll buy you lunch.* |

Each connector is given a section of its own in [Alternatives to *if*](03-alternatives-to-if.md), and the whole inventory — with pronunciation, Russian glosses, and five or six examples apiece — is in [the conjunction dictionary's condition section](../../parts-of-speech/07-conjunctions/catalog/06-condition.md).

### The five pairs that get confused

| The pair | The difference | The test |
|---|---|---|
| *if* vs. *in case* | *if* means wait and see; *in case* means act **now**, because it might happen | *I'll buy batteries **if** the power goes out* (after it does) vs. *…**in case** the power goes out* (today, while the store is open) |
| *unless* vs. *if … not* | *unless* names the one circumstance that would overturn the statement; *if not* covers any negative condition — and *unless* cannot be counterfactual | ✗ *Unless she had reminded me, I'd have forgotten.* → *If she **hadn't** reminded me…* |
| *even if* vs. *even though* | *even if* takes a hypothetical, *even though* takes a fact | ***Even if** it rains, we're going* (it may not) vs. ***Even though** it rained, we went* (it did) |
| *whether* vs. *if* | *whether* asserts that **both** branches lead to the same result | *We'll go **whether or not** it clears* — not just in one case |
| *as long as* vs. *provided that* | the same meaning; *as long as* is conversational, *provided that* belongs to contracts and policies | *Stay **as long as** you're quiet* / *…**provided that** the tenant gives notice* |

One more trap, because it is the only connector that changes the word order of the clause it is *not* in. A fronted *only if* forces subject–auxiliary inversion in the **main** clause: *Only if the board approves **will we** proceed*, never ✗ *Only if the board approves we will proceed*. The same happens after *not unless* and *only when*.

## Conditions with no *if*-clause at all

[Inversion](04-inversion-and-formal.md) is not the only way to lose the *if*. English states conditions with imperatives, with participles, with bare noun phrases, and sometimes with nothing but a pause. All of these are ordinary written American English, not slang, and most of them are shorter than the *if*-version they replace.

| Form | How the condition is carried | Example |
|---|---|---|
| imperative + *and* | the imperative **is** the condition | *Finish by five **and** I'll buy dinner.* |
| imperative + *or* / *or else* | the imperative is the condition, the result is the threat | *Hold still **or** this is going to hurt.* |
| noun phrase + *and* | a bare phrase does the work of a clause | *One more word **and** you're out of here.* |
| *with* + noun phrase | a positive condition, real or unreal | ***With** a little more notice, we'd have finished.* |
| *without* + noun phrase | the negative counterpart | ***Without** her signature, nothing moves.* |
| past participle clause | a reduced passive condition | ***Properly stored**, the vaccine lasts a year.* |
| *-ing* clause | a reduced active condition | ***Working** at that pace, you'll be done by noon.* |
| *given* + noun phrase | grants a premise | ***Given** another week, we could rebuild it.* |
| bare noun phrases in parallel | proverbs, signs, and slogans | ***No ticket, no entry.*** &middot; *No pain, no gain.* |
| *otherwise* / *or else* after a full stop | the condition is the previous sentence, negated | *Leave now. **Otherwise** you'll miss the last train.* |

Two things to watch. First, **a reduced clause takes its subject from the main clause**, so the subjects have to match: *Properly stored, the vaccine lasts a year* is fine, but ✗ *Properly stored, you can keep the vaccine a year* says that you were stored properly. Second, **these forms carry no tense of their own**, so an unreal reading has to come from the result clause: *With a little more notice, we**'d have finished*** is unreal past, and *With a little more notice, we **can** finish* is real — the phrase itself is identical in both.

## The unreal frames outside *if*

The unreal past — the tense shifted one step back to mark distance rather than time — is not confined to *if*-clauses. Half a dozen frames demand it, each one fixing the tense that has to follow. [Wishes and regrets](05-wishes-and-regrets.md) takes them one at a time; this is the summary.

| Frame | What must follow | Time referred to | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *wish* + past simple / *were* | past simple or *were* | present, unreal | a regret about the way things are | *I **wish** I **knew** the answer.* |
| *wish* + past perfect | past perfect | past, unreal | a regret about something that happened | *I **wish** I**'d said** something.* |
| *wish* + *would* + base | *would* + base | future or habitual | annoyance — a demand that somebody else change | *I **wish** you**'d stop** interrupting.* |
| *wish* + *could* + base | *could* + base | present or future | an inability you regret | *I **wish** I **could come**.* |
| *if only* + past simple | past simple or *were* | present, unreal | a stronger, more emotional *wish* | ***If only** I **had** more time.* |
| *if only* + past perfect | past perfect | past, unreal | regret, at full volume | ***If only** I**'d listened**.* |
| *if only* + *would* | *would* + base | future | a plea about somebody else's behavior | ***If only** he**'d call**.* |
| *would rather* + past simple | past simple, **different subject** | present or future | a preference about what somebody else does | *I**'d rather** you **didn't smoke** in here.* |
| *would rather* + past perfect | past perfect, different subject | past | a preference about what somebody else did | *I**'d rather** you **hadn't told** him.* |
| *would rather* + base | bare infinitive, **same subject** | present or future, **real** | an ordinary preference — no unreal past at all | *I**'d rather walk**.* |
| *it's time* + past simple | past simple | present | it should be happening already | *It's **time** we **left**.* |
| *it's about / high time* + past simple | past simple | present | the same, with visible impatience | *It's about **time** you **got** a job.* |
| *as if / as though* + past simple | past simple or *were* | present, unreal | a comparison the speaker knows is false | *He talks **as if** he **owned** the place.* |
| *as if / as though* + present | present | present, possibly real | a comparison that may well be true | *It looks **as if** it**'s going to** rain.* |
| *as if / as though* + past perfect | past perfect | past, unreal | a false comparison with an earlier time | *She looked **as though** she **hadn't slept**.* |
| *but for* + noun phrase | *would* or *would have* in the main clause | present or past, unreal | the single thing that changed the outcome | ***But for** her, I **would have quit**.* |
| *suppose / supposing* + past | past simple or past perfect | present or past, unreal | a hypothetical put up for discussion | ***Suppose** you **lost** the file — what then?* |
| *imagine* + past | past simple or past perfect | present or past, unreal | the same, with an invitation to picture it | ***Imagine** you **had** a year off.* |
| *what if* + past | past simple or past perfect | present or past, unreal | a worried or speculative hypothetical | ***What if** they **said** no?* |

### Why they all behave the same way

None of these past tenses is about past time. *I wish I knew* is about this second; *it's time we left* is about a departure that has not happened yet. The past form is doing a different job — it marks the clause as **not the real world**, and English has only the one set of forms to do it with. The traditional name for the form is the **unreal past**, and *were* after *if*, *wish*, and *as if* is the last surviving piece of the old subjunctive: *If I **were** you*, *I wish she **were** here*.

A note for Russian speakers, because the mismatch here causes more conditional errors than anything else in the topic. Russian marks unreality with a single particle, **бы**, and that particle appears in *both* halves of a conditional. English splits the job in two and uses each marker exactly once: the *if*-clause takes the backshifted tense, and the result clause takes *would*. Translating both *бы*'s as *would* is what produces ✗ *If I would have time, I would help you* — a sentence that reads, to an American ear, as if something has gone wrong with the verb. The repair is mechanical: delete the *would* in the *if*-half and shift its verb back one step instead. Three more consequences of the same split:

- **Never *would* in the *if*-clause of a plain condition.** ✗ *If it would rain, we'd cancel.* The real exceptions are narrow: *if you would* in a polite request (*If you would sign here…*), and *if it would help* / *if that would be easier*, where *would* is about willingness or about a further hypothetical result, not about the condition itself.
- ***If I would have known*** is the American spoken version of the same error, and it is common in speech in some regions. It is not acceptable in writing. Use *If I had known*.
- **One *would* per result clause.** ✗ *I would would have called* is obvious; ✗ *Had I have known* is the same error hidden inside an inversion. See the punctuation and error lists below.

## The modal in the result clause

The result clause is where the speaker's attitude lives. Swapping the modal does not change which conditional you are in — it changes how certain, how permitted, or how obligatory the result is. The **row** you are on (real, unreal-present, unreal-past) fixes the tense of the modal; the **modal** you pick fixes the stance.

| Result form | What it adds | Which patterns | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| present simple | no modality at all — an automatic consequence | zero | *If you press this, the light **comes on**.* |
| imperative | an instruction rather than a prediction | first | *If it rings, **answer** it.* |
| *will* | the plain prediction; the default real result | first | *If it rains, we**'ll** cancel.* |
| *won't* | a predicted non-event | first | *If you don't water it, it **won't** survive.* |
| *be going to* | a plan that already exists, not a decision made on the spot | first | *If they agree, we**'re going to** sign Friday.* |
| *can* | an ability or a permission that the condition unlocks | zero, first | *If you finish early, you **can** go.* |
| *may* | permission, or a prediction the speaker is hedging | first | *If the swelling returns, you **may** need an X-ray.* |
| *might* | a weaker possibility than *may* — the most cautious real result | first | *If we leave now, we **might** still make it.* |
| *must* | an obligation, or a confident deduction | zero, first | *If the light is red, you **must** stop.* |
| *should* | advice, or an expectation that stops short of certainty | first | *If you feel dizzy, you **should** sit down.* |
| *had better* | advice with a threat behind it | first | *If you're driving, you**'d better** leave now.* |
| *would* | the unreal counterpart of *will* | second, mixed | *If I had time, I **would** help.* |
| *could* | unreal ability, or unreal possibility — "would be able to" | second, mixed | *If I had a key, I **could** let you in.* |
| *might* (unreal) | an unreal possibility, weaker than *would* | second | *If she asked, he **might** say yes.* |
| *would have* + past participle | the unreal past counterpart of *will* | third, mixed | *If you'd asked, I **would have** told you.* |
| *could have* + past participle | an unreal past ability, or a missed possibility | third | *If we'd left earlier, we **could have** made the flight.* |
| *might have* + past participle | an unreal past possibility — the weakest of the three | third | *If she'd asked, he **might have** said yes.* |
| *would have to* | a consequence that would be forced on someone | second, third | *If we canceled, we**'d have to** refund everyone.* |

Two notes on the edges of the table.

***Should have*** **is not a result form.** *If you'd asked, I should have told you* does not mean what learners intend; it reads as a reproach aimed at the speaker, not as a consequence. The consequence is *would have told you*.

***Shall*** **does not appear here at all** in American English, outside legal drafting. British-tradition materials sometimes give *If I do this, I shall…*; write *will*. The one American survival — offers and suggestions — can sit in a result clause, but it is rare: *If you're finished, shall we go?*

## Punctuation

| Situation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The condition comes first | comma after the condition clause | *If it rains, we'll cancel.* |
| The result comes first | **no comma** before *if* | *We'll cancel if it rains.* — not ✗ *We'll cancel, if it rains.* |
| An inverted condition comes first | comma, exactly as with *if* | *Had I known, I would have called.* |
| Any other connector | same rule as *if* | *Unless you object, I'll book it.* / *I'll book it unless you object.* |
| The condition interrupts the main clause | paired commas around it | *The deposit, if the inspection fails, is refundable.* |
| A fronted *only if* | no comma; the main clause inverts instead | *Only if you ask will I tell you.* |
| *Otherwise* / *or else* between two clauses | semicolon or period before it; a comma after it is optional | *Leave now; otherwise, you'll miss it.* |
| A conditional question | the question mark goes at the very end | *If she calls, what should I say?* |
| *If so* / *If not* as an opener | comma after it | *If not, we'll try Tuesday.* |
| Two conditions joined by *and* or *or* | no comma between them | *If it rains or the field floods, the game is off.* |
| A condition added as an afterthought | a dash or a comma marks the pause | *We'll take the early flight — unless you'd rather sleep in.* |
| *If only* standing alone | a period in edited prose; the exclamation point is for dialogue | *If only I'd listened.* |

The first two rows cover ninety percent of the cases, and they are one rule seen from two sides: **a subordinate clause that comes first needs a comma to show where it ends; one that comes second does not, because the reader can already see where it began.** That is not a conditional rule at all — it is the same rule that governs *when*, *because*, and *although*.

## What cannot go in an *if*-clause

The tables above say what to write. This one says what the *if*-clause refuses, and gives the repair for each. Every row here is a sentence that Russian, Spanish, or French would let you build.

| Blocked in the *if*-clause | Why | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| ✗ *If it **will rain** tomorrow…* | the clause is already future by position; *will* is redundant | *If it **rains** tomorrow…* |
| ✗ *If I **would have** time…* | *would* belongs to the result half, once | *If I **had** time…* |
| ✗ *If I **would have known**…* | the same error wearing a perfect | *If I **had known**…* |
| ✗ *Had I **have** known…* | an extra *have* smuggled into the inversion | ***Had I known**…* |
| ✗ *If I **would be** rich…* | *would* again, this time replacing the unreal past | *If I **were** rich…* |
| ✗ *If he **will be** ready by six, we'll leave.* | future *will* | *If he**'s** ready by six…* |
| ✗ *Unless she **had** reminded me…* | *unless* cannot carry a counterfactual | *If she **hadn't** reminded me…* |
| ✗ *Unless you **don't** hurry…* | *unless* is already negative; the second negative cancels it | *Unless you **hurry**…* |
| ✗ *If it **would rain**, the crops would recover.* | two *woulds*, one condition | *If it **rained**…* |
| ✗ *If he **would come** tomorrow…* | plan or prediction, not willingness | *If he **comes** tomorrow…* |
| ✗ *Even though* + a hypothetical | *even though* takes facts only | *Even **if** it rains tomorrow…* |
| ✗ *In case* used for an ordinary condition | *in case* means a precaution taken first | *We'll leave **if** it snows* / *We'll leave early **in case** it snows* |

Three forms that look like violations and are not:

- ***If you would sign here***, *we can finish up.* — *would* here means "be willing to," which is what the sentence is about. Same with *if you'll wait a moment*.
- ***If that would be easier***, *I can call instead.* — the *if*-clause contains its own hypothetical result, not the condition's verb.
- ***If he should call***, *take a message.* — *should* is not obligation here; it is the remoteness marker of master-table row 10.

## Register: the same condition at four altitudes

Several patterns mean the same thing and differ only in how formal they sound. This is what to reach for when a sentence is correct but pitched wrong.

| Meaning | Conversational | Neutral | Formal | Legal or ceremonial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a future condition | *If it rains…* | *If it rains…* | *In the event that it rains…* | *In the event of rain…* |
| an unlikely future condition | *If you happen to see him…* | *If you should see him…* | *Should you see him…* | *Should the party fail to appear…* |
| an unreal present condition | *If I was in charge…* | *If I were in charge…* | *Were I in charge…* | *Were the trustee to decline…* |
| an unreal past condition | *If I'd known…* | *If I had known…* | *Had I known…* | *Had the notice been served…* |
| a stipulated term | *as long as you pay* | *provided you pay* | *provided that the tenant pays* | *on condition that the tenant pay* |
| the sole reason something failed | *if it hadn't been for the storm* | *if not for the storm* | *but for the storm* | *had it not been for the storm* |

The registers, and the legal and literary writing that lives in the right-hand columns, are covered in [Inversion and formal conditionals](04-inversion-and-formal.md). Two register facts worth stating plainly here. First, **the inverted forms are not more correct — they are more formal**, and using them in speech sounds theatrical. Second, ***if I was*** **is genuinely common in American speech** in ordinary second conditionals (*If I was younger, I'd try it*), but the fixed advice frame keeps *were*: everybody says *If I were you*, and *If I was you* reads as an error even to speakers who otherwise use *was*. In writing, use *were* throughout.

## Conditionals under a reporting verb

A conditional inside reported speech backshifts only if it has anywhere left to go — and the unreal ones do not.

| Type | Direct | Reported after *said* | What moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | *"If you heat ice, it melts."* | *He said that if you **heated** ice, it **melted**.* — or, just as normally, *heat … melts* | both verbs, optionally |
| First | *"If it rains, we'll cancel."* | *She said that if it **rained**, they **would cancel**.* | both verbs |
| Second | *"If I had a car, I'd drive you."* | *He said that if he **had** a car, he **would drive** me.* | nothing — already unreal past |
| Third | *"If I'd known, I would have called."* | *She said that if she**'d known**, she **would have called**.* | nothing — already at the floor |
| Mixed | *"If I'd taken it, I'd be in Denver."* | *He said that if he**'d taken** it, he**'d be** in Denver.* | nothing |

The rule behind the table: **the unreal past is not a real tense, so there is nothing for a past reporting verb to agree with.** The full argument is in [Backshift](../01-reported-speech/02-backshift.md#what-backshift-never-touches), and the reported-conditional cases are worked through in [Special cases](../01-reported-speech/08-special-cases.md).

## Common mistakes

The dozen errors that account for most of what goes wrong, collected here so the practice below has something to check against. Every one of them is diagnosed at length in [Common mistakes with conditionals](07-common-mistakes.md), including the Russian-interference cases that explain why several of them survive years of otherwise good English.

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *If it **will rain** tomorrow, we'll cancel.* | *If it **rains** tomorrow, we'll cancel.* | The *if*-clause uses the present for future time. Position, not tense, makes it future. |
| *If I **would have known**, I'd have called.* | *If I **had known**, I'd have called.* | One unreal marker per clause: past perfect in the condition, *would have* in the result. |
| *If I **would be** rich, I'd buy a boat.* | *If I **were** rich, I'd buy a boat.* | Same error in the present. *Would* never appears in the condition half. |
| ***Had I have known**, I'd have said something.* | ***Had I known**, I'd have said something.* | The inversion is *had* + subject + past participle. The second *have* has no job. |
| *Unless you **don't** hurry, we'll miss it.* | *Unless you **hurry**, we'll miss it.* | *Unless* already means "if not." Adding *not* reverses the sentence. |
| ***Unless** she had reminded me, I'd have forgotten.* | ***If** she **hadn't** reminded me, I'd have forgotten.* | *Unless* cannot carry a counterfactual; only *if … not* can. |
| *I wish I **would know** the answer.* | *I wish I **knew** the answer.* | *Wish* + *would* is for somebody else's behavior, and never for a state. |
| *I wish I **have** more time.* | *I wish I **had** more time.* | *Wish* requires the unreal past. The present tense after it is impossible. |
| *It's time we **go** home.* | *It's time we **went** home.* | *It's time* + subject takes the unreal past. With no subject, use the infinitive: *It's time to go.* |
| *I'd rather you **will call** tomorrow.* | *I'd rather you **called** tomorrow.* | *Would rather* + a different subject takes the unreal past, never a future. |
| *We'll cancel**,** if the storm hits.* | *We'll cancel if the storm hits.* | No comma before a trailing subordinate clause. |
| ***Even though** it rains tomorrow, we're going.* | ***Even if** it rains tomorrow, we're going.* | *Even though* takes a fact; a future hypothesis needs *even if*. |
| *If I had a car, I **will** drive you.* | *If I had a car, I **would** drive you.* | Half-shifting. Once the condition goes unreal, the result must follow. |
| *Take an umbrella **if** it rains later.* (meaning: take it now) | *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains later.* | *If* waits for the event; *in case* acts before it. |

## Quick reference

**Build any conditional in four steps.**

1. **Time the condition, and ask whether it is real.** Real and general → present simple. Real and future → present simple. Unreal now → past simple or *were*. Unreal in the past → past perfect.
2. **Time the result, and ask the same question.** Real and general → present simple. Real and future → *will*. Unreal now or later → *would*. Unreal in the past → *would have* + past participle.
3. **Choose the modal, not the tense.** Step 2 fixed the tense of the result; swap *will* for *may*, *might*, *can*, *must*, or *should*, or swap *would* for *could* or *might*, to set how certain or how permitted the result is.
4. **Punctuate by position.** Condition first → comma. Result first → no comma.

**The three markers, one each.**

| Marker | Where it goes | Never |
|---|---|---|
| the backshifted tense | the *if*-clause | never in the result clause — that half takes a modal instead |
| *would* | the result clause | never in the *if*-clause of a plain condition |
| the inversion (*were*, *had*, *should*) | replaces *if*, condition clause only | never with *if* still present — ✗ *If had I known* |

**The whole system in one grid.**

```
        CONDITION                        RESULT
  ------------------------------   ------------------------------
  real, always    present simple    present simple   zero
  real, future    present simple    will + base      first
  unreal, now     past / were       would + base     second
  unreal, past    past perfect      would have + V3  third

  any left-hand row + any non-matching right-hand row = mixed
  drop the "if", invert were / had / should       = formal
```

## Questions, negatives, and short forms

A conditional is a sentence like any other: it can be asked, negated, and cut short. Only one of these operations has a rule worth stating twice, and it is the first.

| Operation | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Yes-or-no question | invert the **result** clause only; the *if*-clause keeps statement order | *If it rains, **will we** cancel?* |
| Wh-question | wh-word, then the inverted result clause | *If she calls, **what should** I say?* |
| Unreal question | the same inversion, one or two steps back | *If you had the money, **would you** buy it?* &middot; *If you'd known, **would you have** gone?* |
| A question about the condition | *What if …?* replaces the whole frame | ***What if** she says no?* |
| Negative condition | negate the verb in the *if*-clause, or switch to *unless* | *If you **don't** call, I'll assume you're coming.* |
| Negative result | negate the modal | *If you don't water it, it **won't** survive.* |
| Both halves negative | perfectly normal, and common in the third conditional | *If he **hadn't** called, I **wouldn't have** known.* |
| Negative inversion | *not* goes **after** the subject and is never contracted | ***Had I not** seen it myself…* — not ✗ *Hadn't I seen it* |
| Short answer | the modal and *have* survive, everything else is dropped | *"Would you have told her?" "**I would have**, yes."* |
| Reduced *if*-clause | *if* + adjective or noun, with the subject and *be* deleted | *Send the file **if possible**.* &middot; *Correct the totals **if necessary**.* |
| *If so* / *If not* | one of these replaces an entire repeated clause | *Are you coming? **If so**, bring a chair.* |

The rule to fix in place: **the *if*-clause never inverts to form a question.** ✗ *If will it rain, will we cancel?* is the error, and it is a natural one, because English does invert an *if*-clause for a different purpose — to delete the *if* (*Had I known*, *Should you need*). One inversion replaces *if*; the other asks a question; they cannot both happen in the same clause.

## The adverbial that tells you which row you are on

The fastest way to identify a conditional — your own or somebody else's — is to ignore the verbs for a second and read the time words. They are rarely wrong, and they are what makes mixed conditionals visible.

| Adverbial in the condition | Adverbial in the result | What you have | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| *always*, *every time*, *whenever* | none, or the same | zero | *Whenever it freezes, the pipes rattle.* |
| *tomorrow*, *next week*, *by Friday* | *then*, *later*, none | first | *If it freezes tonight, the pipes will rattle.* |
| none, or *now*, *these days* | *now*, *at the moment* | second | *If I had a car, I'd drive you.* |
| *yesterday*, *last year*, *back then* | *at the time*, *then* | third | *If I'd had a car last year, I'd have driven you.* |
| *last year*, *back in March* | *now*, *today*, *at this point* | mixed, past → present | *If I'd bought the car last year, I'd be driving you now.* |
| none, or *generally*, *these days* | *last month*, *yesterday* | mixed, present → past | *If I had a car, I'd have driven you last week.* |
| *last month*, *in the spring* | *next week*, *in June* | mixed, past → future | *If the permit had cleared in the spring, we'd be opening next week.* |

**When the two halves carry time words from different rows, the sentence is mixed** — and it has to be, because the time words are the facts and the verb forms are only the bookkeeping. Learners who build a conditional from the verb forms first and then attach adverbials produce sentences like ✗ *If I'd bought the car last year, I'd have been driving you now*, where the result form says "closed past" and the adverbial says "this minute." Set the adverbials first, then pick the forms to match them.

## Answers

Each answer gives the form and the reason it is that form. Where two answers are both standard American English, the alternative is given too.

### A. Put the verb in the right form

1. **heat … melts** — Zero conditional: a result that follows every time, so present simple in both halves.
2. **is … will drive** — First conditional: the condition is future but takes the present, and the result takes *will*. Not ✗ *If the train will be late*.
3. **calls … take** — First conditional with an imperative result. The instruction needs no modal at all.
4. **have already finished … will send** — A present perfect condition: the sending waits until the form is complete. *Already* sits between *have* and the participle.
5. **won't go … improves** — *Unless* takes the present for future time; the negation of the sentence lives in the main clause, not in the *unless*-clause.
6. **were … wouldn't sign** — Second conditional in the fixed advice frame. *If I were you* keeps *were* even for speakers who say *was* elsewhere.
7. **had … would call** — Second conditional: *right now* proves the time is present, although the verb is past in form.
8. **were to offer … would … take** — *Were to* frames the offer as pure supposition. The result clause keeps ordinary *would* + base.
9. **had left … would be sitting** — Mixed, past condition and present result: *at six* is past, *right now* is present, so the halves come from different rows.
10. **had known … would have submitted** — Third conditional: the deadline is gone and both halves are unreal past.
11. **hadn't lost … would be** — Mixed past → present: the losing is over, but the absence from Lisbon is happening *today*.
12. **were … wouldn't have had to** — Mixed present → past: carelessness is a standing trait, the borrowing was one event *last month*.
13. **had come … would be flying** — Mixed past → future: *last week* is past, *on Sunday* is still ahead.
14. **should need** — The remote-possibility frame: "in the unlikely case that you need anything." *If you need* would be neutral and equally correct, just less deferential.
15. **happen to see** — Chance rather than plan; nothing is being arranged.
16. **weren't … wouldn't be able to** — Present unreal: the scholarship does exist, so the degree is not actually in danger.
17. **hadn't been … would have lost** — Past unreal: *last spring* fixes the whole sentence in closed past time.
18. **had been paying … wouldn't have asked** — Third conditional with a continuous condition: an imagined past activity in progress. *Had paid* is equally correct and slightly flatter.
19. **spoke** — *Wish* + unreal past for a regret about now. Not ✗ *I wish I speak*.
20. **hadn't told** — *Wish* + past perfect, because the telling already happened.
21. **would turn** — *Wish* + *would*: somebody else's repeated behavior, and the speaker wants it to change. *Every Friday* is the clue.
22. **had booked** — *If only* + past perfect for a regret about a past action; the emotional cousin of *I wish we had booked*.
23. **didn't mention** — *Would rather* with a different subject takes the unreal past, never an infinitive or a future.
24. **went** — *It's time* + a subject takes the unreal past. With no subject the frame switches to an infinitive: *It's time to go home.*
25. **owned** — *As if* + unreal past, because the rest of the sentence says the comparison is false.

### B. Choose the form that fits

1. **were** — The fixed frame *If I were you* resists *was* even in casual speech.
2. **Unless** — It names the single circumstance that would change the outcome. *If not* would need a verb after it: *If you don't tell her*.
3. **if** — The sentence is counterfactual, and *unless* cannot carry a counterfactual.
4. **in case** — The cash is taken now, as a precaution against something that may not happen.
5. **if** — Here the payment happens only when the reader turns out to be working: wait and see.
6. **Even if** — Tomorrow's apology is a hypothesis, so the *if*-form is required.
7. **Even though** — Last night's apology is a fact, so the concessive form is required.
8. **whether** — Both branches lead to the same result, which is precisely what *whether … or not* asserts.
9. **as long as** — A condition imposed as the price of the loan. *In case* would mean you filled the tank as a precaution.
10. **rings** — No *will* in the *if*-clause; position already makes it future.
11. **had** — The condition half of a second conditional takes the past simple. ✗ *would have* is the single most common import error.
12. **had known** — The same rule one step further back, in the third conditional.
13. **Had** — The inverted third conditional is *had* + subject + participle. *Would* is not a connector and cannot open a condition.
14. **owned** — *As if* + unreal past, because the second half of the sentence says the comparison is false.
15. **left** — *It's time* + subject requires the unreal past. *It's time to leave*, with no subject, would take the infinitive.
16. **didn't** — *Would rather* + a different subject takes the unreal past.
17. **Should** — The inverted remote-condition frame, standard in service and business writing.
18. **were to reject** — A hypothetical future supposition. *Would* never appears in the condition half.

### C. Rewrite with inversion

1. **Had I known, I would have said something.** — *Had* + subject + past participle. The *if* goes, and nothing else changes.
2. **Should you need help, ask for Dana at the desk.** — *Should* + subject + base form; the imperative result is untouched.
3. **Were she here, she'd tell you exactly the same thing.** — *Were* + subject. An inversion never uses *was*, whatever the speaker would say with *if*.
4. **Had it not been for the neighbors, the fire would have spread.** — In an inversion the *not* follows the subject, and the negative is never contracted.
5. **Were the deal to fall through, we'd still keep the deposit.** — The *were to* pattern inverts as *were* + subject + *to* + base.
6. **Had they not delayed the permit, we'd be open by now.** — *Not* after the subject again; the mixed result clause is unaffected by the inversion.
7. **Were it not for her patience, I'd have quit in the first week.** — The present-unreal frame with a past result — the inversion does not force the halves to match.
8. **Had you asked me directly, I'd have told you.**
9. **Should anyone call, take a message.**
10. **Were I in your position, I'd wait a week before answering.**

One rule covers items 4 and 6: **an inverted condition is never contracted in the negative.** ✗ *Hadn't I known* and ✗ *Weren't it for her* are questions, not conditions. Write *Had I not known*, *Were it not for her*.

### D. Rewrite as a mixed conditional

1. **If I had studied engineering, I'd be working at the plant now.** — Past cause, present result: past perfect in the condition, plain *would* in the result.
2. **If she hadn't missed the deadline, she'd be on the list.** — Both polarities flip: a real negative outcome becomes an unreal positive one.
3. **If he were a careful driver, he wouldn't have gotten the ticket.** — A standing trait explains a single past event. American English uses *gotten* here.
4. **If I spoke Japanese, I wouldn't have turned down the transfer.** — Present ability, past decision.
5. **If they had renewed the lease, we wouldn't be moving in June.** — Past decision, arrangement still ahead; *would be moving* carries the future.
6. **If we hadn't taken the earlier flight, we'd be stuck in Denver.** — Reversing a real past action leaves an unreal present.
7. **If she weren't afraid of flying, she wouldn't have driven all the way to Tucson.** — The fear is permanent, the drive was one event.
8. **If the contractor hadn't quit, we'd be finished by August.** — Past cause, future result; *by August* is what makes it a third variety rather than a plain mixed present.

### E. Say it in English

1. **I wish I had a driver's license.** — A state now, so *wish* + unreal past. ✗ *I wish I have* is impossible.
2. **I wish I hadn't said that.** — Or, with more feeling, *If only I hadn't said that.* A past event takes the past perfect.
3. **I'd rather you didn't smoke inside.** — The unreal past after *would rather* is what makes it polite; *Please don't smoke* is blunter.
4. **It's about time he got a job.** — *About time* carries the impatience; the verb after it is unreal past.
5. **I would have come if I hadn't been sick.** — A closed past, so the third conditional throughout.
6. **If I hadn't changed jobs last year, I'd be in Moscow right now.** — Mixed past → present: *last year* against *right now*.
7. **If it weren't for my sister, I would never have applied.** — A standing fact with a past result. The formal version is *But for my sister, I would never have applied.*
8. **I'll come tomorrow unless something comes up.** — Real future: present tense after *unless*, *will* in the main clause.
9. **Should the server go down overnight, call me.** — The inverted remote condition is the formal written register asked for; *If the server goes down* would be the neutral one.
10. **He speaks English as if he had grown up in the United States.** — *As if* + past perfect, because the comparison is false. *As if he grew up* would suggest it might be true.

### F. Find and fix the error

1. **If it rains tomorrow, we'll cancel the picnic.** — No *will* in the *if*-clause; its position already makes it future.
2. **If I had known, I would have come to the hospital.** — Two errors: *would have* belongs only in the result clause, and the perfect takes the participle *come*, not the past simple *came*.
3. **If I were rich, I would buy a house on the coast.** — *Would* out of the condition; the unreal present is carried by *were*.
4. **Unless you hurry, we'll miss the train.** — *Unless* already means "if not," so the added *don't* reverses the sentence.
5. **If she hadn't reminded me, I would have forgotten the appointment.** — *Unless* cannot introduce a counterfactual. Only *if … not* can.
6. **I wish I knew the answer.** — *Wish* + *would* is reserved for somebody else's behavior, and it never takes a state verb.
7. **I wish I had more free time this semester.** — *Wish* requires the unreal past; the present tense cannot follow it.
8. **It's time we went home.** — *It's time* + subject takes the unreal past. Without a subject, write *It's time to go home.*
9. **Had I known, I would have said something.** — The inversion is *had* + subject + participle; the extra *have* has no job to do.
10. **I'd rather you called me tomorrow.** — A different subject after *would rather* takes the unreal past, never *will*.
11. **We'll cancel the trip if the storm hits.** — No comma before a subordinate clause that follows its main clause.
12. **Even if it rains tomorrow, we're going.** — *Even though* takes facts; a future hypothesis needs *even if*.
