# Time

> Telling the time, saying the date, every preposition that places an event, the phrasal verbs, the idioms, and the American conventions nobody writes down.

[← All grammar topics](../README.md) &middot; [Library home](../../README.md)

Time is not a word class, which is exactly why it is hard to look up. The pieces are scattered: *at* and *by* are prepositions, *already* and *soon* are adverbs, *hour* and *deadline* are nouns, *put off* and *wrap up* are verbs, and *in the nick of time* belongs to no class at all. A learner who wants to say when something happens has to assemble a sentence out of five different chapters.

This topic gathers them in one place, organized by **what you are trying to say** rather than by what part of speech the word turns out to be.

```
What time is it?        →  quarter after six, six fifteen, ten of seven
When is it?             →  on Monday, in March, at six, last week (no preposition)
How long?               →  for two hours, since Monday, all day long
How often?              →  twice a week, every other day, once in a blue moon
When, relative to now?  →  already, yet, still, ago, soon, by then
Moving it               →  push it back, move it up, reschedule, pencil me in
```

Three things make American time language specific, and each gets its own treatment:

- **The date order is reversed.** *3/7/2026* is March 7 in the United States and July 3 in most of the world, Russia included.
- **The clock is read two ways.** *Six forty-five* and *quarter to seven* are both ordinary, and Americans add *of* and *till* (*ten of seven*) where British English does not.
- **Some words are genuinely ambiguous**, and pretending otherwise does not help: *biweekly*, *next Friday*, *momentarily*, and *12 a.m.* all have two readings in real American use. The sections say so and tell you what to write instead.

For a Russian speaker the recurring problems are structural rather than lexical: *в* splits into *at*, *on* and *in*; *через час* is *in an hour*, not *through* one; *уже* and *ещё* fan out into *already*, *yet*, *still* and *anymore*; *до пяти* is *until five* for duration but *by five* for a deadline; and *полседьмого* means 6:30 while *half seven* means 7:30 — the inversion that produces the most confident wrong answer in the whole topic.
