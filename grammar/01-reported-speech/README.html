Reported Speech#

Everything about reporting what someone said — the tense changes, the pronoun and time shifts, questions, commands, modals, and the 313 verbs you can report with.

← All grammar topics · Library home

Reported speech (also called indirect speech) is how you say what someone else said without quoting them. English handles it with a system of coordinated changes: the tense moves back, the pronouns re-point at whoever is talking now, and the words for here and now re-anchor to wherever the reporting is happening.

"I'll call you tomorrow," she said.        ← direct speech
She said she would call me the next day.   ← reported speech

Three things changed at once — will → would, you → me, tomorrow → the next day — and that coordination is what makes the topic harder than any single rule in it.

Start here if you are in a hurry#

Three rules cause most of the errors:

  1. In a reported question, the word order goes back to normal. She asked where I was, never ✗where was I. And no do: He asked what I wanted, never ✗what did I want. → Questions
  2. Some verbs cannot take a person before their complement. ✗suggested me to leave, ✗explain me the rule, ✗said me. The repairs are suggested that I leave, explain the rule to me, told me. → Advising
  3. Backshift is not automatic. If the statement is still true, Americans routinely leave the tense alone: She said she lives in Boston is perfectly normal. → When not to backshift
