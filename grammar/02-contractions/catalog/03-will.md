# Contractions of *will*

> *I'll*, *it'll*, *there'll* — the future contracted onto the subject.

[← Contractions](../README.md) &middot; [All groups](README.md)

*'ll* is *will* after any subject. In fast speech it almost disappears — *I'll* is often just a lengthened *I* with an /l/ — which is why learners frequently do not hear the future at all. Note that *he'll* and *heal*, *we'll* and *wheel*, *I'll* and *aisle* are near or exact homophones.

---
### I'll

**Pronunciation:** /aɪl/ &middot; *YL*
**Русский:** я буду, я сделаю — сокращение от *I will*
**Expands to:** *I will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Contrast:** *I'll* is an exact homophone of *aisle* and *isle*; only the spelling separates them. And no contraction may end a clause: the answer to *Will you come?* is *I will*, never ✗ *I'll*.

The contraction of *I will*. Besides the plain future it carries the use Russian speakers most often lose — a decision made at the moment of speaking. The phone rings and an American says *I'll get it*, where Russian uses a present-tense perfective (*я открою*); learners produce ✗ *I get it*, which sounds like a statement of habit.

1. Someone's at the door — *I'll* get it.
2. *I'll* be thirty in March.
3. Text me when you land and *I'll* come pick you up.

---

### you'll

**Pronunciation:** /juːl/ &middot; *YOOL*
**Русский:** ты будешь, вы будете — сокращение от *you will*
**Expands to:** *you will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Question form:** *Will you help me?* — *'ll* never inverts, so the question puts the full *will* in front of the subject. There is no ✗ *'ll you help me?*; *You'll help me?* exists only as a surprised echo, said with rising intonation.
**Contrast:** *You'll* and *yule* (as in *yule log*) are homophones, both /juːl/. In fast speech it reduces further, to /jəl/, so *You'll see* comes out as 'yuhl-SEE' — the whole future is one unstressed /l/.

The contraction of *you will*, singular or plural.

1. *You'll* need a passport, not just a driver's license.
2. If you leave now, *you'll* miss the worst of the traffic.
3. Don't worry — *you'll* get used to it.

---

### he'll

**Pronunciation:** /hiːl/ &middot; *HEEL*
**Русский:** он будет, он сделает — сокращение от *he will*
**Expands to:** *he will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Contrast:** *He'll* is an exact homophone of *heal* and *heel*, and the vowel has to stay long: with the short vowel of *bed* you have said *hell*. After another word the /h/ usually drops, so *when he'll be back* runs together as 'when-EEL'.

The contraction of *he will*.

1. *He'll* be back on Monday.
2. Ask Daniel — *he'll* know how the filing system works.
3. I doubt *he'll* remember me.

---

### she'll

**Pronunciation:** /ʃiːl/ &middot; *SHEEL*
**Русский:** она будет, она сделает — сокращение от *she will*
**Expands to:** *she will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Contrast:** Keep the vowel long — *she'll* has the vowel of *see*; the short vowel of *bed* gives *shell*. In fast speech the contraction can shrink to a syllabic /l/, so *She'll call you* is often just 'shl-CALL-you' and the future is easy to miss.

The contraction of *she will*.

1. *She'll* be here around seven.
2. Give Priya the file and *she'll* take it from there.
3. I don't think *she'll* mind.

---

### it'll

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪtəl/ &middot; *IT-uhl*
**Русский:** оно будет, это будет — сокращение от *it will*
**Expands to:** *it will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** informal — everywhere in speech, but rare on the page outside dialogue, email, and text messages
**Contrast:** The /t/ flaps, so *it'll* is two syllables that sound like 'IH-duhl', close to *it all*. Stress tells them apart: *it all* leans on *all*, *it'll* leans on *it*. In anything formal, write *it will*.

The contraction of *it will*. It is one of the commonest things an American says and one of the rarest to see printed — in a report or a cover letter it is spelled out.

1. Don't worry — *it'll* be fine.
2. *It'll* take about twenty minutes to get downtown.
3. If you leave the milk out, *it'll* go bad.

---

### we'll

**Pronunciation:** /wiːl/ &middot; *WEEL*
**Русский:** мы будем, мы сделаем — сокращение от *we will*
**Expands to:** *we will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Contrast:** *We'll* is an exact homophone of *wheel*; American English lost the old *wh-* sound. The trap is length: the vowel is long, and shortening it turns *we'll* into *will*, so *We'll see* is heard as *will see*, which is not a sentence.

The contraction of *we will*.

1. *We'll* let you know by Friday.
2. If the weather holds, *we'll* eat outside.
3. *We'll* have been married ten years this June.

---

### they'll

**Pronunciation:** /ðeɪl/ &middot; *THAYL*
**Русский:** они будут, они сделают — сокращение от *they will*
**Expands to:** *they will*
**Type:** will — subject pronoun + *will*
**Register:** standard — normal in speech and in all but the most formal writing
**Contrast:** *They'll* and *there'll* sound close but do different work: *they'll* points back to people or things already named, while *there'll* introduces something new (*There'll be trouble*). The possessive *their* never contracts — ✗ *their'll* does not exist.

The contraction of *they will*.

1. *They'll* be here by six.
2. Ask the neighbors — *they'll* know when the truck comes.
3. I doubt *they'll* agree to that price.

---

### there'll

**Pronunciation:** /ðɛrl/ &middot; *THAIRL*
**Русский:** будет, будут (в обороте *there will be*) — сокращение от *there will*
**Expands to:** *there will*
**Type:** will — existential *there* + *will*
**Register:** standard — ordinary in speech and in most writing; the full form is only needed where the sentence wants weight
**Contrast:** Not the possessive: *their* never takes *'ll*, and ✗ *their'll* is not a word. In running speech *there'll* is barely distinguishable from *they'll*, and only the sense sorts them out.

The contraction of *there will*, almost always in front of *be*: it is the future of *there is* and *there are*. Like those, it takes its number from what follows rather than from *there* — *there'll be one chair*, *there'll be ten chairs*.

1. *There'll* be about forty people at the wedding.
2. *There'll* always be someone who complains.
3. I don't think *there'll* be enough chairs.

---

### that'll

**Pronunciation:** /ˈðætəl/ &middot; *THAT-uhl*
**Русский:** это будет, этого хватит — сокращение от *that will*
**Expands to:** *that will*
**Type:** will — demonstrative *that* + *will*
**Register:** informal — common in speech, above all in fixed phrases; rare in formal writing
**Contrast:** The /t/ flaps, so *that'll* is two syllables: 'THAD-uhl'. Keep *that'll do* ('that is enough, stop') apart from *that'll do it* ('that finishes the job') — both are everyday, and they are not the same remark.

The contraction of *that will*. Two fixed phrases carry much of its work: *that'll do* means 'enough, stop there', and *that'll be the day* is sarcasm — it says the thing will never happen. A cashier's *that'll be fourteen dollars* is the plain future being polite.

1. *That'll* be fourteen dollars even.
2. *That'll* do — we don't need any more chairs.
3. Marcus, apologize? *That'll* be the day.

---

### who'll

**Pronunciation:** /huːl/ &middot; *HOOL*
**Русский:** кто будет — сокращение от *who will*
**Expands to:** *who will*
**Type:** will — question word + *will*
**Register:** standard — ordinary in speech and in most writing; the full form is only needed where the sentence wants weight
**Question form:** *Who'll* drive? — *who* is the subject, so nothing inverts and the contraction survives inside the question, unlike *you'll*, which has to open out into *Will you…?*
**Contrast:** *Who'll* rhymes with *cool*, not with *whole* — it is not a homophone of *whole* or *hole*.

The contraction of *who will*, in questions and in reported questions.

1. *Who'll* drive us to the airport?
2. We still don't know *who'll* be running the meeting.
3. *Who'll* go first?

---

### what'll

**Pronunciation:** /ˈwʌtəl/ &middot; *WUT-uhl*
**Русский:** что будет, что сделает — сокращение от *what will*
**Expands to:** *what will*
**Type:** will — question word + *will*
**Register:** informal — speech and written dialogue only; formal writing keeps *what will*
**Question form:** *What'll* you have? — with *what* as the object, *will* still inverts with the subject, but the *'ll* rides on *what*, not on *you*: never ✗ *What you'll have?*
**Contrast:** The /t/ flaps, so *what'll* is a near-homophone of *waddle*: *What'll I do?* arrives as 'WAH-dul-eye-doo'. That is why learners miss the question's future entirely — the whole of *will* is one unstressed syllable.

The contraction of *what will*.

1. *What'll* happen if I press this?
2. *What'll* you have to drink?
3. *What'll* it take to get this done today?
